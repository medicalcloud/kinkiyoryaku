# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When one with yellowing has fever around late afternoon and, on the contrary, aversion to cold, this is contracted from sexual taxation. Tension of the bladder, fullness of the lesser abdomen, yellowing of the whole body, a black forehead, and heat in the soles then turn into black jaundice. The abdomen is distended as if with water, the stool is surely black and at times loose. This is a disease of sexual taxation, not water. Abdominal fullness is difficult to cure. Use the following formula.

Xiao Fan san (Niter and Alum Powder) formula

Xiaoshi (niter) and fanshi (alum) (burned), equal amounts.

Powder the two ingredients above, mix with barley gruel, and take a square-inch spoonful, three times a day. The disease leaves with the stool and urine; the urine is pure yellow and the stool pure black — this is the sign.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This distinguishes in detail the pattern of sexual-taxation jaundice. At first it does not become black at once, so it resembles the other yellowings; hence "one with yellowing." But there is fever around late afternoon with aversion to cold instead. In sudden exterior patterns, fever and aversion to cold may appear together without fixed times. In malaria, with fever there is no aversion to cold and with aversion to cold no fever, also without fixed times. In taxation heat of spleen and stomach there is only heat without aversion to cold, always when the sun declines. This alone is specifically in the late afternoon, the *shen* hour, when qi and blood pour into the bladder. Earlier it said "dusk" and here "late afternoon," speaking together of the *shen* and *you* hours; at the *you* hour qi and blood pour into the kidney. From the fever we know yin vacuity generates heat; from the aversion to cold we know the kidney is extremely vacuous and cannot bear guest cold; from its arising around late afternoon we know the defensive qi combines with kidney and bladder, and the vacuous kidney cannot bear the heat. Hence "this is contracted from sexual taxation." The kidney governs the lower burner with the bladder as its bowel, so tension of the bladder, fullness of the lesser abdomen, and heat in the soles must appear together. The forehead is above, but when water is exuberant it tends to go over the forehead, so fire is restrained by water and the kidney's color appears on the forehead — black being the kidney's color. But since it says "yellowing of the whole body," at first it is not black at once. As the disease spreads and the right becomes ever more deficient, the evil runs ever more rampant; hence "then turn into black jaundice," meaning kidney evil spreads through the whole body, not only the forehead. Thus the abdomen is distended as if with water: when water runs rampant, earth is ruined. Thus the stool is black: kidney evil spreads into the intestines and stomach, not only the body. At times loose stools: when earth is ruined it becomes muddy and not firm. But the distension resembles water and is not true water; the lower burner is originally cold water, but it does not actually bind, and urination is spontaneously free; hence "this is a disease of sexual taxation, not water." If abdominal fullness is added, earth is ruined and kidney evil is all the harder to restrain; hence "difficult to cure."

Xiao Fan san governs it because xiaoshi can disperse vacuous, depressed heat, being light and easily shed, and though cold it does not damage the spleen; fanshi can repel water, and wherever it reaches, evil no longer invades — like paper treated with alum that no longer lets water seep through. Used together they disperse depressed heat and resolve kidney toxin; they have nothing to do with treatments of qi and blood, yin and yang, sweating and purging, supplementing and draining; that is why they are good. Xiao Fan san formula.

Make the two ingredients above into a powder, mix with barley gruel, and take a square-inch spoonful, three times a day. The disease [leaves] with the stool [and urine] ...

## Notes

A clause describing in detail the signs of sexual-taxation jaundice (jaundice of kidney vacuity from sexual excess) and its change into black jaundice, using Xiaoshi Fanshi san.

- Late-afternoon fever with aversion to cold, bladder tension, lower abdominal fullness, yellowing of the whole body, a black forehead, and hot soles are given. The abdomen is distended as if with water but it is not water disease; the stool is black and at times loose.
- Xiaoshi Fanshi san, niter and alum taken with barley gruel, is said to remove stagnant heat and dampness from below.
- For "use the following formula," the other sources read "Xiaoshi Fanshi san governs it."
- The base reads 日晡時 and 面反; these are emended to 日晡所 and 而反 because all other sources so read (see the emendation notes).

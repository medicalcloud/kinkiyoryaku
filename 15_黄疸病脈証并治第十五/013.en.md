# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Grain jaundice: cold and heat, not eating, dizziness as soon as one eats, unrest in the heart and chest; turning yellow over time, it is grain jaundice. Yinchen tang (Virgate Wormwood Decoction) governs it.

Yinchen tang formula

Yinchenhao (inchinko; virgate wormwood) 6 liang; zhizi (shishi; gardenia) 14 pieces; dahuang (daio) 2 liang.

Of the three ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the yinchen, reducing by 6 sheng; add the three [read: two] ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses. Urination should become free; the urine is like gleditsia juice, pure red in color. In one night the abdomen subsides, and the yellow leaves through the urine.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The name grain jaundice seems to mean grain causes the disease, but its origin is still external contraction. So although the first section did not mention fever, it specially set out the four characters "wind and cold contend," and cold and heat also occur. "Not eating, dizziness as soon as one eats" says that dizziness is the foremost definite evidence of grain jaundice. Grain jaundice is a stomach disease, but the heart and chest are above the mouth of the stomach, and when turbid qi fumes upward, the heart and chest are restless. While the disease is not severe, the heat is not severe; when depression lasts long, the heat becomes severe and spreads through the flesh and exterior. Hence "turning yellow over time, it is grain jaundice." The medicinals yinchen, zhizi, and dahuang center on opening depression and resolving heat, neither releasing the exterior nor attacking the interior. Yinchen, bitter, acrid, and cold, is good at opening depression in the flesh; zhizi, light, floating, and cool, can resolve internal depression and bring down tortuous fire; dahuang is a purgative, but following zhizi and yinchen it is taken to assist them in opening depression and resolving heat. So yinchen is the largest and dahuang small.

Discussion: The first earlier section on grain jaundice did not mention cold and heat but had blocked urination; the second did not mention unrest of the heart and chest but had necessarily difficult urination; this one alone does not mention urination. Grain jaundice too varies in severity. The earlier "blocked urination" is the severe, urgent form. "Yangming disease with a slow pulse: urination will surely be difficult" is where Yangming signs are already present and a slow pulse with deficiency prevents movement, occasionally found within exterior disease. Here, with cold and heat, it is not a disease of two or three days. "Not eating, dizziness as soon as one eats" shows that, despite dizziness, eating has never stopped. Hence "turning yellow over time," showing it develops slowly over a long time, gradual and moderate in momentum, so urination has not reached blockage. But the note under the formula, "in one night the abdomen subsides," shows that urination is also not easy and the abdomen slightly distended; one simply need not focus on it. Only one formula is given for the three grain-jaundice patterns, because once Yangming disease reaches yellowing, whether long or short, opening depression and resolving heat is appropriate; so this formula is truly the main one. For yin jaundice, later people combined fuzi (bushi) with yinchen, a variant of this formula. Note: unrest of the heart and chest differs from the anguish in the heart of alcohol jaundice: there, heat in the heart reaches a state of helplessness; here "unrest" is only slight vexation, the "vexation and dizziness" of the Yangming slow-pulse pattern.

## Notes

A clause using Yinchenhao tang (Inchinkoto) for grain jaundice with cold and heat, loss of appetite, dizziness after eating, and unrest in the chest, turning yellow over time. It is the representative formula for jaundice and also appears in the Yangming chapter of the *Shanghan lun*.

- Yinchenhao removes damp-heat, zhizi clears heat, and dahuang purges stagnant heat; the urine turns red and the yellow leaves through the urine.
- The base names it Yinchen tang; most other sources read Yinchenhao tang.
- 内三味 ("add the three ingredients") should be two; Wikisource and Xu Bin's edition read 内二味.

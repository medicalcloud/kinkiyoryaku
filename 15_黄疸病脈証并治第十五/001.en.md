# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is floating and moderate. Floating means wind; moderate means impediment. [This] impediment is not wind-strike. The limbs suffer from vexation; the spleen's color must be yellow; stagnant heat thereby moves [outward].

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says in general that jaundice at first arises from wind carrying cold and dampness, and later turns to heat. To distinguish it first: if the inch pulse is floating and moderate, floating and moderate also govern wind specifically. But while floating is wind, speaking from the yellowing, moderate carries dampness; hence "impediment": dampness and heat steam each other and the flesh is impeded. The *Neijing* says, "Wind, cold, and dampness combine to form impediment," so wind alone cannot sum up the disease; hence "impediment is not wind-strike." But heat is the nature of the disease and wind its cause. Wind and heat are yang evils, and yang evils enter the yang; the limbs are the root of all yang; when the evil enters, one suffers from vexation — vexation being wind-heat. The limbs also belong to the spleen; the spleen belongs to earth, and earth's color is yellow; hence "the spleen's color must be yellow." It shows that though the causes of jaundice differ, it must involve internal damage to the spleen. But when it becomes yellow, the heat, on the contrary, is not hard-bound within; hence "stagnant heat thereby moves." This says that jaundice is generally due to depressed heat steaming outward.

Discussion: Zhongjing at the outset sets out the pulse of jaundice, centering on wind and extending to impediment; this clearly says that jaundice involves wind, cold, and dampness together. So later he speaks of "wind and cold contending" and notes, "what those with yellowing contract, they contract from dampness." But looking at the formulas given later, although grain jaundice, sexual-taxation jaundice, alcohol jaundice, and regular jaundice are distinguished, none treats wind, cold, or dampness exclusively; they only clear heat and open depression, using medicinals according to whether the cause lies in the lung, stomach, spleen, or kidney, and never adding supplementation. Is it not that the method of treating jaundice centers on clearing heat and opening depression, and although sweating and purging are also mentioned, breaking qi, warm supplementation, great sweating, and great purging are all inappropriate?

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on jaundice. It explains the formation of jaundice by a floating, moderate inch pulse.

- Floating is wind and moderate is dampness (impediment); wind-damp damages the spleen, heat becomes depressed and moves outward, causing yellowing. "Stagnant heat thereby moves" is often quoted as the key to the pathology of jaundice.
- Xu Bin holds that jaundice should be treated mainly by clearing heat and opening depression, and that great sweating, great purging, and warm supplementation are inappropriate.

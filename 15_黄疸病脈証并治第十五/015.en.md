# Clause 15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Alcohol jaundice with anguish in the heart, or heat and pain: Zhizi Dahuang tang (Gardenia and Rhubarb Decoction) governs it.

Zhizi Dahuang tang formula

Zhizi 14 pieces; dahuang 2 liang; zhishi 5 pieces; chi (fermented soybeans) 1 sheng.

Of the four ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng and take warm in three doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The earlier regular clause on alcohol jaundice also had inability to eat and desire to vomit, and the later changed patterns vary — inhibited urination, hot soles, abdominal fullness. Here only anguish in the heart is singled out as the foremost definite evidence of alcohol jaundice; heat reaching pain is even more severe. Zhizi Dahuang tang is used because alcohol heat damages both qi and blood, and one wants to expel it quickly. So zhishi assists dahuang to bring down qi and resolve heat in the blood aspect, and fermented soybeans assist zhizi to clear the diaphragm and disperse heat in the qi aspect. Alcohol always carries dampness, but since the yin is greatly damaged, drying medicinals are not used lest they consume the fluids, nor percolating medicinals lest they exhaust the fluids; that is, when the heat disperses, the dampness cannot remain. Thus the treatment of all damp-heat diseases with dryness can be understood from this.

[Xu Bin's formula:] zhizi 14 pieces; dahuang 1 liang; zhishi 5 pieces; chi 1 sheng.

## Notes

A clause using Zhizi Dahuang tang for alcohol jaundice with anguish, heat, and pain in the chest.

- It is Zhizi Chi tang (zhizi, fermented soybeans) plus dahuang and zhishi, said to clear heat in the chest and remove heat by purging.
- Most other sources give dahuang as 1 liang.

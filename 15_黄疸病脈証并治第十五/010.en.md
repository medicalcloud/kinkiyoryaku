# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Abdominal fullness, the tongue [read: body] withered and yellow, and dryness [read: agitation] with inability to sleep: this belongs to those with yellowing ("tongue-withered" is suspected to be "body-withered" [base note: 右痿疑作心痿, "the 'withered' above is suspected to be 'heart-withered'"]).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that in jaundice one or two branch signs may appear first, by which one can be sure it is jaundice. Deep is a yin pulse. If with a deep pulse there is, on the contrary, thirst and inhibited urination, what is it if not depressed heat? When heat is depressed, how could there be no yellowing?

Abdominal fullness is an interior sign. If with abdominal fullness there is also a withered-yellow body and agitation with inability to sleep, the stagnant heat is moving outward; this is the beginning of yellowing. Hence "belongs to those with yellowing," showing that one should plan treatment when it is about to form, not wait until it has formed before giving medicine.

## Notes

A clause stating that abdominal fullness, a withered-yellow body, and agitation with insomnia indicate someone who will develop jaundice.

- 舌痿黄 ("tongue withered and yellow") is hard to interpret, and the interlinear note (by the Song editors) suspects an error. The base note reads "右痿疑作心痿," but most other sources read "舌痿疑作身痿" ("tongue-withered is suspected to be body-withered"), so the note has been emended (see the emendation notes). Xu Bin also explains it as "a withered-yellow body," which the translation follows.
- For 燥, Daizhige reads 躁 ("agitation").

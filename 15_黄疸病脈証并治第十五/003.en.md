# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Yangming disease with a slow pulse, it is difficult to eat to fullness; eating to fullness brings vexation and dizziness, and urination will surely be difficult. This is grain jaundice about to develop. Even if purged, the abdominal fullness remains as before; the reason is that the pulse is slow.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that grain jaundice may lean toward cold. Grain jaundice is originally a disease of the Yangming fu organ. If a person has Yangming disease with body heat, sweating, and no aversion to cold, or with internal excess and no bowel movement, the pulse should not be slow, yet it is. Slow means the stomach is deficient, cold is depressed, and the stomach is ill; so one can eat a little but cannot bear eating to fullness. When full, [the food] does not move, so fire gathers and vexation arises, dampness heats the eyes and there is dizziness, and turbid qi flows down so that urination is difficult. But here the yin has received the cold, cold overcomes heat, and the heat has not yet flowed into the bladder, though it tends to arrive gradually; hence "grain jaundice about to develop." It is originally not stomach excess, so purging leaves the abdominal fullness as before. If the stomach were not deficient and cold, water and grain would transform by themselves; how could jaundice form? Hence "the reason is that the pulse is slow."

## Notes

A clause stating that in Yangming disease with a slow pulse (stomach deficiency cold), one cannot eat to fullness, and eating brings vexation, dizziness, and difficult urination, meaning grain jaundice is about to develop. The same text appears in the Yangming chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 195).

- Because it is due to deficiency cold, purging does not relieve the abdominal fullness.
- The base reads 炮炮 and 痃; these are emended to 飽飽 and 眩 because all other sources so read (see the emendation notes).

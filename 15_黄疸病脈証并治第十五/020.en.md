# Clause 20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jaundice with urine of unchanged color, a tendency to spontaneous diarrhea, and abdominal fullness with dyspnea: the heat must not be removed; if the heat is removed, there will surely be hiccup. For hiccup, Xiao Banxia tang (Shohangeto) governs it (the formula appears under wasting and thirst and summer heat stroke [other sources: phlegm-fluid retention]).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that within jaundice there may be true cold with false heat. With internal excess the urine must be red; now its color is unchanged, and spontaneous diarrhea is added — this is deficiency cold. The abdomen can be full from heat, but also from deficiency; excess patterns have dyspnea, but deficiency also pants. If mistakenly taken as heat and attacked, the stomach is made deficient and there is hiccup. Hiccup is qi counterflowing due to stomach deficiency, and counterflow congests phlegm. Hence "for hiccup, Xiao Banxia tang governs it." Hiccup is no small matter, so only shengjiang (shokyo) and banxia (hange) can move phlegm, bring down counterflow, and regulate the stomach; after the stomach is regulated one treats with judgment. It is not that Xiao Banxia tang can at once treat jaundice.

## Notes

A clause stating that jaundice with urine of normal color, a tendency to diarrhea, abdominal fullness, and dyspnea is deficiency cold (true cold, false heat); the heat must not be removed, and if it is mistakenly removed, hiccup results, treated with Xiao Banxia tang.

- For the note "appears under wasting and thirst and summer heat stroke," most other sources read "under phlegm-fluid retention" (Xiao Banxia tang is in the chapter on phlegm-fluid retention and cough).

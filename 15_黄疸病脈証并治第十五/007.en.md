# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When alcohol jaundice is purged, over time it becomes black jaundice: the eyes are green-blue, the face black, the heart feels as if one had eaten minced garlic, the stool is pure black, and scratching the skin produces no sensation. The pulse is floating and weak; though black, it is slightly yellow; hence one knows [it is alcohol jaundice].

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Alcohol is hot and yang by nature, and the upper jiao receives it first; so the earlier note on alcohol jaundice took anguish with heat, inability to eat, and frequent desire to vomit as its definite signs. But the interdependent disease does not stop at the upper part. Water issues from the high source; when damp-heat of the upper jiao is already severe, how could urination be free? Hence "there is always inhibited urination." Heat in the heart is natural, but the sole is the region of the kidney; when damp-heat flows down, the kidney receives it, and the sole too becomes hot; hence "these are its signs." But since it comes from heat in the heart, it cannot be equated with the blocked urination of grain jaundice or the hot soles of sexual-taxation jaundice.

Among the changed patterns of alcohol jaundice, there are also those in which the heat leaves the heart and there is no heat, the speech clear and distinct, the pathogen finally pouring into Yangming, with abdominal fullness, desire to vomit, and a dry nose. If the pathogen is near the top, the pulse must be floating and vomiting should be induced; if near the bottom, the pulse must be deep and wiry and purging is appropriate. For treating Yangming there are only the two methods of vomiting and purging. "First" means that if some disease remains, one considers again.

In alcohol jaundice with heat in the heart, what is feared is bound heat not moving. If there is a desire to vomit, it is precisely the moment when the pathogenic heat wants to come out; hence "inducing vomiting cures it."

Further, alcohol jaundice mistakenly purged may produce mixed changed patterns resembling sexual-taxation jaundice; but the heart, the pulse, and the yellow within the black must differ slightly. Hence "when alcohol jaundice is purged, over time it becomes black jaundice." Alcohol originally damages the upper part, and the pulse has not yet become deep, so the lower part is not yet hot. Mistaken purging lets the Yangming disease pathogen enter Shaoyin by a branch, and gradually the kidney is damaged; when damaged, there is black jaundice. Since *yi* and *gui* share a source, the liver too falls ill and the eyes are green-blue; kidney qi rises and the face is black. Yet the heart still feels as if one had eaten minced garlic; so although the lower part is ill, the alcohol heat has not been removed. Pure black stool is kidney pathogen rising. Numb skin is earth damaged, causing impediment. But though the kidney pathogen is exuberant, the healthy qi is actually deficient, so the pulse is floating and weak. If so, it closely resembles sexual-taxation jaundice; how does one distinguish it as alcohol jaundice? Though the spleen is damaged and yellow and the kidney damaged by mistaken purging, it is actually due to alcohol, so the pulse remains floating, and within the black there must be, unlike true sexual-taxation jaundice, a slight yellow. Saying "though black, it is slightly yellow; hence one knows" shows a fine distinction.

## Notes

A clause stating that alcohol jaundice mistakenly purged becomes, over time, black jaundice (green-blue eyes, black face and stool), but a floating, weak pulse and a slight yellow within the black show it to be from alcohol jaundice, distinguishing it from sexual-taxation jaundice.

- Xu Bin's commentary explains the alcohol-jaundice clauses of units 4 through 7 together.
- The base reads 瓜之; it is emended to 爪之 ("scratching") because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jaundice with abdominal fullness, inhibited and red urine, and spontaneous sweating: this is harmony of the exterior and repletion of the interior; it should be purged. Dahuang Huashi tang [other sources: Dahuang Xiaoshi tang] is appropriate.

Dahuang Huashi tang

Dahuang, huangbo (phellodendron), and huashi [other sources: xiaoshi], 4 liang each; zhizi 15 pieces.

Of the four ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the huashi [xiaoshi], boil again down to 1 sheng, and take in a single dose.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of jaundice with interior and without exterior [disease]. The yellow color of jaundice appears in the exterior; yet the abdomen is full and urine inhibited and red, so we know there is interior heat. Jaundice is very hard to sweat; if sweat comes spontaneously, the exterior resolves through sweating. Hence "harmony of the exterior and repletion of the interior." Repletion means evil; where there is evil it should be removed; so Dahuang Xiaoshi tang governs it. Dahuang and xiaoshi resolve replete heat in qi and blood; huangbo, bitter and cold, governs the lower burner; zhizi, though light, floating, and above, can bring the interior heat down from above, so it is the envoy; and being light and floating, it suits depressed binding.

## Notes

A clause purging jaundice with abdominal fullness, inhibited red urine, and spontaneous sweating, in which the exterior is harmonious and heat is replete in the interior.

- The base names the formula Dahuang Huashi tang with huashi (talc), but all other sources read Dahuang Xiaoshi tang with xiaoshi (niter; written 硝石, or 消石 in icntcm and Xu Bin's edition); Xu Bin also explains it as xiaoshi. The base's huashi is probably an error, but the base is not emended because the five sources do not agree on a single character (硝 vs. 消).
- Dahuang, huangbo, and zhizi purge damp-heat, and xiaoshi removes stagnant heat.

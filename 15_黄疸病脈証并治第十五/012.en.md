# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jaundice with thirst is difficult to cure; jaundice without thirst can be cured. If it arises in the yin region, the person will surely retch; [if] in the yang region, the person has shivering chills and fever.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In treating jaundice, distinguishing inside and outside, yin and yang, is most important; so thirst, retching, and cold and heat are singled out to distinguish them. The yellow color of jaundice is the image of depressed heat steaming outward. With thirst, the internal heat is even more severe, inside and outside both ill, so it is difficult to cure. Without thirst, the heat diffuses outward and the right qi within moves by itself, so it can be cured. Yin governs the inner qi, so when retching comes from within, we know the yin region is counterflowing and depressed. Yang governs the outer defense, and cold and heat arise in the flesh and exterior, so when the disease is in the yang region there are shivering chills and fever. But the methods of distinction in these two clauses apply to all diseases, not only jaundice; only, since jaundice is a pattern extending from inside to outside, its depth and degree should be examined especially carefully.

## Notes

A clause judging the prognosis and location of jaundice by thirst (thirst means hard to cure), retching (yin region, interior), and chills with fever (yang region, exterior).

# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When one has jaundice with fever, vexation and panting, fullness of the chest, and a dry mouth, it is because at the onset of the disease the sweat was forced out with fire, and the two heats combined. But what those with yellowing contract, they contract from dampness. When the whole body is feverish, the face [other sources: and] yellow, and the belly hot, the heat is in the interior and it should be purged.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that, apart from grain, sexual-taxation, and alcohol jaundice, jaundice in general may be contracted through mistaken fire; and it distinguishes that contraction from dampness is the norm of jaundice, while heat in the interior is a variant of heat jaundice, so that people treat them separately. Jaundice need not lie solely in the upper burner, but there are cases with fever, vexation and panting, chest fullness, and dry mouth, heat and dryness both in the upper burner. Here an exterior disease without sweating had its sweat forced out with fire; the heat into which cold had changed and the heat forced by fire combined, so the qi was depressed and the flesh could not bear it and turned yellow. But dry fire cannot suddenly make a person yellow; all yellowing must be due to depressed dampness. So it also says generally, "what those with yellowing contract, they contract from dampness," meaning fire without dampness cannot produce yellowing. If the whole body is feverish and yellow and the belly is hot, the fever seems exterior, but the hot belly means interior signs predominate. Hence "the heat is in the interior and it should be purged," meaning one should not first attack the fire-heat of the upper burner.

## Notes

A clause stating that jaundice with fever, vexation, panting, chest fullness, and dry mouth results from heat compounded by mistaken fire-induced sweating, but that jaundice is originally contracted from dampness; with whole-body fever and a hot belly, the heat is interior and should be purged.

- "What those with yellowing contract, they contract from dampness" is often quoted as emphasizing dampness as the cause of jaundice.
- For 面 in 面黄, most other sources read 而 ("feverish and yellow").
- The base reads 口燥有; it is emended to 者 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

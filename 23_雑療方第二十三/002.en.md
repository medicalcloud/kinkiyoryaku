# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sanwu Beiji wan (Three-Ingredient Emergency Pill) formula (appears in the *Qianjin*; Sikong Pei Xiu says it may also be used as a powder; first mix it into a liquid and pour it into the mouth so that it enters between the teeth; it is extremely effective)

Dahuang 1 liang; ganjiang 1 liang; badou (croton seed) 1 liang (skin and heart removed, dry-fried, ground separately into a paste).

Each of the medicines above must be fine and fresh. First pound dahuang and ganjiang into powder, grind the badou and add it, pound together a thousand times, and use as a powder. Mixing with honey into pills is also good [base: 亦加蜜; other sources: 亦佳，密]; store in a sealed vessel and do not let [the qi] escape. It governs all sudden and violent diseases of the heart and abdomen. For malign strike or visiting hostility, with distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, sudden pain like an awl's stab, rapid breathing and clenched jaw, or sudden death from lodged corpse, give three or four pills the size of a soybean with warm water [base: 緩水 "lukewarm water"] or vinegar [base: 若酒 "or wine"]. If they do not go down, lift the head and pour them in so that they go down the throat; after a while there should be improvement. If not, give three more pills; there should be rumbling in the abdomen, and with vomiting and purging there is immediate improvement. If the jaw is clenched, one must break the teeth and pour it in.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The subtlety of this formula lies in ganjiang. Badou and dahuang are drastic purgatives, moving both cold and heat; with ganjiang guarding the center, the stem of life always remains, and it also frees the spirit light and restores the right nature; so it can treat all malign strike and sudden death.

[Part of Xu Bin's text:] ... pound together a thousand times and use as a powder. Mixing with honey into pills is also good. Store in a sealed vessel and do not let the qi escape.

... pain like an awl's stab, rapid breathing and clenched jaw, or sudden death from lodged corpse: give [pills] the size of a soybean with warm water or vinegar.

## Notes

The formula of Sanwu Beiji wan, used for sudden violent diseases of the heart and abdomen, malign strike, visiting hostility, sudden death, and the like.

- A formula purging drastically with badou and dahuang while guarding the center with ganjiang.
- For the final 口噤 ("clenched jaw"), the base reads 口禁, but all other sources read 噤, so it has been emended (see the emendations). For 禁 in the earlier "rapid breathing and clenched jaw," most other sources read 噤.

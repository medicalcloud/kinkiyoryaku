# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chaihu (saiko) Yinzi (Bupleurum Drink) formula, modified for the four seasons, to abate deficiency heat of the five zang

In the three months of winter, add chaihu 8 *fen*, baizhu (byakujutsu) 8 *fen*, chenpi (chinpi; tangerine peel) 5 *fen*, dafu binlang (daifukubinro; areca) 4 pieces (used with husk and seed), shengjiang (shokyo) 5 *fen*, and jiegeng (kikyo) 7 *fen*. In the three months of spring, add zhishi (kijitsu) and reduce baizhu: six ingredients in all. In the three months of summer, add shengjiang 3 *fen*, zhishi 5 *fen*, and gancao (kanzo) 3 *fen*: eight ingredients in all. In the three months of autumn, add chenpi 3 *fen*: six ingredients in all.

Chop each of the above and divide into three packets. Boil one packet with 3 sheng of water down to 2 sheng and take warm in three doses, one dose about every interval it takes a person to walk four or five li. If the four limbs are congested, add a little gancao, divide into three small packets, and boil each small packet with 1 sheng of water down to 7 ge and take warm; then combine the dregs into one dose and boil again, making four doses in all (probably not Zhongjing's formula).

Helile wan (Karirokugan; Chebule Pill) formula for long-term use (probably not Zhongjing's formula)

Helile (kariroku; dried), chenpi, and houpo (koboku), 3 liang each.

Powder the three ingredients above and make pills with refined honey the size of firmiana seeds. Take 20 pills with wine or a drink, increasing up to 30 pills.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This should be read in contrast with what the *Neijing* says: "All damage by cold becomes heat disease." Cold-damage pathogen comes from outside; pathogen from outside is a disease of the meridians and collaterals and is in excess pathogen. So here it says "five zang" to distinguish it from the exterior and "deficiency heat" to distinguish it from excess pathogen. That is, when the space among the five zang is assailed by deficiency pathogen and the qi therefore stagnates and does not flow freely, deficiency pathogen produces heat between exterior and interior. Precisely because it is deficiency pathogen, it occurs in all four seasons; and precisely because it is deficiency pathogen, it does not vary as exterior pathogen does in passing through the meridians. So one simply adjusts according to the qi of the four seasons, adding and subtracting as supplementing or draining is suitable. Chaihu is a harmonizing and resolving agent for exterior and interior, yin and yang, and by nature can raise the life-giving qi of Shaoyang; so it is the sovereign. Baizhu supplements the center and nourishes healthy qi; so it is the minister. In the body, when there is diffusion, the healthy qi flows freely; when there is congestion, qi surges and becomes heat. So jiegeng, opening and lifting the qi of the upper jiao, chenpi, disinhibiting the qi of the middle jiao, and binlang, easing the qi of the abdomen, are couriers. Shengjiang assists chaihu in diffusing outward and assists binlang in dispersing inward. Though named for abating deficiency heat, it does not rely entirely on supplementation, nor does it use cold agents, meaning that this heat is congested heat in the qi aspect, neither yin-deficiency fever nor externally contracted exterior pathogen.

More chaihu is added in winter because at this time the qi of Shaoyang is about to come out of the earth, so adding more chaihu to assist it makes yang grow, and when yang grows, the three yang are naturally at peace. In spring, sprouts gradually emerge, breaking their shells and seeking to stretch, so zhishi is added to set the mechanism in motion; baizhu is reduced for fear that dry earth would keep wood from flourishing. In summer, heat damages the original qi, and gancao's effect is like that of renshen (ninjin), so it alone is increased to assist baizhu in strengthening the center qi. But in late summer damp-heat is exuberant and qi stagnates, so the medicine is like that of spring with gancao added and baizhu not reduced, only adding zhishi and shengjiang so that diffusing and supplementing proceed together to aid its growth. The autumn medicine is the same as winter's; at this time qi gradually contracts, so a little chenpi is added to warm the center and ease the spleen. That is, the autumn–winter order of gathering and storing naturally differs from spring and summer.

[Xu Bin's list of the seasonal formulas:] The above: three months of winter (chaihu slightly more). Chaihu, chenpi, dafu binlang, shengjiang, jiegeng, zhishi — the above: three months of spring (compared with winter, baizhu reduced and zhishi added). Chaihu, baizhu, dafu binlang, chenpi, shengjiang, jiegeng, zhishi, gancao — the above: three months of summer (compared with spring, more gancao, still using baizhu). Chaihu, baizhu, dafu binlang, chenpi, shengjiang, jiegeng — the above: three months of autumn (the same as the three months of winter, only chenpi slightly more).

Divide each packet into three small packets, and boil each small packet with 1 sheng of water down to 7 ge and take warm.

Commentary: Helile is warm by nature, heavily bitter and lightly sour, with the effect of bringing qi down and dispersing phlegm. Used with wuweizi (gomishi) and wubeizi it astringes; with jupi (kippi) and houpo it brings qi down; with renshen it treats cough. It says "for long-term use" because helile's bringing down of qi carries sourness within bitterness, disinhibiting and also astringing; so the *Bencao* says it "breaks bound qi of the chest and diaphragm and frees fluids," and also that it "stops diarrhea and treats chronic cough." Hence it can disperse the hundred diseases of the abdomen and may be taken long-term. Helile 3 liang; chenpi 3 liang; houpo 3 liang.

## Notes

The first two formulas of the chapter on miscellaneous treatments: Chaihu Yinzi, modified by season, to abate deficiency heat of the five zang, and Helile wan for long-term use. Both carry the note "probably not Zhongjing's formula."

- Paragraphs 1–3 (from the name of the Chaihu Yinzi formula through "... (probably not Zhongjing's formula). Helile wan formula for long-term use (probably not Zhongjing's formula)") are missing from the base because of a lost leaf and have been supplied from Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*).
- The composition of Chaihu Yinzi is unclear. Xu Bin treats winter as the basic formula and lists spring, summer, and autumn as modifications compared with it.

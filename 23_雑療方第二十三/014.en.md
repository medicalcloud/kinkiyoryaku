# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

To rescue one who has hanged himself or herself: if from morning to dusk, even if already cold, the person can surely be treated; if from dusk to morning, it is a little difficult. Perhaps this should be said to be because angry [most other sources: yin] qi is exuberant. However, in summer the nights are shorter than the days, and it is hot, so it should still be

treatable. It is also said: if [base: 苦] the area below the heart is slightly warm, even after more than a day it can still be treated. The method:

Slowly hold and untie [the person]; do not cut the rope. Lay the person down calmly on bedding, top and bottom. One person treads on both shoulders with the feet and with the hands pulls the hair slightly, keeping it always taut and not letting go. One person presses on the chest with the hands and moves it repeatedly. One person rubs [other sources: strokes] the arms and shins and bends and stretches them; if already stiff, just gradually force them to bend, and also press the abdomen. After doing this for about the time it takes to cook a meal, breath comes out of the mouth, the person breathes and opens the eyes; but still keep pulling and pressing without stopping, yet do not cause distress or exhaustion. After a while, give a little cinnamon decoction and thin rice gruel to hold in the mouth, moistening the throat; when the person can gradually swallow, stop a little. If further two people blow into both ears through tubes, it is even better. This method is the best; there is no one who does not revive.

Whenever someone dies from summerheat stroke, one must not let the person get cold; if cold, the person dies at once. The method of treatment:

Bend grass into a belt, put it around the navel of the person struck by summerheat, and have two or three people urinate into it to warm it. One may also use hot mud mixed with the bent grass, or place the bottom of an overturned tile bowl or a cart's axle-box against the person and urinate into it, letting it flow away. This refers to being stranded on the road with no hot water; one should have people urinate into it, many people, to warm it. If there is hot water, it may be given; do not use mud or the axle-box, for fear these things are cold. Since summerheat stroke occurs in the summer months, if hot mud or a warm axle-box is available, they may also be used.

Formula to rescue the drowned

Take more than two shi of ashes from the stove and bury the person from head to foot; water comes out of the seven orifices and the person revives at once.

The above methods for treating hanging, drowning, and summerheat stroke all come from what Zhang Zhongjing devised. Their meaning is extraordinary, almost beyond ordinary understanding and beyond what materia medica can address; truly a great art of saving people. Writers on cold damage often discuss summerheat disease, but it is not this summerheat from encountering heat (appears in the *Waitai* and *Zhouhou*).

Formula to treat falls from a horse and all injuries of sinews and bones (appears in the *Zhouhou fang*)

Dahuang 1 liang (cut, soaked [base: 侵] in hot water, added when the decoction is done); scarlet silk (the size of a hand, burned to ash); tangled hair (the size of a hen's egg, used burned to ash); a long-used cooking cloth, 1 chi (burned to ash); old cattail (a handful, 3 cun); taoren 49 pieces (skin and tips removed, dry-fried); gancao (the size of a middle finger joint, mix-fried and chopped).

Decoct the seven ingredients above in a child's urine, the amount according to need; when the decoction is done, add a large cup of wine, then add the dahuang, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses. First chop half an old cattail mat and boil it into a bath, bathe [the patient], and cover with clothes and bedding. Shortly there will be several bowel movements and the pain will cease at once. Do not be alarmed if the stool and bathwater are red; it is static blood.

## Notes

Methods to rescue those who have hanged themselves, collapsed from summerheat, or drowned, and a formula for injuries of sinews and bones from falling from a horse and the like.

- The rescue method for hanging is sometimes noted as resembling artificial respiration and chest compression.
- The paragraph break before 治 ("treatable") follows a line break in the base.
- This part is absent from Xu Bin's text, so there is no commentary.

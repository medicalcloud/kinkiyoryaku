# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zishi Hanshi san (Fluorite Cold-Food Powder) formula, which treats cold damage so that after recovery it does not recur (appears in the *Qianjin yi*)

Zishiying (murasakisekiei; fluorite), baishiying (hakusekiei; quartz), chishizhi (shakusekishi), zhongru (stalactite) (crushed in a pestle and refined), guolougen (trichosanthes root), fangfeng (bofu) [base: fangyang], jiegeng (kikyo), wenge (bunko), and guijiu (podophyllum), 10 *fen* each; taiyi yuliang (limonite) 10 *fen* (calcined); ganjiang (kankyo); guizhi (keishi; bark removed) 4 *fen* [other sources have "fuzi (bushi; blast-fried, skin removed)" after ganjiang, and read "4 *fen* each"].

Pound the thirteen ingredients above into powder and take a square-inch spoonful with wine.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Savoring this formula thoroughly, one can grasp the method of gathering residual pathogen and harmonizing yin and yang after illness. "Cold damage" means the disease pathogen came from outside and something has not yet been completely cleared. One wishes to heal it so that it does not recur, but there is no longer pathogen to drive out, and supplementing would only stir the qi to no purpose. So the various mineral medicinals, which enter yin, secure the root, and clear heat, harmonize the yin; ganjiang, fuzi, and guizhi, which enter yang and move the root qi, restore the yang; fangfeng searches out latent wind; jiegeng opens and lifts lung qi; wenge disperses bound heat; and guijiu removes toxic malign qi. Among them, zhongru supplements the lung, yuliang boosts the spleen, and red and white chishizhi and zishiying supplement the heart, nourish the lung, and settle what floats. Though their supplementing and nourishing differ, in harmonizing yin they are one. Ganjiang strengthens the yang of the central palace, guizhi moves the yang of the upper jiao, and fuzi restores the yang of the lower jiao; these too differ, but in restoring yang they are one. Combined with gualou it has the power to regulate the prescription; combined with jiegeng it has the subtlety of opening. Then yin and yang are balanced and qi and blood regulated; from where would the disease recur? But the formula's name still has the two characters "cold food," which do not appear below the formula; perhaps a phrase about taking it with cold food was later lost. I cannot yet decide and await further consideration.

[Xu Bin's formula:] zishiying 10 *fen*; baishiying 10 *fen*; chishizhi 10 *fen*; zhongru (calcined) 10 *fen*; gualougen (karokon) 10 *fen*; fangfeng 10 *fen*; jiegeng 10 *fen*; wenge 10 *fen*; guijiu, 10 *fen* each; taiyi yuliang 10 *fen* (calcined); ganjiang, fuzi (blast-fried, skin removed), and guizhi (bark removed), 4 *fen* each. Pound the ten ingredients above into powder and take a square-inch spoonful with wine.

## Notes

The formula of Zishi Hanshi san (from the *Qianjin yifang*) to prevent recurrence after recovery from cold damage.

- Xu Bin explains it as harmonizing yin with mineral medicinals and restoring yang with ganjiang, fuzi, and guizhi.
- The base lacks fuzi and lists only twelve ingredients, yet says "thirteen"; the other sources include fuzi, making thirteen. For 防風 (fangfeng), the base reads 防羊; all other sources read 防風, so it has been emended (see the emendation notes).

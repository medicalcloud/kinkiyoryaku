# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is wiry and large. Wiry means diminution; large means scallion-stalk. Diminution means cold; scallion-stalk means vacuity. When vacuity and cold contend, it is called drumskin. In women there is half-birth (miscarriage) and spotting; in men there is blood loss and loss of essence.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks generally of vacuity taxation in which vacuous yin is exuberant and true yang declines. So the sunken, small, wiry, fine pulses are central, and even where scallion-stalk or large pulses occur, the signs still show the picture of declining yang.

A man who appears healthy, with no disease to blame, yet has a vacuous, weak, faint, fine pulse: this is vacuity heat in the yin aspect with weak original yang. When lying down, the outer qi enters the yin and the exterior becomes vacuous again, so he is prone to night sweating.

If a person aged fifty or sixty, whose yang qi has declined so that the pulse should be small and weak, has instead a large pulse, it seems not to be yang vacuity of the fine, small kind. But impediment running along both sides of the spine — "both sides" being the two sides of the spine — belongs to the taiyang channel, the yin being unable to flow to the back. Rumbling intestines and saber lumps and goiter mean the yang of the upper burner is vacuous and the nutritive heat of jueyin rises along the channel. So we know that the large pulse does not mean surplus yang. Hence "all are contracted through taxation."

If the pulse is sunken, small, and slow, there is no doubt that it is declining yang. The combination of the three pulses, sunken, small, and slow, means yang qi is entirely deficient; hence the name "qi desertion." When qi deserts, the body is an empty shell: walking quickly exhausts the qi with panting and gasping; the limbs have no yang and are cold; the abdomen has no yang and is full; in severe cases the stomach vacuity is extreme and there is loose diarrhea, and the spleen vacuity is extreme and food is not digested.

If the pulse is wiry under light pressure and large under heavy pressure, wiry is diminution and cold; large is scallion-stalk and vacuity. In all, the inside is vacuous and the outside cold, with qi bound in the yang aspect. Hence "when vacuity and cold contend, it is called drumskin." Drumskin is like the skin of a drum: the evil floating on the outside is replete. Then the inner qi is vacuous, and women cannot keep the fetus secure or regulate the menses, so there is miscarriage and spotting; men cannot store essence or command the blood, so there is blood loss and loss of essence.

## Notes

A clause attributing the wiry, large pulse (drumskin pulse) to vacuity cold, and taking it as a cause of miscarriage and spotting in women and blood loss and loss of essence in men. The same text appears again in the later chapters on fright palpitations and bleeding and on miscellaneous diseases of women.

- The drumskin pulse is taut on the outside but empty within on pressure, likened to the skin of a drum.
- Xu Bin's commentary explains the clauses of units 8 through 11 together as vacuity taxation of yang vacuity.

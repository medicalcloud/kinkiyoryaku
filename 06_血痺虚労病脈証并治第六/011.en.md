# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is wiry and large. Wiry means diminution; large means hollow. Diminution means cold; hollow means deficiency. When deficiency and cold contend, it is called tympanic. In women there is half-birth (miscarriage) and metrostaxis; in men there is blood loss and loss of essence.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks generally of deficiency/consumption in which deficient yin is exuberant and true yang declines. So the deep, small, wiry, thready pulses are central, and even where hollow or large pulses occur, the signs still show the picture of declining yang.

A man who appears healthy, with no disease to blame, yet has a deficient, weak, feeble, thready pulse: this is deficiency heat in the yin aspect with weak original yang. When lying down, the outer qi enters the yin and the exterior becomes deficient again, so he is prone to night sweats.

If a person aged fifty or sixty, whose yang qi has declined so that the pulse should be small and weak, has instead a large pulse, it seems not to be yang deficiency of the thready, small kind. But impediment running along both sides of the spine — "both sides" being the two sides of the spine — belongs to the Taiyang meridian, the yin being unable to flow to the back. Rumbling intestines and saber lumps and goiter mean the yang of the upper jiao is deficient and the ying-nutrient heat of Jueyin rises along the meridian. So we know that the large pulse does not mean surplus yang. Hence "all are contracted through taxation."

If the pulse is deep, small, and slow, there is no doubt that it is declining yang. The combination of the three pulses, deep, small, and slow, means yang qi is entirely deficient; hence the name "qi desertion." When qi deserts, the body is an empty shell: walking quickly exhausts the qi with dyspnea and gasping; the limbs have no yang and are cold; the abdomen has no yang and is full; in severe cases the stomach deficiency is extreme and there is loose diarrhea, and the spleen deficiency is extreme and food is not digested.

If the pulse is wiry under light pressure and large under heavy pressure, wiry is diminution and cold; large is hollow and deficiency. In all, the inside is deficient and the outside cold, with qi bound in the yang aspect. Hence "when deficiency and cold contend, it is called tympanic." Tympanic is like the skin of a drum: the pathogen floating on the outside is in excess. Then the inner qi is deficient, and women cannot keep the fetus secure or regulate the menses, so there is miscarriage and metrostaxis; men cannot store essence or command the blood, so there is blood loss and loss of essence.

## Notes

A clause attributing the wiry, large pulse (tympanic pulse) to deficiency cold, and taking it as a cause of miscarriage and metrostaxis in women and blood loss and loss of essence in men. The same text appears again in the later chapters on fright palpitations and bleeding and on miscellaneous diseases of women.

- The tympanic pulse is taut on the outside but empty within on pressure, likened to the skin of a drum.
- Xu Bin's commentary explains the clauses of units 8 through 11 together as deficiency/consumption of yang deficiency.

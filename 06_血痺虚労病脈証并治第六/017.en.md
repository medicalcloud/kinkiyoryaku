# Clause 17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The five taxations with extreme vacuity, emaciation, abdominal fullness, and inability to eat or drink; [damage by] food, worry, drink, sexual activity, hunger, taxation, and damage to the qi of the channels, nutritive, and defensive, with dry blood within, encrusted skin, and dark eyes: moderate the center and supplement vacuity; Dahuang Zhechong wan (Rhubarb and Ground Beetle Pill) governs it.

Dahuang Zhechong wan formula

Dahuang 10 *fen* (steamed); huangqin 2 liang; gancao 3 liang; taoren 1 sheng; xingren 1 sheng; shaoyao 4 liang; gandihuang 10 liang; ganqi (dried lacquer) 1 liang; mengchong (gadfly) 1 sheng; shuizhi (leech) 100 pieces; qicao (grub) 1 sheng; zhechong half a sheng.

Powder the twelve ingredients above, mix with refined honey into pills the size of aduki beans, and take five pills with wine, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The five taxations are vacuity taxation diseases of blood, qi, flesh, bone, and sinew. But it is always through damage to the spleen and stomach that the vacuity becomes hard to restore. So when vacuity is extreme there is emaciation, the large muscles about to waste away. Abdominal fullness means the spleen qi does not move. Inability to eat or drink means the stomach does not transport and transform. The sources of the disease differ — food, worry, drink, sexual activity, hunger, taxation, damage to the qi of the channels, nutritive, and defensive — but all can gradually reach the extreme. If the person has blood within that overflowed into the spaces of circulation at the time of the damage and has dried without leaving, it makes the disease linger. Its external sign is always encrusted skin; "encrusted" means like scales. The liver governs the blood and the eyes; when the qi of dry blood rises within to the liver, it fumes up to the eyes and they are dark. Here the root of the disease must be pulled out before the external signs recede. So ganqi, taoren, and the four insects break the blood. But long stasis always generates heat, and the qi stagnates and does not move. So huangqin clears heat, xingren disinhibits qi, dahuang moves it, and gancao, shaoyao, and dihuang rescue the original yin. Then what is tense within because of this can gradually be moderated, and what is exhausted in vacuity because of this can gradually be supplemented. Hence "moderate the center and supplement vacuity; Da[huang Zhechong wan]" ... [the rest is missing].

## Notes

A clause using Dahuang Zhechong wan for extreme vacuity taxation with dry static blood within ("dry blood"), scaly rough skin, and darkening around the eyes.

- Static blood arises from the five taxations and the six damages (food, worry, drink, sexual activity, hunger, taxation).
- Dahuang Zhechong wan breaks static blood with dahuang, taoren, ganqi, and insects (mengchong, shuizhi, qicao, zhechong), and nourishes yin-blood with dihuang, shaoyao, and gancao. "Moderate the center and supplement vacuity" is understood to mean that removing the static blood moderates the center and supplements the vacuity.
- The amount of dahuang is "10 *fen*," a unit inconsistent with the liang of the other medicinals.
- The base reads 為丸; it is emended to 和丸 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- Xu Bin's commentary is cut off at the end.

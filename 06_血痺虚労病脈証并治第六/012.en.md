# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Deficiency/consumption with abdominal tension, palpitations, nosebleeds, abdominal pain, loss of essence with dreams, aching limbs, vexing heat in the hands and feet, and a dry throat and mouth: Xiao Jianzhong tang (Minor Center-Fortifying Decoction) governs it.

Xiao Jianzhong tang formula

Guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces; shaoyao 6 liang; shengjiang 2 liang; jiaoyi (malt sugar) 1 sheng.

Of the six ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, add the malt sugar, put it on a low fire again to dissolve it, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day (it must not be used for those who retch often, because it is sweet. The *Qianjin* uses it for men and women who, due to accumulated cold and stagnant qi, or after a major illness, have not returned to normal [base: 不服常], suffering from heavy limbs, aching bones and flesh, gasping shortness of breath [base: 両両], dyspnea and exhaustion on moving, fullness of the chest with hurried breath, stiffness and pain of the lower back and back, deficiency palpitations [base: 浮] in the heart, dry throat and lips, and a face [base: 体] with little color, or no taste in food, distension of the ribsides and abdomen, a heavy head that cannot be raised, lying much and rising little — in severe cases for years, in mild cases a hundred days — gradually becoming emaciated and weak, so that once the qi of the five zang organs is exhausted it is hard to return to normal [base: 復当]; all six pulses insufficient, deficiency cold with lack of qi, tension of the lesser abdomen, emaciation with all kinds of illness; it is called Huangqi Jianzhong tang, and also contains renshen 2 liang).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The signs discussed in the previous section generally belong to yang deficiency. Yang deficiency is qi deficiency. People with qi deficiency should generally have the spleen aided. So Xiao Jianzhong tang governs it. In deficiency/consumption, the qi of the original yang cannot command essence and blood within; the ying-nutrient withers and becomes deficient, the inner qi becomes tense, and there are palpitations, nosebleeds, abdominal pain, and loss of essence with dreams. The qi of the original yang cannot fill the limbs, mouth, and throat without; the yang is deficient and dry, and there are aching limbs, vexed hands and feet, and dry throat and mouth. If the great qi in the chest turns once, the qi of the dry-heat disease moves by itself. So guizhi, shaoyao, gancao, shengjiang, and dazao greatly harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, and the single ingredient malt sugar is added to establish the central qi. This is the ancestor of the later Buzhong Yiqi tang. Though it lacks shengma and chaihu, the principle of raising the clear and lowering the turbid is present in this formula.

Discussion: The human body is nothing more than the four words yin, yang, qi, and blood. When the qi is hot, yang is exuberant; when the blood is hot, yin is exuberant. But this is not true exuberance; with true exuberance qi and blood would be vigorous, and one would be robust and free of disease. When yin cannot harmonize with yang, and yang relies on its dryness to beat and rise, it becomes hyperactive, producing thirst, dyspnea, vexation, and blood loss. But the yin is actually deficient and cold, so there are inhibited urination, tension and fullness of the lesser abdomen, yin cold with emission of semen, aching and wasting with inability to walk, cold semen and childlessness, cold glans, dizziness and hair loss. When yang cannot harmonize with yin, and yin carries its fire so that heat qi rises within, there is agitation, producing night sweats, impediment, saber lumps and goiter, dyspnea and gasping, and blood loss with loss of essence. But the yang is actually insufficient, so there are cold hands and feet, abdominal fullness with loose diarrhea, inability to digest food, abdominal pain, and dry throat and mouth. Blood loss and loss of essence occur in both yin deficiency and yang deficiency because extreme yin can generate heat.

So when the pulse is seen on the floating, large side, one should know that yin cannot hold yang. The kidney is the master of yin; strive to make heart and kidney interact, and essence and blood will be sufficient of themselves. When the pulse is seen on the thready, small side, one should know that yang cannot overcome yin. The spleen is the master of yang; supplement its central qi, and the three yang will be at peace of themselves. So Zhongjing specially singled out these two great doors as the standard for later generations in treating deficiency/consumption. When yin heat reaches its extreme and becomes dry, this is a ruined pattern of deficiency/consumption. So Zhu Fengyi (Zhu Gong) supplemented what earlier people had not reached with the single method of enriching yin; but how can that be the proper method for treating deficiency/consumption? Later people, seeing that some recover with enriching yin, argue endlessly about whether or not to use renshen. How could they know that Zhongjing centers on moving yang and securing yin, using supplementing the center and calming the kidney separately, relying neither solely on renshen nor solely on enriching yin, and so wields his blade with ease?

Xiao Jianzhong tang originally aims at transforming the qi within the spleen, and the flesh is what the spleen generates. Huangqi goes to the flesh and makes the stomach qi excess, so it is added to supplement insufficiency. Thus guizhi and shaoyao supplement the yin and yang of the whole body, and huangqi and malt sugar supplement the yin and yang within the spleen. For shortness of breath and fullness of the chest, shengjiang is added because fluid qi stagnates the yang, and shengjiang diffuses it. For abdominal fullness, dazao is removed and fuling added to eliminate fluid and correct the spleen qi. For qi that does not flow smoothly, banxia is added to remove counterflow, which is the means of supplementing the right.

## Notes

A clause using Xiao Jianzhong tang for deficiency/consumption with abdominal tension and pain, palpitations, nosebleeds, seminal emission, aching limbs, hot hands and feet, and dryness of the mouth and throat.

- Xiao Jianzhong tang is Guizhi tang with doubled shaoyao and added malt sugar; it is said to warm and supplement the middle jiao (spleen and stomach) and generate qi and blood. It also appears in the *Shanghan lun*.
- Being sweet, it is not used for those who retch often.
- The *Qianjin* passage in the interlinear note records the indications of Huangqi Jianzhong tang, and the base has many errors (不服常 for 不復常, 両両 for 吸吸, 匈満 for 胸満, 虚浮 for 虚悸, 而体少色 for 面体少色, 復当 for 復常, judging from Daizhige, Xu Bin's edition, etc.). The translation supplies these readings.
- The amount of shengjiang is 2 liang in the base and 3 liang in most other sources.
- The paragraph of Xu Bin's commentary after his "Discussion" ("Xiao Jianzhong tang originally aims at ...") explains the next formula, Huangqi Jianzhong tang.

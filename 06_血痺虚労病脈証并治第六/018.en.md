# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Appended formulas

Zhi Gancao tang (Mix-Fried Licorice Decoction) from the *Qianjin yi* (also called Fumai tang, Pulse-Restoring Decoction): treats vacuity taxation with insufficiency, sweating with oppression, and a bound pulse with palpitations. [Such a person,] though moving about as usual, will not survive a hundred days; one in critical condition dies in eleven days.

Gancao 4 liang (mix-fried); guizhi and shengjiang, 3 liang each; maimendong half a jin; maren (hemp seed) half a jin; renshen and ejiao, 2 liang each; dazao 30 pieces; shengdihuang (fresh rehmannia) 1 jin.

Of the nine ingredients above, with 7 sheng of wine and 8 sheng of water first boil eight ingredients down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, add the ejiao and dissolve it completely, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is a marvelous formula for moistening dryness and restoring the pulse in vacuity taxation. In those with vacuity taxation and insufficiency, if yin and yang have not become separated and the nutritive and defensive can follow their order even a little, the original qi may gradually recover. If sweating is due to a strong nutritive and weak defensive, one does not feel refreshed by the sweating but instead becomes oppressed; this is yin not harmonizing with yang. The pulse is what is called "blocking the nutritive qi so that it has nowhere to escape; this is the vessel," speaking of the vigor of its movement. Now the pulse is bound because the nutritive qi does not move; palpitations mean the blood is deficient and the heart has lost its nourishment. When the nutritive qi is already stagnant and further sweats outward, how can it not wither at once? So although the viscera and bowels inside and out are not yet cut off and one moves about as usual, it is pronounced that he "will not survive a hundred days," knowing that the yin is lost and the yang will be cut off by itself. In critical cases the heart is cut off first, so death comes in eleven days. That is, when the heart is suspended and cut off, death should come in nine days; adding the generating number of fire, water has nothing to continue it, and death is certain.

So guizhi and gancao move the yang of the body, shengjiang and dazao diffuse the yang within, and moistening, nourishing things such as renshen, ejiao, maren, maimendong, and shengdihuang are gathered to enrich the dryness of the five viscera, so that the yang can again move within the nutritive, and the pulse restores itself. It is named Zhi Gancao tang because earth is the mother of the myriad things: with shengdihuang governing the heart, maimendong the lung, ejiao the liver and kidney, maren the spleen, and renshen the original qi, mix-fried gancao is further made the general director harmonizing the center. Later people like to use only ejiao, maimendong, and the like, and fear shengjiang and guizhi; how could they know that the dry qi of congealed yin cannot be transformed without yang?

[Xu Bin's formula:] gancao 4 liang (mix-fried); guizhi 3 liang; shengjiang 3 liang; maimendong half a sheng; maren 1 sheng; renshen 2 liang; ejiao 2 liang; dazao 30 pieces; shengdihuang 1 jin.

[For the next unit:] Tagan san (Otter Liver Powder) from the *Zhouhou*: treats cold taxation, and also governs demonic infixation spreading through a household. Taxation is never without heat, yet it is called cold because yin-cold qi is of a kind with the evil. So the evil carries cold into the liver and binds the qi of the ethereal soul, shaoyang loses its authority, and the qi of generation is cut off; so death is certain. Further, the evil qi relies on the right qi to cause disease, and the power of medicine reaches it with difficulty; so it is hard to cure. The otter is a yin animal; its liver alone waxes and wanes with the moon, so it obtains the rightness of taiyin. Liver and liver are of a kind, so it treats cold taxation: the evil meets the right and is transformed. Otter flesh is all cold, but the liver alone is warm by nature; so it is especially suited to cold taxation. It also governs demonic infixation spreading through a household because these all belong to yin evil and must be transformed by right yin.

## Notes

An appended formula, Zhi Gancao tang (Fumai tang) from the *Qianjin yi fang*, for vacuity taxation with sweating and oppression, a bound pulse, and palpitations. It is the same formula as Zhi Gancao tang in the lower taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* ("bound or intermittent pulse, stirring palpitations of the heart").

- Mix-fried gancao, renshen, and dazao supplement qi; shengdihuang, maimendong, ejiao, and maren nourish yin-blood; guizhi and shengjiang free the yang.
- The amounts of maimendong and maren are half a jin in the base and half a sheng in all other sources.
- The latter part of Xu Bin's commentary, on Tagan san, belongs to the clause of the next unit.
- Xu Bin's explanation that adding the "generating number of fire" to "nine days" gives eleven is numerological, and its meaning is uncertain.

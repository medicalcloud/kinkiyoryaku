# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: From what is blood impediment disease contracted? The master said: Honored and prosperous people have weak bones and abundant flesh. When, further, due to [base: 困 "exhausted by"] fatigue, they sweat, and while lying down move about restlessly, and on top of this receive a slight wind, they contract it. If the pulse is simply feeble and hesitant of itself, and small and tight at the inch opening and guan, it is appropriate to draw out the yang qi with needling, harmonizing the pulse; when the tightness leaves, recovery follows.

Blood impediment with yin and yang both feeble, feeble at the inch opening and guan, small and tight at the chi, and the external sign of numbness of the body, like wind impediment: Huangqi Guizhi Wuwu tang (Ogikeishigomotsuto; Astragalus and Cinnamon Twig Five-Ingredient Decoction) governs it.

Huangqi Guizhi Wuwu tang formula

Huangqi (ogi) 3 liang; shaoyao (shakuyaku) 3 liang; guizhi (keishi) 3 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) 6 liang; dazao (taiso) 12 pieces.

Of the five ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng and take 7 ge warm, three times a day (one formula includes renshen [ninjin]).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Blood impediment is discussed together with deficiency/consumption to show that this pattern originally arises from constitutional deficiency and fatigue and must not be treated by the same methods as other impediments. Honored and prosperous people are habituated to ease and do not exert their strength, so their bones are weak; they eat rich food, so their flesh is abundant. When they are fatigued and sweat, the qi is exhausted and the exterior deficient. Then when lying down the spirit-qi is not gathered in, or they move about restlessly, and a slight wind takes advantage. At this time their own qi is originally weak, fatigue consumes qi, sweating makes yang qi deficient, and lying down makes yang qi hide. So the outer yang qi cannot close and secure the ying-nutrient qi, and as they turn and move, the wind, though slight, is like entering an empty valley; then wind and blood contend and impediment results.

The pulse is where the ying-nutrient qi pours. On receiving wind, what was originally moderate turns feeble, and what was originally slippery turns hesitant. Although wind-damp contends in the middle and upper jiao, the vanguard of the pathogen has already reached the lower jiao, so the chi is small and tight. But although the pathogen has reached below, the origin of the disease is always that the yang is deficient, unable to secure the outside or to guard and fill the inside. Hence "it is appropriate to draw out the yang qi with needling." When the yang qi arrives and the pulse is harmonized, upper and lower are connected, the pathogen cannot stay long, and the tightness leaves; hence recovery.

Yin and yang are the inch opening and renying. In general [the pulse] is hesitant and feeble, but here the word "hesitant" is dropped, and the feeble pulse is the main point. "Small and tight at the chi" means that on careful searching there is a small tightness. This is the form of the disease pathogen entering straight in, just as in the method of gauging disease in the Bright Hall chapter, "sharp below, pointing downward." But this is wind, dampness, and blood contending throughout the body, impeding its yang qi and causing numbness. So guizhi strengthens qi and moves yang, shaoyao harmonizes yin, and shengjiang and dazao harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense of the upper jiao; joining forces to drive out wind, the origin of the disease is uprooted, and the slight pathogen that has entered is like the end of a strong crossbow's flight. This is Guizhi tang (Keishito) without gancao (kanzo) and with huangqi added. The intent of the method lies in drawing out yang, so the slowness of gancao is disliked as not equal to the strong power of huangqi.

## Notes

A clause on the cause and treatment of blood impediment (*xuebi*: a disease mainly of numbness and diminished sensation of the body).

- It is said to occur in honored and prosperous people who do not exercise and are overweight, when they are fatigued, sweat, and are exposed to a slight wind while sleeping.
- Mild cases are treated by needling to draw out yang qi; severe cases (yin and yang both feeble, small and tight at the chi, numbness of the body) with Huangqi Guizhi Wuwu tang.
- Huangqi Guizhi Wuwu tang is Guizhi tang with gancao replaced by huangqi and shengjiang doubled.
- For 因 in 重因疲労, the base reads 困, but most other sources read 因 ("further, due to fatigue"), so it has been emended (see the emendation notes).

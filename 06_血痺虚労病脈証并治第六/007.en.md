# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Those who habitually lose essence have tension and tautness of the lesser abdomen, coldness of the glans, dizziness (one version reads eye pain), and hair loss. An extremely vacuous, scallion-stalk, slow pulse indicates clear-grain diarrhea, blood loss, and loss of essence. When the pulse is scallion-stalk, stirring, faint, or tight, and men lose essence and women have sexual dreams, Guizhi Longgu Muli tang (Cinnamon Twig, Dragon Bone, and Oyster Shell Decoction) governs it.

Guizhi jia Longgu Muli tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Dragon Bone and Oyster Shell) formula (the *Xiaopin* says: for those who are vacuous and weak with floating heat and sweating, remove the gui and add baiwei (cynanchum) and fuzi, 3 *fen* each; hence it is called Erjia Longgu tang).

Guizhi, shaoyao, shengjiang, 3 liang each; gancao 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces; longgu; muli.

Of the seven ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng and take warm in three doses.

Tianxiong san (Aconite Tuber Powder) formula

Tianxiong (elongated aconite tuber) 3 liang (blast-fried); baizhu 8 liang; guizhi 6 liang; longgu 3 liang.

Pound the four ingredients above into a powder and take half a *qianbi* with wine, three times a day. If there is no effect, increase it slightly.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks generally of vacuity taxation in which vacuous yang is exuberant and true yin is vacuous. So the pulses on the floating, large side are central, and even where sunken, wiry, faint, or tight pulses occur, the signs still show the picture of yin vacuity.

A man who appears healthy, with no disease to blame, yet has a large or extremely vacuous pulse: both are the usual pulses of taxation patterns.

If the complexion is thin, the yang essence has descended. When the yang essence descends, vacuity dryness follows, so there is thirst; if severe, the yin is vacuous and fire stirs, and there is blood loss. Moreover, the original qi does not continue, so there is panting; the heart qi is insufficient, so there are palpitations. The pulse is floating instead of sunken; the *Neijing* says "floating is blood vacuity," hence "the interior is vacuous."

If the pulse is vacuous, sunken, and wiry, it seems not to be yin vacuity of the floating, large kind. But since there is no cold or heat, it is not a sudden contraction of wind-cold. Shortness of breath and abdominal tension still mean that the original qi is vacuous within. Inhibited urination means the kidney cannot govern excretion. A white complexion means the blood cannot nourish. Occasional dimming of vision means the yin fire cannot bear movement. Nosebleeds mean yin fire presses the blood of the clear passages. Fullness of the lesser abdomen means the kidney is not in order. If the lower origin were not exhausted by taxation, how could this be?

If the pulse is large, it is already taxation; if it is also floating, the signs are vexed hands and feet. The yin is already insufficient and the vacuous yang blazes again. So spring and summer aid the yang and it worsens; autumn and winter aid the yin and it improves. When yin is vacuous, there is yin cold without original yang to secure it, and semen comes out spontaneously. The kidney governs the lower burner; when vacuity lasts long, there is aching and wasting with inability to walk.

If a man's pulse is floating, weak, and rough, floating and weak mean vacuous yang is at work, and rough means water is deficient. That he is childless is certain; his essential qi is clear and cold. There is yang that floats up, but no yang that generates yin.

If one is habituated to losing essence, the kidney is vacuous. The lesser abdomen is the residence of the kidney; when vacuous, yin is lost and it is taut and tense. The glans is the outward manifestation of liver and kidney; when vacuous, there is no yang and it is cold. The eyes are liver wood, nourished by kidney water, and liver and kidney share a source; when vacuous, nourishment is lost and there is dizziness. The hair is the flower of the kidney; when vacuous, its luster departs and it falls out. Then if the pulse is extremely vacuous, scallion-stalk, and slow, with vacuity and cold, the qi cannot be secured and there is clear-grain diarrhea; the blood cannot be secured and blood is lost; the essence cannot be secured and essence is lost. But the pulses of those who lose essence vary. If they are scallion-stalk, stirring, faint, or tight, men, with vacuous yin carrying fire, lose essence, and women, with vacuous yin carrying fire, have sexual dreams.

Guizhi Longgu Muli tang governs it because people with yin vacuity should generally have the kidney aided. So guizhi and shaoyao free yang and secure yin; gancao, shengjiang, and dazao harmonize the nutritive and defensive of the middle and upper burners so that yang can generate yin; and longgu and muli, which calm the kidney and settle the heart, are the main agents for supplementing yin. As for Tianxiong san, fearing that those who lose essence may have yang vacuity of the middle burner, the formula above is modified by adding tianxiong and baizhu. Later generations lost this idea entirely and only enrich yin. Are they not truly offenders against Zhongjing?

[Xu Bin's formula:] guizhi 2 liang; shaoyao 3 liang; shengjiang 3 liang; gancao 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces; longgu 3 liang; muli 3 liang.

## Notes

A clause describing the signs and pulses of those who habitually lose essence (repeated seminal emission), with Guizhi jia Longgu Muli tang and Tianxiong san.

- Tension of the lower abdomen, cold genitals, dizziness, and hair loss are given as signs.
- Guizhi jia Longgu Muli tang is Guizhi tang plus longgu and muli; it is said to harmonize yin and yang, secure essence, and calm the heart spirit. It treats seminal emission in men and sexual dreams in women.
- The clause names the formula Guizhi Longgu Muli tang; most other sources read Guizhi jia Longgu Muli tang. The base gives no amounts for longgu and muli; other sources have 3 liang each.
- Tianxiong san has no indication in the text, and some regard it as a later addition.
- 白微 in the interlinear note is an error for 白薇 (baiwei), which the other sources read.
- Xu Bin's commentary explains the clauses of units 2 through 7 together.

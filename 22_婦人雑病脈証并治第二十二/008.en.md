# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Women's diseases arise from deficiency, accumulated cold, and bound qi, causing the various kinds of menstrual cessation. When it has lasted for years, the blood is cold and accumulates and binds at the uterine gate, and cold damages the meridians and collaterals.

When it congeals and hardens above, there is retching and vomiting of drool and spittle; in time it becomes lung abscess, and the body is damaged.

When it coils and binds in the middle, there is cold mounting around the navel; or pain in both rib-sides, connected with the zang organs; or it binds as heat in the center, with pain at Guanyuan, a rapid pulse without sores, and skin like fish scales. At times it attaches to men; it is not only in women's bodies.

When it is below and not yet much, the menstrual periods are irregular, causing pulling pain in the genitals and aversion to cold in the lesser abdomen; or it radiates to the lower back and spine, rooted below at Qijie, with acute pain at Qichong, aching and vexation of the knees and shins, and sudden dizziness and veiling, like reverse-flow or epilepsy; or there is anxiety and sorrow, grief and much anger. All of these are diseases below the girdle, not the work of ghosts and spirits.

In time there is emaciation, the pulse is deficient, and there is much cold. The thirty-six diseases take a thousand forms and ten thousand variations. Examine the pulse for yin and yang, deficiency and excess, tight and wiry, and apply needles and medicines; the endangered can be treated and brought to safety. Though the disease may be the same, the pulses each have different sources. You should distinguish and remember this, and not say it is otherwise.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This passage sets out the origins of women's diseases. They differ from men's entirely because they arise from menstruation; apart from this they are the same as men's. Their transformation into various diseases depends on the strength or weakness of constitution, deficiency or excess of the time, and the predominance of upper or lower, cold or heat, so the signs differ, and treatment follows sometimes the branch, sometimes the root. These can be inferred from the various diseases described before and after; here it speaks only of the general outline.

The several phrases from "women's diseases" to "uterine gate" are the guiding principle of the whole chapter, and the six characters "deficiency, accumulated cold, bound qi" are the guiding principle of the principle. If a person is not deficient, pathogen cannot take advantage; because of deficiency, cold contracted by chance is not transformed and accumulates. Qi moves when hot and congeals when cold. When cold qi congeals, stagnates, and accumulates, it binds; binding means not dispersing. When blood meets cold qi and does not move, the menses stop [lacuna] there are differences of slight and severe, upper and lower; hence "various." "When it has lasted for years, the blood is cold" [lacuna] with cold the blood is cold. The uterine gate is the gate in the vagina leading to the uterus, the pathway of the menses; with cold there is stasis, and with accumulation it obstructs the coming of the monthly water.

The several phrases from "cold damages the meridians and collaterals" to "damaged" form one section. Cold accumulates at Guanyuan, slight at first, but after yang declines, ying-nutrient and wei-defense interfere with each other, bound cold qi pours in and the meridians and collaterals are damaged; following one another it enters upward, congealing and hardening above; visiting pathogen combines with it, causing retching and vomiting of drool and spittle; in time qi congests, the upper jiao becomes hot, and heat damages the lung, forming an abscess. At first only the qi received cold and bound; by now it gradually reaches the body; hence "the body is damaged." This is a transformation of the disease located above.

The four phrases of "in the middle" form one section. If the original qi of the upper jiao is exuberant and no visiting pathogen combines with it, the pathogenic cold cannot invade above and coils and binds in the middle. The navel governs the middle jiao, so there is cold mounting around the navel; cold mounting is cold pain. But the rib-sides are governed by the liver, whose meridian, Jueyin, starts below and is governed at the rib-sides, so there are cases where the pain is connected with the zang organs. Not all are so; hence "or."

The several phrases from "or it binds as heat in the center" to "women's bodies" form one section. People's constitutions differ: one with weak center qi is invaded by cold and develops mounting; if the person's center qi is habitually hot, the pathogen from below combines with it and becomes a disease of heat in the center, and the cold at Guanyuan cannot be dissolved by the visiting heat, so the pain remains. But the stomach is hot, so the pulse is rapid; it is not from heat in the ying-nutrient aspect, so there are no sores. Though there are no sores, wherever the visiting heat reaches, the ying-nutrient qi becomes dry, so the skin is like fish scales — rough and not smooth. "At times it attaches to men; it is not only in women's bodies" means that cold qi contracts and cannot reach others, but with heat in the center the qi is hot, and when men and women have intercourse, the man feels the heat and is affected too, so it is not only women's skin that becomes rough. The two sections above speak of transformations of the disease located in the middle, originally cold or else heat.

The four phrases of "below" form one section. If the cold below Guanyuan is much, the cold is low and the menses are not wholly obstructed, but only the timing is irregular. The cold is near the genitals, so the genitals hurt; 掣 means pulling pain. Then, with little yang qi in the lesser abdomen, there is aversion to cold. This speaks of a transformation of the disease located below.

The four phrases of "or it radiates to the lower back and spine" form one section. When the disease invades the meridians and collaterals below, it moves up and down among the joints without fixed place, and there are cases where everything from the lower back and spine to Qichong, the knees, and shins aches. This speaks of transformation of the disease into the joints.

The four phrases of "sudden" form one section. When the pathogen has entered deeply and the spirit qi receives it, yin fire blazes and the yang of the head declines, causing dizziness and veiling; yang qi is depleted and the spirit light has no master, causing reverse-flow and epilepsy. When the zang qi is already dry, even slight anxiety and sorrow provoke grief and much anger. This speaks of transformation of the disease in the spirit qi.

Reverse-flow, epilepsy, and grief may seem like ghosts and spirits, but people do not know that all the above are diseases below the girdle vessel, not ghosts and spirits. 帯下 means "below the girdle," not what people today call white vaginal discharge. When the disease first arises, there is cold or heat according to the cold or heat of the body; in time the original qi is consumed and the flesh wastes, hence emaciation; in time the meridians become deficient and yang qi scant, hence much cold. The thirty-six diseases are the twelve masses, nine pains, seven harms, five damages, and three chronic diseases; they are detailed in the first fascicle.

The two phrases "examine the pulse for yin and yang, deficiency and excess, tight and wiry" sum up the method of treatment for the whole chapter. Though the variations are endless, they never go beyond yin and yang, deficiency and excess; only tight and wiry are mentioned because at the beginning of menstrual obstruction it generally belongs to cold: when qi binds the pulse is wiry, and when cold is severe it is tight. It shows that these two pulses are primary, with accompanying pulses consulted. Needles and medicines each have their appropriate uses. But though the form of the disease is the same, the pulses each differ, and the position where they differ is the source of the disease; hence "the pulses each have different sources." This passage contains the most essential words for pattern identification and treatment in gynecology, so it bids one distinguish and remember them, and admonishes as well.

## Notes

A general discussion of women's diseases. Taking deficiency, accumulated cold, and bound qi as the causes, it describes the signs according to whether the disease is located above, in the middle, or below, and teaches that needles and medicines should be applied after examining the pulse for yin and yang, deficiency and excess. It is written in four-character rhymed verse.

- For 凝堅 ("congeals and hardens"), the base reads 疑堅; for 痛 in "pain at Guanyuan," the base reads 病; and for 癲 in "reverse-flow or epilepsy," the base reads 㒹; all other sources read the emended characters, so they have been emended (see the emendations).
- "When it is below and not yet much" is variously interpreted, and Xu Bin's text has the note 此字疑誤 ("this character is perhaps in error").
- Xu Bin takes the "thirty-six diseases" as the twelve masses, nine pains, seven harms, five damages, and three chronic diseases. Xu Bin's text reads "twenty-six diseases."

# Clause 9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: A woman of about fifty suffers from diarrhea that does not stop for several tens of days, with fever as soon as dusk comes, internal tension of the lesser abdomen, abdominal fullness, vexing heat in the palms, and dry lips and mouth. Why? The master said: This disease belongs to [diseases] below the girdle. Why? She once had a half-term delivery (miscarriage), and static blood remains in the lesser abdomen and has not gone. How is this known? Her signs include dry lips and mouth; hence it is known. Wenjing tang should govern it.

Wenjing tang (Menses-Warming Decoction) formula

Wuzhuyu 3 liang; danggui, xiongqiong, and shaoyao, 2 liang each; renshen, guizhi, ejiao, mudanpi, shengjiang, and gancao, 2 liang each; banxia half a sheng; maimendong (ophiopogon) 1 sheng (heart removed).

Of the twelve ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil down to 3 sheng and take warm in three doses. It also governs women with cold in the lesser abdomen who have long failed to conceive, and is also taken for flooding with blood loss, excessive menstrual flow, or menses that do not come at the expected time.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This passage speaks of a severe case of "the blood cold for years, accumulating and binding at the uterine gate." So even for a woman late in life whose menses have stopped, one must judge by the signs to establish the method. A woman of fifty has already exhausted her tiangui, and it should not be seen as arising from menstrual blood. Yet diarrhea not stopping for several tens of days shows it is not a chance contraction. Fever as soon as dusk comes shows the disease belongs to yin. Internal tension of the lesser abdomen makes clear that the disease belongs to the lower burner. The resulting abdominal fullness, though a spleen disease, is rooted in the lower burner. Vexing heat in the palms: the palm belongs to the heart, the heart governs blood, and when blood is depressed there is heat and vexation. The lips and mouth must receive the nutritive qi of the spleen to be moistened by fluids; when the nutritive qi is depressed, yin fire follows, hence dryness, not thirst. With thirst [lacuna]

## Notes

A clause stating that a woman of about fifty with diarrhea, evening fever, tension of the lower abdomen, vexing heat in the palms, and dry lips has static blood remaining in the lower abdomen from a past miscarriage, treated with Wenjing tang.

- Wenjing tang warms with wuzhuyu and guizhi, nourishes blood with danggui, xiongqiong, shaoyao, and ejiao, removes static blood with mudanpi, and moistens with maimendong. It is also used for infertility and menstrual irregularities.
- Xu Bin's text (Kanripo) is missing its leaves from the middle of the commentary in this unit to the end of the chapter. So the commentary breaks off and runs straight into the end of the commentary on the formula for children's gan worms eating the teeth at the end of the chapter. Units 10–21 have no commentary by Xu Bin.

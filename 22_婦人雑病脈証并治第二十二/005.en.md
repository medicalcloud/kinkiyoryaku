# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman feels as if there were a piece of roasted meat in her throat, Banxia Houpo tang (Hangekobokuto) governs it.

Banxia Houpo tang (Pinellia and Magnolia Bark Decoction) formula (the *Qianjin* has: "fullness of the chest, hardness below the heart, something stuck in the throat as if there were roasted meat, which cannot be spat out or swallowed down")

Banxia (hange) [amount missing; most other sources: 1 sheng]; houpo (koboku) 3 liang; fuling (bukuryo) 4 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) 5 liang; dried suye (perilla leaf) 2 liang.

Of the five ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil down to 4 sheng and take warm in four doses, three in the day and one at night.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This clause is what is later called "cold damages the meridians and collaterals, congealing and hardening above." "Roasted meat" is likened to dried meat. The words of the *Qianjin*, "something stuck in the throat as if there were roasted meat, which cannot be spat out or swallowed down," describing a piece of roasted meat, are very clear and apt. This disease does not stem from the intestines and stomach, so it does not hinder eating, drinking, urination, or defecation; it does not stem from exterior pathogen, so there is no bone pain or cold and heat. It is qi damaged by accumulated cold, not harmonizing with blood; the qi in the blood overflows and floats into the throat, and meeting water-damp qi it congeals and is hard to move. Women's blood aspect often receives cold, and with accumulated cold and bound qi they most easily contract this disease, but men occasionally have it too. Banxia Houpo tang is Erchen tang (Nichinto) minus chenpi (chinpi) and gancao (kanzo), plus houpo, zisu, and shengjiang. Banxia brings down counterflow qi and houpo also disperses binding; so they govern it. Shengjiang and fuling diffuse stagnation at the highest level and bring down its dampness. Suye is acrid in flavor, fragrant in qi, purple in color, and warm in nature; it can enter yin and harmonize blood and also return qi to the blood. So for all kinds of blood loss, taken in pills with adzuki beans, it can keep the blood from moving frenetically; and when summer heat damages heart yin in summer, it can bring down summer heat depression. Used for the sensation of roasted meat, it harmonizes qi with blood so that it no longer floats upward.

Discussion: I once treated Wang Xiaoyi, whose throat was always choked and blocked, and coughing brought nothing out. I gave Banxia Houpo tang and he was cured at once, but afterward it recurred each time. On detailed questioning, he said that at night under the lamp he always saw halos like balls of five colors, and the inside of his spine sometimes ached. He was also robust and vigorous, so I knew that the lower part had first received cold with insufficient yin qi, the liver had instead become depressed and hot, and in severe cases the bound cold stirred slightly, carrying kidney qi surging upward, blocking and choking the throat. So I added large doses of gouqi, juhua, mudanpi, and rougui to this formula; the halos gradually disappeared and the throat was also cured. So "men occasionally have it too" is true and no falsehood.

## Notes

A clause using Banxia Houpo tang for the sensation of a piece of roasted meat stuck in the throat (later called "plum-pit qi").

- Xu Bin identifies it with "cold damages the meridians and collaterals, congealing and hardening above" in unit 8 below (the general discussion of women's diseases), and, noting that men also have it, cites his own case.
- The base lacks the amount of banxia.

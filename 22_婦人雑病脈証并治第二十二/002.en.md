# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman has cold damage with fever and her menses have just come, she is lucid in the daytime but has delirious speech at dusk, as if seeing ghosts: this is heat entering the blood chamber. In treating it, do not offend the stomach qi or the upper two burners, and it will surely heal by itself.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of heat entering the blood chamber where the blood does not necessarily bind, and from the beginning [the blood] contends with the evil and causes trouble. "Cold damage" is the one called without sweating and with aversion to cold. "Fever" is the sign of this disease. "Her menses have just come": "come" means the time when the menses first flow. When the evil is exuberant and the menstrual qi is also exuberant and they meet, cold evil must damage the nutritive, so the evil contends with the blood. Blood belongs to yin and governs the night; so in the daytime, though there is fever, she is lucid, but at dusk it enters the yin aspect, and the evil, carrying yin qi, causes delirious speech. "As if seeing ghosts" is extreme delirious speech. "This is heat entering the blood chamber" means that although the blood chamber is inside, the exterior evil has in fact not offended the stomach or the inside of the upper two burners. So "this" means only this, not exterior evil entering the interior. The method of treatment is simply to harmonize the exterior evil and slightly clear the heat of the blood chamber as well. If one mistakenly takes it as visiting evil entering inside and attacks it, much damage is done. Hence "do not offend the stomach qi or the upper two burners, and it will surely heal by itself." It says "surely" because there is originally no disease inside to attack, so even without treatment it will surely heal.

## Notes

A clause stating that when, in a woman's cold damage, the menses have just come and delirious speech occurs at dusk, it is heat entering the blood chamber, which heals by itself if the stomach qi and the upper and middle burners are not offended. The same clause appears in the lower taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 145).

- For 者, the base reads 然, but all other sources read 者, so it has been emended (see the emendations).

# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman has cold damage with fever and her menses have just come, she is lucid in the daytime but has delirium at dusk, as if seeing ghosts: this is heat entering the blood chamber. In treating it, do not offend the stomach qi or the upper two jiao, and it will surely heal by itself.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of heat entering the blood chamber where the blood does not necessarily bind, and from the beginning [the blood] contends with the pathogen and causes trouble. "Cold damage" is the one called without sweating and with aversion to cold. "Fever" is the sign of this disease. "Her menses have just come": "come" means the time when the menses first flow. When the pathogen is exuberant and the menstrual qi is also exuberant and they meet, pathogenic cold must damage the ying-nutrient, so the pathogen contends with the blood. Blood belongs to yin and governs the night; so in the daytime, though there is fever, she is lucid, but at dusk it enters the yin aspect, and the pathogen, carrying yin qi, causes delirium. "As if seeing ghosts" is extreme delirium. "This is heat entering the blood chamber" means that although the blood chamber is inside, the exterior pathogen has in fact not offended the stomach or the inside of the upper two jiao. So "this" means only this, not exterior pathogen entering the interior. The method of treatment is simply to harmonize the exterior pathogen and slightly clear the heat of the blood chamber as well. If one mistakenly takes it as visiting pathogen entering inside and attacks it, much damage is done. Hence "do not offend the stomach qi or the upper two jiao, and it will surely heal by itself." It says "surely" because there is originally no disease inside to attack, so even without treatment it will surely heal.

## Notes

A clause stating that when, in a woman's cold damage, the menses have just come and delirium occurs at dusk, it is heat entering the blood chamber, which heals by itself if the stomach qi and the upper and middle jiao are not offended. The same clause appears in the lower Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 145).

- For 者, the base reads 然, but all other sources read 者, so it has been emended (see the emendations).

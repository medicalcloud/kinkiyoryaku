# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman has wind attack and after seven or eight days cold and heat continue to come, occurring in attacks at set times, and her menses have just stopped, this is heat entering the blood chamber. The blood must bind, so it makes [the disease] like malaria, occurring in attacks at set times. Xiao Chaihu tang governs it (the formula appears under retching and vomiting).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

There are four cases of heat entering the blood chamber in women. Entry into the blood chamber always brings delirium, but here there is no delirium, only a malaria-like state. That is, cold damage occurs in both men and women, but in women there is something uniquely different; so it begins with "a woman has wind attack," that is, the wind pattern governed by Guizhi tang in cold damage. After seven or eight days the exterior pathogen has already resolved. There is cold and heat again; hence "continue to come." But the heat is not continuous; hence "at set times." Asked about her menses, they had come and just stopped. Clearly residual heat not yet exhausted takes advantage of deficiency and enters, so some of the residual blood must bind; hence cold and heat at set times. But it is not comparable to transmission from Taiyang into Shaoyang; this disease arises from the heat of bound blood; hence "makes it like malaria." Though not comparable to transmission into Shaoyang, Xiao Chaihu tang is still used, because the qi of the blood chamber is governed by the liver, the liver and gallbladder are exterior and interior to each other, and the gallbladder, receiving pathogen through the liver, falls ill as with malaria, for which other medicines are not suitable; so it again focuses on harmonizing the half-exterior half-interior. That is, when the qi of the upper jiao is harmonized, the suddenly bound blood will [lacuna] move. If it were attacked drastically, as in the Didang tang pattern, that would violate the prohibitions of Shaoyang.

## Notes

A clause stating that when, in a woman's wind attack, cold and heat arise at set times like malaria after the menses have just stopped, it is heat entering the blood chamber, treated with Xiao Chaihu tang. The same clause appears in the lower Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 144).

- The "blood chamber" is said to refer to the uterus (or the thoroughfare vessel or liver). Units 2–4 below are also clauses on heat entering the blood chamber.

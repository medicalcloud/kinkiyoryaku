# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman has wind attack with fever and aversion to cold, and her menses have just come, and after seven or eight days the heat is gone, the pulse slow, and the body cool and calm, but the chest and rib-sides are full, as in chest bind, with delirium: this is heat entering the blood chamber. One should needle Qimen, taking it according to the excess.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of the menses and the disease meeting without immediately causing trouble, but after the disease resolves, [the blood] instead contends with the pathogen and causes suffering in the chest and rib-sides. Wind attack differs somewhat from the cold damage above, but fever, aversion to cold, and the menses just coming are no different from the earlier exuberance of pathogen coinciding with exuberance of the menses. Seven or eight days later the heat is gone, the pulse slow, and the body cool and calm: the menses flowed during the disease without hindrance. But after seven or eight days, the chest and rib-sides instead become full as in chest bind, with delirium: the heat that entered the blood chamber did not escape into the meridians but bound in the house of the liver, so the places through which its vessel passes become full, in severe cases like chest bind, and when yin fire is exuberant there is delirium. But though the fullness is in the chest and rib-sides, it is not Shaoyang exterior pathogen; though like chest bind, it is not Taiyang exterior pathogen entering the interior; though there is delirium, it is not stomach excess. So saying "this is heat entering the blood chamber" again shows that one must not mistakenly attack the stomach or the upper two jiao. One should needle Qimen; Qimen is the territory of the liver. This is a disease of liver excess; draining the excess brings recovery. Hence it says "according ..." (the rest is missing).

## Notes

A clause stating that when, in a woman's wind attack, the menses have just come and, after the fever subsides, the chest and rib-sides become full as in chest bind with delirium, it is heat entering the blood chamber, treated by needling Qimen. The same clause appears in the lower Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 143).

- For 実 ("excess"), the base reads 仮, but all other sources read 実, so it has been emended (see the emendations).
- The end of Xu Bin's commentary breaks off.

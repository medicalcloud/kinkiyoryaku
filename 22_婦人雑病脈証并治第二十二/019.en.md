# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: A woman is ill; she eats and drinks as usual but has vexing heat and cannot lie down, and instead breathes leaning against something. Why? The master said: This is called shifted bladder: she cannot urinate. Because the bladder's tie is twisted, this disease results. Simply disinhibit the urine and she will recover. Shenqi wan (Jinkigan) governs it suitably.

Shenqi wan (Kidney Qi Pill) formula

Gandihuang (kanjio) 8 liang; shuyu (shoyo) (Chinese yam) 4 liang; shanzhuyu (sanshuyu; cornus) 4 liang; zexie (takusha) [amount missing]; fuling (bukuryo) 3 liang; mudanpi 3 liang; guizhi (keishi) and fuzi (bushi; blast-fried), 1 liang each.

Powder the eight ingredients above and mix with refined honey into pills the size of firmiana seeds. Wash down 15 pills with wine, increasing up to 25 pills, twice a day.

Shechuangzi san (Jashoshisan; Cnidium Seed Powder) formula, a suppository to warm the vagina.

Shechuangzi (cnidium seed) kernels.

Powder the one ingredient above, mix with a little white powder until well combined, make into the size of a jujube, wrap in silk floss, and insert it; it warms naturally.

## Notes

A clause stating that inability to lie down with vexing heat and inability to urinate, though eating and drinking are normal, is shifted bladder (a twisted tie of the bladder), treated with Shenqi wan to promote urination. It also records Shechuangzi san, a suppository to warm the vagina.

- The base lacks the amount of zexie; most other sources give 3 liang.
- Before Shechuangzi san, Daizhige has it in the form of a clause: "For cold in a woman's genitals, a suppository to warm the vagina: Shechuangzi san governs it."
- Xu Bin's text is missing the leaves for this part, so there is no commentary.

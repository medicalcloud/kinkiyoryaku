# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: This has bound into concretions and conglomerations, called the malaria mother. Treat it urgently; Biejia Jian wan (Turtle Shell Decocted Pill) is appropriate.

Biejia Jian wan formula: biejia 12 *fen* (mix-fried); wushan (belamcanda) 3 *fen* (burned); huangqin 3 *fen*; chaihu (bupleurum) 6 *fen*; shufu (wood louse) 3 *fen* (dry-fried); ganjiang 3 *fen*; dahuang 3 *fen*; shaoyao 5 *fen*; guizhi 3 *fen*; tingli (lepidium seed) 1 *fen* (dry-fried); shiwei (pyrrosia) 3 *fen* (remove the hairs); houpo 3 *fen*; mudan (moutan) 5 *fen* (remove the core); qumai (dianthus) 2 *fen*; ziwei (campsis flower) 3 *fen*; banxia 1 *fen*; renshen 1 *fen*; [zhe]chong (ground beetle) 5 *fen* (dry-fried); ejiao (donkey-hide gelatin) [base: 附膠] 3 *fen* (mix-fried); fengchao (wasp nest) 4 *fen* (mix-fried); chixiao (red niter) 12 *fen*; qianglang (dung beetle) 6 *fen* (dry-fried); taoren (peach kernel) 2 *fen*.

Powder the twenty-three ingredients above. Take 1 dou of ash from beneath a blacksmith's forge and soak the ash in 1 hu 5 dou of clear wine; when the wine has reduced by half, put the biejia into it and boil until it dissolves like glue or lacquer; wring out the juice, add the other medicinals, decoct, and make pills the size of firmiana seeds. Take seven pills on an empty stomach, three times a day (the *Qianjin* formula uses twelve pieces of biejia, and also seaweed (haizao) 3 *fen*, daji (euphorbia) 1 *fen*, and [zhe]chong 5 *fen*, without shufu and chixiao, and makes pills by mixing the medicinals with the biejia decoction).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The malaria evil resides in the province of shaoyang, neither inside nor outside; this is where the defensive qi goes and returns. The defensive qi moves through yin and yang, and the malaria evil relies on it, alternately replete and vacuous. Thus the mutual victories of right and evil naturally do not depart from the waxing and waning of the yin and yang of heaven. The qi of heaven changes every half month, and when the qi of heaven changes, the qi of the human body changes too. Otherwise, when the qi of heaven and of man has changed twice, the malaria evil, however exuberant, is like the end of a strong crossbow's flight. Hence "breaking out on the first day of the month, it should recover on the fifteenth; if not, it should resolve at the end of the month." The moon wanes and becomes full, becomes full and wanes, and advances to grow its dark part; so we know the qi of heaven grows. It fills and empties, empties and fills, and retreats and diminishes; so we know the evil qi disappears.

If it still does not recover, the right qi gradually fills and does not receive the evil, so [the evil] borrows matter in the liver region of the ribsides to take form. Hence "this has bound into concretions and conglomerations." Before this, the evil had nothing to rely on, yin and yang alternated, and a day of recovery could be expected. Once there are concretions, the evil relies on them to secure itself, and the evil has, on the contrary, taken root. Hence "malaria mother." Having come from nothing into being, it must grow from small to large; the evil prevails and the right dwindles, with no prospect of recovery. Hence "treat it urgently."

Biejia Jian is used because biejia enters the liver, removes evil, and nourishes the right, and combined with wine in which forge ash has been soaked it removes conglomerations; so it is the sovereign. Xiao Chaihu tang, Guizhi tang, and Da Chengqi tang are the main medicines of the three yang, so they are the ministers. But gancao is disliked for being soft and moderating and reducing the strength of the medicines, and zhishi for breaking qi and going straight down, so they are removed. In addition ganjiang and ejiao are added to help renshen and baizhu nourish the right; these are the assistants. Conglomerations always borrow blood and rely on phlegm, so the four insects and taoren, with banxia, dissolve blood and transform phlegm. All accumulations arise from bound qi; when the qi is disinhibited, the accumulation dissolves. So wushan and tingli disinhibit lung qi, and with shiwei and qumai clear evil heat, transform qi, and disperse binding. When blood gathers because of evil, it becomes hot; so mudan and ziwei, which remove hidden fire in the blood and replete heat in the diaphragm, are the envoys. The *Qianjin* formula's removal of shufu and chixiao and addition of haizao and daji to soften hardness and transform water is even better.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] renshen 1 *fen*; zhechong 5 *fen* (dry-fried); ejiao 3 *fen* (mix-fried); fengke (wasp nest) 4 *fen* (mix-fried); chixiao 12 *fen*; qianglang 6 *fen* (dry-fried); taoren 2 *fen*.

... soak the ash; when the wine has reduced by half, put the biejia into it and boil until it dissolves like glue or lacquer; wring out the juice, add the other medicinals, decoct, and make pills the size of firmiana seeds. Take seven pills on an empty stomach, three times a day.

## Notes

A clause using Biejia Jian wan when prolonged malaria has formed a mass below the ribs (the "malaria mother," often identified with splenomegaly). It answers the question in the previous unit.

- Biejia Jian wan is a large formula of twenty-three ingredients centered on biejia, combining elements of Xiao Chaihu tang, Guizhi tang, and Da Chengqi tang with blood-quickening, phlegm-transforming, and water-disinhibiting medicinals and insects.
- The base formula has many errors. Other sources read 䗪虫 or 蟅虫 for 虫, 阿膠 for 附膠, and 赤硝 for 赤消. The translation supplies zhechong and ejiao.
- Xu Bin mentions baizhu along with renshen, but the formula contains no baizhu.
- The interlinear note, by the Song editors, records differences from the *Qianjin* formula.

# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In warm malaria, the pulse seems normal, the body has no cold but only heat, the bones and joints ache and are vexed, and there is occasional retching. Baihu jia Guizhi tang (White Tiger Decoction plus Cinnamon Twig) governs it.

Baihu jia Guizhi tang formula

Zhimu 6 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shigao 1 jin; jingmi 2 ge; gui (remove the bark) 3 liang.

Cut the above; for each dose take 5 *qian* with one and a half cups of water, decoct down to eight-tenths, remove the dregs, and take warm. When sweat comes out, recovery follows at once.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In the *Neijing*'s discussion of malaria, apart from *jie* malaria as a general term, there are only three items: cold first and heat after, heat first and cold after, and heat only without cold; so there are only three names, cold malaria, warm malaria, and pure-heat malaria. Its two passages on warm malaria seem to distinguish depth, but [such a reading] does not know that malaria with heat first is not common, and it is raised again earlier to contrast its order with cold malaria; this is the kind in which winter evil is stored in the kidney and breaks out, always with heat first. There is not a separate warm malaria from cold damage in winter in which wind first damages [the body] and is between the skin and the intestines and stomach, the later-damaging cold is also between the skin and the intestines and stomach, and the attacks occur at entirely different times. Then warm malaria with heat first has much heat and works just like pure-heat malaria. So Zhongjing generally sets out the two characters "warm malaria," and what he notes beneath — no cold in the body, only heat, aching and vexed bones and joints, occasional retching — are all signs of pure-heat malaria. He only says "the pulse seems normal," to distinguish it from "the pulse of malaria is of itself wiry." Warm malaria from failing to store in winter and receiving evil, and pure-heat malaria in which the lung originally has heat and an external contraction is added, both have evil not solely in shaoyang; so Baihu jia Guizhi tang governs them. This treats according to the pattern of taiyang and yangming, solely clearing heat in the upper burner.

[Some say that] warm malaria, compared with pure-heat malaria, seems to (lacuna) arise from the kidney, so it is not appropriate to treat only the upper burner. [They] do not know that in warm malaria, when one meets summerheat and the sweat drains, the evil qi and sweat both come out, so for the residual evil already out, treating only the exterior aspect of the upper burner is the urgent matter. Since the evil originally came from the exterior, driving it out through the exterior is correct. Otherwise, if Zhongjing's "warm malaria" referred to warm malaria with heat first, would Zhongjing really have classed warm malaria from winter damage to the kidney under vacuity detriment and given no formula? And if this warm malaria formula referred solely to warm malaria from winter damage to the kidney, so that it does not explicitly say it treats pure-heat malaria, would pure-heat malaria not be malaria, with no formula given?

[Xu Bin's formula:] zhimu 6 liang; shigao 1 jin; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); jingmi 2 ge; gui (remove the bark) 3 liang. Of the five ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil until the rice is cooked and the decoction is ready, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

... comes out, recovery.

## Notes

A clause using Baihu jia Guizhi tang for warm malaria (heat only without chills, joint pain, occasional retching).

- Baihu tang clears interior heat and guizhi resolves the exterior and relieves joint pain.
- "The pulse seems normal" means it is not the wiry pulse characteristic of malaria but seems normal.
- The base formula is in the Song boiled-powder format ("cut; for each dose 5 *qian* with one and a half cups of water"), whereas Xu Bin's edition has the older format ("with 1 dou of water boil until the rice is cooked"), the same as the preparation of Baihu tang in the *Shanghan lun*.
- 知毋 is an error for 知母.

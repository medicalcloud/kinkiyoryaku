# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When yin qi is isolated and cut off and yang qi alone breaks out, there is heat with shallow breathing and vexation and oppression, and the hands and feet are hot with a desire to retch. This is called pure-heat malaria (*dan nüe*). If there is only heat without cold, the pathogenic qi is stored within in the heart and lodges outside between the parted flesh, causing wasting and emaciation.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This abridges the *Neijing*'s pure-heat malaria, in which "the lung originally has heat and happens to receive wind-cold; it is stored within in the heart and lodges outside in the parted flesh, with only heat and no cold." So Zhongjing's words are both narration and explanation. When the lung is hot and its qi excess, and at the time of the attack yang is exuberant, it is in all cases because yin qi is isolated and cut off, and then yang qi alone breaks out. When yang qi alone breaks out, the heat is severe; when the heat is severe it damages the qi, so the qi is short; when the qi is short and the heat does not disperse, there is vexation and oppression. When yin is cut off the hands and feet are hot; when vexation and oppression do not stop, there is retching. This is the origin of the name pure-heat malaria.

The reason for only heat without cold is that it breaks out only in the yang qi and does not reach the yin, so the disease is entirely in the yang and received by the upper jiao. The upper jiao contains only the heart and lung; since there is only heat, we know the pathogenic qi is stored within in the heart. Since the heat reaches the flesh and skin, we know it lodges outside in the parted flesh. Vigorous fire eats the qi, so we know there must be wasting and emaciation. Then why, when the heart qi is already hot, does it not first scorch the lung but become external heat? Because the lung qi was originally excess and the pathogen came from outside; hence "stored in the heart." This differs from cases where the heart is deficient and the heat is gathered inside. So it cannot scorch the lung and only the outside is hot. But when it reaches wasting and emaciation, over time it gradually reaches the lung.

## Notes

A clause describing "pure-heat malaria," with heat only and no chills. It is said to summarize the *Suwen*'s treatise on malaria.

- Because yin qi has declined and only yang qi is exuberant, there are heat, shortness of breath, vexation, hot hands and feet, and nausea; if prolonged, wasting.
- No formula is given. Some suggest using the next formula, Baihu jia Guizhi tang (Byakkokakeishito).
- The base lacks the character 癉 (leaving a space); it is supplied because all other sources have it (see the emendation note).

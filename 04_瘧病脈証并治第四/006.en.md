# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Malaria with much cold is called male malaria (*mu nüe*); Shuqi san (Dichroa Leaf Powder) governs it.

Shuqi san formula

Shuqi (dichroa leaf) (burned to remove the rank odor); yunmu (muscovite) (burned for two days and nights); longgu (dragon bone), equal amounts.

Pound the three ingredients above into a powder, and before the attack take half a *qian*[bi] with fermented rice water. For warm malaria, add half a *fen* of shuqi and take one *qian*-spoon at the time of the attack (one formula reads yunshi for yunmu).

Appended formulas from the *Waitai miyao*

Muli tang (Oyster Shell Decoction): treats male malaria.

Muli 4 liang (dry-fried); mahuang (remove the nodes) 4 liang; gancao 2 liang; shuqi 3 liang.

Of the four ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water first boil the shuqi and mahuang, remove the froth on top, and when 6 sheng is obtained add the other medicinals, boil down to 2 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm. If there is vomiting, do not take more.

Chaihu qu Banxia jia Gualou tang (Bupleurum Decoction minus Pinellia plus Trichosanthes Root): treats malaria with thirst, and also treats taxation malaria.

Chaihu 8 liang; renshen, huangqin, gancao, 3 liang each; gualougen 4 liang; shengjiang 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces.

Of the seven ingredients above, with 1 dou 2 sheng of water boil down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, twice a day.

Chaihu Gui Jiang tang (Bupleurum, Cinnamon Twig, and Dried Ginger Decoction) [base: 桂蔞]: treats malaria with much cold and slight heat, or cold only without heat (one prescription works like magic).

Chaihu half a jin; guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); ganjiang 2 liang; gualougen 4 liang; huangqin 3 liang; muli 3 liang (dry-fried); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 1 dou 2 sheng of water boil down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. After the first dose there is slight vexation; after another dose sweat comes out, and recovery follows.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Cold first and heat after is already cold malaria. Then there are cases in which the heart qi is originally vacuous and external evil attacks it, relying on tangible drool; the evil afflicts the pericardium, the qi cannot penetrate to the flesh and exterior, and there is much cold. Probably one is first damaged by intangible cold evil, which then enters inward and combines with drool to become tangible cold; the cold is replete and damages the heart; hence it is called male malaria, because the heart is the male (yang) organ. Later writers' calling pure cold "female" is mistaken. Only when intangible cold carries tangible drool is the evil within the pericardium trapped by the outside and unable to get out. So shuqi is used to force out the tangible drool. Changshan (dichroa root) is good at inducing vomiting for malaria, and shuqi is the sprout of changshan, especially light and empty by nature, acting on the upper part. Yunmu, sweet and neutral, removes evil qi within and treats dead flesh without, and has the function of reaching through to the heart and spleen. Longgu gathers dampness and quiets the spirit, and can secure the heart qi and calm the five viscera. So shuqi is the mainstay and the two medicinals are assistants.

[Xu Bin's formula:] shuqi (washed to remove the rank odor); yunmu (burned for two days and nights); longgu, equal amounts.

Three appended formulas from the *Waitai miyao*.

[Muli tang:] muli 4 liang; mahuang 4 liang (remove the nodes); gancao 2 liang; shuqi 3 liang.

Male malaria is generally due to evil disturbing the pericardium so that the sovereign fire cannot reach outward. So muli, salty and cold, which softens hardness, disperses binding, and can also calm the kidney and make it interact with the heart, is the sovereign; shuqi is still used to make the evil be vomited; and mahuang and gancao are added to help the outward tendency.

Chaihu qu Banxia jia Gualougen tang treats malaria with thirst and also treats taxation malaria. In the *Shanghan lun*, alternating cold and heat is because the shaoyang evil is half exterior and half interior. The malaria evil is also half exterior and half interior; entering and contending with yin, there is cold; coming out and contending with yang, there is heat. This is the picture of shaoyang. There may be shaoyang with signs of other channels as well; but if it were another channel entirely unrelated to shaoyang, it would not be malaria. Hence Xiao Chaihu tang is also a main formula for treating malaria. Replacing banxia with gualougen when there is thirst is also the established method of treating shaoyang. Since it combines attacking and supplementing, it also governs taxation malaria.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula for Chaihu Gui Jiang tang:] huangqin 3 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); muli 2 liang (dry-fried).

The yang qi in the chest disperses and moves among the parted flesh. Now the evil qi impedes it, so the yang that guards the outside is depressed and hidden in the yin that keeps the inside, and the impeded blood is already congealed by cold and does not disperse. When the defensive qi meets its twenty-five circuits through the yang, the disease breaks out; but the evil that has entered the nutritive has no tendency to come out, and what has long been impeded in the nutritive also does not come out to contend with the yang. Hence much cold and little heat, or cold only without heat. Xiao Chaihu tang is originally a formula that stops both yin and yang; since there is much cold, adding guizhi and ganjiang advances it toward yang, and the impeded, fixed evil can be opened. Adding muli further softens its hard fortress; then yin and yang open up and connect freely, and it resolves with great sweating. Hence "one prescription works like magic." This is master Yu's (Yu Chang's) discussion, which is extremely fine, so I have recorded it in full.

Discussion: Concerning the onset of malaria, the *Neijing* first says, "Water qi and defensive qi reside together," and also, "The evil lodges at Wind Mansion." Wind Mansion is where the evil lodges, and within the defensive qi there is no lack of evil residing together. But [people] do not know that evil qi and defensive qi cannot be spoken of as mixed, and that their combining is greatly to be avoided. Why? When the defensive qi combines with the evil, the disease starts; when it separates from the evil, the disease stops. Combined in the yin, there is cold; in the yang, heat. Separated from the yin, the cold stops; from the yang, the heat stops. So Wang Yutai (Wang Kentang) said, "When there is much cold, one should raise the yang; if it does not combine with the yin, the cold stops by itself. When there is much heat, one should lower the yin so that it does not combine with the yang; then the heat stops by itself. When cold and heat alternate, raise one and lower the other, and separate them from the middle with percolating, disinhibiting medicinals so they do not combine; then recovery follows." He devised a main formula: chaihu 1.5 *qian*, and shengma, gegen, fangfeng, and qianghuo 5 *fen* each, all sweet and acrid with clear qi, raise the yang qi to separate it from the yin, so the cold stops by itself; zhimu 1 *qian*, shigao 3 *qian*, and huangqin 5 *fen*, all cold in nature and descending, draw the yin qi downward to separate it from the yang, so the heat stops by itself; zhuling 1.5 *qian* separates and disinhibits yin and yang so they do not combine; chuanshanjia (pangolin scale) 1 *qian* leads the medicinals out of the yin into the yang, piercing through the channels; ginger-processed houpo 1 *qian* disinhibits qi; and sanhe qu 1.5 *qian* moves phlegm. Using this as the base with modifications, it always worked.

Again, for someone with malaria for two years, Zihe (Zhang Congzheng) said, "The shifting of yin and yang always begins at the four extremities," and so he tightly bound those places and let out the blood, so that the evil could go but could not combine, and the patient recovered at once. I saw a child with first-time malaria who could not take medicine, and recalled the *Neijing*'s method of "blocking the empty orifices"; the empty orifices are the chest. So before the attack came I had him take 1 liang of crystal sugar all at once, storing it in the middle to block the path of combining; it never failed to work at once. Why is this? That yin and yang interact and combine to produce malaria is certainly the pivotal point in treating malaria, but people do not know that the place of combining starts at the four extremities and meets at the central duct. This is the pivot within the pivot, beyond what earlier sages reached, so I append it to detail the mechanism of the disease.

Further discussion: Zhongjing's treatment of malaria always makes gouging out the evil the urgent matter. But in fact there are cases where the disease lingers long, the right qi declines, and it cannot expel the evil. So Lizhai (Xue Ji) said, "Whenever a person has long-standing malaria that no medicine has helped, use Buzhong Yiqi tang with banxia added, renshen 1 liang, and roasted ginger 5 *qian*; it stops [the malaria] without trying to stop it." This is a perfect statement. When I saw poor people who could not afford renshen, I had huangqi, baizhu, danggui, heshouwu, and juhong in equal amounts made into pills with the natural juice of fresh ginger, and had them take three or four jin regardless of whether the evil had cleared; not one in a hundred failed. Zhongjing treated sudden illness; this is the principle of treating long illness.

As for quartan malaria (every third day), taking [onsets on] *zi*, *wu*, *mao*, and *you* days as shaoyin malaria, *yin*, *shen*, *si*, and *hai* as jueyin malaria, and *chen*, *xu*, *chou*, and *wei* as taiyin malaria is certainly correct. But there is also the distinction of day and night. Danxi (Zhu Zhenheng) treated two people with malaria, both breaking out on *yin*, *shen*, *si*, and *hai* days. One broke out at *si* and abated at *shen*; [he] said that daytime onset is a yang disease within yin and called for supplementing qi and resolving the exterior, giving Xiao Chaihu tang with doubled chaihu and renshen, plus cangzhu, baizhu, qingpi, chenpi, chuanxiong, and gegen. The other broke out at *hai* and abated at *yin*; [he] said that nighttime onset is a yin disease within yin and called for supplementing blood and coursing the liver, using Xiao Chaihu tang combined with Siwu tang plus qingpi. Each was given ten packets, decocted with ginger one double-hour before the attack, one packet a day; by the eighth packet, on the same day, both had a great sweat and recovered. His subtle distinction of yin and yang can truly supplement where Zhongjing falls short. But all these have the theory of the three yin because the malaria qi has gradually deepened; it is not the principle that malaria evil, all arriving from outside, belongs to the five viscera from the very start. If it belonged to the five viscera, exterior medicines could not cure it. Hence the *Neijing* has the theory of five-viscera malaria.

## Notes

A clause using Shuqi san for malaria with much cold (male malaria), with three formulas appended from the *Waitai miyao* (Muli tang, Chaihu qu Banxia jia Gualou tang, Chaihu Gui Jiang tang).

- Shuqi (the sprout of changshan, dichroa) is a medicinal that stops malaria and induces vomiting; it is taken before the attack.
- Some sources read 牝瘧 ("female malaria") for 牡瘧 (Wikisource *Jingui yaolüe*, Daizhige). Many consider "female" more logical for malaria with predominant yin (cold), but Xu Bin argues that "male" is correct because the heart is the male organ.
- For 柴胡桂蔞湯, most other sources read 柴胡桂薑湯; since the formula contains ganjiang (dried ginger), 薑 appears correct, and the translation uses Chaihu Gui Jiang tang.
- Errors in the base: 雲毋 for 雲母, 亦冶 for 亦治. For 一銭匙 most other sources read 一銭匕. After 半銭, Xu Bin's edition and others have 匕.
- Xu Bin's long commentary includes, in its latter part, the full text of a discussion by Yu Chang (Yu Jiayan), treatments by Wang Kentang, Zhang Congzheng, Xue Ji, and Zhu Zhenheng, and his own cases. "Crystal sugar" (水晶糖) seems to be a kind of rock sugar, but this has not been verified.

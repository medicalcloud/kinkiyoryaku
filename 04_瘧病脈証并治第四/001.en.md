# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: The pulse of malaria is of itself wiry. Wiry and rapid means much heat; wiry and slow means much cold. Wiry, small, and tight: purging it brings recovery. Wiry and slow: it may be warmed. Wiry and tight: sweating may be induced, or acupuncture and moxibustion used. Floating and large: vomiting may be induced. Wiry and rapid is wind arising: stop it by adjusting food and drink.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Malaria is a half-exterior half-interior disease, not an external disease that breaks out suddenly. Thus the *Neijing* says, "Damaged by summerheat in summer, one will surely have *jie* malaria in autumn"; "Damaged first by cold and then by wind, it is cold malaria"; "Damaged first by wind and then by cold, it is warm malaria"; and "It is inside the skin and outside the intestines and stomach." Precisely because it is half exterior and half interior, the pulse must be wiry. Wiry is the qi of *jia* wood of the east; its channel belongs to shaoyang; it is a yin pulse in cold damage and a yang pulse in miscellaneous disease. The signs are at the border of exterior and interior, and the pulse too is between yin and yang. Hence "the pulse of malaria is of itself wiry." "Of itself" means that even when wind or cold has been contracted, the pulse is simply wiry of itself.

So the pulse has a fixed form; with rapidity it is heat, with slowness it is cold. This is the main outline. As for treatment: tight is also a cold pulse; small and tight means [the evil] has entered inward. Since a large pulse is yang, what is small, tight, and inward is yin, and yin must not be dispersed through the exterior; hence "purging it brings recovery." Slow is already cold, so there is no doubt that it should be warmed. Wiry and tight (not sunken) is a cold pulse but not a yin pulse; since it is not yin, sweating, acupuncture, and moxibustion may be used. The malaria pulse is generally wiry; if it is suddenly floating and large, we know the evil is high and shallow, and what is high is made to go out over the top; hence "vomiting may be induced."

But the half-exterior half-interior is the province of shaoyang, and in shaoyang disease sweating, vomiting, and purging are prohibited; why should malaria alone be different? Zhongjing gives the three methods of sweating, vomiting, and purging because the evil differs, roughly using the three methods as outlets for driving out the evil; it is not truly great sweating, vomiting, and purging as in cold damage. Not only are sweating, vomiting, and purging not to be relied on; once the evil lingers and is hard to expel, even medicine cannot be relied on. So Zhongjing, having said "wiry and rapid means much heat," states a further point: "Wiry and rapid is wind arising: stop it by adjusting food and drink." This shows that if the heat does not stop it will reach extreme heat; extreme heat generates wind; when wind arises, liver wood insults earth and passes its heat to the stomach, steadily consuming the fluids, and the yang becomes ever more one-sided and does not return. This cannot be sought from medicine alone; one must stop the blazing heat by adjusting food and drink, with things that generate fluids and stop thirst such as pear juice and sugarcane juice. This is exactly the meaning of the *Neijing*'s "when wind is excessive within, treat with sweet and cold."

## Notes

A clause giving the general outline of the pulse and treatment of malaria (*nüe*: a disease with periodic chills and fever, thought to include true malaria).

- The basic malaria pulse is wiry; with rapidity there is much heat, with slowness much cold.
- Treatment is divided by pulse: wiry, small, and tight — purge; wiry and slow — warm; wiry and tight — sweat, acupuncture, moxibustion; floating and large — induce vomiting.
- When the pulse is wiry and rapid with exuberant heat, the heat is stopped not only with medicine but by adjusting food and drink (sweet, cold things).

# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chest bi-impediment with blocked qi in the chest and shortness of breath: Fuling Xingren Gancao tang (Bukuryokyoninkanzoto; Poria, Apricot Kernel, and Licorice Decoction) governs it; Ju Zhi Jiang tang (Kitsukikyoto; Tangerine Peel, Unripe Bitter Orange, and Fresh Ginger Decoction) also governs it.

Fuling Xingren Gancao tang formula

Fuling (bukuryo) 3 liang; xingren (kyonin) 50 pieces; gancao (kanzo) 1 liang.

Of the three ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil down to 5 sheng and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. If not cured, take more.

Ju Zhi Jiang tang formula

Jupi (kippi; tangerine peel) 1 jin; zhishi (kijitsu) 3 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) half a jin.

Of the three ingredients above, with 5 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng and take warm in two doses (the *Zhouhou* and *Qianjin* say: it treats chest bi-impediment with a feeling of fullness in the chest [base: 福海], choking blockage, a creeping itch-like sensation, roughness in the throat, and spitting of dry foam).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In chest bi-impediment, feeling especially that the qi is blocked and the breath short has, compared with dyspnea, a further picture of closure and blockage, an extreme surplus of qi. We know the congestion and stagnation below is great. So xingren disinhibits the lung qi, fuling is added to guide out retained fluid, and gancao supplements the center. Otherwise, fearing slight cold, jupi and zhishi disinhibit the qi of the middle and upper jiao, and shengjiang is added to diffuse it. Chest bi-impediment originally belongs to deficiency, yet it is treated thus because the blockage of qi is severe; so the branch is treated first and the root afterward.

[Xu Bin's formula:] jupi 1 jin; zhishi 3 liang; shengjiang half a jin.

## Notes

A clause using Fuling Xingren Gancao tang and Ju Zhi Jiang tang for chest bi-impediment with blocked qi in the chest and breathlessness.

- Fuling Xingren Gancao tang removes water-fluid retention and disinhibits the lung qi; Ju Zhi Jiang tang opens stagnant qi and brings down stomach qi. Many say the former is for cases with predominant retained fluid and the latter for predominant qi stagnation.
- For 福海 in the interlinear note, other sources read 愊愊 ("full"), and 福海 appears to be a graphic error; the translation follows 愊愊.
- The base reads 気寒 ("qi cold"); it is emended to 気塞 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

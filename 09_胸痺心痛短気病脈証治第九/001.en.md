# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: In the pulse one should take note of excess and insufficiency. When yang is feeble and yin is wiry, there is chest bi-impediment with pain. The reason is that one must blame its extreme deficiency. Now since the yang is deficient, we know [the disease] is in the upper jiao. The reason for chest bi-impediment with heart pain is that the yin is wiry.

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on chest bi-impediment, heart pain, and shortness of breath. It explains the pathology of chest bi-impediment (a disease of blockage and pain in the chest) by the pulse "yang feeble, yin wiry." Xu Bin's commentary is attached after the next unit.

- "Yang feeble" means the pulse at the cun (yang position) is feeble, i.e., deficiency of upper-jiao yang qi. "Yin wiry" means the pulse at the chi (yin position) is wiry, i.e., exuberance of yin-pathogenic cold in the lower jiao. Yin cold overwhelming the deficient upper-jiao yang causes chest bi-impediment.
- It is often related to chest pain disorders such as angina pectoris.

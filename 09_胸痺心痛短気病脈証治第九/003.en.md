# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chest impediment disease with panting, coughing and spitting, pain in the chest and back, shortness of breath, a sunken, slow pulse at the inch opening, and a small, tight, rapid pulse at the bar: the following formula governs it.

Gualou Xiebai Baijiu tang (Trichosanthes, Chinese Chive, and White Wine Decoction) formula

Gualoushi (trichosanthes fruit) 1 piece (pounded); xiebai (Chinese chive bulb) half a sheng; baijiu (white wine) 7 sheng.

Boil the three ingredients above together down to 2 sheng and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section actually annotates the signs and pulse of chest impediment; all later mentions of chest impediment should be summed up by this. Only, since there are slight variations, it is set out first as the main signs, main pulse, and main formula of chest impediment. The human chest is like heaven: yang qi is at work, so clarity and descent operate in season, the coming and going of breath does not err from its regular measure, and the fluids moisten and nourish above and below. Without congestion and impediment, [the chest] is empty and not filled. [With impediment] the breath becomes uneven, with panting, and spittle arises with coughing. The chest is the front and the back is the rear; when the qi within is impeded, both front and back hurt. When the upper qi cannot regularly descend, the lower qi cannot rise in time, and there is shortness of breath. The inch opening governs yang; being vacuous, hidden, and not beating, it is sunken and slow. The bar governs yin; yin cold contending, it is small, tight, and rapid. Rapid means dry fire within the yin. So gualou, which opens the dry impediment in the chest, is the sovereign; xiebai, acrid and warm, moves the impeded, fixed qi, and baijiu frees and moves the nutritive and defensive, as assistants. The intent is that when the yang qi in the chest spreads, the dryness is moistened, the phlegm opens by itself, and all the signs are cured.

Discussion: Since the sunken, slow pulse at the inch opening and the small, tight, rapid pulse at the bar are taken as the main pulse of chest impediment, while earlier it was said, "When yang is faint and yin is wiry, there is chest impediment with pain," which is correct? Answer: This precisely shows the subtlety of Zhongjing's deliberate discussion of patterns. In the chest impediment pattern the yang is already vacuous; being vacuous it does not move; not moving, the fluids inevitably congeal and stagnate into phlegm. So chest impediment is originally similar to propping rheum and phlegm-rheum. But in propping rheum and phlegm-rheum the rheum is heavy and stagnates the qi, whereas in chest impediment the qi is pared away by yang vacuity and phlegm-rheum follows. So Zhongjing did not list chest impediment among propping rheum and phlegm-rheum, and within chest impediment did not fix on a single pulse but said, "In the pulse one should take note of excess and insufficiency; when yang is faint and yin is wiry, there is chest impediment with pain," and further noted, "one must blame its extreme vacuity." This shows that the chest impediment pattern should be blamed entirely on yang vacuity; it is neither an exterior disease entering from outside nor comparable to phlegm-rheum accumulating within. So gualou and xiebai, moistening dryness and freeing yang, are the mainstay; it is not that dissolving phlegm and bringing down qi are excluded, but the intent truly differs from treating rheum. So the detailed distinction of a sunken, slow inch opening roughly calls the pulse in the yang faint; the detailed distinction of a small, tight, rapid bar roughly calls the pulse in the yin wiry; and "one should take note of excess and insufficiency" is a rough expression. Having one examine the front and back of the bar in general terms of yin and yang, so that one identifies upper-burner yang vacuity, the causes and treatments of the chest impediment pattern and of propping rheum, phlegm-rheum, and the like are clearly distinguished.

## Notes

A clause giving the main signs, pulse, and formula of chest impediment.

- Panting, cough, spitting, pain in the chest and back, and shortness of breath are the main signs; the pulse is sunken and slow at the inch (upper-burner yang vacuity) and small, tight, and rapid at the bar (middle-burner yin cold).
- In Gualou Xiebai Baijiu tang, gualoushi removes phlegm from the chest, xiebai frees the yang, and baijiu (a kind of alcohol, said to be either rice wine or vinegar) moves the medicine. It is regarded as the basic formula for chest impediment.
- For "the following formula governs it," the other sources give the formula name (Gualou Xiebai Baijiu tang).

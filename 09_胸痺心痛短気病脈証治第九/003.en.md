# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chest bi-impediment disease with dyspnea, coughing and spitting, pain in the chest and back, shortness of breath, a deep, slow pulse at the inch opening, and a small, tight, rapid pulse at the guan: the following formula governs it.

Gualou Xiebai Baijiu tang (Karogaihakuhakushuto; Trichosanthes, Chinese Chive, and White Wine Decoction) formula

Gualoushi (trichosanthes fruit) 1 piece (pounded); xiebai (Chinese chive bulb) half a sheng; baijiu (white wine) 7 sheng.

Boil the three ingredients above together down to 2 sheng and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section actually annotates the signs and pulse of chest bi-impediment; all later mentions of chest bi-impediment should be summed up by this. Only, since there are slight variations, it is set out first as the main signs, main pulse, and main formula of chest bi-impediment. The human chest is like heaven: yang qi is at work, so clarity and descent operate in season, the coming and going of breath does not err from its regular measure, and the fluids moisten and nourish above and below. Without congestion and impediment, [the chest] is empty and not filled. [With impediment] the breath becomes uneven, with dyspnea, and spittle arises with coughing. The chest is the front and the back is the rear; when the qi within is impeded, both front and back hurt. When the upper qi cannot regularly descend, the lower qi cannot rise in time, and there is shortness of breath. The inch opening governs yang; being deficient, hidden, and not beating, it is deep and slow. The guan governs yin; yin cold contending, it is small, tight, and rapid. Rapid means dry fire within the yin. So gualou, which opens the dry impediment in the chest, is the sovereign; xiebai, acrid and warm, moves the impeded, fixed qi, and baijiu frees and moves the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, as assistants. The intent is that when the yang qi in the chest spreads, the dryness is moistened, the phlegm opens by itself, and all the signs are cured.

Discussion: Since the deep, slow pulse at the inch opening and the small, tight, rapid pulse at the guan are taken as the main pulse of chest bi-impediment, while earlier it was said, "When yang is feeble and yin is wiry, there is chest bi-impediment with pain," which is correct? Answer: This precisely shows the subtlety of Zhongjing's deliberate discussion of patterns. In the chest bi-impediment pattern the yang is already deficient; being deficient it does not move; not moving, the fluids inevitably congeal and stagnate into phlegm. So chest bi-impediment is originally similar to thoracic fluid retention and phlegm-fluid retention. But in thoracic fluid retention and phlegm-fluid retention the retained fluid is heavy and stagnates the qi, whereas in chest bi-impediment the qi is pared away by yang deficiency and phlegm-fluid retention follows. So Zhongjing did not list chest bi-impediment among thoracic fluid retention and phlegm-fluid retention, and within chest bi-impediment did not fix on a single pulse but said, "In the pulse one should take note of excess and insufficiency; when yang is feeble and yin is wiry, there is chest bi-impediment with pain," and further noted, "one must blame its extreme deficiency." This shows that the chest bi-impediment pattern should be blamed entirely on yang deficiency; it is neither an exterior disease entering from outside nor comparable to phlegm-fluid retention accumulating within. So gualou and xiebai, moistening dryness and freeing yang, are the mainstay; it is not that dissolving phlegm and bringing down qi are excluded, but the intent truly differs from treating retained fluid. So the detailed distinction of a deep, slow inch opening roughly calls the pulse in the yang feeble; the detailed distinction of a small, tight, rapid guan roughly calls the pulse in the yin wiry; and "one should take note of excess and insufficiency" is a hesitant expression. Having one examine the front and back of the guan in general terms of yin and yang, so that one identifies upper-jiao yang deficiency, the causes and treatments of the chest bi-impediment pattern and of thoracic fluid retention, phlegm-fluid retention, and the like are clearly distinguished.

## Notes

A clause giving the main signs, pulse, and formula of chest bi-impediment.

- Dyspnea, cough, spitting, pain in the chest and back, and shortness of breath are the main signs; the pulse is deep and slow at the cun (upper-jiao yang deficiency) and small, tight, and rapid at the guan (middle-jiao yin cold).
- In Gualou Xiebai Baijiu tang, gualoushi removes phlegm from the chest, xiebai frees the yang, and baijiu (a kind of alcohol, said to be either rice wine or vinegar) moves the medicine. It is regarded as the basic formula for chest bi-impediment.
- For "the following formula governs it," the other sources give the formula name (Gualou Xiebai Baijiu tang).

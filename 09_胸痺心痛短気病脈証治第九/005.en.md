# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chest bi-impediment with stuffiness in the heart, lingering qi bound in the chest, fullness of the chest, and counterflow from below the ribs surging [base: 槍] into the heart: Zhishi Xiebai Guizhi tang (Unripe Bitter Orange, Chinese Chive, and Cinnamon Twig Decoction) governs it; Renshen tang (Ginseng Decoction) also governs it.

[Zhishi Xiebai Guizhi tang formula:] zhishi 4 pieces; houpo 4 liang; xiebai half a jin; guizhi 1 liang; gualou[shi] 1 piece (pounded).

Of the five ingredients above, with 5 sheng of water first boil the zhishi and houpo down to 1 sheng [other sources: 2 sheng], remove the dregs, add the other medicinals, bring to a boil several times, and take warm in three doses.

Renshen tang formula

Renshen, gancao, ganjiang, and baizhu, 3 liang each.

Of the four ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Chest bi-impediment with added stuffiness in the heart and fullness of the chest resembles the pictures of stuffiness and chest bind, but it is the yang of the upper jiao being feeble and guest qi stirring the diaphragm. The note "lingering qi bound in the chest" is the guest qi. Further, counterflow from below the ribs surging into the heart means that not only the upper jiao but also the middle jiao is deficient, and yin pathogen occupies it, counterflowing and surging. So to xiebai and gualou are added zhishi and houpo to open the binding, and guizhi to move yang and course the liver. "Renshen tang also governs it" because the disease is due to central deficiency; once the most severe part is removed, one may supplement the right to transform the pathogen. The deficiency of chest bi-impediment is originally feebleness of yang qi, not deficiency of ying-nutrient qi. Yang does not call for supplementation; diffuse and free it, and the yang qi flows freely; flowing freely, yang becomes exuberant. So the xiebai formulas focus on moving yang and do not supplement. Here "Renshen tang also governs it" because the counterflow below the ribs is due to deficiency of central qi, so the center is supplemented.

[Xu Bin's formula:] zhishi 4 pieces; xiebai half a jin; guizhi 1 liang; houpo 4 liang.

[Renshen tang:] renshen 3 liang; gancao 3 liang; ganjiang 3 liang; baizhu 3 liang.

## Notes

A clause using Zhishi Xiebai Guizhi tang (excess) and Renshen tang (deficiency) for chest bi-impediment with chest stuffiness and fullness and qi surging up from below the ribs. For the same signs, two formulas are given according to whether the cause is excess or deficiency.

- Zhishi Xiebai Guizhi tang adds zhishi, houpo, and guizhi to gualou and xiebai to open stagnant qi. Renshen tang has the same composition as Lizhong wan (tang) in the *Shanghan lun*, warming and supplementing the middle jiao.
- The base lacks the heading for the Zhishi Xiebai Guizhi tang formula; most other sources have it.
- For 槍 in 逆槍心, all other sources read 搶 ("surge"); the translation follows 搶.

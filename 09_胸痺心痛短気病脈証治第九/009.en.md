# Clause 9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Heart pain penetrating through to the back and back pain penetrating through to the heart: Wutou Chishizhi wan (Aconite Main Root and Halloysite Pill) governs it.

Chishizhi wan formula

Shujiao 1 liang (one method: 2 *fen*); wutou 1 *fen* (blast-fried); fuzi half a liang (blast-fried; one method: 1 *fen*); ganjiang 1 liang (one method: 1 *fen*); chishizhi 1 liang (one method: 2 *fen*).

Powder the five ingredients above and make honey pills the size of firmiana seeds; take one pill before meals, three times a day.

Jiutong wan (Nine Pains Pill): treats the nine kinds of heart pain.

Fuzi 3 liang (blast-fried); raw langya (agrimony root bud) 1 liang (mix-fried until fragrant); badou 1 liang (remove the skin and core, dry-fry, grind into a paste); renshen, ganjiang, and wuzhuyu (evodia), 1 liang each.

Powder [base: 沫] the six ingredients above and make refined-honey pills the size of firmiana seeds; take them down with wine. A strong person first takes three pills, three times a day; a weak person, two pills. It also treats sudden malign strike with abdominal distension and pain and inability to speak; and it treats cold accumulated over years flowing and pouring into the heart and chest with pain, as well as cold swelling with ascending qi, and blood disorders from falling off a horse or a carriage — it governs all of these. Dietary prohibitions are as usual.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The heart and the back originally belong to the two faces, and the empty orifice between them is where the healthy qi is stored to connect above and below. Now when the heart hurts, it penetrates to the back, and when the back hurts, it penetrates to the heart; clearly the healthy qi is insufficient and pathogenic cold is bound in the middle. So wutou, fuzi, ganjiang, and shujiao warm and bring down the qi, and chishizhi enters the heart to nourish the blood, and settles and gathers in what floats, to calm the middle. When the pathogen is gone, the healthy qi in the chest recovers by itself and the pain stops.

[If there is no effect,] increase the dose slightly. Appended formula.

Heart pain never departs from cold, and there may also be somewhat stagnant accumulations. So this too takes ganjiang and fuzi as the mainstay, adding wuzhuyu to bring down turbid yin, langya to remove floating wind, and badou to expel what is lodged and stagnant. Pathogen does not attach except where there is deficiency, so renshen is added to nourish the right. It also treats sudden malign strike, cold accumulated over years, and blood disorders because, when the right is nourished, the pathogen driven out, and the qi freed, all the signs are cured. [Xu Bin's formula:] fuzi 3 liang (blast-fried); raw langya 1 liang; badou [1] liang (remove the skin, grind into a paste); ganjiang 1 liang; wuzhuyu 1 liang; renshen 1 liang.

## Notes

A clause using Wutou Chishizhi wan for severe yin-cold heart pain in which heart and back pain penetrate each other, with the appended formula Jiutong wan.

- Wutou Chishizhi wan stacks strongly warming medicinals — wutou, fuzi, ganjiang, shujiao — with chishizhi to astringe the heart qi.
- The clause names the formula Wutou Chishizhi wan; the heading reads Chishizhi wan.
- After the formula, most other sources have "if there is no effect, increase the dose slightly," and this phrase also appears in Xu Bin's commentary; the base lacks it.
- Jiutong wan is an appended formula added by the Song editors, said to treat nine kinds of heart pain (said to be from worms, infixation, wind, palpitation, food, retained fluid, cold, heat, and coming and going).
- For 沫之, all other sources read 末之 ("powder it").

# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Glomus in the heart, various counterflows, and suspended pain of the heart: Guizhi Shengjiang Zhishi tang (Cinnamon Twig, Fresh Ginger, and Unripe Bitter Orange Decoction) governs it.

Gui[zhi Sheng]jiang Zhishi tang formula

Guizhi and shengjiang, 3 liang each; zhishi 5 pieces.

Of the three ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng and take warm in three doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

From here on, chest impediment is not mentioned; there need not be definite signs of chest impediment. Glomus in the heart is the picture of yin evil congealing and binding. Without initial counterflow of qi it would not reach this. But when it comes to heart pain as if suspended, earlier the evil formed glomus in the heart because of counterflow, and later the evil bound in the heart so that below it is, on the contrary, as if empty. So guizhi removes the evil, and shengjiang and zhishi diffuse, disperse, and bring down the qi.

[Xu Bin's formula:] guizhi 3 liang; shengjiang 3 liang; zhishi 5 liang.

## Notes

A clause using Guizhi Shengjiang Zhishi tang for glomus in the heart (epigastrium), various counterflows of qi, and a pain as if the heart were suspended.

- Guizhi brings down the upward-counterflowing qi, shengjiang harmonizes stomach qi, and zhishi opens the glomus.
- The formula heading reads Gui Jiang Zhishi tang; most other sources read Guizhi Shengjiang Zhishi tang.

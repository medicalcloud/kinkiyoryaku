# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A person who appears healthy, without cold or heat, who is short of breath and cannot breathe sufficiently, has excess.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that in treating disease one must know where the deficiency lies. So to know the pulse of a disease, one should first examine the forms of excess and insufficiency in the pulse; the most deficient place is the place that admits the pathogen. For example, in front of the guan is yang, and the yang pulse governs yang; yang and feeble is deficiency. Behind the guan is yin, and the yin pulse governs yin; yin and wiry is deficiency pathogen. But the wiry pulse is something yin has, and though it is said "wiry means diminution," the deficiency is not yet severe. Yang should be surging and large; if it is feeble, the deficiency is severe. When deficient, pathogen takes advantage of it, and there is chest bi-impediment with pain. Impediment means the yang qi in the chest does not function; pain means that when yang does not function, yin fire causes stabbing pain. Then if there were no deficiency, how could yin fire take advantage? Hence "the reason is that one must blame its extreme deficiency."

But deficiency alone cannot cause pain. Now from the feeble yang we know the deficiency is in the upper jiao; the reason for chest bi-impediment with heart pain is the wiriness within the yin, that is, pathogenic cold within the yin taking advantage of the deficiency of the upper jiao, producing impediment and pain. From this we know that deficiency is the cause that invites pathogen, and wiriness is the pathogen that attacks the deficiency. But though there is pathogen, it likewise comes back to deficiency, because the yang is feeble.

If a person who appears healthy has no cold or heat, it is not an exterior pathogen; and the signs of chest bi-impediment and heart pain are not seen. Yet if he is short of breath and cannot breathe sufficiently, what is this if not pathogen obstructing the qi of breathing? Hence "excess." Then this is not even chest bi-impediment. The two clauses are given together to show the method of distinguishing deficiency and excess.

## Notes

A clause stating that shortness of breath without cold and heat (exterior signs) and without signs of chest bi-impediment is an excess pattern in which pathogen (phlegm-fluid retention, food stagnation, etc.) obstructs the flow of qi. It contrasts with the deficiency (chest bi-impediment) of the previous clause.

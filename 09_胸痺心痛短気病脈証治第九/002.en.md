# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A person who appears healthy, without cold or heat, who is short of breath and cannot breathe sufficiently, has repletion.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that in treating disease one must know where the vacuity lies. So to know the pulse of a disease, one should first examine the forms of excess and insufficiency in the pulse; the most vacuous place is the place that admits the evil. For example, in front of the bar is yang, and the yang pulse governs yang; yang and faint is vacuity. Behind the bar is yin, and the yin pulse governs yin; yin and wiry is vacuity evil. But the wiry pulse is something yin has, and though it is said "wiry means diminution," the vacuity is not yet severe. Yang should be surging and large; if it is faint, the vacuity is severe. When vacuous, evil takes advantage of it, and there is chest impediment with pain. Impediment means the yang qi in the chest does not function; pain means that when yang does not function, yin fire causes stabbing pain. Then if there were no vacuity, how could yin fire take advantage? Hence "the reason is that one must blame its extreme vacuity."

But vacuity alone cannot cause pain. Now from the faint yang we know the vacuity is in the upper burner; the reason for chest impediment with heart pain is the wiriness within the yin, that is, cold evil within the yin taking advantage of the vacuity of the upper burner, producing impediment and pain. From this we know that vacuity is the cause that invites evil, and wiriness is the evil that attacks the vacuity. But though there is evil, it likewise comes back to vacuity, because the yang is faint.

If a person who appears healthy has no cold or heat, it is not an exterior evil; and the signs of chest impediment and heart pain are not seen. Yet if he is short of breath and cannot breathe sufficiently, what is this if not evil obstructing the qi of breathing? Hence "repletion." Then this is not even chest impediment. The two clauses are given together to show the method of distinguishing vacuity and repletion.

## Notes

A clause stating that shortness of breath without cold and heat (exterior signs) and without signs of chest impediment is a repletion pattern in which evil (phlegm-rheum, food stagnation, etc.) obstructs the flow of qi. It contrasts with the vacuity (chest impediment) of the previous clause.

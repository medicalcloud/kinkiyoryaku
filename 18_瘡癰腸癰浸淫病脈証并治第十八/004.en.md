# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Intestinal abscess: the lesser abdomen is swollen and glomus-like, painful as soon as it is pressed, like strangury, yet the urine is normal of itself; there is fever from time to time, spontaneous sweating, and again aversion to cold. When the pulse is slow and tight, the pus has not yet formed; one may purge it, and there should be blood. When the pulse is surging and rapid, the pus has already formed; one must not purge. Dahuang Mudan tang governs it.

Dahuang Mudan tang (Rhubarb and Moutan Decoction) formula

Dahuang 4 liang; mudan (moutan) 1 liang; taoren (peach kernel) 50 pieces; guazi (melon seed) half a sheng; mangxiao 3 ge.

Of the five ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, bring to the boil again [base: 丹], and take it in a single dose. If there is pus, it should come down; if there is no [base: "first"] pus, blood should come down.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Swelling abscess [base and others: "intestinal abscess"] is most troublesome in its swelling, not comparable to the tense abdominal skin of intestinal abscess, so it is named directly after the swelling. The lesser abdomen is glomus-like: the inside is replete and not soft. It hurts as soon as it is pressed because blood with form is causing the disease. It is like strangury because when the blood aspect is hot, the flow is not easy. The blood aspect is in pain but the qi is not diseased, so the urine is still normal. Although the lesser abdomen governs the lower burner, no signs of the bladder or kidney are seen — this is just what the *Neijing* calls "opening and closing are not achieved, cold qi follows it, sinks into the vessels, and forms a fistula." But in the intestinal abscess above, heat-toxin lodges in the abdomen, so there is no heat in the body; here alone there is fever from time to time, because the nutritive of the yang channels is hot, causing tidal fever and spontaneous sweating; but the heat binds below, hot outside and cold inside, so again there is aversion to cold. A slow, tight pulse means the blood has not entirely spoiled and the pulse has not yet turned to heat, so it is slow, stagnant, tight, and contracted; one knows the pus has not yet formed. Its toxic qi can be purged; the toxic qi is already near the lower part of the blood, so there should be blood. If the pulse is surging and rapid, the qi of toxic heat is pervasive and not contained; the pus has already formed, and the spoiled, turbid matter with form must be scooped out from between skin and flesh; it cannot be dissolved from within; hence "one must not purge." Dahuang Mudan tang is a purging formula: mudan and taoren drain the blood network vessels, dahuang and mangxiao purge the bound heat, and dongguazi brings qi down, disperses heat, and is good at regulating yangming and restoring its right qi. But though this formula is a purgative, it is really a medicine that dissolves from within; so if there is a little pus, it goes out below, and if there is no pus, it brings out the blood that has suffered the toxin, and the swelling subsides.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] dahuang 4 liang; mudan 1 liang; taoren 50 pieces; guazi half a sheng (that is, dongguazi, winter melon seed); mangxiao 3 ge.

## Notes

A clause on intestinal abscess with swelling and glomus of the lower abdomen and pain on pressure, distinguishing by the pulse whether pus has formed, and using Dahuang Mudan tang.

- A formula that purges heat with dahuang and mangxiao, removes static blood with mudanpi and taoren, and expels pus with guazi (dongguazi).
- "Dahuang Mudan tang governs it" comes after "when the pulse is surging and rapid ... one must not purge," but it is usually understood as the formula for the stage before pus has formed (when purging is permitted).
- For the base's 丹煎, most other sources read 再煎 ("boil again"), and for 如先膿, all other sources read 如無膿 ("if there is no pus"); the translation follows them.
- Xu Bin's text reads 腫癰 ("swelling abscess") for 腸癰 and 膿未成 ("pus not yet formed") for 膿巳成.

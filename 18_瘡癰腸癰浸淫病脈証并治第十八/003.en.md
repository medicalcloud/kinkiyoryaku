# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Intestinal abscess as a disease: the body's skin is encrusted like scales, the abdominal skin is tense, and on pressure it is soft, as if swollen, but there are no accumulations in the abdomen, no heat in the body, and the pulse is rapid. This is abscess pus within the pus [other sources: "intestine" or "abdomen"]. Yiyi Fuzi Baijiang san governs it.

Yiyi Fuzi Baijiang san (Coix, Aconite, and Patrinia Powder) formula

Yiyiren (coix) 10 *fen*; fuzi 2 *fen*; baijiang (patrinia) 5 *fen*.

Pound the three ingredients above into powder; take a square-inch spoonful, decoct with 2 sheng of water until reduced by half, and take in a single dose (it should come down with the urine).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The previous section discussed sores and abscesses in general: rotting from heat in the nutritive qi, a disease not of the exterior but of the bodily frame, so it was distinguished by a rapid pulse without heat and, instead, shivering aversion to cold. The intestinal abscess discussed here is a disease of the intestines and stomach, so it would seem there should be only abdominal pain without reaching the outside; but this ignores that an abscess is a disease of the blood vessels, that the intestine is yangming, and that yangming governs the flesh of the whole body. So the body is surely encrusted; encrusted means like scales. In the *Jingui*, encrustation is mentioned three times. For lung abscess it says "encrustation in the chest": the lung governs the qi of the whole body but not its blood, yet the chest is the house of the lung, and heat passing into the nutritive damages its blood vessels, hence encrustation. For the five taxations with dry blood it says "encrusted skin": dry blood is spoiled blood, and spoiled blood damages blood; moreover, dry blood is stored either in the intestine or the stomach, both belonging to yangming, so it also governs encrusted skin — but taxation disease always first damages yin, so there is often darkness of both eyes. In intestinal abscess the toxin is in the intestine; the intestine belongs to yangming, which governs the flesh, so the body is encrusted. The abdomen is the house of the intestine, so the abdominal skin is tense: the qi of toxic heat drums upward. Qi has no form, so it is soft on pressure; and though the tense skin looks swollen, there are in fact no accumulations. The disease is not in the exterior, so there is no heat in the body; yet though there is no heat, the pulse is rapid, because an abscess is a disease of the blood and the pulse governs the blood. Hence "this is intestinal abscess." Yiyi, being cold, removes heat, and also brings qi down, overcomes dampness, disinhibits the intestines and stomach, and breaks toxic swelling; so it is the sovereign. Baijiang is good at expelling pus, breaking blood, and disinhibiting bound heat-toxin qi; so it is the minister. Fuzi guides heat and moves binding; so it is the paradoxical assistant.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] yiyiren 7 *fen*; fuzi 2 *fen*; baijiang 5 *fen* (that is, kucai).

## Notes

A clause using Yiyi Fuzi Baijiang san for intestinal abscess (suppurative disease of the intestine) with rough skin, a tense but soft abdominal skin, no fever, and a rapid pulse.

- A formula that removes heat and expels pus with yiyiren, expels pus and breaks blood with baijiang, and moves binding with a small amount of fuzi.
- For 膿 ("pus") in the base's "abscess pus within the pus," Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*) and Daizhige read 腸 ("intestine"), and Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*), another source, and Xu Bin's text read 腹 ("abdomen"). Xu Bin's commentary reads "this is intestinal abscess."
- For "no accumulations in the abdomen, no heat in the body," the base reads 積飛 and 身而熱, but all other sources read 聚 and 無, so they have been emended (see the emendations).

# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Suffering from incised wounds: Wangbuliuxing san governs it.

Wangbuliuxing san (Vaccaria Powder)

Wangbuliuxing (vaccaria) 10 *fen* (gathered in the eighth month); fine leaves of shuodiao (Sambucus chinensis) 10 *fen* (gathered on the seventh day of the seventh month); white [base: 自] bark of mulberry root growing to the southeast 10 *fen* (gathered on the third day of the third month); gancao 18 *fen*; chuanjiao (Sichuan pepper) 3 *fen* (remove the seeds [base: 日] and closed ones, and remove the sweat [base: 汁]); huangqin 2 *fen*; ganjiang 2 *fen*; shaoyao 2 *fen*; houpo 2 *fen*.

Of the nine ingredients above, the three from the mulberry root bark upward are burned to ash with their nature preserved, not letting them burn too far. Pound and sift each separately, combine and process into a powder, and take a square-inch spoonful. For a small wound, dust it on directly; for a large wound, simply take it internally. It may also be taken after childbirth. If there is wind-cold, do not use the eastern mulberry root. The first three items are all dried in the shade for a hundred days.

Painong san (Pus-Expelling Powder) formula

Zhishi 16 pieces; shaoyao 6 *fen*; jiegeng (platycodon) 1 *fen* [other sources: 2 *fen*].

Pound the three ingredients above into powder; take one egg yolk, and with an amount of the powder equal to the yolk, knead them together

until well combined, mix with a drink, and take once a day.

Painong tang (Pus-Expelling Decoction) formula

Gancao 2 liang; jiegeng 3 liang; shengjiang 1 liang; dazao 10 pieces.

Of the four ingredients above, with 3 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng and take 5 ge warm, twice a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is not the formula for the incised wound above, long injured and without sweat, but a formula for incised wounds in general; hence "suffering from incised wounds: Wangbuliuxing san governs it." Wangbuliuxing is bitter and neutral by nature and can free the blood vessels, and so, paradoxically, it can stop the bleeding of incised wounds and drive out pain. Shuodiao also frees qi and blood and is especially good at opening impediment. The flesh of the whole body is governed by the lung, and sangbaipi best disinhibits lung qi; a root growing to the southeast faces the yang and its vital qi is especially complete, so it restores the governing qi of the flesh. So these three are used in very large amounts as sovereigns. Gancao resolves toxin and harmonizes the ying-nutrient and is used in especially large amount as minister. Pepper and ginger nourish the yang in the chest, houpo courses the internally bound qi, and huangqin and shaoyao clear heat in the yin aspect, as assistants. If there is wind-cold, this belongs to visiting pathogen in the meridians and collaterals, and mulberry bark only disinhibits lung qi and cannot drive out external pathogen, so it is not used.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] chuanjiao 3 *fen* (remove the seeds and closed ones, and remove the sweat); houpo 2 *fen*; ganjiang 2 *fen*.

... sift, combine and process into a powder, and take a square-inch spoonful. For a small wound, dust it on directly; for a large wound, simply take it internally.

Painong san formula. Commentary: egg yolk and shaoyao harmonize yin qi, and zhishi combined with jiegeng frees the qi of the whole body, so the pus moves by itself. People know zhishi can bring down internal qi, but how would they know that combined with jiegeng it can disinhibit the qi of the whole body and expel pus?

Pound the three ingredients above into powder; take one egg yolk, and make the powder equal to the yolk.

[On Painong tang:] Commentary: gancao and jiegeng open and lift lung qi, and shengjiang and dazao harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense of the middle and upper jiao, freeing the internal qi so that pus does not congeal. These two formulas are for treating in general sores and abscesses that cannot be dispersed, not devised solely for intestinal abscess or swelling abscess.

## Notes

A clause using Wangbuliuxing san for incised wounds, with the formulas Painong san and Painong tang also recorded.

- Wangbuliuxing, shuodiao, and sangbaipi are burned to ash and used, dusted on the wound or taken internally.
- Painong san and Painong tang have no indication clauses; Xu Bin regards them as formulas for sores and abscesses in general that cannot be dispersed.
- From the end of the Painong san instructions ("until well combined") through Painong tang, a leaf of the base is missing, and the text has been supplied from Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*).
- For the note on chuanjiao, "除日及閉口者汁," most other sources read "除目及閉口者汗" or "除目及閉口去汗," which the translation follows. For 自皮, all other sources read 白皮 ("white bark").

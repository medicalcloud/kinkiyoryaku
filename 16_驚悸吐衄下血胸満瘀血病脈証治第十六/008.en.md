# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is wiry and large. Wiry means diminution; large means hollow. Diminution means cold; hollow means deficiency. When cold and deficiency strike each other, it is called tympanic. In women there is half-birth (miscarriage) and metrostaxis; in men, blood loss.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section speaks of the pulse of bleeding below, not of vomiting blood or nosebleed. A wiry pulse means the wei-defensive qi is bound, so it is diminution and cold. A large pulse means the qi is not secure, so it is hollow and deficiency. Wiry and large means wiry at first pressure, by which yang is examined, and large at somewhat heavier pressure, by which yin is examined; without asking we know that above the pathogen is in excess and below the right is deficient. Hence "when cold and deficiency strike each other, it is called tympanic," meaning like leather with something on top and empty beneath. When the lower part is deficient, there is no yang to command it; the blood does not move along the meridians but leaks downward. Men and women are alike; hence "in women there is half-birth and metrostaxis; in men, blood loss" — blood leaking away below as if lost.

## Notes

A clause taking the wiry, large tympanic pulse as the pulse of bleeding below due to cold and deficiency (miscarriage and metrostaxis in women, blood loss in men). It is nearly the same text as in the chapter on blood impediment and deficiency/consumption (unit 11).

- For 撃 in 寒虚相撃, Daizhige and Xu Bin's edition read 搏 ("contend").

# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is wiry and large. Wiry means diminution; large means scallion-stalk. Diminution means cold; scallion-stalk means vacuity. When cold and vacuity strike each other, it is called drumskin. In women there is half-birth (miscarriage) and spotting; in men, blood loss.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section speaks of the pulse of bleeding below, not of vomiting blood or nosebleed. A wiry pulse means the defensive qi is bound, so it is diminution and cold. A large pulse means the qi is not secure, so it is scallion-stalk and vacuity. Wiry and large means wiry at first pressure, by which yang is examined, and large at somewhat heavier pressure, by which yin is examined; without asking we know that above the evil is replete and below the right is vacuous. Hence "when cold and vacuity strike each other, it is called drumskin," meaning like leather with something on top and empty beneath. When the lower part is vacuous, there is no yang to command it; the blood does not move along the channels but leaks downward. Men and women are alike; hence "in women there is half-birth and spotting; in men, blood loss" — blood leaking away below as if lost.

## Notes

A clause taking the wiry, large drumskin pulse as the pulse of bleeding below due to cold and vacuity (miscarriage and spotting in women, blood loss in men). It is nearly the same text as in the chapter on blood impediment and vacuity taxation (unit 11).

- For 撃 in 寒虚相撃, Daizhige and Xu Bin's edition read 搏 ("contend").

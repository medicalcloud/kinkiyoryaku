# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Vomiting of blood with cough, counterflow, and ascending qi, a rapid pulse with heat, and inability to lie down: the patient dies.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Vomiting of blood generally arises first from yang deficiency and later becomes yin deficiency. When it reaches yin deficiency, the fire grows daily; there is fire that scorches yin but no yang that generates yin. Coughing consumes and scatters the lung qi; counterflow with ascending qi means the liver carries the ministerial fire upward. A rapid pulse with heat means there is no yin. Inability to lie down means that even lying at night the blood does not return to the liver; wood withers and fire burns, and the sovereign fire turns into dry fire. Yin and yang are both deficient, and ominous signs combine with a tendency to immediate exhaustion. Hence "the patient dies."

## Notes

A clause stating that vomiting of blood with cough, counterflow and ascending qi, a rapid pulse, fever, and inability to lie down is a fatal pattern in which both yin and yang are exhausted.

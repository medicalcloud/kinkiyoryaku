# Clause 15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Bleeding below, with blood first and stool after: this is near blood. Chixiaodou Danggui san (Sekishozutokisan) governs it (the formula appears under Hu Huo).

Insufficiency of heart qi with vomiting of blood and nosebleed: use the following formula.

Xiexin tang (Shashinto; Heart-Draining Decoction) (also treats sudden turmoil)

Dahuang (daio) 2 liang; huanglian (oren) and huangqin (ogon), 1 liang each.

Of the three ingredients above, with 3 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng and take it in a single dose.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

With blood first and stool after, we know that before defecation the blood has already gathered at the anus — near; hence "this is near blood." The lower jiao is governed by kidney and bladder, the residence of water. If below there were no lingering dampness mixing with the blood, stool and urine would be normal and the blood would return to the meridians by itself; how would it overflow? So chixiaodou (sekishozu) is the mainstay to remove damp-heat of the yin aspect, and danggui guides the blood back to the meridians. Its action is very direct, unlike distant blood, whose damage is in spleen and kidney, with many twists of warming and cooling, supplementing and draining.

Vomiting of blood may arise from long illness with heat above, vexation, and cough, or from drinking to excess. If it is due to heart deficiency, then with deficiency heat gathers within, fire becomes exuberant and scorches yin, the blood surges and counterflows upward; coming out through the clear passages it is nosebleed, through the turbid passages vomiting. So it is treated centering on insufficiency of heart qi, meaning it cannot be equated with the gradual onset in the various yin deficiencies and in heavy drinkers. So huangqin and huanglian clear its heat and dahuang purges its stasis, and it is called "Heart-Draining Decoction," because the disease has already invaded the heart and dawdling would brew disaster.

[Xu Bin's formula:] dahuang 2 liang; huanglian 1 liang; huangqin 1 liang.

## Notes

A paragraph combining a clause using Chixiaodou Danggui san for "near blood" (bleeding before defecation, said to be near the anus and rectum) and a clause using Xiexin tang for vomiting of blood and nosebleed due to insufficient heart qi (exuberant heart fire).

- Chixiaodou Danggui san, from the chapter on lily bulb disease, Hu Huo, and yin-yang toxin, removes damp-heat and returns the blood to the meridians.
- Xiexin tang (San Huang Xiexin tang (Sanoshashinto)), of three ingredients — dahuang, huanglian, huangqin — drains the fire of heart and stomach to stop bleeding.
- For 心気不足 ("insufficiency of heart qi"), the *Qianjin* is said to read 心気不定 ("unsettled heart qi"), and some argue that "unsettled" is correct since this is a formula for bleeding due to heat.
- For "use the following formula," the other sources read "Xiexin tang governs it."

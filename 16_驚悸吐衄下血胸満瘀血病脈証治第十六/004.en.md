# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Those who habitually have nosebleeds must not be made to sweat; if they sweat, the forehead will surely sink, the pulse will be tight and tense, the eyes will stare straight without being able to move, and they will not sleep.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Since nosebleed is a matter of the yang meridians, it seems it could be resolved from outside; but [such a view] does not know that sweat is blood-fluid governed by the heart. Those with habitual nosebleed have lost too much blood; if they are also made to sweat, they lose their yin again and the yang qi is weakened by it. The forehead is the region of the heart; with yin lost and yang weakened, it must sink — sinking meaning not firm and full. The pulse belongs to the heart; when blood cannot nourish it, it loses its gentle, moderate qi and becomes tight and tense. The eyes see by receiving blood; long nosebleed followed by sweating makes yin desert, so the eyes stare straight and cannot move. With heart blood deficient yang is disturbed; disturbed, the fire counterflows and one cannot sleep.

## Notes

A prohibition clause: those who frequently have nosebleeds must not be made to sweat. The same clause appears in the middle Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 86).

- Blood and sweat share a source; making one who has lost blood sweat loses more yin-blood, causing a sunken forehead, a tight pulse, fixed staring eyes, and insomnia.

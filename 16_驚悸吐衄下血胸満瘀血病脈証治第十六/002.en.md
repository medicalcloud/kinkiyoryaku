# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When the pulse [other sources: the cubit pulse] is floating and the eyes are dim and yellow, the nosebleed has not stopped. When the dim yellow is gone and the eyes are clear, one knows the nosebleed has now stopped.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Nosebleed is blood of the clear passages, going from the governing vessel through Wind Mansion over the vertex and down into the nose. It is depressed heat of liver and kidney, fire surging into the yang channels so that channel blood comes out wildly. So it is said that in nosebleed the cubit pulse is floating: the cubit governs the lower burner, and when liver and kidney have heat and are vacuous, the cubit is floating; hence earlier "a floating cubit pulse means damage to the kidney." The eyes belong to the liver; when yangming heat qi overwhelms them, the eyes are dim and yellow; *yi* and *gui* share a source. So with a floating cubit and dim yellow eyes, the evil is exuberant and the nosebleed has not stopped. When the dim yellow is gone, the heat has declined, and further the eyes are clear — clear meaning bright and fresh. Knowing that the kidney heat has resolved, the liver too is well. Blood belongs to yin; with no heat pressing it, where would nosebleed come from? Hence "one knows."

## Notes

The clauses on nosebleed begin here. A floating pulse with dim, yellow eyes indicates the nosebleed has not stopped; clear eyes indicate it has.

- For 夫 in 夫脈浮, most other sources read 尺 ("the cubit pulse is floating"), and Xu Bin also explains it as a floating cubit pulse.
- The base reads 目睛急了; it is emended to 慧了 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

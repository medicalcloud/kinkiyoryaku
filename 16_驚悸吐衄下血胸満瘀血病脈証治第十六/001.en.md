# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is stirring and weak. Stirring means fright; weak means palpitations.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The earlier chapter on running piglet already said, "There is fright and fear, and pathogenic fire; all are contracted through the outbreak of fright." Here fright and palpitations are again set out separately, not to detail the origin of the disease, but to say that some diseases involve fright and mania with restlessness, and some only palpitations of the heart without calm. Fright is pathogen attacking the heart, on the side of excess, so the cun pulse is stirring — stirring meaning there are grains like beans. Palpitation is the spirit unable to govern, on the side of deficiency, so the cun pulse is weak — weak meaning the pulse comes without force. Stirring and weak means pathogen attacks while the heart is originally deficient, so fright and palpitations appear together. But the pulse is still divided: stirring is the outbreak of the qi of fright, weak is the form of the qi of palpitation. Hence "stirring means fright, weak means [palpitations]."

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on fright palpitations, spitting of blood, nosebleed, bloody stool, chest fullness, and static blood. A stirring cun pulse indicates fright (excess), a weak one palpitations (deficiency).

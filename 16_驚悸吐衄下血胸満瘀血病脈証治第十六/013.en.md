# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Unceasing vomiting of blood: Baiye tang (Hakuyoto; Arborvitae Leaf Decoction) governs it.

Baiye tang

Baiye (hakuyo; arborvitae leaf) and ganjiang (kankyo), 3 liang each; ai (mugwort) 3 handfuls.

Boil the three ingredients above with 5 sheng of water and 1 sheng of horse-dung juice combined, down to 1 sheng, and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The emphasis here is on the word "unceasing": all cold, cooling, blood-stopping medicinals have failed. Vomiting of blood originally arises from yang deficiency unable to guide the blood back into the meridians; but blood is lost and yin deficient, so baiye, the most yin-nourishing, is the sovereign; aiye (gaiyo), which goes to the meridians, the minister; ganjiang, warming the stomach, the assistant; and horse-dung juice, guiding the fire down, the envoy. In my view, if there is no horse-dung juice, a boy's urine will do. Note: the *Bencao* records this formula as one handful of baiye, three slices of ganjiang, and one stick of ejiao (akyo; mix-fried), boiled together with 1 sheng of horse-dung juice added. I do not know which is correct; it awaits verification.

## Notes

A clause using Baiye tang for unceasing vomiting of blood.

- Arborvitae leaf stops bleeding, ganjiang and mugwort warm and return the blood to the meridians, and horse-dung juice guides the blood downward.
- The base names the formula 後湯; it is emended to 柏葉湯 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a sick person's face has no color, there is no cold or heat, and the pulse is sunken and wiry, there will be nosebleed. If [the pulse] is floating and weak and vanishes under the hand, there will be bleeding below. If there is vexation and cough, there will surely be vomiting of blood.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The three characters "face has no color" are central in this clause. In the human body yin and yang hold each other together, and yin is in fact commanded by yang; blood is yin. So when yang can command yin, blood does not come out wildly. Now the face has no color, so we know its gentle yang warmth is insufficient; when it is insufficient, yin fire takes advantage. If the pulse were even, it could still hold, like a poor man with no troubles. But if there is no color and no exterior evil of cold and heat, and the pulse is sunken and wiry — sunken meaning the defensive qi is hidden, wiry meaning it is bound — the true yang is declining and dry qi is in surplus; the blood follows dry fire into the clear passages, overflows upward, and becomes nosebleed. If floating and weak — floating meaning it does not interact with yin, weak meaning vacuous yang without force — yang is vacuous and floats up; and if it even vanishes when pressed, the yin of the lower burner has no original yang to hold it, and the blood leaks downward. When the face has no color, the person's yang qi is already deficient and yin fire takes advantage; if suddenly vexation and cough appear — vexation belonging to the heart and cough to the lung — heart and lung are ill and the yang in the chest cannot resist the yin fire, and the blood follows vacuous fire surging into the turbid passages and comes out of the mouth. The three cases above all arise from insufficiency of true yang, the blood having nothing to command it. So for people with blood patterns, bitter-cold is generally not as good as sweet-warm, and supplementing the lung not as good as supplementing the kidney. Why? When the kidney is supplemented, true yang arises by itself; this is why Shenqi wan is the treasure for vacuity detriment. And supplementing the kidney is not as good as supplementing the spleen. Why? When the spleen is supplemented, the central qi moves vigorously; this is why Jianzhong tang is valued in the *Jingui*.

## Notes

A clause judging nosebleed, bleeding below, or vomiting of blood by the pulse in a pale patient without cold or heat.

- A sunken, wiry pulse means nosebleed; floating, weak, and vanishing under pressure means bleeding below; vexation and cough mean vomiting of blood.
- Xu Bin attributes all these hemorrhages to insufficiency of true yang and favors sweet-warm over bitter-cold medicinals, supplementing with Shenqi wan or Jianzhong tang.

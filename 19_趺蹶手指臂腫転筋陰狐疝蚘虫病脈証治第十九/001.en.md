# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When one suffers from instep stumbling and can only go forward but cannot step back, needle the calf to a depth of 2 cun. This is damage to the Taiyang meridian.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In the human body, the Yangming vessels and collaterals are in front and the Taiyang ones behind. So when Yangming qi is vigorous and without disease, one can walk forward, and when Taiyang qi is vigorous and without disease, one can move backward. Now, after the instep turns over, one stumbles and cannot do as a normal person — able to walk forward but not to step back — and one must needle the calf 2 cun deep. The calf is where the Taiyang vessel passes, and the pathogen gathers between Heyang and Chengjin on the Taiyang vessel, so it must be needled and drained. That is, the damage is only in the Taiyang meridian. But the Taiyang meridian has very many points, and the calf must be needled because the calf, the belly of the lower leg, originally belongs to Yangming, and the Taiyang vessel passes here; needling it lets the qi of Taiyang and Yangming communicate, so forward and backward movement is as one wishes.

## Notes

A clause stating that instep stumbling in which one can walk forward but cannot step back is damage to the Taiyang meridian, treated by needling the calf.

- Units 1–6 of this chapter (unit 6 up to "decoct to a thin gruel" in the instructions) are missing from the base because of a lost leaf and have been supplied from Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*).
- Xu Bin takes it as needling around Heyang and Chengjin on the calf.

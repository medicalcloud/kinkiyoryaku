# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cramps as a disease: the person's arms and legs are straight, the pulse moves up and down and is slightly wiry. When the cramps enter the abdomen, Jishibai san governs it.

Jishibai san (Chicken Droppings Powder) formula

Jishibai (the white part of chicken droppings).

Make the one ingredient above into powder; take a square-inch spoonful, mix with 6 ge of water, and take warm.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Cramp disease is, in general, earth being unable to settle water. When it reaches straightness of the arms and legs, wind is spreading into the spleen. A pulse moving up and down, slightly wiry, carries a sense of tetany. Zhongjing said, "The pulse of one with tetany is hidden, firm, and straight up and down," and also "the pulse is hidden and wiry"; all are images of wind entering. When further the cramps enter the abdomen, the liver pathogen is attacking the spleen directly. At this time it is like bandits invading the royal city, with no leisure for gentle treatment. So jishibai, which brings qi down and disperses accumulation more quickly than dispelling wind and calming the spleen, is used first to quell the internal disorder, and afterward one gradually plans for pacification.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] Make jishibai into powder; take a square-inch spoonful, mix with 6 ge of water, and take warm.

## Notes

A clause using Jishibai san for rigid limbs, a slightly wiry pulse, and cramps reaching into the abdomen.

# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: When there is abdominal pain with worms, how is it distinguished by the pulse? The master said: With pain in the abdomen, the pulse should be deep or wiry; if instead it is surging and large, there are roundworms.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Abdominal pain does not always involve worms, but there is pain due to worms. At the beginning, one must rely on the pulse to distinguish it. Thus: abdominal pain is generally cold striking the healthy qi — what is called "when pathogen and right contend, it becomes cold mounting." Cold is yin, so the pulse must be deep, and the wei-defensive qi must bind, so it is wiry. If instead it is surging and large, a yang pulse has been obtained; the pulse not corresponding to the disease means it is not due to an external cause. Hence "there are roundworms." But the pain characteristic of roundworms themselves is not detailed; this passage emphasizes only the distinction by pulse.

## Notes

A clause stating that abdominal pain with a pulse that is not deep or wiry but instead surging and large is due to roundworms.

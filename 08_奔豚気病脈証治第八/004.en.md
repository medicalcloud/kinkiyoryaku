# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

After sweating has been induced, if heated needles are used to make the patient sweat, and the needling sites are exposed to cold, so that lumps rise and are red, there will surely be running piglet, with qi rising from the lower abdomen to the heart. Apply one cone of moxa on each lump and give Guizhi jia Gui tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Cinnamon), which governs it.

Guizhi jia Gui tang formula

Guizhi 5 liang; shaoyao 3 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 3 liang; dazao 12 pieces.

Of the five ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil over a low fire down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of a method of treating inside and outside together when residual Taiyang pathogen has not been exhausted, running piglet is added, and lumps also arise. In Taiyang disease sweating has been induced, and again heated needles are used to make the patient sweat, because the Taiyang pathogen had not yet submitted. But heated needling frightens and sets off the running piglet qi, so the qi rises from the lesser abdomen to the heart. Then treating the residual pathogen and attacking the surging qi would be very easy. But since the needling sites are further exposed to cold, with red lumps, treating both together is difficult. So Guizhi tang governs the Taiyang pathogen, gui is added to subdue the running piglet qi, and the red lumps are separately treated with moxibustion by the method of external treatment; thus both are achieved. It is so because there is no abdominal pain or alternating fever and chills, so the disease is solely in Taiyang.

## Notes

A clause using moxibustion on the lumps and Guizhi jia Gui tang when, after sweating induced with heated needles, the needling sites are chilled and form red lumps, and running piglet qi surges upward. Almost the same clause appears in the middle Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 117).

- Guizhi jia Gui tang is Guizhi tang with an increased amount of guizhi; it is said to bring down surging qi.
- 賁豚 is the same as 奔豚 ("running piglet"), which the other sources read.
- The base reads 炙 for 灸 and 3 liang for gancao; these are emended because all other sources read 灸 and 2 liang (see the emendation notes).

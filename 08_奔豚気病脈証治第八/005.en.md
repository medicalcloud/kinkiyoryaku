# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

After sweating has been induced, palpitations below the umbilicus mean running piglet is about to develop; Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao tang (Poria, Cinnamon Twig, Licorice, and Jujube Decoction) governs it.

Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao tang formula

Fuling half a jin; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 15 pieces; guizhi 4 liang.

Of the four ingredients above, with 1 dou of ganlan water (worked water), first boil the fuling, reducing by 2 sheng; add the other medicinals, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day (method for ganlan water: take 2 dou of water, put it in a large basin, and lift it up with a ladle; when five or six thousand [base: fifty or sixty] pearl-like bubbles appear on the water chasing one another, take it and use it).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that even without the outbreak of fright, when the sovereign fire is extremely vacuous and the kidney evil stirs slightly, it will also encroach on the heart and become running piglet. Sweat is the fluid of the heart; after sweating we know [the heart] is vacuous. If residual evil had not invaded the kidney at the time of sweating, how could there be palpitations below the umbilicus? When there are palpitations, the kidney evil has stirred; so we know running piglet is about to develop. So fuling is combined with guizhi and gancao to subdue the kidney evil exclusively, and only dazao is added to calm the stomach, seemingly without much regard for the exterior evil. That is, after sweating the exterior evil is already little, and since it is only about to develop, its force is still slight; so percolating the dampness and cultivating the earth, the yin qi declines by itself. Ganlan water is used to aid its tendency to descend quickly.

(When five or six thousand pearl-like bubbles appear chasing one another, take it and use it.) Discussion: In discussing patterns, Zhongjing always combines several clauses to cover all variations. So for the single pattern of running piglet, since it arises from the outbreak of fright, he combines the four kinds to show that the cause is the same and the patterns differ, so that one knows where running piglet comes from. Then he says that the qi surges from the lesser abdomen to the throat, to show the extreme of this disease. Then he speaks of it with abdominal pain and alternating cold and heat, to show that this pattern may come from an exterior not yet cleared and lie half exterior and half interior — that is, mostly internal. Then he speaks of it with lumps and no other disease, to show that this pattern may lie only in taiyang, not mixed with other channels — that is, mostly external. Then he speaks of palpitations below the umbilicus after sweating, about to become running piglet but not yet, to show that this pattern may arise after the exterior has gone, with residual evil invading the kidney — that is, mostly water qi. So "surging to the throat," "surging to the chest," "surging to the heart," and "palpitations below the umbilicus" become progressively shallower, clearly. Using harmonization, using subduing of the kidney, using gui and not using gui — the deliberate treatment is subtle. For the pattern of running piglet, nothing is left out regarding cause, signs, and treatment. If one does not grasp Zhongjing's intent in setting formulas, one fears to use drastic medicines while mild prescriptions have little effect. How could it truly be that ancient formulas do not suit the present?

## Notes

A clause using Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao tang (Ling Gui Gan Zao tang) for palpitations below the umbilicus after sweating, about to become running piglet. The same clause appears in the middle taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 65).

- Fuling as the chief removes water qi, and guizhi brings down the surging.
- Ganlan water is water ladled up repeatedly until it froths; it was thought to soften the nature of the water.
- In the base the final character 之 of the clause begins the next paragraph.
- For "five or six thousand" bubbles in the interlinear note, the base reads "fifty or sixty"; all other sources read "five or six thousand," as does the interlinear note in Xu Bin's commentary, so it has been emended (see the emendation notes).
- The base reads 甘爛水; it is emended to 甘瀾水 because all other sources so read (see the emendation notes).
- Xu Bin's "Discussion" treats the running piglet clauses of this chapter together.

# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman is pregnant and has gripping pain in the abdomen, Danggui Shaoyao san (Tokishakuyakusan) governs it.

Danggui Shaoyao san (Angelica and Peony Powder) formula

Danggui 3 liang; shaoyao (shakuyaku) 1 jin; fuling (bukuryo) 4 liang; baizhu (byakujutsu) 4 liang; zexie (takusha) half a jin; xiongqiong half a jin (one version: 3 liang).

Pound the six ingredients above into powder; take a square-inch spoonful mixed with wine, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

㽲痛 means lingering pain, not like the wringing pain of cold mounting or the stabbing pain of blood and qi. When the healthy qi is insufficient, yin can override yang, water qi overcomes earth, the spleen is depressed and cannot stretch, and in its depression seeks to stretch; when earth qi is not regulated, the pain lingers. So danggui and shaoyao nourish blood, fuling and baizhu support the spleen, zexie drains the old surplus water, and xiongqiong frees the blood and qi seeking their way. Huangqin (ogon) is not used because 㽲痛 arises from deficiency and so carries a little cold. But hot medicinals are not used because it is originally not great cold; when the healthy qi governs, the slight cold goes by itself.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] zexie half a jin; xiongqiong 3 liang (or half a jin).

## Notes

A clause using Danggui Shaoyao san for abdominal pain during pregnancy.

- A formula nourishing blood with danggui, shaoyao, and chuanxiong and removing water with fuling, baizhu, and zexie. In later times it was widely used for various women's diseases.
- 㽲痛 is often understood as sudden gripping pain, but Xu Bin takes it as "lingering pain."

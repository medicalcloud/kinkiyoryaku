# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: Among women there are those with spotting, those with continued bleeding that never stops after a half-term delivery (miscarriage), and those with bleeding during pregnancy. If during pregnancy there is pain in the abdomen, it is uterine obstruction; Jiao Ai tang governs it.

Xiong Gui Jiao Ai tang (Ligusticum, Angelica, Ass-Hide Glue, and Mugwort Decoction) formula (one version adds 1 liang of ganjiang; Hu's [Daizhige: Hu Qia's] version treating uterine stirring in women has no ganjiang): xiongqiong (ligusticum), ejiao (ass-hide glue), and gancao, 2 liang each; aiye (mugwort leaf) and danggui, 3 liang each; shaoyao 4 liang; gandihuang (dried rehmannia) [amount missing].

Of the seven ingredients above, boil together in 5 sheng of water and 5 sheng [other sources: 3 sheng] of clear wine down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, add the glue and let it dissolve completely, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. If there is no improvement, make more.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This passage speaks in general of using Jiao Ai tang to warm and supplement the blood in women's bleeding, pregnancy being one case. But the causes differ. Spotting without cause is habitual blood vacuity with visiting evil added. Continuous unceasing bleeding after a half-term delivery is blood vacuity from blood loss, with right qi hard to restore. Bleeding in pregnancy due to a conglomeration, as above, certainly occurs; but when it is accompanied by abdominal pain, it is due to uterine obstruction. Obstruction means the blood about to move is obstructed and the qi is not in accord; it is not harm from a chronic mass. So in all cases Jiao Ai tang governs it. Chuanxiong, danggui, dihuang, and shaoyao are Siwu tang; nothing surpasses it in nourishing yin and supplementing blood. When blood moves frenetically, it always carries wind and becomes phlegm-turbidity. Ejiao is made mainly from mule hide and can dispel wind; boiled with water of the Ji River, it can clarify turbidity. Ai is warm by nature and good at moving, guiding blood back to the channels. Gancao harmonizes them, keeping the four ingredients from leaning to yin. These are the strengths of the three, but the skill of the formula really lies in ejiao and aiye.

## Notes

A clause using Xiong Gui Jiao Ai tang for women's spotting, bleeding after miscarriage, and bleeding during pregnancy, especially uterine obstruction with abdominal pain in pregnancy.

- It consists of Siwu tang (danggui, chuanxiong, shaoyao, dihuang) plus ejiao, aiye, and gancao, and is regarded as the source of the later Siwu tang.
- The base lacks the amount of gandihuang. Xu Bin's text and Daizhige give 6 liang.

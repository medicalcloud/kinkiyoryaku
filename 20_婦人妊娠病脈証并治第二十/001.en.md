# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When a woman has a normal pulse, with the yin pulse small and weak, and she is thirsty and cannot eat, without cold or heat, it is called pregnancy. Guizhi tang (Keishito) governs it (the formula appears under [diarrhea]). By rule, at sixty days this pattern should appear. If a physician has treated it contrarily, going back one month, and vomiting and diarrhea are added, then cut it off.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

A normal pulse means no disease pulse is seen, just as in a healthy person. In front of the guan is yang; behind the guan is yin. Small and weak means the pulse is small in form, not large, soft, weak, and without strength, but not thin. When all the pulses are normal and only the yin pulse of the lower jiao appears slightly different, there is no disease in the middle and upper jiao. If nonetheless thirst and inability to eat appear, the thirst is not upper-jiao heat, and the inability to eat is not a stomach disease. Shaoyang has the sign of taciturnity with no desire to eat, but now there is no cold or heat, so there is no Shaoyang exterior pattern to suspect either. So the thirst is yin fire congesting upward, the inability to eat is nausea obstructing food, and the small, weak yin pulse is the fetal origin consuming qi. Hence "it is called pregnancy" — that is, conception.

Guizhi tang is used because, in an exterior pattern, this decoction resolves the flesh and harmonizes ying-nutrient and wei-defense, and in an interior pattern it transforms qi and regulates yin and yang. Now, at the beginning of pregnancy, there is originally no disease above or below; congealed qi in the child's chamber overflows above and below. So only white shaoyao (shakuyaku) secures the yin qi so that it cannot overflow upward, and guizhi (keishi), gancao (kanzo), shengjiang (shokyo), and dazao (taiso) support the yang of the upper jiao and harmonize the stomach qi. It is enough to fill the yang qi above so that it can resist the encroaching yin qi. Not treating the disease is precisely the way to treat it. Otherwise, to resolve the thirst as pathogenic heat, or to dry the inability to eat as spleen weakness — would that not be a mistake?

"By rule, at sixty days this pattern should appear" means the fetus has formed and its qi intrudes upward, so treatment should take the fetal qi as primary. In "if a physician has treated it contrarily," 逆 means mistakenly. "Going back one month": if the period has not reached sixty days, the fetus has not yet formed; and if vomiting and diarrhea are added due to the physician's mistaken treatment, the spleen and stomach have truly suffered damage, so one should simply focus on cutting off the root of the disease, not clinging to the doctrine of securing the fetus and making mistakes through hesitation. Hence "cut it off."

## Notes

A clause stating that a normal pulse with only the chi pulse small and weak, together with thirst and poor appetite and no cold or heat, indicates pregnancy (morning sickness), and Guizhi tang is used.

- "Then cut it off" is variously interpreted as stopping treatment or cutting off the root of a disease caused by mistreatment; Xu Bin takes it as "cutting off the root of the disease."
- The Guizhi tang formula is in chapter 17 (unit 35). For 利中, Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*) and Xu Bin's text read 下利中 ("under diarrhea").

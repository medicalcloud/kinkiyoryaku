# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman is six or seven months pregnant, with a wiry pulse and fever, the fetus increasingly distended, abdominal pain, and aversion to cold, the lesser abdomen feels as if fanned. The reason is that the child's zang organ is open. One should warm the zang organ with Fuzi tang (the formula has not been seen).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The sixth and seventh months of pregnancy are when the stomach and lung nourish the fetus. A wiry pulse: when wei-defensive qi binds, the pulse is wiry. Fever: being struck by cold internally can also produce heat. Cold certainly governs distension, so a wiry pulse makes the stomach distended. In the sixth and seventh months the stomach and lung nourish the fetus, and the qi is stagnated by cold, so at first the distension is still tolerable, but by this point the fetus is increasingly distended. With cold inside there is abdominal pain and aversion to cold. Aversion to cold may belong to the exterior, but here it is joined with abdominal pain, so one knows cold has damaged the interior. "The lesser abdomen as if fanned" means bouts of cold, as if being fanned; it depicts the peculiarity of the aversion to cold, and it is only in the lesser abdomen. This is because the child's zang organ has received cold and cannot close, so the lesser abdomen alone is severe. The child's zang organ is the uterus; open means not contracted. Fuzi can enter the kidney and warm the lower jiao; hence "it is suitable to warm the zang organ with Fuzi tang." The original formula is lost from the notes, but I think it is nothing other than the Fuzi tang of the *Shanghan lun*, fuzi combined with renshen, fuling, baizhu, and shaoyao.

## Notes

A clause stating that at six or seven months of pregnancy, a wiry pulse, fever, abdominal distension and pain, and a feeling of the lower abdomen being fanned with cold are due to the uterus being cold and open, and should be warmed with Fuzi tang.

- The formula has not survived, but Xu Bin suggests it is the Fuzi tang of the Shaoyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (fuzi, renshen, fuling, baizhu, shaoyao).
- 𨳩 is a variant form of 開 ("open").

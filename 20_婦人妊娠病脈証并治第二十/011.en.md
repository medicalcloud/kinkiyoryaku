# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a woman has injured the fetus, being pregnant with abdominal fullness, unable to urinate, heaviness from the waist down, as if there were water qi: in the seventh month of pregnancy, taiyin should nourish [the fetus] but does not; this is repletion of heart qi. One should needle and drain Laogong and Guanyuan; when urination is slightly freed, there is recovery (appears in the *Yuhan*).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Injured fetus means the fetal qi has lost nourishment and has truly suffered some damage, with the disease flowing into the lower burner, not comparable to visiting evil contracted by chance in the middle and upper burners. In pregnancy the abdomen of course should be large, but large is simply large and soft is simply soft; fullness of the abdomen due to injury is somewhat different. Inability to urinate is heart fire not descending; because of this, qi below the waist stagnates, hence heaviness. "As if there were water qi" means it is not water qi. But abdominal fullness, inhibited urination, and heaviness below the waist are all found in water disease; how to distinguish them? If the pulse is deep and does not rise on pressure, with shivering and dizziness, it is true water. Now none of these is present. In the seventh month hand taiyin should nourish the fetus, but there is evil in the heart qi, so fire is exuberant and melts metal; metal cannot keep its clear purification, and qi does not transform, so urination is inhibited. When upper-burner qi is starved, lower-burner qi stagnates, hence heaviness. All of it is due to heart fire melting above and not descending, so Laogong, a point of the heart, is needled; Guanyuan is needled as well to disinhibit the kidney with which it interacts, so that the qi does not become replete again. When urination is slightly freed, heart fire descends by itself, the lung regains its balance, and the fetus does not lose nourishment; hence recovery.

Discussion: Zhongjing's chapter on pregnancy has ten formulas in all: seven pills and powders and three decoctions. 湯 (decoction) means 蕩 (to sweep away); in pregnancy securing the fetus should be primary, so neither attacking nor supplementing should be abrupt, and it is approached gradually. The medicinals include nothing greatly cold or hot, and none that clog the diaphragm, because in securing the fetus, nourishing yin and regulating qi are what is urgent.

## Notes

A clause stating that abdominal fullness, inability to urinate, and heaviness below the waist in the seventh month of pregnancy occur because hand taiyin (lung), which should nourish the fetus, cannot do so owing to repletion of heart qi, treated by needling Laogong and Guanyuan.

- It is based on the theory that a particular channel nourishes the fetus in each month of pregnancy (hand taiyin lung channel in the seventh month).
- 玉㞼 corresponds to 玉函 (the *Jingui yuhan jing*). One of the other sources has a note that this and another clause are probably appendices by Song officials.
- The latter part of Xu Bin's commentary discusses the formulas of the pregnancy chapter as a whole.

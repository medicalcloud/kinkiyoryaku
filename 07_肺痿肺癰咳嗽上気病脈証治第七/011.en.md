# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Great counterflow with ascending qi and inhibited throat; to stop the counterflow and bring down the qi, Maimendong tang (Ophiopogon Decoction) governs it.

Maimendong tang formula

Maimendong 7 sheng; banxia 1 sheng; renshen 2 liang; gancao 2 liang; jingmi 3 ge; dazao 12 pieces.

Of the six ingredients above, with 1 dou 2 sheng of water boil down to 6 sheng and take 1 sheng warm, three times in the day and once at night.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is, among cough with counterflow and ascending qi, the kind with pathogenic fire and no pathogenic wind. So the inhibited throat is specially singled out, and the medicinals generally regulate and supplement lung and stomach, with only the single ingredient banxia to remove counterflow. Moreover, its effect is noted as "to stop the counterflow and bring down the qi," showing that it treats fire counterflow and not pathogenic wind. Shengjiang is not used because it can diffuse and stir up the fire qi.

## Notes

A clause using Maimendong tang for violent upward counterflow of qi with an inhibited throat (dry throat, phlegm hard to expectorate).

- Maimendong tang uses a large amount of maimendong to moisten the yin of lung and stomach, banxia to bring down counterflow qi, and renshen, gancao, jingmi, and dazao to nourish stomach qi. It is regarded as the representative formula for cough and ascending qi due to yin deficiency of lung and stomach.
- For 大逆上気 ("great counterflow"), most other sources read 火逆上気 ("fire counterflow"); Xu Bin also explains it as pathogenic fire.

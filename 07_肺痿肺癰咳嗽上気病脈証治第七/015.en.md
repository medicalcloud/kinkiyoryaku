# Clause 15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Lung distension with cough and ascending qi, vexation and agitation with panting, and a floating pulse: there is water below the heart. Xiao Qinglong jia Shigao tang (Minor Green-Blue Dragon Decoction plus Gypsum) governs it.

Xiao Qinglong jia Shigao tang formula (the *Qianjin* has the same signs and treatment; the *Waitai* [other sources: "further"] adds "pain below the ribs drawing into the supraclavicular fossa")

Mahuang, shaoyao, guizhi, xixin, gancao, and ganjiang, 3 liang each; wuweizi and banxia, half a sheng each; shigao 2 liang.

Of the nine ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the mahuang, remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, and boil down to 3 sheng. A strong person takes 1 sheng; a thin, weak person takes less; three times a day. Children take 4 ge.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Compared with the previous clause, this likewise has cough, panting, ascending qi, lung distension, and a floating pulse. But in the previous clause the eyes seem to bulge out, so panting predominates. Predominant panting is blamed on cold, so mahuang and gancao are the mainstay, and shigao is added to clear the heat into which the cold has changed. This one adds only vexation and agitation. In the *Shanghan lun*, one struck by cold who has a wind pulse with vexation and agitation is governed by [Da] Qinglong tang; so this too is governed by Xiao Qinglong tang. But when there is congestion, the qi must become hot, so shigao is still added.

[Xu Bin's formula:] mahuang 3 liang; shaoyao 3 liang; guizhi 3 liang; xixin 3 liang; ganjiang 3 liang; gancao 3 liang; wuweizi half a sheng; banxia half a sheng.

## Notes

A clause using Xiao Qinglong jia Shigao tang for lung distension with vexation, agitation, panting, and water (fluid) below the heart.

- Xiao Qinglong tang (mahuang, shaoyao, guizhi, xixin, gancao, ganjiang, wuweizi, banxia) removes exterior cold and the fluid below the heart, and shigao is added to clear the vexation and agitation (heat).
- In the interlinear note, for 台 in 外台加…, most other sources read 更 ("the *Qianjin* has the same signs and treatment, and further adds 'pain below the ribs drawing into the supraclavicular fossa'").
- The base reads 煩燥; it is emended to 煩躁 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

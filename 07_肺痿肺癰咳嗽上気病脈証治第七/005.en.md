# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Ascending qi with panting and agitation belongs to lung distension; it is about to become wind water; inducing sweating will cure it.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that among lung abscess patterns, those whose original qi is exhausted are hard to treat, while those with evil can still be treated. Lung abscess is due to wind, and wind by nature moves upward, so there is always ascending qi first. If there is also puffy swelling of the face and shoulder breathing, the qi rises and does not descend. And if the pulse is floating and large, the original qi can no longer be gathered in; one can neither supplement nor induce sweating. Moreover, since inside and out all is counterflow qi, not comparable to wind, can it all be drained away by sweating? Hence "incurable." If diarrhea is added, yang deserts from above and yin from below; hence "even worse."

If there is ascending qi with only panting and agitation, the panting is the fanning of wind and the agitation the vexation of wind. The drool and foam counterflowing upward are about to carry the force of the wind and become wind water. Now if the wind first drains through the flesh and exterior, the water, no longer stirred by the wind, will naturally flow along its course and come out below. Hence "sweating will cure it."

## Notes

A clause calling ascending qi with panting and agitation "lung distension," curable by sweating; it contrasts with the incurable case of the previous unit.

- Lung distension is evil filling and distending the lung, a repletion pattern. Since it is about to become wind water (edema due to wind, in the chapter on water qi), expelling the evil by sweating cures it.
- Xu Bin's commentary contrasts, together with the previous unit, the incurable case of exhausted original qi with the treatable case with evil. Xu Bin explains both clauses as lung abscess patterns, but they are generally regarded as clauses on lung distension.

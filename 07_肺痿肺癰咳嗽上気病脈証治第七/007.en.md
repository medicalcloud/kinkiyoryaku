# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cough with ascending qi and a sound like a water fowl (frog) in the throat: Shegan Mahuang tang (Yakanmaoto; Belamcanda and Ephedra Decoction) governs it.

Shegan Mahuang tang formula

Shegan (yakan; belamcanda) 13 pieces (one method: 3 liang); mahuang (mao) 4 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) 4 liang; xixin (saishin), ziwan (aster), and kuandonghua (coltsfoot flower), 3 liang each; wuweizi (gomishi; schisandra) half a sheng; dazao (taiso) 7 pieces; banxia (hange), 8 large pieces (washed; one method: half a jin).

Of the nine ingredients above, with 1 dou 2 sheng of water first boil the mahuang to two boilings, remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, boil down to 3 sheng, and take warm in three doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Whenever there is cough with ascending qi, there is pathogen. The sound of a water fowl in the throat is phlegm drawn up by fire and unable to descend. But fire is generated by wind; water stirred by wind makes the sound. So mahuang and xixin, which drive out the external pathogen, are the sovereign; shegan, which opens bound heat qi, moves water-damp toxin, and is especially good at clearing lung qi, is the minister; and the rest are medicinals that bring down counterflow, dissolve phlegm, and diffuse and disperse. Only the single item wuweizi gathers in the already depleted qi, so that the healthy qi gathers itself and the pathogenic qi leaves by itself; this is out of concern that the long-deficient lung qi cannot bear forceful dispersal.

Discussion: Atrophy of the lung lobes is the lung's own qi falling ill through repeated loss of fluids, wilting under the pressure of heat. So there is cough and foamy drool, but no signs of ascending qi or dyspnea counterflow. Then whenever there is ascending qi and dyspnea counterflow with foul-smelling phlegm, it is lung abscess; when there is no foul phlegm but only a water-fowl sound, it is fire drawing up the phlegm. If one distinguishes and treats by this, there will naturally be no error. But water is a thing that moistens and descends; how can it counterflow upward and make a sound? Recently I saw someone doing fire cupping: he put fire into a jar and covered the patient's affected spot with it, and at once it drew up the inner cold with great force. Then I understood: fire by nature moves upward; when fire gathers above, qi is drawn up from below, a force that cannot be stopped. Ascending qi with a water sound follows this principle too. How can this be cured except by draining the lung pathogen? So in treating this disease, adding shegan is best, baiqian (byakuzen) is next, and zeqi (sun spurge) next; all can open binding and bring down water.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] ziwan 3 liang; kuandonghua 3 liang; wuweizi half a sheng; dazao 7 pieces; banxia half a sheng (washed).

## Notes

A clause using Shegan Mahuang tang for cough with ascending qi and a wheezing sound in the throat ("water-fowl sound").

- The "water-fowl sound" likens the sound of phlegm rattling in the throat to the croaking of a frog (*shuiji*); it is regarded as cold fluid in the lung blocking the airway.
- In Shegan Mahuang tang, mahuang and xixin disperse cold, shegan opens phlegm, ziwan, kuandonghua, and banxia treat cough and phlegm, and wuweizi gathers the lung qi.
- 紫苑 is the same as 紫菀 (ziwan), and 欵冬花 as 款冬花 (kuandonghua).
- The "fire cupping" in Xu Bin's commentary is cupping therapy, in which a vessel heated with fire is applied to the skin to create suction.

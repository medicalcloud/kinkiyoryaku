# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cough with ascending qi: this is lung distension. When the person pants, the eyes look as if they would bulge out, and the pulse is floating and large, Yuebi jia Banxia tang (Eppikahangeto; Maidservant from Yue Decoction plus Pinellia) governs it.

Yuebi jia Banxia tang formula

Mahuang (mao) 6 liang; shigao (sekko) half a jin; shengjiang (shokyo) 3 liang; dazao (taiso) 15 pieces; gancao (kanzo) 2 liang; banxia (hange) half a sheng.

Of the six ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, boil down to 1 sheng [other sources: 3 sheng], and take warm in three doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Cough is fire overwhelming the lung; frequent ascending qi means the lung's body cannot be at rest even a little. Hence "this is lung distension." Dyspnea is the exhaled qi of distension. Eyes as if bulging out means distension with congested qi that does not descend. If, further, the pulse is floating and large, the distension is truly due to exuberant pathogen. So Yuebi tang (Eppito) clears the pathogen and banxia is added to bring down the counterflow; then the distension stops by itself.

## Notes

A clause using Yuebi jia Banxia tang for lung distension (violent cough and ascending qi, dyspnea so hard the eyes seem to bulge) with a floating, large pulse.

- Yuebi tang (mahuang, shigao, shengjiang, gancao, dazao) removes exterior pathogen and lung heat, and banxia brings down counterflow qi.
- "Boil down to 1 sheng and take in three doses" is too little for three doses; most other sources read 3 sheng, and the base appears to be in error.

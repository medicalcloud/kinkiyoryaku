# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cough with fullness in the chest, shivering chills, a rapid pulse, a dry throat without thirst, frequently bringing up turbid, fishy-smelling spittle, and after a long time spitting pus like rice gruel: this is lung abscess, and Jiegeng tang (Kikyoto; Platycodon Decoction) governs it.

Jiegeng tang formula (also treats blood impediment)

Jiegeng (kikyo) 1 liang; gancao (kanzo) 2 liang.

Of the two ingredients above, with 3 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng and take warm in two doses; then pus and blood will be vomited [base: "pus and blood color"].

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is lung abscess already formed — the so-called "when heat passes into the ying-nutrient, one inhales and it does not go out"; the pathogenic heat is bound in the ying-nutrient aspect of the lung. So bitter jiegeng brings down the bound heat and opens and lifts the lung qi, and raw gancao clears heat and resolves toxin. This too is a method of opening impediment. Hence it also notes, "after two doses, pus and blood will be vomited."

[Note on the appended formula "Jiegeng Bai san from the *Waitai*":] This is the pattern of the earlier Jiegeng tang. But here gancao is replaced by beimu (baimo) and badou (hazu), making it extremely swift and disinhibiting. In such patterns the danger lies within a breath, and the disasters caused by dawdling are countless. So if one clearly sees that the person is strong or the pattern dangerous, one should use this to rescue urgently, and not, disliking its harshness, sit waiting for death. Jiegeng 3 *fen*; beimu (fritillaria) 3 *fen*; badou (croton seed) 1 *fen* (remove the skin, dry-fry, and grind into a paste).

## Notes

A clause using Jiegeng tang for lung abscess in which pus has formed.

- Chest fullness, chills, a rapid pulse, a dry throat, fishy-smelling phlegm, and spitting pus like rice gruel are the signs of lung abscess at the stage when pus has formed.
- Jiegeng tang consists of two ingredients, jiegeng and gancao, said to expel pus, clear heat, and resolve toxin. It is also used for sore throat in the Shaoyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun*.
- For 色 in the final 則吐膿血色, most other sources read 也 (a sentence-final particle).
- The base reads 振脈寒数; it is emended to 振寒脈数 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- The latter part of Xu Bin's commentary belongs to the appended formula "Jiegeng Bai san from the *Waitai*" at the end of the chapter, and has been placed here.

# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: This is atrophy of the lung lobes disease. If the mouth is parched and dry, coughing causes a dull pain in the chest, and the pulse instead is slippery and rapid, this is lung abscess, with coughing and spitting of pus and blood. A rapid, deficient pulse indicates atrophy of the lung lobes; a rapid, excess pulse indicates lung abscess.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that atrophy of the lung lobes and lung abscess both arise from heat, but atrophy of the lung lobes is a disease of formless qi, the qi wilting and not rousing, forming gradually; it differs from lung abscess, in which the pathogen enters the blood aspect, producing tangible blood, so that the lung is congested and blocked, its source being wind.

The chest is the residence of the lung. When heat is in the upper jiao, the lung is scorched by heat and there is coughing; this is the so-called coughing due to heat and atrophy of the lung lobes due to coughing. But there are also people who cough for a long time without developing atrophy of the lung lobes; so we know atrophy has its causes. Hence "from sweating": this is desertion of the thin fluids. "From vomiting": this is damage to the thick fluids. "From wasting-thirst": heart fire consumes the yin. Or when the intestines are dry and constipated and one forces purging to seek relief, the spleen's fluids are lost through purging. (lacuna) belong to the side of dry heat with yin loss; then the fluids in the stomach are not conveyed to the lung, the lung loses its nourishment, and the lung wilts.

Precisely because it is due to heat, the pulse at the inch opening is rapid. The inch opening pulse should center on the right cun, but since the pulses of both hands all belong to the lung, the rapidity should not be confined to the right cun. A rapid pulse is heat, and heat should make the mouth dry; that coughing brings up turbid spittle and foamy drool seems contradictory. [But such a view] does not know that only when the lung is free of disease can it convey essence to the skin and hair, and the hair and vessels combine the essence and move qi to the bowels. When it wilts, it is impeded and does not function, and the water qi of food and drink conveyed upward cannot be gathered in, transported, and transformed, so it becomes turbid foam and comes out of the mouth. Hence "this is atrophy of the lung lobes disease": because of heat it loses its function of clearing and descending.

If the mouth is parched and dry, there is an excess pathogen within. A dull pain on coughing means there is a specific site of damage. Further, a slippery pulse means the pathogen is in excess, not deficient. There is no doubt that it is lung abscess; in severe cases there is coughing and spitting of pus and blood. Precisely because both belong to heat, the pulse is rapid in both; but deficiency and excess differ. Hence "deficient is atrophy of the lung lobes; excess is lung abscess." The meaning of "excess" is clear from the word "slippery" above. But the later section, in its note on the essential pattern of lung abscess, also says "the pulse is feeble and rapid"; this is not contradictory. Slippery and rapid is when it has formed and the pathogen is exuberant; feeble and rapid is at the outset, when the fire is hidden.

## Notes

The answer to the question of the previous unit, distinguishing atrophy of the lung lobes from lung abscess (a suppurative lung disease) by dryness of the mouth, chest pain, and the pulse.

- Atrophy of the lung lobes: rapid, deficient pulse with turbid spittle and foam. Lung abscess: slippery, rapid, excess pulse, parched mouth, chest pain on coughing, and spitting of pus and blood.
- 辟辟 is taken to describe a dry mouth.
- The base reads 濃血 ("thick blood"); it is emended to 膿血 ("pus and blood") because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- Xu Bin's commentary also explains the question of the previous unit.

# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Weijing tang (Ikeito; Reed Stem Decoction) from the *Qianjin*: treats cough with slight fever, vexation and fullness, and encrusted [skin] over the chest; this is lung abscess.

Weijing (ikei; reed stem) 2 sheng; yiyiren (yokuinin) half a sheng; taoren (tonin) 50 pieces; guaban (melon seeds) half a sheng.

Of the four ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the weijing; when 5 sheng is obtained, remove the dregs, add the other medicinals, boil down to 2 sheng, and take 1 sheng. On the second dose, something like pus should be vomited.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is a yang formula for treating lung abscess. Cough with slight fever means the pathogen is in the yang aspect. Vexation and fullness mean it carries dampness. As for encrusted skin over the chest, the inner body is diseased, so the encrustation appears only over the chest; there is bound heat in the qi and blood of the upper chest. So weijing, light, floating, sweet, and cold, resolves the qi heat of the yang aspect; taoren drains the bound heat of the blood aspect; yiyiren brings down the dampness in the lung; and guaban clears the bound heat and makes the rotten turbidity be vomited — what is called "what is above, make it go out over the top." [Xu Bin's formula:] weijing 2 sheng; yiyiren half a sheng; taoren 50 kernels; guaban half a sheng.

## Notes

An appended formula, Weijing tang from the *Qianjin*, for lung abscess. Later it came to be widely used as the representative formula for lung abscess.

- Weijing clears lung heat, yiyiren disinhibits dampness and expels pus, taoren removes static blood, and winter melon seeds remove phlegm and pus.
- "Encrusted [skin] over the chest" is taken to mean rough, scaly skin on the chest.
- 瓜辦 is the same as 瓜弁 ("melon seeds").

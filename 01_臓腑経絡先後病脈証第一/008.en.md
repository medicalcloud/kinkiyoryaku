# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: There is "arriving before [the time] has arrived," "[the time] arriving but [the qi] not arriving," "arriving but not departing," and "arriving in excess." What do these mean?

The master said: After the winter solstice, at midnight on the *jiazi* day, shaoyang arises. In the time of shaoyang, yang begins to grow and the weather becomes warm and mild. If before the *jiazi* day the weather becomes warm and mild on that account, this is arriving before [the time] has arrived. If the *jiazi* day has come but the weather is not yet warm and mild, this is arriving but not arriving. If the *jiazi* day has come but the weather is very cold and the cold does not abate, this is arriving but not departing. If the *jiazi* day has come and the weather is as warm as the height of summer in the fifth and sixth months, this is arriving in excess.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This discusses excess and insufficiency in the coming of the qi of heaven. It does not mention medicine, but the idea of adapting to the season is contained within it. In the order of the four seasons, what has completed its work retires and what is to come advances; so it is said generally that [the season] "arrives." But there are irregularities, hence the questions about arriving early, not arriving, not departing, and excess. So it speaks of the completion of the year's work: from the *jiazi* day after the winter solstice shaoyang arises [for sixty days], then yangming sixty days, taiyang sixty days, taiyin sixty days, shaoyin sixty days, and jueyin flourishes; each sixty days, six times sixty [making three hundred and sixty], and the year's work is complete.

Speaking of the time when shaoyang flourishes: becoming warm and mild when it should not yet be is arriving early; not becoming warm and mild when it already should be is not arriving; severe cold not abating is not departing; excessive warmth and heat is excess. For the *jiazi* days of other times, too, one generally infers by this method. People live amid the interchange of qi: some respond in accordance with the season, some decline or flourish contrary to the season, and some fall ill from abnormal qi out of season. So one must carefully examine the dynamics of the seasonal qi, as with the arising of shaoyang, as the basis for treating disease. Therefore the *Six Divisions and Visceral Manifestation* chapter says, "Seeking its arrival always returns to spring."

## Notes

A clause explaining four cases in which the seasonal climate does not follow the calendar; its content is related to the theory of the circulation of qi.

- From the first *jiazi* day after the winter solstice, successive sixty-day periods are assigned to shaoyang, yangming, taiyang, taiyin, shaoyin, and jueyin, an idea also found in *Nanjing* difficulty 7.
- It distinguishes warmth coming too early, warmth not coming when due, cold not departing, and excessive heat. Such climatic irregularities were considered causes of disease.
- Xu Bin's text writes "six sixes, thirty-six" (六六三十六); it is understood here as six periods of sixty days, i.e. 360 days.

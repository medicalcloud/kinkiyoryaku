# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: If a sick person's voice is quiet and he often cries out in alarm, the disease is between the bones and joints. If the voice is muffled and does not carry, the disease is between the heart and the diaphragm. If the voice is thin, small, fine, and drawn out, the disease is in the head (one version reads "pain").

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section is the physician's method of listening. The *Neijing* says, "Liver wood: in tones it is *jue*, in voice it is shouting, in its changes it is clenching. Heart fire: in tones *zhi*, in voice laughing, in changes worry. Spleen earth: in tones *gong*, in voice singing, in changes retching. Lung metal: in tones *shang*, in voice weeping, in changes coughing. Kidney water: in tones *yu*, in voice groaning, in changes shivering." But the reach of the voice must differ among the upper, middle, and lower jiao, which [the *Neijing*] did not detail. So Zhongjing combined disorders of the voice with their loudness to distinguish whether the disease is below, in the middle, or above.

"If the voice is quiet and he often cries out in alarm, the disease is between the bones and joints." Quiet silence belongs to yin, and Jueyin liver wood is, in emotion, alarm, and in voice, shouting. Now [the voice] is quiet yet often cries out in alarm, so we know it belongs to Jueyin. Being Jueyin, we know the disease must arise from the lower jiao and enter deeply into the bones, belonging to the spaces between sinews and joints.

"If the voice is muffled and does not carry, the disease is between the heart and the diaphragm." Although voices are distinguished by the five zang organs, all resonate in lung metal, so they are clear and not hoarse. Now it is muffled and does not carry: the great qi in the chest does not turn and blocks metal qi, so it cannot be like the echo of an empty valley. Therefore we know the disease is in the chest, between [heart and] diaphragm. The classic says, "When the center is exuberant, the zang organs full, the qi overpowering, and damaged by anger, the voice is like speaking from inside a room; this is dampness of the central qi." Is this not it?

"If the voice is thin, small, fine, and drawn out, the disease is in the head." The kidney vessel originally encircles the neck and returns; kidney Shaoyin and bladder Taiyang are exterior and interior to each other, and the Taiyang vessel rises to the vertex. Now kidney qi follows the Taiyang meridian to reach the vertex, so the kidney's voice, groaning, instead passes upward and becomes thin, small, and drawn out. If that qi attacks straight upward, there is disease in the head. Put simply, when there is disease in the head, one fears only that the qi of the voice will attack upward, so one suppresses the voice, making it small, drawing it out finely.

## Notes

A clause on listening: judging the location of disease by the voice.

- A quiet voice with sudden alarmed cries indicates disease of the joints; a muffled voice that does not carry indicates disease of the chest; a thin, drawn-out voice indicates disease of the head.
- The usual interpretation is that a person with painful joints keeps still and cries out suddenly when moving; a person with chest disease has blocked qi so the voice does not carry; a person with headache speaks softly because a loud voice reverberates in the head. Xu Bin explains this through meridian theory.
- 喑喑然 and 啾啾然 are both words describing the quality of the voice.

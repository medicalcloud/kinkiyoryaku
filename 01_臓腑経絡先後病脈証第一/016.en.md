# Clause 16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When diseases of the five viscera each obtain [what they need], they are cured. Diseases of the five viscera each have things they detest; each falls ill according to what it does not like. When a sick person suddenly craves what he would ordinarily not eat, there will surely be fever.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that the five flavors can cure disease and can also aggravate it, because the likings of the five viscera differ. Hence "when the five viscera each obtain what they need, they are cured." The lung desires to gather in, so one quickly eats sour to gather it in; the lung suffers from qi counterflowing upward, so one quickly eats bitter to discharge it. The heart desires softness, so one quickly eats salty to soften it; the heart suffers from slackness, so one quickly eats sour to gather it in. The liver desires dispersal, so one quickly eats acrid to disperse it; the liver suffers from tension, so one quickly eats sweet to relax it. The spleen desires relaxation, so one quickly eats sweet to relax it; the spleen suffers from dampness, so one quickly eats bitter to dry it. The kidney desires firmness, so one quickly eats bitter to firm it; the kidney suffers from dryness, so one quickly eats acrid to moisten it. Then each obtains its aid and is cured.

But there are flavors that each organ detests. For example, acrid is originally the flavor of the lung, but when a qi disease damages the lung, acrid runs to the qi, so acrid becomes what the lung detests. Hence "in qi disease, do not eat much acrid." Bitter is originally the flavor of the heart, but when a blood disease damages the heart, bitter runs to the blood, so bitter becomes what the heart detests. Hence "in blood disease, do not eat much bitter." Sour is originally the flavor of the liver, but when a sinew disease damages the liver, sour runs to the sinews, so sour becomes what the liver detests. Hence "in sinew disease, do not eat much sour." Sweet is originally the flavor of the spleen, but when a flesh disease damages the spleen, sweet runs to the flesh, so sweet becomes what the spleen detests. Hence "in flesh disease, do not eat much sweet." Salty is originally the flavor of the kidney, but when a bone disease damages the kidney, salty runs to the bones, so salty becomes what the kidney detests. Hence "in bone disease, do not eat much salty." These are things each detests because of disease, not by their original nature.

But there are also things that are not detested because of disease but are originally disliked by the organ, and eating much of them produces disease. For example, metal fears fire, and bitter is the flavor of heart fire, so it is what lung metal dislikes. Hence "eating much bitter makes the skin wither and the hair fall out." Fire fears water, and salty is the flavor of kidney water, so it is what heart fire dislikes. Hence "eating much salty makes the vessels congeal and the color change." Wood fears metal, and acrid is the flavor of lung metal, so it is what liver wood dislikes. Hence "eating much acrid makes the sinews hypertonic and the nails wither." Earth fears wood, and sour is the flavor of liver wood, so it is what spleen earth dislikes. Hence "eating much sour makes the flesh thick and hard and the lips curl up." Water fears earth, and sweet is the flavor of spleen earth, so it is what kidney water dislikes. Hence "eating much sweet makes the bones ache and the teeth fall out." In each case, damage follows the disliked flavor and becomes disease.

Although the likes and dislikes of the five viscera have a fixed nature, there is also the principle that they change with disease. For example, in bone disease one should not eat salty, yet one suddenly craves salty; if the qi of the disease were not depressed and hot, how could the disposition change? Hence "there will surely be fever." When evil overcomes the right, the organ qi becomes hot because of the evil, and when hot, its likings are reversed.

Discussion: That the five viscera are cured by obtaining flavors that aid them with respect to what they desire and what they suffer from is certainly the right principle of correcting imbalance and remedying harm. But speaking of changes, in discussing what they obtain there are also things outside the normal principle that one must know. For example, fear is the emotion of the kidney, and excessive fear damages the kidney; thought is spleen earth, and thought conversely overcomes fear. Cold is the body of the kidney, and extreme cold damages the blood; dryness can dry up water, so dryness can overcome cold. Salty is the flavor of the kidney, and excessive salty damages the blood; sweet is the flavor of earth, and sweet conversely overcomes salty. Anger is the emotion of the liver, and excessive anger damages the liver; sorrow is lung metal, and sorrow conversely overcomes anger. Wind is governed by the liver, and extreme wind damages the sinews; dryness is the qi of metal, and dryness can overcome wind. Sour is the flavor of the liver, and excessive sour damages the sinews; acrid is the flavor of metal, and acrid conversely overcomes sour. Thought is the emotion of the spleen, and excessive thought damages the spleen; anger is liver wood, and anger conversely overcomes thought. Dampness is the transformation of the spleen, and extreme dampness damages the flesh; wind is the qi of wood, and wind can overcome dampness. Sweet is the flavor of earth, and excessive sweet damages the flesh; sour is the flavor of wood, and sour conversely overcomes sweet. Joy is the emotion of the heart, and excessive joy damages the heart; fear is kidney water, and fear conversely overcomes joy. Heat is the body of the heart, and extreme heat damages the qi; cold is governed by the kidney, and cold can overcome heat. Bitter is the flavor of the heart, and excessive bitter damages the qi; salty is kidney water, and salty conversely overcomes bitter. Worry is the emotion of the lung, and excessive worry damages the lung; joy is heart fire, and joy conversely overcomes worry. Heat is not the nature of the lung, and heat damages the skin and body hair; cold can rescue metal, and cold can overcome heat. Acrid is the flavor of metal, and acrid damages the skin and body hair; bitter is the flavor of the heart, and bitter conversely overcomes acrid. In all these, opposites rescue each other. This too is the five viscera each obtaining something and the disease being cured. When it is due to a change in the disease, treatment too obtains [results] by change.

## Notes

A clause stating that each of the five viscera has what it likes (what it obtains) and what it detests; obtaining what it likes cures it, and what it detests makes it ill.

- The latter part says that when a patient suddenly craves something not usually liked, the organ qi has changed because of the disease, and this is a sign of coming fever.
- Xu Bin's commentary explains in detail, citing the *Suwen*'s theories of the five flavors and five emotions (chapters on visceral qi and the seasons, on the five qi, on the formation of the five viscera, and the great treatise on yin and yang correspondences).
- Most other sources read 各有所得者愈, with 所 added.

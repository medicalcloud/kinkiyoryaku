# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When the inhalation is slightly rapid, the disease is in the middle jiao and is excess; purging it brings immediate recovery. If deficient, it cannot be cured. When [the disease] is in the upper jiao, the inhalation is hurried; when in the lower jiao, the inhalation is distant (deep and long). These are all difficult to cure. One whose body shakes and trembles with breathing cannot be cured.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This uses cases with predominant inhalation to show whether the disease is deficient or excess and to distinguish the ease or difficulty of treatment. One exhalation and one inhalation is normal; when inhalation predominates, clearly something is making it abnormal. It becomes slightly rapid, and the inhaled qi goes back and forth through the middle jiao quickly; hence "the disease is in the middle jiao and is excess." So purging it opens the congestion and brings recovery. If it is not excess but deficiency, the root of the liver and kidney is not firm, and the qi floats lightly and deserts upward, so it cannot be cured.

But whether the disease is above or below makes a difference. If in the upper jiao, the yang of the heart and lung is deficient and cannot generate yin; the yang that should aid the lower part turns into reverting yang and does not enter below. Since the path of the heart and lung is near, the inhalation is hurried. If in the lower jiao, the yin of the liver and kidney is deficient; the yin that should join with the upper part turns into dry fire and has difficulty rising up quickly. Since the path of the liver and kidney is far, the inhalation is slow. Inhalation is the master of gathering in the original qi; both hurried and slow [inhalation] are due to deficiency of original qi, so they are difficult to cure. If the body trembles and shakes with the coming and going of breath, the qi of the ying-nutrient and wei-defense going back and forth is already exhausted, and the end is near. Hence "cannot be cured."

## Notes

Following the previous clause (abnormal exhalation), this clause judges the location and prognosis of disease by abnormal inhalation.

- Slightly rapid inhalation indicates excess in the middle jiao, curable by purging; deficiency is incurable.
- Hurried inhalation indicates the upper jiao, distant (deep, long) inhalation the lower jiao; both are hard to cure.
- Shaking of the body with each breath is extreme dyspnea, with a poor prognosis.
- Xu Bin's commentary explains 遠 ("distant") as 遅 ("slow").

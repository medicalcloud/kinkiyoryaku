# Clause 9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: If a sick person's floating pulse is in front [of the bar], the disease is in the exterior. If the floating is behind [the bar], the disease is in the interior: the lower back aches, the back is stiff, he cannot walk, and he will surely be short of breath and exhausted.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

A floating pulse originally governs the exterior, but Zhongjing specially distinguished exterior and interior within the floating pulse, wishing people to know its variations. The floating pulse is yang, so when the pulse at all three positions is floating, it is a taiyang pattern. But the inch, bar, and cubit have fixed positions: in front of the bar is yang, behind the bar is yin. When the floating pulse is in front, a yang pulse is in a yang position, so there is no doubt that the disease is in the exterior. When the floating is behind the bar, a yang pulse is in a yin position; yin belongs to the interior, so the disease is in the interior.

Li Guhe says, "Floating at the inch: headache and dizziness, wind arising, or wind-phlegm gathering in the chest. At the bar: earth declining and wood flourishing. At the cubit: urine and stool not flowing freely." This also follows this idea. However, if a yin position had a yin pulse, there would be signs such as cold and diarrhea; now it has a yang pulse, meaning that though the disease is in the interior, it carries yang. Therefore the disease is not in the lesser abdomen but produces lower back pain and stiffness of the back with inability to walk. Moreover, the qi of the lower burner is damaged and cannot connect upward with the chest, so the breath is short, short to exhaustion. This is yang within yin, with the evil in the channels of the interior rather than the viscera of the interior. Hence it is cited as the example of a floating pulse behind.

Discussion: Distinguishing the yin and yang of the floating pulse by front and back to determine exterior and interior is a theory first set forth by Zhongjing. But his words are full of implication and should be extended by analogy, not held to rigidly. There are plenty of people without disease whose pulse is floating in front of the bar and low and weak behind it; do they also belong to the exterior? And people without disease whose pulse is floating behind the bar and low in front; do they belong to the interior? Therefore Zhongjing specifically set out the two words "sick person," so we know that such a judgment is made only when there is a doubtful exterior pattern. If the pulse is floating before the disease begins, it is exterior. When the pulse becomes even and the exterior [signs] lessen, and after lessening the pulse becomes floating again, has the exterior broken out again? This too should be inferred as interior. This is the meaning beyond the words.

## Notes

A clause distinguishing exterior and interior disease by whether a floating pulse is in front (inch) or behind (cubit).

- Floating at the inch indicates exterior disease; floating at the cubit indicates interior disease (lower back, back, lower burner). Many interpret a floating cubit pulse as kidney vacuity, with lower back pain, stiffness, and shortness of breath as signs of kidney vacuity.
- "Li Guhe" in Xu Bin's commentary: the quoted text matches Li Shizhen's (sobriquet Binhu) *Binhu maixue*, so it may be an error for "Li Binhu," but this has not been verified. The translation follows the text.

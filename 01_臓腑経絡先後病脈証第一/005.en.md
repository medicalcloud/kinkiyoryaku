# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: One who moves the shoulders when breathing has hardness in the heart. One whose breathing draws up [the qi of] the chest so that qi ascends coughs. One who breathes with the mouth open and is short of breath has lung atrophy and spits foam.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of the finest point of the listening method. First, by the qi going in and out with breathing, one distinguishes whether the disease is above or below, replete or vacuous. Thus, on the regular principle that one exhalation and one inhalation make one breath, it first distinguishes three cases in which the outgoing qi predominates, to show that the disease is in the upper burner.

The breath goes out through the nose; each exhalation must be followed by an inhalation. Exhalation is governed by the heart and lung, inhalation by the kidney and liver, and the interval by the spleen and stomach. Since what governs them is distinct, the moments of going out and coming in should also be distinguished in detail. Therefore, regarding cases with more exhalation, it describes the breath without mentioning inhalation along with it.

"One who moves the shoulders when breathing has hardness in the heart": breathing with more going out means fire flees upward; reaching the point of moving the shoulders, it is severe. If the evil in the heart were not replete and the qi could move downward a little, how would it come to this? Hence "hardness in the heart."

"One whose breathing draws up [the qi of] the chest so that qi ascends coughs": ascending qi is counterflow; when the breath draws the qi of the chest upward in counterflow, the lung metal's function of gathering and descending does not operate, and it counterflows upward as cough.

"One who breathes with the mouth open and is short of breath has lung atrophy and spits foam": shortness of breath is vacuity. Opening the mouth means there is drool and foam obstructing, with a tendency not to let the qi return; then the lung qi must be blocked, producing lung atrophy with spitting of foam.

These three all show, through exhalation, that the disease is in the heart and lung. Yet it does not simply say "exhalation" but says "breath," probably because even when the outgoing qi is great, without a small return within one cannot exhale greatly. So it sets out the six characters "moving the shoulders," "drawing," and "opening the mouth," so that the disease in exhalation is vividly shown; but it cannot say "exhalation" alone.

## Notes

A clause judging disease by the manner of breathing.

- Breathing with the shoulders moving indicates repletion of evil in the chest; breathing that draws the chest qi upward indicates cough; open-mouthed short breathing indicates lung atrophy (a wasting disease of the lung; see the later chapter on lung atrophy, lung abscess, cough, and qi ascent).
- "In the heart" (心中) is usually taken to mean within the chest (including the epigastrium), not the heart itself.

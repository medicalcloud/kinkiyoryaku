# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: In disease, there are cases where one must urgently rescue the interior and cases where one must rescue the exterior. What does this mean? The master said: When, in illness, the physician has purged and there follows unceasing diarrhea with undigested grain, with pain in the body, one must urgently rescue the interior. Afterward, if there is pain in the body and the stool has become normal by itself, one must urgently rescue the exterior.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that a physician must know the order of urgency and priority. Treating exterior and interior separately is the normal principle. But when there is an exterior and also an interior, arising from mistaken purging, one cannot resolve both exterior and interior as one does when residual evil is not yet cleared. Even though the body aches, one must not treat the exterior, because if one delays a little, the exterior evil will all enter the interior. Hence "one must urgently rescue the interior." When the stool has become normal but the body still aches, one must again not slight it as residual evil, because the interior has already had diarrhea, and if one delays a little, the interior will again receive the exterior evil and the diarrhea will not stop. Hence it again says, "one must urgently rescue the exterior."

## Notes

A clause on the order of treatment when exterior and interior patterns coexist; its content is almost the same as a clause in the middle taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 91).

- When mistaken purging has caused unceasing diarrhea with undigested food, rescue the interior first even if there is body pain (an exterior sign) (in the *Shanghan lun*, with Sini tang (Counterflow Cold Decoction)).
- If body pain remains after the diarrhea has stopped, rescue the exterior (in the *Shanghan lun*, with Guizhi tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction)).
- 清 in 清便 is equivalent to 圊 ("latrine"), referring to defecation and urination.

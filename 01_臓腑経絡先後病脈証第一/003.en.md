# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: A sick person may show qi and color on the face; I wish to hear the explanation. The master said: If the tip of the nose is green-blue, there is pain in the abdomen, and severe cold, the patient dies (one version says: if the abdomen is cold and the pain is severe, the patient dies). If the tip of the nose is slightly black, there is water qi. If the color is yellow, there is cold above the chest. If the color is white, there is blood loss. If it is slightly red out of season, the patient dies. If the eyes are perfectly round [and fixed], it is tetany, and it cannot be cured. Further, a green-blue color indicates pain, black indicates taxation, red indicates wind, yellow indicates difficult defecation, and a fresh, bright color indicates lingering fluid.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section is the physician's method of inspection. The method of inspection values [observing] the movement and stillness of spirit and qi, but here the question concerns only qi and color appearing on the face, so the answer adapts the *Neijing*'s method of examining the "bright hall." The bright hall is the nose. The *Neijing* says, "The bone of the bright hall is high and raised, level and straight. The five viscera are arranged in order in the center; the six bowels flank them on both sides. The head and face rise to the court (forehead); the royal palace is at the lower extreme (between the eyes)." This says that each of the five colors has its own region.

But the nose tip is especially important. Hence: "If the tip of the nose is green-blue, there is pain in the abdomen." The nose tip belongs to the spleen; green-blue is the color of the liver. Liver wood, carrying kidney cold, overwhelms earth; above it shows on the nose, below in the abdomen. If there is also severe cold, it is a sudden illness with yang collapse, which governs sudden death; hence "with severe cold, the patient dies." If the nose tip is slightly black, black is the kidney's color, but if it is slight it is not deep and dark, and there is no abdominal pain; so it governs only water qi and is not a sudden illness. If the color is yellow, earth is depressed and its own color shows; unless there were cold fluid in the chest blocking it, it could not become depressed; hence "there is cold above the chest." If the color is white, the classic says, "When blood has deserted, the color is white, withered, and lusterless"; hence "blood loss." But the *Lingshu* chapter on the five colors takes white as cold; one should know that if no signs of blood loss are seen, one should judge it as cold.

If slightly red, earth receiving the color of fire seems suitable; but [such a view] does not know that the nose is also the outer indicator of the lung. If it is slightly red out of season, it is not fire that generates earth but fire that restrains metal; it governs visceral dryness and death.

The eyes are where the essence of the five viscera gathers and where spirit-qi arises. If they are perfectly round, the eyes stare without moving, leading to tetany. This is the cutting off of yin. That parturient women often have tetany is also yin collapse. Taken together, perfectly round eyes are undoubtedly the cutting off of yin; hence "cannot be cured."

The following several phrases, "further, a green-blue color," continuing from the sentence on the eyes, seem to speak only of the eyes. But the *Neijing* chapter on the five colors first says, "Green-blue and black are pain; yellow and red are heat; white is cold," and later, "Yellow and red are wind; green-blue and black are pain; white is cold; yellow and moist is fat; [it is] pus; intense red is blood; intense pain is hypertonicity; intense cold is numbness of the skin," and below that, "Each of the five colors appears in its region"; so this seems to be a general statement. Again the five colors chapter says, "Always examine the center of the court: thin and lustrous is wind; thick and turbid is impediment; on the earth [region] is reversal. This is the norm. In each case speak of the disease by its color." The center of the court is between the eyebrows; the earth [region] is the great division (beside the mouth). So we know that the five colors are spoken of generally, combining the upper and lower parts of the bright hall.

Green-blue is pain because all pain belongs to the liver. Black is taxation: with taxation, yang qi is attacked within, heat lodges in the kidney, the kidney overwhelms the heart, the heart becomes ill first and the kidney responds; hence black. Wind is a yang evil, so "red is wind"; the *Neijing* quoted above also says "red is heat": wind, hence heat. When yellow, the spleen is depressed, so defecation is difficult. Since it has already said "if the color is yellow, there is cold above the chest," and here again "difficult defecation," one should know that when cold blocks above, the spleen is depressed below. Further, the later chapter (on water qi) says, "A person with water disease has 'sleeping silkworms' under the eyes, and the face and eyes are fresh and lustrous"; hence "a fresh, bright color indicates lingering fluid." Things like the *Qianjin*'s statement — "When the eyes are red, the disease is in the heart; white, in the lung; green-blue, in the liver; yellow, in the spleen; black, in the kidney; an indescribable yellow, in the chest" — are a separate method of examining the eyes. Here it is correct to take [the colors] as spoken of with respect to the bright hall.

## Notes

A clause on inspection: judging disease by the facial color, especially the color of the nose tip (the "bright hall").

- The five colors are assigned to diseases: green-blue to pain and cold, black to water qi and taxation, yellow to cold in the chest and constipation, white to blood loss, red to wind and heat.
- "Eyes perfectly round" describes eyes that stare fixedly, said to indicate severe tetany. The base reads 痊 ("recovered"), which appears to be an error; the icntcm text and Xu Bin's edition read 痓, and Wikisource and Daizhige read 痙 (both "tetany"). It is translated here as tetany.
- "Out of season" is said to mean outside the season (summer) when red should appear.
- Xu Bin's commentary draws heavily on the *Lingshu* chapter on the five colors.

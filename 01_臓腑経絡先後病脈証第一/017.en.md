# Clause 17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When various diseases are in the zang organs and one wishes to attack them, one should attack them according to what [the pathogen] has obtained (combined with). For example, to one who is thirsty, give Zhuling tang (Polyporus Decoction). All others follow this.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

To treat a disease upon seeing it is the normal principle. Why then does this clause single out the two words "in the zang organs" above, point specifically to the single sign of thirst below, and further take the single Zhuling tang as the standard? One should know that if the thirst is confined to dryness and heat in the upper jiao, gualougen (trichosanthes root) is the precise medicinal; if the thirst is in the stomach, gegen (pueraria) is the precise medicinal; if the thirst is in the yang aspect, Baihu tang (White Tiger Decoction) is suitable; if the thirst belongs to residual pathogen of Taiyang, Wuling san (Five Ingredients Powder with Poria) is suitable. Only when the thirst is in the zang organs, not solely in the bowels, and Zhuling tang is suitable, must Zhuling tang be the means of attacking what [the pathogen] has obtained.

Thus in Zhongjing's *Shanghan lun*, one clause says, "In Shaoyin disease, with diarrhea for six or seven days, cough and retching, thirst, heart vexation, and inability to sleep, Zhuling tang governs it," and another says, "In Yangming disease, with a floating pulse, fever, thirst with desire to drink water, and inhibited urination, Zhuling tang governs it." The former pattern is Shaoyin disease, with the disease below; in the latter, urination is inhibited, so the disease is also below. The disease is below, and pathogenic heat further binds with water-fluid retention in the middle, so this medicine that disinhibits water and moistens dryness must be the precise one. This is what is meant by "according to what it has obtained," unlike treating thirst indiscriminately. This is the method of treating the root. Hence "all others follow this."

## Notes

A clause stating, with the example of Zhuling tang, that when disease is in the zang organs (interior), one should identify what the disease pathogen has combined with ("what it has obtained") and attack accordingly.

- "According to what it has obtained" is often understood to mean that when the pathogen has combined with water, blood, phlegm, food, etc., one treats according to that. If there is thirst with water and heat combined, Zhuling tang disinhibits water and removes heat.
- The two *Shanghan lun* clauses cited by Xu Bin are the Zhuling tang clauses of the Shaoyin chapter (corresponding to clause 319) and the Yangming chapter (corresponding to clause 223).
- Xu Bin's edition appends the formula of Zhuling tang at the end. The base has no formula in this chapter.

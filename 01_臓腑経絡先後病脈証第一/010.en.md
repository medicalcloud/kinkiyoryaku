# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: The classic says, "Reverting yang moves alone." What does this mean? The master said: This is having yang without yin; hence it is called reverting yang.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Reverting yang is solitary yang. Hence the classic says "moves alone." Zhongjing glosses it as "without yin."

Note: The discussion in the *Qianjin* says, "In cold damage in the winter months, be careful not to fumigate. If fumigated, it counterflows; [the heat] lodges in the breath and causes dyspnea; the guest heat cannot be held and causes the mouth to fester with sores. The yin vessels also come loose, the blood scatters and does not flow, the right yang then reverts, the yin does not go [out], and following the guest heat it rushes madly inward, causing chest bind; the spleen qi then weakens, and clear urine flows freely." From this one can understand the reason for having yang without yin, and also the signs in which reverting yang appears.

## Notes

A short clause explaining the term "reverting yang."

- "Reverting yang moves alone" refers to a state in which yin has declined and yang alone rises one-sidedly; it is explained as "having yang without yin." Which classic is quoted is unclear.
- Xu Bin cites the *Qianjin*'s discussion of cold damage (yang becoming one-sided through mistreatment by fumigation) as an example. The quotation is obscure, and the translation is tentative.

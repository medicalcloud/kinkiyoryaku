# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When the pulse at the inch opening is stirring, it stirs according to the time when [an organ] flourishes. For example, when the liver flourishes [in spring], the color is green-blue; in each of the four seasons [the complexion] follows its color. If in the liver's [season] the color should be green-blue but is instead white, the color and pulse are not those of the season, and in all such cases there should be illness.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that the way of medicine values following the seasons, and that color and pulse correspond in principle. "Inch opening" refers generally to the cun, guan, and chi of both hands. Beating with force is "stirring." Flourishing according to the flourishing time [of the organ] is appropriate. The color also corresponds; this is the method of examining color at the bright hall. This is not only about the liver; the liver is taken as an example. Green-blue is the flourishing qi of the liver; if, at the time of its flourishing, the color is white instead, the liver is restrained by the lung and cannot follow the flourishing of the season. So if the color is contrary to the season there is illness; if the pulse is contrary to the season there is also illness; if the color is contrary to the pulse or the pulse contrary to the color, there is also illness. Hence "the color and pulse are not those of the season."

## Notes

A clause on the correspondence of the seasons with pulse and complexion.

- Each of the five zang organs flourishes in a season (liver in spring, heart in summer, spleen in the late-summer earth periods, lung in autumn, kidney in winter); normally the pulse and complexion then correspond to that organ.
- If in spring (the liver's season) the complexion is white (the lung's color) instead of green-blue, metal is restraining wood, and illness is expected.
- Xu Bin takes "inch opening" here to mean the whole wrist pulse (cun, guan, and chi).
- Xu Bin's edition repeats the final words 皆当病.

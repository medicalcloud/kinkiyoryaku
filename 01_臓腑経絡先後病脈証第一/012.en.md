# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: When the pulse deserts, if [the disease] enters the zang organs, death follows at once; if it enters the bowels, recovery follows at once. What does this mean? The master said: This is not true of one disease only; it is so of all diseases. Take, for example, yellow-water sore: those that start from the mouth and flow toward the four limbs can be treated; those that flow from the four limbs and enter the mouth cannot be treated. Disease that is outside can be treated; disease that enters the interior is quickly fatal.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The earlier statement that "deep and excess contend" means the pathogen is heavy, so the zang organs cannot withstand it. Here the pathogen is slight, but because the healthy qi is deficient, it likewise deserts; if it enters the zang organs, death follows at once; if it enters the bowels, there is recovery. Do the bowels endure deficiency while the zang organs do not? [Such a question] fails to see that, in all diseases, coming out to the yang is shallow and being transmitted to the yin is deep. Therefore it says, "This is not true of one disease only; it is so of all diseases."

The metaphor of yellow-water sore, starting from the mouth or from the limbs, is plain and easy to understand. The mouth belongs to yin and the limbs to yang. According to the distinction of yin and yang there is the difference between treatable and untreatable. Extending this to other diseases, the principle of zang-fu organs is the same. But the words "zang organs" and "bowels" are confusing and hard to fathom, while "interior" and "exterior" are plain and easy to grasp. So it concludes, "Disease that is outside can be treated; disease that enters the interior is quickly fatal," wishing people to distinguish by "interior" and "exterior" which of zang organs or bowels [the disease] enters.

## Notes

Following the previous clause, it states as a principle for all diseases that disease moving outward (to the bowels) recovers and disease moving inward (to the zang organs) is fatal.

- Using yellow-water sore (see the later chapter on sores, intestinal abscess, and yellow-water sore) as an example, those spreading from the mouth (center) to the limbs (periphery) recover, while those moving from the limbs to the mouth do not.
- The meaning of "the pulse deserts" (脈脱) is unclear: some take it as a pulse that has disappeared, others as the pulse condition of "sudden syncope." Xu Bin takes it as desertion due to deficient healthy qi even though the pathogen is slight.

# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: When the pulse deserts, if [the disease] enters the viscera, death follows at once; if it enters the bowels, recovery follows at once. What does this mean? The master said: This is not true of one disease only; it is so of all diseases. Take, for example, spreading sores: those that start from the mouth and flow toward the four limbs can be treated; those that flow from the four limbs and enter the mouth cannot be treated. Disease that is outside can be treated; disease that enters the interior is quickly fatal.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The earlier statement that "sunken and repletion contend" means the evil is heavy, so the viscera cannot withstand it. Here the evil is slight, but because the right qi is deficient, it likewise deserts; if it enters the viscera, death follows at once; if it enters the bowels, there is recovery. Do the bowels endure vacuity while the viscera do not? [Such a question] fails to see that, in all diseases, coming out to the yang is shallow and being transmitted to the yin is deep. Therefore it says, "This is not true of one disease only; it is so of all diseases."

The metaphor of spreading sores, starting from the mouth or from the limbs, is plain and easy to understand. The mouth belongs to yin and the limbs to yang. According to the distinction of yin and yang there is the difference between treatable and untreatable. Extending this to other diseases, the principle of viscera and bowels is the same. But the words "viscera" and "bowels" are confusing and hard to fathom, while "interior" and "exterior" are plain and easy to grasp. So it concludes, "Disease that is outside can be treated; disease that enters the interior is quickly fatal," wishing people to distinguish by "interior" and "exterior" which of viscera or bowels [the disease] enters.

## Notes

Following the previous clause, it states as a principle for all diseases that disease moving outward (to the bowels) recovers and disease moving inward (to the viscera) is fatal.

- Using spreading sores (see the later chapter on sores, intestinal abscess, and spreading sores) as an example, those spreading from the mouth (center) to the limbs (periphery) recover, while those moving from the limbs to the mouth do not.
- The meaning of "the pulse deserts" (脈脱) is unclear: some take it as a pulse that has disappeared, others as the pulse condition of "sudden reversal." Xu Bin takes it as desertion due to deficient right qi even though the evil is slight.

# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Floating pulse, inhibited urination, slight fever, and wasting-thirst: it is appropriate to disinhibit urination and induce sweating; Wuling san (Goreisan) governs it.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

A floating pulse with slight fever means the exterior has not yet cleared. Wasting-thirst with inhibited urination means there is heat in the interior. So guizhi (keishi) governs the exterior, baizhu (byakujutsu), fuling (bukuryo), and zexie (takusha) govern the interior, and drinking plenty of hot water aids the tendency to go out and down. This treats the shallow, near form of wasting-thirst. Note: this and the previous clause are both wasting-thirst. In the previous clause urination is copious, so we know yin is deficient with bound heat; in this clause urination is inhibited with slight fever, so it is guest pathogen entering inward. Hence the treatments differ entirely. But guest pathogen entering inward is not true wasting-thirst; they are discussed together to show the distinction.

## Notes

A clause using Wuling san for a floating pulse, slight fever, inhibited urination, and thirst. It is similar in content to the Wuling san clause in the middle Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 71).

- Xu Bin contrasts it with the previous clause (wasting-thirst with copious urine), saying this is guest pathogen entering inward and not true wasting-thirst.

# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A man with wasting and thirst and, on the contrary, copious urination — drinking one dou and urinating one dou: Shenqi wan governs it (the formula appears under leg qi).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

(The formula appears under postpartum.) Yin cannot restrain yang, and the kidney loses its authority over opening and closing, so urination is copious and unrestrained. But it is not true yang in surplus with excess pathogenic qi greatly hyperactive; it is what is called "when qi is exuberant, urination is frequent." So the Liuwei wan ingredients strengthen the master of water to restrain the brilliance of yang, and still rely on gui and fuzi to restore the true yang; then the small fire is extinguished and yin and yang are balanced.

## Notes

A clause using Shenqi wan (Bawei Shenqi wan) for wasting and thirst in a man who urinates as much as he drinks (lower wasting).

- It is attributed to deficiency of kidney yin and yang, unable to restrain water. It is sometimes related to the polyuria of diabetes.
- The interlinear note "appears under leg qi" refers to Master Cui's Bawei wan appended in the chapter on wind attack and joint wind. Daizhige says "in miscellaneous diseases of women," and the interlinear note in Xu Bin's commentary says "under postpartum."

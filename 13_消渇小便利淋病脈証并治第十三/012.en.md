# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Thirst with desire to drink water and a dry mouth and tongue [base: 若]: Baihu jia Renshen tang governs it (the formula appears under summer heat stroke).

Floating pulse with fever, thirst with desire to drink water, and inhibited urination: Zhuling tang governs it.

Zhuling tang formula

Zhuling (remove the skin); fuling; ejiao; huashi; zexie; 1 liang each.

Of the five ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water first boil four ingredients down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the ejiao and dissolve it, and take 7 ge warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This too is of the wasting and thirst kind. But thirst with desire to drink water and a dry mouth mean the lung qi is already hot, and further yang is deficient and the dryness of yin appears outside, its heat floating. So Baihu tang treats the fire and clears the heat, and renshen supplements the deficiency. It is entirely different from those that treat only the middle and lower jiao and disperse bound heat.

This is Wuling san with guizhi and baizhu replaced by huashi and ejiao. A floating pulse with fever seems to be heat in the exterior; but with thirst, desire for water, inhibited urination, and severe internal heat, the already waning exterior heat is not worth worrying about, while the changes of yin heat and lodged water threaten to be endless. So fuling and zexie guide out the water, and ejiao and huashi are added, making enriching yin and flushing yang the urgent task. Yet the decoction is named after zhuling alone because zhuling is good at removing water-fluid retention in the stomach; so we know this formula centers on removing water-fluid retention.

## Notes

Two clauses using Baihu jia Renshen tang and Zhuling tang for two types of thirst: the former for heat of lung and stomach with dry mouth and tongue, the latter for a floating pulse, fever, thirst, and inhibited urination with water and heat bound together. Both also appear in the Yangming chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clauses 222 and 223).

- For 若 in 口乾若燥, most other sources read 舌 ("dry mouth and dry tongue"), which the translation follows.
- In the base the two clauses are in a single paragraph.
- Zhuling tang disinhibits water with zhuling, fuling, and zexie, disinhibits heat with huashi, and nourishes yin with ejiao.

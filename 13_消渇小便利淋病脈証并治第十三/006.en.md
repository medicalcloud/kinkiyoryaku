# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Thirst with desire to drink water that does not stop: Wenge san (Bunkosan; Clamshell Powder) governs it.

Wenge san formula

Wenge (bunko; clam shell) 4 liang.

Pound the single ingredient above into a powder, and take a square-inch spoonful mixed with 5 ge of boiling water.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Thirst with desire to drink water means there is heat in the interior; if it does not stop, the heat is firmly bound. Wenge is salty by nature and an extremely yin substance; it can soften hardness, moisten dryness, and remove heat; so it governs it. It is used alone to take its exclusive, downward-entering action to clear dry heat of the middle and lower jiao. The three formulas above for wasting-thirst all make treating the middle and lower jiao urgent; we know that wasting-thirst originally arises from Jueyin, and in severe cases the two yang bind and involve the kidney, so treatment should not rashly stir the upper jiao.

Discussion: Thirst with desire for hot drinks differs from thirst with desire for water. Desire for hot drinks is dry heat of the stomach; desire for water is congested qi with dry yin. So though there is water and it seems there should be no thirst, there is thirst and desire for water, and the treatment is entirely different. People today lump them together whenever they see thirst, and many errors result. Is this not clear from Zhongjing's treatments before and after? There are also people whose lips and mouth become dry when lying down at night, but whose mouths become moist as soon as they sit up and the yang rises; this is only yin dryness, so when yang is obtained and qi transforms, the dryness stops. It is merely less severe than thirst with desire for water during the day.

## Notes

A clause using Wenge san for thirst that does not stop even after drinking water.

- A single ingredient, wenge (clam shell), taken as a powder; it is said to clear heat and stop thirst.
- Most other sources give wenge as 5 liang.

# Clause 9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Those with strangury must not be made to sweat; if sweating is induced, there will surely be blood in the urine.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

These three clauses discuss strangury in general. The first section says strangury disease lies entirely in the lower burner; so in the earlier eleventh chapter it says "heat in the lower burner" and that it governs strangury and blockage. Here it says "the urine is like millet grains": millet is white and dribbling, and in severe cases like rice crumbs. But since qi and blood differ, later people named five kinds of strangury. When the qi of the lesser abdomen is not harmonious and loses its solid foundation, it is taut and tense. When heat evil rises, the pain draws into the umbilicus.

The section on the instep yang says that although strangury need not always be due to the stomach, when the instep yang pulse is rapid, it belongs to heat in the stomach, and there are separately the signs of dispersing grain and craving drink, hard stool, and frequent urination; this is strangury close to wasting thirst.

The section on those with strangury says that since strangury is an internal disorder of the lower burner, sweating is prohibited; mistaken sweating causes blood in the urine, for releasing the yang stirs the blood. No formula is given because strangury is governed by the lower burner; when there is stomach heat it is close to wasting thirst, and when there is kidney heat it resembles inhibited urination; so the formulas before and after may be used interchangeably with judgment.

## Notes

A clause prohibiting sweating in those with strangury. The same prohibition appears in the middle taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 84).

- 便血 here is taken to mean blood in the urine.
- Xu Bin's commentary explains the three strangury clauses of units 7 through 9 together.

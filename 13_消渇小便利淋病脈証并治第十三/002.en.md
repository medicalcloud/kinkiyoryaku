# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is floating and slow. Floating means deficiency; slow means taxation. Deficiency means the wei-defensive qi is insufficient; taxation means the ying-nutrient qi is exhausted. The anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse is floating and rapid. Floating means qi; rapid means grain is dispersed and [the stool] is very hard (one version reads "tight"). When qi is exuberant, urination is frequent; when urination is frequent, [the stool] is hard. When hardness and rapidity contend, there is wasting-thirst.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section discusses that the pulse of wasting-thirst should be verified by combining the inch opening and the Fuyang. Those with wasting-thirst do not have a disease of form, but the central qi is not pure, transport and transformation are hurried, the original qi is not substantial, and the ying-nutrient and wei-defense become deficient by themselves. So the pulse at the inch opening is floating and slow. Floating not due to the exterior belongs to qi not being gathered; hence "floating means deficiency." Slow not due to cold belongs to the ying-nutrient not being full; hence "slow means taxation" — taxation meaning something like exhaustion. When the qi is not gathered, it cannot join forces to enter inward and follow its normal circulation; hence "deficiency means the wei-defensive qi is insufficient." When the ying-nutrient is not full, it cannot assist the qi to move vigorously, and a slow, sluggish state appears; hence "taxation means the ying-nutrient qi is exhausted." Wasting-thirst originally belongs to the side of heat, yet the cun pulse shows only deficiency and not rapidity; we know that in wasting-thirst the bound heat is below and need not appear at the inch opening.

The Fuyang is the pulse governing the two yang specifically. It is floating and rapid. Floating means the qi beats without descending; hence "floating means qi." Rapid means the spleen is strong and constrained, the grain is easily dispersed, and the heat becomes ever harder; hence "rapid means grain is dispersed and very hard." 溲 is urine. Surplus qi is fire, and fire is swift by nature, so urination is frequent. With frequent urination, the yin qi is consumed and yang is hyperactive without restraint; hence hard — "hard" meaning heat binding severely. The heat is not resolved by urination, yang is hyperactive and yin lost; hence "contend." Yin lost and yang ever more hyperactive; hence "there is wasting-thirst." This says that the disease of wasting-thirst is bound in the two yang, and the pulse should be examined entirely at the Fuyang. But earlier it said "hunger without desire to eat," and here it says "grain is dispersed," so it seems to differ slightly in deficiency and excess from pathogen bound in Jueyin.

## Notes

A clause on the pulse of wasting-thirst, giving the floating, slow cun pulse (deficiency of ying-nutrient and wei-defense) and the floating, rapid anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse (stomach heat dispersing grain, hard stool, copious urine).

- 乙 in the interlinear note 乙作緊 is 一 ("one version") in all other sources.
- The base reads 運, 跌陽, and 数数即堅 in places; these are emended to 遅, 趺陽, and 溲数即堅 because all other sources so read (see the emendation notes).

# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

*Jinxiao fang*

Zhu Fu tang (Atractylodes and Aconite Decoction): treats wind deficiency with a heavy, dizzy head, extreme distress, and inability to taste food. It warms the flesh, supplements the center, and benefits the essential qi.

Baizhu 2 liang; fuzi 1.5 pieces (blast-fried, remove the skin); gancao 1 liang (mix-fried).

Cut the three ingredients above; for each dose take 5 *qianbi* with 5 slices of ginger and 1 jujube, decoct in one and a half cups of water down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs, and take warm.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The kidney qi is empty and deficient, and pathogenic wind takes advantage of it, wandering with no outlet. The wind carries the turbid yin qi in the kidney and counterflows upward to attack, so that the head is dizzy and the distress extreme; and the stomach qi too is deficient, so one cannot taste food. This cannot be cured by light, rising wind formulas. So fuzi is used to warm the water organ, and baizhu and gancao to warm the earth organ. Once water and earth are warm — like the water in a well in winter — since water and earth are warm, the gentle qi of yang can be restored at once, and the turbid yin qi descends by itself without being driven out.

## Notes

An appended formula, Zhu Fu tang from the *Jinxiao fang*, for dizziness and heaviness of the head with loss of appetite due to wind deficiency. It has the same composition as Baizhu Fuzi tang in the earlier chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke (baizhu, fuzi, gancao, ginger, jujube).

- The amounts and wording such as "cut; for each dose 5 *qianbi*" are in the Song boiled-powder format.

# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yuebi jia Zhu tang (Maidservant from Yue Decoction plus Atractylodes) from the *Qianjin fang*: treats flesh exhaustion in which, when there is heat, the body's fluids desert, the interstices open, sweat drains greatly, there is pestilential wind qi, and the legs of the lower jiao are weak.

Mahuang 6 liang; shigao half a jin; shengjiang 3 liang; gancao 2 liang; baizhu 4 liang; dazao 15 pieces.

Of the six ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, boil down to 3 sheng, and take warm in three doses. For aversion to wind, add 1 fuzi, blast-fried.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is a formula for treating wind at its extreme turned into heat. When wind prevails, heat prevails, so the inside becomes extremely hot with much sweating, and the fluids are about to desert. When the fluids desert and the exterior becomes ever more deficient, the interstices can no longer be secured, the sweat drains without stopping and is about to drain greatly. Wind enters the ying-nutrient and becomes pestilential (*li*). The *Neijing* says, "*Li* is the ying-nutrient qi becoming hot and putrefying." Now wind entering the ying-nutrient and becoming heat is pestilential wind qi. When wind prevails the qi floats, and the lower jiao is originally deficient, so it comes to the point where reverting yang moves alone and the turbid yin does not descend; there is nothing to nourish the yin, the yin becomes ever more deficient, and the legs of the lower jiao become weak. So mahuang frees the impeded qi, shigao clears heat in the qi aspect, shengjiang and dazao harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, and gancao and baizhu regulate the healthy qi of the spleen. Mahuang is used despite much sweating, relying on baizhu to support the right and shigao to nourish the yin to restrain it; hence the name Yuebi jia Zhu tang. This is what is called "using a man's courage while removing his violence." When sweat drains greatly and aversion to wind is added, one must guard against yang collapse; hence fuzi is added.

[Xu Bin's formula:] mahuang 6 liang; shigao half a jin; gancao 2 liang; shengjiang 2 liang.

Of the six ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water first decoct the mahuang, remove [the froth] on top (lacuna) take 2 [sheng].

## Notes

An appended formula, Yuebi jia Zhu tang from the *Qianjin fang*, for flesh exhaustion with heat, profuse sweating, and weak legs. Yuebi jia Zhu tang also appears in the later chapter on water qi.

- It is Yuebi tang (mahuang, shigao, shengjiang, gancao, dazao) with baizhu added.
- For 厲風気, some sources read 歴節風気 or 癘風気; the meaning is uncertain. Xu Bin explains it by the *Neijing*'s *li* (ying-nutrient qi becoming hot and putrefying).
- The base reads 内極; it is emended to 肉極 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

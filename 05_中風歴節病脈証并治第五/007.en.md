# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sour flavor damages the sinews; when the sinews are damaged they slacken; this is called draining. Salty damages the bones; when the bones are damaged they wilt; this is called withering. When withering and draining contend, it is called severing drain. When the ying-nutrient qi does not flow, the wei-defense cannot move alone; ying-nutrient and wei-defense both become feeble, the sanjiao has nothing to command, the four members are cut off, the body becomes emaciated, only the feet swell large, yellow sweating comes out, and the shins are cold. If there is fever, it is joint wind.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This discusses how food and drink damage the yin so that the ying-nutrient and wei-defense are both impeded, with swollen feet and cold shins resembling joint wind, which should be distinguished only by fever. Food and drink damage the yin, but each flavor goes to and attacks what it favors. Sour is the flavor of the liver; excessive sour damages the sinews. The sinews bind the bones and make the joints work smoothly; when damaged they slacken and do not contract, and the liver qi does not gather; hence the name "draining." Salty is the flavor of the kidney; excessive salty damages the kidney. The kidney makes the hair flourish and fills the bones; when damaged, the marrow is exhausted and the essence deficient, and the kidney qi wilts and tires; hence the name "withering." Liver and kidney are the root of a person. When the kidney does not flourish and the liver does not gather, the root dissolves and the source is cut off; hence "severing drain."

When food and drink damage the yin, the ying-nutrient receives it first, so the ying-nutrient qi does not flow. The ying-nutrient and wei-defense depend on each other; when the ying-nutrient is damaged, the wei-defense cannot function alone. As it drags on, ying-nutrient and wei-defense both become feeble. The sanjiao commands the internal qi and fills and penetrates the limbs, but having lost the nourishment of ying-nutrient and wei-defense, it has nothing to rely on to command. To command is to hold together. The qi of the four members is not held together and is cut off. The four members are the limbs. The original qi is exhausted and the body emaciated. The feet, being lowest, are not reached by yang qi, so they swell large and the shins are cold. When the qi in the ying-nutrient is depressed, it becomes hot and yellow sweating comes out. But all this is disease of the yin aspect, not joint wind. Joint wind carries external pathogenic damp and is heavy and painful. Only external pathogen necessarily produces fever; hence "if there is fever." That means there is pathogen in the exterior aspect too, passing from the flesh through the joints; then it is joint wind.

Discussion: Joint wind and yellow sweating are most difficult to distinguish. Seeing that Zhongjing twice says "if there is fever, it is joint wind," it seems that joint wind has fever and yellow sweating does not. But in describing yellow sweating Zhongjing also always says "the body is hot," so we know yellow sweating can also have fever; there is simply no joint wind without fever. Yellow sweating arises from entering water to bathe while sweating, and joint wind too may arise from entering water while sweating so that water damages the heart. So if yellow sweating has yellow sweating and joint wind may also have yellow sweating, we know that the sweat of joint wind is sometimes not yellow; there is simply no yellow sweating whose sweat is not yellow. Joint wind speaks of "aching limb joints" and "pain like pulling," while yellow sweating does not mention pain; so we know that pain of the limb joints belongs to joint wind alone. Yellow sweating speaks of thirst, swelling of the limbs, head, and face, "cold in the upper jiao with much drool in the mouth," and "obstruction in the chest with inability to eat, gathering pain instead, and restlessness at dusk with inability to sleep," while joint wind has only swollen feet and yellow sweating; so we know all the above signs belong to yellow sweating alone. Why is this? Both yellow sweating and joint wind are dampness depressed into heat, lingering without end; but in joint wind the dampness flows into the joints, while in yellow sweating the pathogenic damp gathers in the diaphragm. So yellow sweating has no pain of the limb joints, and joint wind has no upper-jiao signs.

## Notes

A clause describing damage to sinews and bones from excessive sour and salty flavors, with declining ying-nutrient and wei-defense, swollen feet, yellow sweating, and cold shins, and distinguishing it from joint wind by the presence of fever.

- Excessive sour damages the liver (sinews), so the sinews slacken ("draining"); excessive salty damages the kidney (bones), so the bones wilt ("withering"). Both together are called "severing drain."
- Xu Bin's "Discussion" examines in detail the differentiation of joint wind and yellow sweating (see the chapter on water qi).

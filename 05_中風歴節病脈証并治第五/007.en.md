# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sour flavor damages the sinews; when the sinews are damaged they slacken; this is called draining. Salty damages the bones; when the bones are damaged they wilt; this is called withering. When withering and draining contend, it is called severing drain. When the nutritive qi does not flow, the defensive cannot move alone; nutritive and defensive both become faint, the triple burner has nothing to command, the four members are cut off, the body becomes emaciated, only the feet swell large, yellow sweat comes out, and the shins are cold. If there is fever, it is joint-running.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This discusses how food and drink damage the yin so that the nutritive and defensive are both impeded, with swollen feet and cold shins resembling joint-running, which should be distinguished only by fever. Food and drink damage the yin, but each flavor goes to and attacks what it favors. Sour is the flavor of the liver; excessive sour damages the sinews. The sinews bind the bones and make the joints work smoothly; when damaged they slacken and do not contract, and the liver qi does not gather; hence the name "draining." Salty is the flavor of the kidney; excessive salty damages the kidney. The kidney makes the hair flourish and fills the bones; when damaged, the marrow is exhausted and the essence vacuous, and the kidney qi wilts and tires; hence the name "withering." Liver and kidney are the root of a person. When the kidney does not flourish and the liver does not gather, the root dissolves and the source is cut off; hence "severing drain."

When food and drink damage the yin, the nutritive receives it first, so the nutritive qi does not flow. The nutritive and defensive depend on each other; when the nutritive is damaged, the defensive cannot function alone. As it drags on, nutritive and defensive both become faint. The triple burner commands the internal qi and fills and penetrates the limbs, but having lost the nourishment of nutritive and defensive, it has nothing to rely on to command. To command is to hold together. The qi of the four members is not held together and is cut off. The four members are the limbs. The original qi is exhausted and the body emaciated. The feet, being lowest, are not reached by yang qi, so they swell large and the shins are cold. When the qi in the nutritive is depressed, it becomes hot and yellow sweat comes out. But all this is disease of the yin aspect, not joint-running. Joint-running carries external damp evil and is heavy and painful. Only external evil necessarily produces fever; hence "if there is fever." That means there is evil in the exterior aspect too, passing from the flesh through the joints; then it is joint-running.

Discussion: Joint-running and yellow sweat are most difficult to distinguish. Seeing that Zhongjing twice says "if there is fever, it is joint-running," it seems that joint-running has fever and yellow sweat does not. But in describing yellow sweat Zhongjing also always says "the body is hot," so we know yellow sweat can also have fever; there is simply no joint-running without fever. Yellow sweat arises from entering water to bathe while sweating, and joint-running too may arise from entering water while sweating so that water damages the heart. So if yellow sweat has yellow sweat and joint-running may also have yellow sweat, we know that the sweat of joint-running is sometimes not yellow; there is simply no yellow sweat whose sweat is not yellow. Joint-running speaks of "aching limb joints" and "pain like pulling," while yellow sweat does not mention pain; so we know that pain of the limb joints belongs to joint-running alone. Yellow sweat speaks of thirst, swelling of the limbs, head, and face, "cold in the upper burner with much drool in the mouth," and "obstruction in the chest with inability to eat, gathering pain instead, and restlessness at dusk with inability to sleep," while joint-running has only swollen feet and yellow sweat; so we know all the above signs belong to yellow sweat alone. Why is this? Both yellow sweat and joint-running are dampness depressed into heat, lingering without end; but in joint-running the dampness flows into the joints, while in yellow sweat the damp evil gathers in the diaphragm. So yellow sweat has no pain of the limb joints, and joint-running has no upper-burner signs.

## Notes

A clause describing damage to sinews and bones from excessive sour and salty flavors, with declining nutritive and defensive, swollen feet, yellow sweat, and cold shins, and distinguishing it from joint-running by the presence of fever.

- Excessive sour damages the liver (sinews), so the sinews slacken ("draining"); excessive salty damages the kidney (bones), so the bones wilt ("withering"). Both together are called "severing drain."
- Xu Bin's "Discussion" examines in detail the differentiation of joint-running and yellow sweat (see the chapter on water qi).

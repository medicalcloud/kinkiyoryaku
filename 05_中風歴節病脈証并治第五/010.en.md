# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sanhuang tang (Three Yellows Decoction) from the *Qianjin*: treats wind stroke with hypertonicity of the hands and feet, aching pain in all the joints, vexing heat and a disordered heart, aversion to cold, and no desire to eat or drink for days.

Mahuang 5 *qian*; duhuo (pubescent angelica root) 4 *qian*; xixin 2 *qian*; huangqi 2 *qian*; huangqin 3 *qian*.

Of the five ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng and take warm in three doses. After one dose there is a slight sweat; after two doses, a great sweat. For heat in the heart, add dahuang 2 *fen*; for abdominal fullness, add zhishi 1 piece; for qi counterflow, add renshen 3 *qian*; for palpitations, add muli 3 *qian*; for thirst, add gualougen 3 *qian*; for pre-existing cold, add fuzi 1 piece.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is wind entering among the ying-nutrient, wei-defense, and limb joints and disturbing them for a long time, so that the pathogen then attacks the kidney. Hypertonicity of the hands and feet means yang does not move. Aching pain in all the joints means yin does not flow. Vexing heat with a disordered heart means heat gathers in the heart. Aversion to cold and no desire to eat or drink for days mean the kidney has received pathogen and cannot interact with the heart or act as the gate of the stomach. So mahuang frees the yang and opens impediment, combined with huangqi to go to the flesh and with huangqin to clear pathogenic heat; duhuo and xixin, which specifically attack the kidney pathogen, are the mainstay. The additions set out for heat in the heart, abdominal fullness, qi counterflow, palpitations, thirst, and pre-existing cold are the standard for treating pathogen that has entered inward.

[Xu Bin's formula:] mahuang 5 *fen*; duhuo 4 *fen*; xixin 2 *fen*; huangqi 2 *fen*; huangqin 3 *fen*.

... renshen 3 *fen*; for palpitations, add muli 3 *fen*; for thirst, add gualougen 3 *fen*; for pre-existing cold, [add fuzi 1 piece].

## Notes

An appended formula, Sanhuang tang from the *Qianjin fang*, for wind stroke with hypertonic hands and feet, joint pain, vexing heat, aversion to cold, and loss of appetite.

- Mahuang, duhuo, and xixin disperse wind-cold, huangqi supplements the exterior, and huangqin clears heat. Modifications according to symptoms are appended.
- The amounts are in *qian* in the base, but all other sources read *fen*; the base's *qian* appears to be a later alteration.
- The base reads 腹痛 ("abdominal pain") for 腹満, 大黄二銭 for 二分, and lacks 分 in 分温三服; these are emended because all other sources agree (see the emendation notes).

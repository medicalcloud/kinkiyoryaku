# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is deep and weak. Deep governs the bones; weak governs the sinews. Deep is the kidney; weak is the liver. When one enters water while sweating, as if the water damaged the heart, there is joint wind [pain] with yellow sweating; hence it is called joint wind (*lijie*).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that joint wind disease also arises from external pathogen, but this kind is caused by water qi. The pulse at the inch opening is deep and weak; deep and weak are yin pulses. Being deep, it is far from the flesh; hence "deep governs the bones." Within the deep, weakness appears; the sinews are near the bones and soft; hence "weak governs the sinews." The bones are governed by the kidney and the sinews by the liver. But although the disease is in the sinews and bones, liver and kidney, it is in fact due to external pathogen; hence "it is obtained from sweating." But how can external pathogen damage the sinews and bones? Water is a yin thing; hence "as when one enters water while sweating, the water damages the heart and gradually reaches them." Then dampness flows into the joints and pain runs through the joints. The external water and the heart fire depress each other, and yellow sweating comes out. But it is not comparable to the paralysis of wind stroke, so it is called "joint wind," meaning that external pathogen enters carrying dampness, contends with the yin, and passes from joint to joint causing pain.

## Notes

The start of the clauses on joint wind disease (*lijie bing*: a disease in which the joints ache one after another, often identified with rheumatoid arthritis and similar conditions).

- The deep, weak pulse indicates deficiency of liver and kidney (sinews and bones); the cause is entering water while sweating, so that water-damp invades.
- "Joint wind" is said to be named for pain "running through" the joints one after another.
- "Yellow sweating" is related to the yellow sweating disease of the later chapter on water qi.

# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is sunken and weak. Sunken governs the bones; weak governs the sinews. Sunken is the kidney; weak is the liver. When one enters water while sweating, as if the water damaged the heart, there is joint-running [pain] with yellow sweat; hence it is called joint-running (*lijie*).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that joint-running disease also arises from external evil, but this kind is caused by water qi. The pulse at the inch opening is sunken and weak; sunken and weak are yin pulses. Being sunken, it is far from the flesh; hence "sunken governs the bones." Within the sunken, weakness appears; the sinews are near the bones and soft; hence "weak governs the sinews." The bones are governed by the kidney and the sinews by the liver. But although the disease is in the sinews and bones, liver and kidney, it is in fact due to external evil; hence "it is obtained from sweating." But how can external evil damage the sinews and bones? Water is a yin thing; hence "as when one enters water while sweating, the water damages the heart and gradually reaches them." Then dampness flows into the joints and pain runs through the joints. The external water and the heart fire depress each other, and yellow sweat comes out. But it is not comparable to the paralysis of wind-strike, so it is called "joint-running," meaning that external evil enters carrying dampness, contends with the yin, and passes from joint to joint causing pain.

## Notes

The start of the clauses on joint-running disease (*lijie bing*: a disease in which the joints ache one after another, often identified with rheumatoid arthritis and similar conditions).

- The sunken, weak pulse indicates vacuity of liver and kidney (sinews and bones); the cause is entering water while sweating, so that water-damp invades.
- "Joint-running" is said to be named for pain "running through" the joints one after another.
- "Yellow sweat" is related to the yellow sweat disease of the later chapter on water qi.

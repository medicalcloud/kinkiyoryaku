# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Joint-running pain [base: 痛; other sources: 病 "disease"] with inability to bend or stretch and aching pain: Wutou tang (Aconite Main Root Decoction) governs it.

Wutou tang formula: treats leg qi with aching pain and inability to bend or stretch.

Mahuang, shaoyao, huangqi [base: 黄氐], 3 liang each; gancao; chuanwu (Sichuan aconite main root) 5 pieces (cut up, decoct in 2 sheng of honey down to 1 sheng, then take out the wutou [base: 烏豆]).

Of the five ingredients above, cut up four, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng, remove the dregs, add to the honey decoction, decoct again, and take 7 ge. If there is no effect, take it all.

Fanshi tang (Alum Decoction): treats leg qi surging into the heart.

Fanshi 2 liang.

Boil the single ingredient above in 1 dou 5 sheng of fermented rice water three to five times, and soak the feet in it; this is good.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Joint-running disease is of the moving-impediment type. Dampness is received from below and wind makes it flow and pour; so the feet may swell, there must be fever, and further there is aching pain with inability to bend or stretch. So gancao and shaoyao harmonize the yin, mahuang and huangqi free the yang qi of the flesh, and the swift-acting power of chuanwu is borrowed to move what is impeded and fixed.

[Xu Bin's formula:] mahuang 2 liang; shaoyao 3 liang; huangqi 3 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); wutou 5 pieces (cut up, decoct in 2 sheng of honey down to 1 sheng, then take out the wutou).

Fanshi gathers dampness and resolves toxin, so it is used as external treatment. But it can treat even surging into the heart. When leg qi surges into the heart, it is always because kidney water carries the leg qi to encroach upon the heart. When fanshi repels the water, the force can no longer encroach; hence it has the effect of protecting the heart.

## Notes

A clause using Wutou tang for joint-running with severe pain and inability to bend or stretch, with Fanshi tang, an external treatment for leg qi.

- Wutou tang removes cold-damp and stops pain with the strong warming power of wutou (chuanwu). It is decocted with honey to moderate the toxicity of wutou.
- 知 in "if there is no effect" (不知) means the effect becoming apparent.
- Errors in the base: most other sources read 病歴節 for the opening 痛歴節; 黄氐 is an error for 黄耆 (huangqi), and 烏豆 for 烏頭 (other sources read 烏頭). The translation follows these.
- The base gives no amount for gancao; Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*) has 3 liang, mix-fried.
- The indication after the Wutou tang heading ("treats leg qi ...") and Fanshi tang are often considered later additions.

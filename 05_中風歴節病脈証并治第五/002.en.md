# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is floating and tight. Tight means cold; floating means vacuity. Cold and vacuity contend, and the evil is in the skin. Floating means blood vacuity: the network vessels are empty, and the thief evil is not drained, [lodging] either on the left or on the right. [On the affected side] the evil qi makes it slack instead, while the right qi [on the other side] is tense; the right qi pulls on the evil [side], and the mouth and eyes are distorted and cannot move as they should. When the evil is in the network vessels, the flesh and skin are numb. When the evil is in the channels, there is heaviness that cannot be borne. When the evil enters the bowels, the patient does not recognize people. When the evil enters the viscera, the tongue has difficulty speaking and the mouth drools.

Houshi Hei san (Marquis Hou's Black Powder): treats great wind, with vexing heaviness of the limbs and aversion to cold and insufficiency in the heart (the *Waitai* says it treats wind epilepsy).

Juhua (chrysanthemum) 40 *fen*; baizhu 10 *fen*; xixin 3 *fen*; fuling (poria) 3 *fen*; muli 3 *fen*; jiegeng (platycodon) 8 *fen*; fangfeng (saposhnikovia) 10 *fen*; renshen 3 *fen*; fanshi (alum) 3 *fen*; huangqin 5 *fen*; danggui 3 *fen*; ganjiang 3 *fen*; xiongqiong (Sichuan lovage) 3 *fen*; guizhi 3 *fen*.

Pound the fourteen ingredients above into a powder and take a square-inch spoonful with wine, once a day. For the first twenty days, take it mixed with warm wine. Avoid all fish, meat, and garlic, and always eat cold food; stop after sixty days. Then the medicine accumulates in the abdomen and does not go down. Hot food makes it go down at once; cold food naturally helps the power of the medicine.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section centers on the word "tight," saying that in wind-strike leaning toward cold, the evil enters from outside and the signs inevitably deepen gradually. In wind-strike, when the pulse at the inch opening is floating and tight, tight is a cold pulse and floating is vacuity; so yin and yang cannot harmonize, and the pulse shows on the outside. When vacuity and cold contend, the evil binds and stagnates in the outer skin. But floating is due to blood vacuity. The network vessels are nourished by blood; when [the blood] is vacuous, the network vessels are empty and lose nourishment, and have no strength to fend off evil. So the evil is not drained and becomes exuberant in the skin. On the left or right, the side combined with the evil has much qi and is slack, while the healthy side of the right [qi] has instead little qi and is tense. One side tense and one slack, right and evil pulling on each other, the mouth and eyes are distorted and cannot move at will as in a normal person. This still belongs to disease of the network vessels near the skin.

If the evil stays in the network vessels and does not leave, it has just entered the defensive [aspect], the qi cannot move, and the skin is numb; yet it is still outside the channels. If it is in the channels, the evil has entered the nutritive vessels, and the bones within and flesh without all lose nourishment, so there is heavy fixation; yet it is still within the body's frame. When it enters the bowels, evil in the bowels must return to the stomach, for the stomach is the general director of the six bowels. Then when wind enters the stomach, the stomach heat must become exuberant, steaming the fluids and binding them into phlegm and drool; the qi congests and falls into the pathways. If there is any congestion in the branch vessel of the stomach that connects with the heart, it blocks the orifice through which the spirit-qi comes and goes, so the patient does not recognize people. Try pressing the renying pulses on both sides of the neck, as in the popular practice after Chen Tuan: the qi immediately congests and counterflows, and one fails to recognize people. Renying is the stomach pulse. So can one doubt that the reason for not recognizing people is congestion of stomach qi? When it enters the viscera, all the viscera receive the evil, and when it is exuberant it must burst into the heart and disorder the spirit light. When the spirit light has no master, the tongue slackens and speech is difficult, and the Ridge Spring opens so that drool and foam flow.

This is the standard method for treating those with wind-strike carrying cold that has not yet turned to heat. Wind enters from outside, carrying cold to gain force; this is the great-wind pattern, with vexing heaviness of the limbs. Is it not that the limbs, the root of all yang, are impeded by the evil so that the yang qi does not move? But since it appears only in the limbs, is it not better than unbearable heaviness of the body? The signs also include aversion to cold and insufficiency in the heart; is it not gradually about to encroach on the heart? But dry heat has not yet overwhelmed the heart; is it not better than failing to recognize people?

So Houshi Hei san uses renshen, fuling, danggui, and chuanxiong to supplement qi and blood as the sovereign; juhua, baizhu, and muli to nourish liver, spleen, and kidney as the ministers; adds fangfeng and guizhi to move the impeded, fixed qi, xixin and ganjiang to drive out the hidden cold within, and jiegeng and huangqin to open and lift lung heat, as the assistants; and fanshi, which wherever it goes repels dampness, resolves toxin, and astringes the heart qi, and the power of wine, which circulates through the whole body, as the envoys. Then the old wind will all come out and new wind will not be received. Moreover it must be made into a powder, taken with wine and stopped after sixty days, and cold food always eaten so that the medicine accumulates in the abdomen and does not go down. Since the evil gradually invades the heart and there is aversion not to heat but to cold, we know it is due to yin cold. If the yang in the chest is not treated, the wind will never come out. So first the medicine fills the empty orifices of the chest and strengthens the central qi; then the evil does not enter inward and must disperse outward. This is the principle of the *Neijing*'s "blocking its empty orifices is [the work of] a good practitioner." If one treated only the wind evil of exterior and interior, it would come out, but with the heavy gates wide open it would come out and enter again, and become impossible to stop.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] fuling 3 *fen*; danggui 3 *fen*; chuanxiong 3 *fen*; muli 3 *fen*.

## Notes

A clause describing the wind-strike pulse and the stages of symptoms according to the depth of the evil (network vessels, channels, bowels, viscera), with Houshi Hei san.

- A floating, tight pulse is taken as cold evil entering where blood is vacuous and the network vessels empty. The affected side is slack and the healthy side tense, so the face is distorted.
- The staging by depth — network vessels (numbness), channels (heaviness), bowels (impaired consciousness), viscera (speech disorder, drooling) — became the basis of the later classification of wind-strike (striking the network vessels, channels, bowels, viscera).
- Houshi Hei san has a distinctive administration: eating cold food so that the medicine stays in the abdomen. Some regard this formula as a later addition.
- In the base the paragraph breaks in the middle of the indication, so that 者 begins the next paragraph.
- The base reads 酒服方寸一; it is emended to 匕 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- "Chen Tuan" in Xu Bin's commentary is a Daoist of the early Song; the reference is probably to a massage technique attributed to him, but this has not been verified.

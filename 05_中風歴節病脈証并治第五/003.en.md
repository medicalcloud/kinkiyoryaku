# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is slow and slowdown. Slow means cold; moderate means deficiency. Moderate in the ying-nutrient means blood loss; moderate in the wei-defense means wind stroke. When pathogenic qi strikes the meridians, the body itches and there are dormant papules (hives). When heart qi is insufficient and pathogenic qi enters the center, there is fullness in the chest and shortness of breath.

Fengyin tang (Wind-Drawing Decoction): removes heat, paralysis, and epilepsy.

Dahuang, ganjiang, longgu, 4 liang each; guizhi 3 liang; gancao and muli, 2 liang each; hanshuishi (glauberite), huashi, chishizhi (red halloysite), baishizhi (white halloysite), zishiying (fluorite), and shigao, 6 liang each.

Pound the twelve ingredients above, sieve coarsely, and store in a leather bag. Take a three-finger pinch, boil it in 3 sheng of well-flower water (the first water drawn at dawn) to three boilings, and take 1 sheng warm (a heat-removing formula that treats wind-drawing in adults, and fright epilepsy and convulsions in children occurring dozens of times a day, which physicians cannot cure. Master Chao says: for leg qi Fengyin tang is appropriate).

Fangji Dihuang tang (Stephania and Rehmannia Decoction): treats disease with a mania-like condition, wandering about wildly, talking to oneself without stopping, no cold or heat, and a floating pulse.

Fangji 1 *qian*; guizhi 3 *qian*; fangfeng 3 *qian*; gancao 1 *qian*.

Soak the four ingredients above in a cup of wine overnight and wring out the juice. Cut up 2 jin of fresh dihuang and steam it as long as it takes to steam a dou of rice, catching its juice in a copper vessel. Then wring out the dihuang juice, combine [with the first juice], and take in two doses.

Toufeng Mo san (Head-Wind Rubbing Powder) formula: one large fuzi (blast-fried); salt, an equal amount.

Make the two ingredients above into a powder. After washing [the head] (base: 沭), take a square-inch spoonful and rub it on the affected area, letting the medicine's power move.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section centers on the word "moderate," speaking of wind stroke leaning toward wind, with differences in depth. A slow pulse at the inch opening carries slight cold. Slowdown is originally a wind pulse; seen together with slow, it is wind deficiency. Then moderate in the ying-nutrient means the blood does not fill and is lost; moderate in the wei-defense means the qi is bound by wind and does not beat. Since the pathogen belongs to wind, when it strikes the meridians the body itches and there are dormant papules. This is what the chapter on water qi means by "When wind is strong, there are dormant papules and the body itches; itching is draining wind." Insufficient heart qi is what the chapter on wind and cold of the five zang organs means by "One whose heart is damaged is fatigued." Entering the center with fullness in the chest and shortness of breath is what the chapter on chest bi-impediment means by "In chest bi-impediment the qi in the chest is blocked and the breath is short."

[Part of Xu Bin's formula for Fengyin tang:] Pound the twelve ingredients above, sieve coarsely, store in a reed bag, take a three-finger pinch, with 3 [sheng] of well-flower water ...

When pathogenic wind combines within, fire-heat arises within, the five zang organs become hyperactive, and it bursts into the heart. So guizhi, gancao, longgu, and muli free the yang qi and [connect] heart and kidney as the sovereign. But the Jueyin wind-wood and Shaoyang ministerial fire reside together; when fire breaks out, wind must arise; when wind arises, it must carry the force of wood to insult spleen earth. So the spleen qi does not move, fluids gather into phlegm and pour into the extremities, causing paralysis. So dahuang is used to flush out the pathogens of wind, fire, dampness, and heat as the minister, followed by ganjiang, which stays and does not move, to supplement as the paradoxical assistant. Further, huashi and shigao clear metal to cut down wood; chishizhi and baishizhi thicken earth to remove dampness; hanshuishi aids the yin of kidney water; and zishiying supplements the deficiency of the heart spirit; these are the envoys. So it governs wind-drawing and fright epilepsy in both adults and children. Master Chao used it to treat leg qi because the nature of stones reaches downward and can overcome damp-heat so that it does not attack the heart.

Discussion: Hejian (Liu Wansu) said, "Wind disease is mostly due to intense heat, not to being struck by wind from outside. Truly, through improper care of life, heart fire suddenly becomes intense, kidney water becomes deficient and weak and cannot restrain it; then yin is deficient and yang excess, heat qi is pent up, the heart spirit is clouded, the sinews and bones do not function, and one suddenly collapses without awareness. Mostly it is due to excess of the five emotions — joy, anger, sorrow, worry, and fear." This is a most accurate discussion, but saying "there is no external wind at all" cannot avoid being one-sided. He did not know that heat can generate wind and wind can also generate heat. So Zhongjing said, "A feeble, rapid pulse is caused by wind stroke"; this leans toward being struck by external wind. He also takes "the pulse at the inch opening floating and tight" as wind stroke, which is in fact wind-cold gradually deepening in the skin and meridians. He also takes "the pulse at the inch opening slow and slowdown" as a wind stroke pulse, but further distinguishes: "slowdown in the ying-nutrient means blood loss," meaning the body's own qi is first ill and external wind takes advantage of it; "moderate in the wei-defense means wind stroke," meaning it is so because the wind is strong, and gradually enters inward. Immediately below he gives the formula of Fengyin tang, summed up in the words "removes heat," while the formula name centers entirely on wind; wind is a yang pathogen, hence heat. So we know that what comes from blood loss is heat generating wind, aided by external pathogen, and what comes from wind stroke is wind generating heat, stagnating the fluids so that phlegm and drool congest the diaphragm. This means Zhongjing long ago drew the first threads of Hejian's heat-centered theory. That he does not center solely on heat means that there truly are cases in which yang is deficient and external pathogen enters, producing sudden collapse, hemiplegia, tense sinews, and convulsions. Cases such as the various wiltings arising entirely from lung heat and passing into the five zang organs to produce clouding, convulsions, oppression, and sudden loss of voice — all belonging to fire — or limbs that cannot be retracted, stiffness of the tongue root, wilted feet, and audible phlegm — all belonging to the upper part — are all damp-heat diseases, entirely unrelated to the principle that wind stroke, whether mainly deficiency, wind, or cold, is still wind stroke.

This too is wind bursting into the heart. When wind rises, the qi surges; when the qi surges, drool stagnates; when drool stagnates, dampness lingers; when dampness lingers and fire is congested, the pathogen gathers in the heart. So fangji, fangfeng, guizhi, and gancao remove the pathogen, and fresh dihuang in the largest amount clears heart fire and cools blood heat. Mania-like behavior, wandering about wildly, and talking to oneself without stopping are all signs of blazing heart fire. Moreover, with no cold or heat, we know the disease is not in the exterior or interior. That it is in the exterior with a floating pulse is undoubtedly exuberant fire with blood deficiency. Later formulas such as Dihuang Yinzi and Xijiao Dihuang tang in fact descend from this. Head wind is a one-sided, fixed disease, so fuzi is used to force it out and salt to clear the pathogen.

[Xu Bin's formula:] fangji 1 *fen*; gancao 1 *fen*; guizhi 3 *fen*; fangfeng 3 *fen*.

Make the two ingredients above into a powder; after washing [the head], take a square-inch spoonful and rub it on the affected area, letting the medicine's power move.

## Notes

Describes wind stroke with a slow, slowdown pulse (leaning toward wind) and three formulas: Fengyin tang, Fangji Dihuang tang, and Toufeng Mo san.

- Moderate in the ying-nutrient is blood loss, moderate in the wei-defense is wind stroke; pathogen in the meridians produces hives, and when heart qi is insufficient and pathogen enters, there are chest fullness and shortness of breath.
- Fengyin tang contains many minerals and is said to treat paralysis and convulsions due to heat.
- Fangji Dihuang tang uses a large amount of fresh dihuang and is said to treat mania-like states due to heat invading the heart.
- Toufeng Mo san is an external method of rubbing a powder of fuzi and salt into the head.
- These formulas have long been suspected to be later additions (the Song editors' notes also mention the *Waitai* and Master Chao's *Zhubing yuanhou lun*).
- Errors in the base: for 沭了, all other sources read 沐了 ("after washing"); for 後 in 日数十後, all other sources read 発 ("attacks"). 防巳 is 防已.
- The amounts of Fangji Dihuang tang are in *qian* in the base and in *fen* in Daizhige and Xu Bin's edition.
- Between his explanations of Fengyin tang and Fangji Dihuang tang, Xu Bin includes a "Discussion" criticizing Liu Wansu's (Hejian's) theory that wind stroke is due to heat.

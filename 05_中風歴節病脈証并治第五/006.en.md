# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Shaoyin pulse is floating and weak. Weak means the blood is insufficient; floating means wind. When wind and blood contend, there is pain like pulling. In a corpulent person, a hesitant, small pulse, shortness of breath, spontaneous sweating, and joint wind pain with inability to bend or stretch are all caused by drinking alcohol and being exposed to wind while sweating.

When all the limb joints ache, the body is emaciated, the legs are swollen as if about to fall off, there is dizziness and shortness of breath, and a queasy desire to vomit, Guizhi Shaoyao Zhimu tang (Keishishakuyakuchimoto; Cinnamon Twig, Peony, and Anemarrhena Decoction) governs it.

Guizhi Shaoyao Zhimu tang formula

Guizhi (keishi) 4 liang; shaoyao (shakuyaku) 3 liang; gancao (kanzo) 2 liang; mahuang (mao) 2 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) 5 liang; baizhu (byakujutsu) 5 liang; zhimu (chimo) 4 liang; fangfeng (bofu) 4 liang; fuzi (bushi) 2 liang (blast-fried).

Of the nine ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng and take 7 ge warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says generally that joint wind is due to wind-damp; whether it is in the stomach or the kidney differs, but all are caused by drinking alcohol and being exposed to wind while sweating. That is, it is joint wind disease due to wind. The Fuyang is the pulse of the spleen and stomach. Slippery is excess, so we know the grain qi is in excess. Floating is exuberant heat, so sweat comes out spontaneously. But why does the grain not move and become excessive? Has not alcohol-dampness first damaged it? Why does the stomach become hot? Has not wind bound with its dampness? The Shaoyin pulse is the left chi, governing the kidney and the yin. If weak, the yin is not strong, so we know the blood is insufficient. The kidney pulse is originally deep; floating without reason, we know it is wind. Wind and blood contend, pathogen and right struggle, so there is pain like pulling, as if tugged. But how can wind reach the Shaoyin? Has not alcohol-dampness carried the wind and taken advantage of it?

A corpulent person is a fat person. Fat people have much dampness; a hesitant, small pulse is a sign of impediment. Then the qi is bound by dampness and the breath short; wind acts as the agent and sweat comes out spontaneously; the blood is impeded by the pathogen, and there is pain with inability to bend or stretch. But though fat people certainly have much dampness, why does the pulse suddenly become hesitant and small? Has not alcohol-dampness afflicted it? Why does the pain increase and the sweating not stop? Is it not dampness carrying wind? The pulses and signs differ, but being due to wind is the same. Hence "all are caused by drinking alcohol and being exposed to wind while sweating."

This is a formula for a type of joint wind disease due to external wind-damp pathogen together with deficiency of both spleen and kidney. All the limb joints aching is dampness flowing into the joints. Hence, when the body is impeded by the pathogen, there is emaciation. Dampness is received from below, and may also pour down from above, but in general dampness tends to return downward; so the legs are swollen as if about to fall off. The kidney is deficient and carries wind, so there is dizziness. The wei-defensive qi arises from the lower jiao; when the kidney's original qi is deficient, the sanjiao has no master, and Taiyang and Yangming restrain each other, causing disease. So the stomach qi wants to move down but Taiyang pulls its qi up; Taiyang wants to move up but stomach dampness contends with it and it is impeded; hence shortness of breath and a queasy desire to vomit. Guizhi tang (Keishito) without dazao (taiso), with mahuang added, helps free the yang; baizhu and fangfeng are added to extend the spleen qi; zhimu and fuzi are added to regulate yin and yang. Wishing to restrain the cold, the depressed heat above is already severe; wishing to treat the heat, the kidney yang below is already impeded; so both are added together.

## Notes

A clause stating the cause of joint wind disease by the Shaoyin pulse and using Guizhi Shaoyao Zhimu tang. It continues from the previous unit on the anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse.

- A floating, weak Shaoyin pulse (the chi pulse, or the pulse at Taixi on the foot) indicates insufficient blood and wind. Together with the hesitant, small pulse, shortness of breath, spontaneous sweating, and joint pain of corpulent people, all are attributed to drinking and exposure to wind while sweating.
- Guizhi Shaoyao Zhimu tang is regarded as the representative formula for joint wind disease with swollen, painful joints, emaciation, dizziness, and nausea. Guizhi, mahuang, and fangfeng remove wind; baizhu and fuzi remove dampness and cold; zhimu and shaoyao clear heat and nourish yin.
- The base reads 血汗出 ("blood-sweat comes out"); it is emended to 自汗出 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- The amount of fuzi is 2 liang in the base and Daizhige, 2 pieces in Wikisource and others.
- The first section of Xu Bin's commentary breaks off at 此皆飲酒汗出当.

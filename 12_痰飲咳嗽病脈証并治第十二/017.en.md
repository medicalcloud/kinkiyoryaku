# Clause 17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shortness of breath with slight rheum should be removed through the urine. Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang governs it (the formula appears above); Shenqi wan (Kidney Qi Pill) also governs it (the formula appears under "leg qi" [base: chest qi]).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Phlegm-fire in the elderly mostly belongs to fire: yin qi is deficient and fire surges, and the clear yang in the chest is insufficient to resist, so it entangles without end; it is treated mainly with cooling and nourishing yin. Phlegm-rheum is tangible rheum dragging on without end, dampness binding into phlegm, originally a disease mainly of cold-damp. If the central qi moves vigorously, it cannot accommodate it. Hence "should be harmonized with warm medicinals," taking their warming of the center, fortifying of the spleen, transforming of qi, and moving of phlegm.

As for phlegm-rheum below the heart: below the heart is not the stomach itself but above the stomach and below the heart, belonging to the upper burner. Its qi has received the yin evil of cold-damp, which surges into the chest and ribsides, producing propping fullness. "Propping" means occupying fixedly without leaving, like glomus. The yin evil suppresses the rising yang, and the eyes see darkness, hence dizziness. Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang is precisely the so-called warm medicine: the warmth of guizhi and gancao transforms qi, the warmth of baizhu fortifies the spleen, and the neutral fuling goes downward to dissolve rheum qi. Fuling alone is large in amount, entrusted as the sovereign.

"Shortness of breath with slight rheum" is the earlier "in mild cases shortness of breath." But propping rheum, lingering rheum, and water in the heart all have shortness of breath; in all, water lodges below the heart; hence "should be removed through the urine." Phlegm-rheum does not mention shortness of breath because phlegm-rheum is large in momentum, the water running in the intestines, and not merely obstructing qi. Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang can certainly fortify the stomach and bring down water, but the power of Shenqi wan is even greater. The reason rheum lingers and does not move is that the power of earth is weak. The disease seems to belong to water prevailing, but [one should know that] earth actually relies on true water to moisten dryness and transform things; hence "taiyin damp earth." Water is the kidney. Now dihuang nourishes the true yin, shanzhuyu augments the liver, fuling and shanyao regulate the spleen, mudanpi cools the qi of liver and kidney so that the ministerial fire subsides by itself, zexie drains the bladder to free the kidney qi, guizhi transforms qi, and fuzi augments the true yang to set in motion the yang qi of the lower burner, so that the gate of the kidney is free and not congested; then the spleen qi regulates itself, and when regulated it moves vigorously. This is what the ancients meant by "when the qi of spleen and kidney flows, all three burners are at peace." So it can make the rheum leave through the urine. But regulating yin and yang and nourishing the root actually make it the main formula for vacuity detriment; driving out rheum is a side skill.

## Notes

A clause stating that shortness of breath due to slight rheum is treated by removing the rheum through urination, giving Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang and Shenqi wan.

- Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang warms the spleen yang of the middle burner to remove rheum; Shenqi wan supplements kidney yang to move water. Many say they are used respectively for cases rooted in the spleen and in the kidney.
- For 脚 in the note 方見脚気中, the base reads 胸; most other sources read 脚, which is followed in the emended text ("leg qi," referring to Master Cui's Bawei wan appended in the chapter on wind-strike and joint-running). Daizhige and Xu Bin's edition say "in miscellaneous diseases of women."
- Xu Bin's commentary also explains the clauses of units 15 and 16.

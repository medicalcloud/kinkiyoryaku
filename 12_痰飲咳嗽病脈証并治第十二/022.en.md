# Clause 22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Suspended rheum: Shizao tang (Ten Jujubes Decoction) governs it.

Shizao tang formula

Yuanhua (genkwa flower) (dry-fried); gansui; daji; equal amounts each.

Pound and sieve the three ingredients above. With 1 sheng 5 ge of water first boil ten plump large jujubes down to 9 ge, remove the dregs, and add the medicinal powder. A strong person takes one *qianbi*, a thin, weak person half a *qian*, taken at dawn. If there is no purging, add another half *qian* the next day. After a satisfying purge, nourish oneself with gruel.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

A fine pulse does not belong exclusively to rheum, but combined with slippery it is the image of water. Floating means guest water has entered from the exterior, so the pulse is not yet sunken. "Floating, fine, and slippery" means floating is not originally rheum, but floating with fine and slippery is rheum. It says "damage by rheum" rather than "there is rheum" to show it is sudden damage by external rheum, not lodged, accumulated water.

Zhongjing once said, "A wiry, rapid pulse: the cold should be purged." So we know a wiry, rapid pulse is yin within yang; hence "there is cold rheum." Since the disease already has yin within yang, when great cold or great heat comes, the disease qi changes again with the season — what Dongyuan called "recurrent disease." Recurrent disease is deep and easily confused; hence "difficult to treat in winter and summer."

A sunken pulse means there is water; hence suspended rheum. Wiry means the qi is bound, hence pain. Shizao tang governs it because gansui, bitter and cold, can drain water-damp from the channel tunnels, and is especially swift and direct; daji, bitter, acrid, and cold, can drain water-damp from the viscera and bowels and is the chief for restraining drool; yuanhua, bitter and warm, can break the nests of water-rheum; hence "to break aggregations one must use yuanhua." Dazao is used with them because daji with jujube does not damage the spleen. Suspended rheum is originally a suddenly contracted pattern, so in attacking it harshness and speed are not avoided. If it is somewhat slower, producing water qi, hurried panting, and puffy swelling, the *Sanyin fang* powders the Shizao tang medicinals and makes pills with jujube flesh to treat it; this can be called skillful adaptation.

## Notes

A clause using Shizao tang for suspended rheum. Shizao tang also appears in the lower taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun*.

- Shizao tang takes three drastic water-expelling medicinals — yuanhua, gansui, and daji — with a decoction of jujubes, which protect the stomach. It is taken on an empty stomach at dawn, and after purging one recuperates with gruel.
- Xu Bin's commentary also explains the pulse clauses of units 19 through 21.

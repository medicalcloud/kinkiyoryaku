# Clause 22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Pleural fluid retention: Shizao tang (Jussoto; Ten Jujubes Decoction) governs it.

Shizao tang formula

Yuanhua (genka; genkwa flower) (dry-fried); gansui (kanzui); daji (daigeki); equal amounts each.

Pound and sieve the three ingredients above. With 1 sheng 5 ge of water first boil ten plump large jujubes down to 9 ge, remove the dregs, and add the medicinal powder. A strong person takes one *qianbi*, a thin, weak person half a *qian*, taken at dawn. If there is no purging, add another half *qian* the next day. After a satisfying purge, nourish oneself with gruel.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

A thready pulse does not belong exclusively to retained fluid, but combined with slippery it is the image of water. Floating means guest water has entered from the exterior, so the pulse is not yet deep. "Floating, fine, and slippery" means floating is not originally retained fluid, but floating with thready and slippery is retained fluid. It says "damage by retained fluid" rather than "there is retained fluid" to show it is sudden damage by external fluid retention, not lodged, accumulated water.

Zhongjing once said, "A wiry, rapid pulse: the cold should be purged." So we know a wiry, rapid pulse is yin within yang; hence "there is cold fluid retention." Since the disease already has yin within yang, when great cold or great heat comes, the disease qi changes again with the season — what Dongyuan called "recurrent disease." Recurrent disease is deep and easily confused; hence "difficult to treat in winter and summer."

A deep pulse means there is water; hence pleural fluid retention. Wiry means the qi is bound, hence pain. Shizao tang governs it because gansui, bitter and cold, can drain water-damp from the meridian tunnels, and is especially swift and direct; daji, bitter, acrid, and cold, can drain water-damp from the zang-fu organs and is the chief for restraining drool; yuanhua, bitter and warm, can break the nests of water-fluid retention; hence "to break aggregations one must use yuanhua." Dazao (taiso) is used with them because daji with jujube does not damage the spleen. Pleural fluid retention is originally a suddenly contracted pattern, so in attacking it harshness and speed are not avoided. If it is somewhat slower, producing water qi, hurried dyspnea, and puffy swelling, the *Sanyin fang* powders the Shizao tang medicinals and makes pills with jujube flesh to treat it; this can be called skillful adaptation.

## Notes

A clause using Shizao tang for pleural fluid retention. Shizao tang also appears in the lower Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun*.

- Shizao tang takes three drastic water-expelling medicinals — yuanhua, gansui, and daji — with a decoction of jujubes, which protect the stomach. It is taken on an empty stomach at dawn, and after purging one recuperates with gruel.
- Xu Bin's commentary also explains the pulse clauses of units 19 through 21.

# Clause 30 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sudden vomiting, stuffiness below the heart, water in the diaphragm, with dizziness and palpitations: Xiao Banxia jia Fuling tang (Minor Pinellia Decoction plus Poria) governs it.

Xiao Banxia jia Fuling tang formula

Banxia 1 sheng; shengjiang half a jin; fuling 3 liang (one method: 4 liang).

Of the three ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng 5 ge and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Retching without matter is *ou*; vomiting with matter is *tu*. Sudden retching and vomiting means vomiting suddenly in one who originally had no disease; that is, a person with retained fluid happens to be touched by cold. When the pathogen is exhausted it should relax at once; that the stuffiness below the heart remains means the initial vomiting was because the stomach did not accept the pathogen — had the stomach accepted it, there would be diarrhea. So the vomiting and stuffiness are due neither to exterior pathogen outside nor to stomach damage inside, but to water in the diaphragm counterflowing. As for dizziness and palpitations, the yin pathogen cannot pour downward and covers above, so invading the eyes it causes dizziness and encroaching on the heart it causes palpitations; that the water is in the diaphragm is all the clearer. So it is treated not like stuffiness from mistaken purging, but only by adding fuling to Xiao Banxia tang to remove retained fluid and bring down counterflow.

[Note on the clause "first thirst then retching" (unit 42):] Retained fluid may be long-standing or temporary. Here "first thirst then retching": with thirst one must drink much; one who never had retching suddenly shows it after thirst, so it is undoubtedly water-fluid retention. Hence "this belongs to those with retained fluid," temporarily damaged by drink. Xiao Banxia tang is the specific formula for stopping retching; adding fuling makes the water come out through the urine. No thirst-stopping or spleen-fortifying medicinals are used because once the water is gone there is no disease; cooling it would damage yang, and drying it would damage the stomach.

[Xu Bin's formula:] banxia 1 sheng; shengjiang half a jin; fuling 3 liang (one version: 4 liang).

## Notes

A clause using Xiao Banxia jia Fuling tang for sudden vomiting, stuffiness below the heart, dizziness, and palpitations, attributed to water in the diaphragm counterflowing upward.

- Fuling is added to Xiao Banxia tang to remove water through the urine.
- The latter part of Xu Bin's commentary belongs to the clause "first thirst then retching" at the end of the chapter (unit 42) and has been placed here.

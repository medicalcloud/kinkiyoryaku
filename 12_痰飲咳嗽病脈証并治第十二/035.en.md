# Clause 35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Long-standing [base: "a person"] cough of several years: if the pulse is weak, it can be treated; if excess, large, and rapid, the patient dies. If the pulse is deficient, he must suffer from veiling dizziness, because he originally has thoracic fluid retention in the chest; treatment belongs to the category of retained fluid.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Long-standing cough of several years, thirty years. This speaks generally of long-standing cough: if the pathogenic qi is slight it can be treated; if exuberant it is difficult. That is what is called "if the cough pulse is floating and soft, the patient lives; if floating and straight, he dies." The ancients also judged by combining signs: "Cough with emaciation and a hard, large pulse: death. Cough with wasting, fever, and a small, tight, tense pulse: death. Cough with retching, abdominal distension, and diarrhea, with a wiry, tense pulse: death." One should know that hard, tense, straight, and large are all images of excess and largeness, exuberance of pathogen. But there rapidity is not mentioned; we know that what is feared in chronic cough is hardness and tension, in which case the pathogen is truly exuberant and the right deficient, and rapidity is not enough to express it. But rapid combined with excess and large implies hardness and tension; hence "death." Among them, a deficient pulse is of the soft kind, the opposite of excess. If the cough were not due to retained fluid, after so long the genuine qi of the zang organs would surely be damaged; how could one not die? Hence "if the pulse is deficient, he must suffer from veiling dizziness"; veiling dizziness is an image of retained fluid. So he adds, "he originally has thoracic fluid retention in the chest," to show the errors of earlier treatment: since the long illness is due to thoracic fluid retention, he does not die. Then, long as it is, how can one abandon the root of the disease in planning treatment? Hence "treatment belongs to the category of retained fluid," implying Shizao tang (Jussoto) is also appropriate; only, fearing extreme deficiency, it leaves it to the practitioner's judgment. But in the end treatment does not go beyond driving out retained fluid.

## Notes

A clause judging the prognosis of cough lasting several years by the pulse: a weak pulse is curable, an excess, large, rapid pulse fatal. A deficient pulse with dizziness is due to thoracic fluid retention in the chest and should be treated as retained fluid.

- For the opening 久咳 ("long-standing cough"), the base reads 人咳; all other sources read 久咳, so it has been emended (see the emendation notes).
- The base reads 必若冒; it is emended to 苦 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

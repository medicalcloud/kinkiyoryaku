# Clause 35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A person [other sources: "long-standing"] who has coughed for several years: if the pulse is weak, it can be treated; if replete, large, and rapid, the patient dies. If the pulse is vacuous, he must suffer from veiling dizziness, because he originally has propping rheum in the chest; treatment belongs to the category of rheum.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Long-standing cough of several years, thirty years. This speaks generally of long-standing cough: if the evil qi is slight it can be treated; if exuberant it is difficult. That is what is called "if the cough pulse is floating and soft, the patient lives; if floating and straight, he dies." The ancients also judged by combining signs: "Cough with emaciation and a hard, large pulse: death. Cough with wasting, fever, and a small, tight, tense pulse: death. Cough with retching, abdominal distension, and diarrhea, with a wiry, tense pulse: death." One should know that hard, tense, straight, and large are all images of repletion and largeness, exuberance of evil. But there rapidity is not mentioned; we know that what is feared in chronic cough is hardness and tension, in which case the evil is truly exuberant and the right vacuous, and rapidity is not enough to express it. But rapid combined with replete and large implies hardness and tension; hence "death." Among them, a vacuous pulse is of the soft kind, the opposite of replete. If the cough were not due to rheum, after so long the true qi of the viscera would surely be damaged; how could one not die? Hence "if the pulse is vacuous, he must suffer from veiling dizziness"; veiling dizziness is an image of rheum. So he adds, "he originally has propping rheum in the chest," to show the errors of earlier treatment: since the long illness is due to propping rheum, he does not die. Then, long as it is, how can one abandon the root of the disease in planning treatment? Hence "treatment belongs to the category of rheum," implying Shizao tang is also appropriate; only, fearing extreme vacuity, it leaves it to the practitioner's judgment. But in the end treatment does not go beyond driving out rheum.

## Notes

A clause judging the prognosis of cough lasting several years by the pulse: a weak pulse is curable, a replete, large, rapid pulse fatal. A vacuous pulse with dizziness is due to propping rheum in the chest and should be treated as rheum.

- For the opening 人咳, all other sources read 久咳 ("long-standing cough").
- The base reads 必若冒; it is emended to 苦 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

# Clause 34 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In those with propping rheum, cough with vexation and pain in the chest does not cause sudden death, lasting a hundred days or a year; Shizao tang is appropriate (the formula appears above).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

"In those with propping rheum" traces [the disease] to its origin. Propping rheum originally does not hurt, but spreading into chest impediment it hurts; the qi counterflows upward causing cough, and fire congests above causing vexation; already there is a path to death. That one does not die suddenly but lasts a hundred days or even years shows the vacuity, but fortunately the original qi is not yet exhausted. Tracing the disease, propping rheum is the root; if the root is not pulled out, there will never be recovery. Lingering without recovery is precisely because physicians shrink back on account of the vacuity. Hence "Shizao tang is appropriate," showing that in attacking disease one should not avoid harshness, nor wait leisurely for death.

## Notes

A clause stating that even propping rheum with cough, vexation, and chest pain lasting a long time should be treated by expelling water with Shizao tang.

- For 一百日一歳, most other sources read 一百日或一歳 ("a hundred days or a year").

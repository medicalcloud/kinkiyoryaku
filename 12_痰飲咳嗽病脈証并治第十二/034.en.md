# Clause 34 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In those with thoracic fluid retention, cough with vexation and pain in the chest does not cause sudden death, lasting a hundred days or a year; Shizao tang (Jussoto) is appropriate (the formula appears above).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

"In those with thoracic fluid retention" traces [the disease] to its origin. Thoracic fluid retention originally does not hurt, but spreading into chest bi-impediment it hurts; the qi counterflows upward causing cough, and fire congests above causing vexation; already there is a path to death. That one does not die suddenly but lasts a hundred days or even years shows the deficiency, but fortunately the original qi is not yet exhausted. Tracing the disease, thoracic fluid retention is the root; if the root is not pulled out, there will never be recovery. Lingering without recovery is precisely because physicians shrink back on account of the deficiency. Hence "Shizao tang is appropriate," showing that in attacking disease one should not avoid harshness, nor wait leisurely for death.

## Notes

A clause stating that even thoracic fluid retention with cough, vexation, and chest pain lasting a long time should be treated by expelling water with Shizao tang.

- For 一百日一歳, most other sources read 一百日或一歳 ("a hundred days or a year").

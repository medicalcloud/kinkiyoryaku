# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A sick person's pulse is hidden; he wants to have spontaneous diarrhea, and the diarrhea is, on the contrary, a relief; yet despite the diarrhea, the area below the heart continues to be hard and full. This is because the prolonged fluid retention is about to leave. Gansui Banxia tang (Kanzuihangeto; Kansui and Pinellia Decoction) governs it.

Gansui Banxia tang formula

Gansui (kanzui; kansui root), 3 large pieces; banxia (hange) 12 pieces (boil in 1 sheng of water down to half a sheng and remove the dregs); shaoyao (shakuyaku) 5 pieces; gancao (kanzo), one piece the size of a finger (mix-fried; one version reads "none").

Of the four ingredients above, with 2 sheng of water boil down to half a sheng, remove the dregs, mix the medicinal juice with half a sheng of honey, decoct down to 8 ge, and take in a single dose.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Zhongjing says, "When the pulse is any kind of deep, blame water," and also, "A deep pulse indicates prolonged fluid retention," and "A wiry pulse indicates pleural fluid retention." Hidden too has the sense of deep. But retained fluid with pain is chest bi-impediment, of which it says "the pulse at the inch opening is deep and slow"; so we know "pulse" here refers to the inch opening. Wanting to have spontaneous diarrhea is not due to external contraction or internal damage, nor to a medicinal error. The diarrhea being a relief means the retained fluid has lessened and the person feels refreshed. But the root of the disease has not been pulled out, and external fluid retention adds to it, so it is hard and full again; hence "continues to be hard and full." Though hard and full, what leaves leaves by itself and what continues continues by itself; its momentum has already started; hence "about to leave." Gansui can reach where the water is and remove it, and banxia dries water and also brings down counterflow qi; so they are the sovereigns, attacking it while it is about to leave. Gancao opposes gansui, yet it is added to take their power of battle and victory. Honey frees the sanjiao and regulates the spleen and stomach, and also restrains their discordant toxicity; so it is added. Diarrhea damages the spleen, so shaoyao cooperates with gancao to supplement spleen yin and secure the original qi.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula for the later Ling Gan Wuwei Jiang Xin Xia tang, etc., inserted here:] fuling (bukuryo) 4 liang; gancao 2 liang; xixin (saishin) 2 liang; ganjiang (kankyo) 2 liang; banxia half a sheng.

## Notes

A clause using Gansui Banxia tang for prolonged fluid retention with a hidden pulse, spontaneous diarrhea that brings relief, yet continued hardness and fullness below the heart.

- Gansui (a strong water-expelling medicinal) and gancao were later listed among the "eighteen incompatibilities," but here they are used together, and Xu Bin says their "power of battle and victory" is precisely what is sought.
- The amount of shaoyao, "5 pieces," is an odd unit, and opinions vary.
- The formula at the end of Xu Bin's commentary appears to be part of a later formula such as Ling Gan Wuwei Jiang Xin Xia tang that has been inserted here.

# Clause 33 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In those with chronic cough, a wiry pulse means there is water; Shizao tang governs it (the formula appears above).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The *Maijing* says, "A feeble pulse at the guan indicates cough," and also, "A feeble, tense lung pulse indicates cough with spitting of blood; a wiry pulse indicates water." Hence "in those with chronic cough, a wiry pulse means there is water." But the *Maijing* also says, "One-sidedly wiry is water; deep is prolonged fluid retention; surging and slippery is much phlegm." So we know the "wiry" here must be one-sidedly wiry, and the pulse is neither deep nor slippery. Only, if there is cough with a wiry pulse, there is water. Shizao tang: water-fluid retention is tangible, so expelling it suddenly is not objectionable.

Discussion: The clause on cough is a great juncture of deficiency detriment. Zhongjing did not set up a separate section but only appended it after phlegm-fluid retention and also scattered it in the atrophy of the lung lobes section; so we know that treating cough should center on clearing phlegm-fluid retention, though among cases there are differences of carrying cold and carrying qi.

## Notes

The clauses on cough begin here. A clause using Shizao tang when a person with chronic cough has a wiry pulse, indicating water-fluid retention.

- Xu Bin notes that cough is appended to phlegm-fluid retention in this chapter, meaning that cough should be treated mainly by removing phlegm-fluid retention.

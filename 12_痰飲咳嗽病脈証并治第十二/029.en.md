# Clause 29 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Abdominal fullness with dry mouth and tongue: there is water qi between the intestines. Ji Jiao Li Huang wan (Stephania, Zanthoxylum Seed, Lepidium, and Rhubarb Pill) governs it.

Fang[ji] Jiao[mu] Ting[li] [Da]huang wan formula

Fangji; jiaomu (zanthoxylum seed); tingli (dry-fried); dahuang; 1 liang each.

Powder the four ingredients above and make honey pills the size of firmiana seeds; take one pill with a drink before meals, three times a day, increasing gradually. If there is fluid in the mouth with thirst, add half a liang of mangxiao.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Below the central stomach duct is the abdomen; abdominal fullness naturally cannot be blamed on the upper burner. The mouth and tongue are above; if the upper burner has no disease, why are they dry? Thus we know that the abdominal fullness is a disease of the large intestine, and the dry mouth and tongue are water qi damaging the yin. The large intestine governs the fluids; when the yin is damaged, the fluids cannot reach upward, and the mouth and tongue are dry. Hence "there is water qi between the intestines." The medicinal fangji, not called "mu," is Han fangji. Between the intestines is the lower burner, governed by blood. Han fangji drains damp-heat in the blood and disinhibits the qi of the large intestine. Jiaomu is the seed of zanthoxylum; zanthoxylum by nature descends well, and its seed especially disinhibits water. Tingli discharges closure of qi and expels water; dahuang discharges closure of blood and brings down heat; so they govern it. If there is fluid in the mouth, the jueyin of the large intestine has not been damaged by rheum, so the yin fluids are not lost, but the fluids of the stomach are instead consumed by congested heat, hence thirst; the heat is in the stomach as a replete evil, so mangxiao is added to purge it urgently and rescue the stomach. Starting with one small pill is especially clever — what is called "using a drastic medicine gently."

## Notes

A clause using Ji Jiao Li Huang wan for abdominal fullness with dry mouth and tongue due to water qi between the intestines.

- Fangji, jiaomu, and tinglizi expel water and dahuang purges.
- The clause calls it Ji Jiao Li Huang wan, the heading Fang Jiao Ting Huang wan; other sources read Fangji Jiaomu Tingli Dahuang wan and the like.
- Xu Bin takes the fangji here to be Han fangji (distinct from the mufangji of the earlier Mufangji tang).

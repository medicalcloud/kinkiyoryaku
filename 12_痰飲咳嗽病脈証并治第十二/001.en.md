# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: There are four kinds of rheum; what are they? The master said: There is phlegm-rheum, suspended rheum, spillage rheum, and propping rheum.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Rheum is not phlegm but actually tangible water. Its causes differ and its locations differ, so there are the distinctions of suspended, spillage, and propping. Suspended is like something hanging in the air, suspended above the diaphragm and not descending. Spillage is like water soaking to the sides, filling and overflowing throughout the limbs and body. Propping is like fruit on a branch, off to one side and not centered; hence the *Shanghan lun* has a clause on propping bind. Phlegm-rheum is rheum mixed with drool, lingering long without leaving; some of it congeals and some does not; what congeals is phlegm and what does not is rheum.

Discussion: Later people, not understanding the meaning of the four rheums, added lingering rheum to make five rheums, not knowing that lingering rheum is phlegm-rheum. Both are below the heart and in the diaphragm. But lingering rheum lingers temporarily and leaves by itself when the original qi becomes a little fuller; phlegm-rheum stays long without leaving, and in severe cases overflows the stomach and obstructs the flesh. So phlegm-rheum that does not yet obstruct the flesh can all be called lingering rheum. It is unlike suspended rheum, whose water counterflows above suddenly and irresistibly; unlike spillage rheum, which overflows through the whole body, diffuse and impossible to purge; unlike propping rheum, which binds one-sidedly at the junction of the network vessels of lung and large intestine, obstructing qi so that yangming counterflows and cannot follow its path and one cannot lie down. It and phlegm-rheum have the same cause and location, differing only in duration. Since phlegm-rheum is already listed as one of the four rheums, why list lingering rheum separately and add to confusion in identifying patterns?

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on phlegm-rheum and cough. Rheum (water stagnating in the body) is divided into four kinds: phlegm-rheum, suspended rheum, spillage rheum, and propping rheum.

- "Phlegm-rheum" is also the general name of this chapter; there is phlegm-rheum in the narrow sense (water in the stomach and intestines) and in the broad sense (all four rheums).
- Xu Bin criticizes the later theory of "five rheums" (adding lingering rheum), holding that lingering rheum is merely a temporary form of phlegm-rheum.

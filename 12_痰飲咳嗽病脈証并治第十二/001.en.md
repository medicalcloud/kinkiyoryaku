# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: There are four kinds of retained fluid; what are they? The master said: There is phlegm-fluid retention, pleural fluid retention, subcutaneous fluid retention, and thoracic fluid retention.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Retained fluid is not phlegm but actually tangible water. Its causes differ and its locations differ, so there are the distinctions of suspended, spillage, and propping. Suspended is like something hanging in the air, suspended above the diaphragm and not descending. Spillage is like water soaking to the sides, filling and overflowing throughout the limbs and body. Propping is like fruit on a branch, off to one side and not centered; hence the *Shanghan lun* has a clause on propping bind. Phlegm-fluid retention is retained fluid mixed with drool, lingering long without leaving; some of it congeals and some does not; what congeals is phlegm and what does not is retained fluid.

Discussion: Later people, not understanding the meaning of the four rheums, added prolonged fluid retention to make five rheums, not knowing that prolonged fluid retention is phlegm-fluid retention. Both are below the heart and in the diaphragm. But prolonged fluid retention lingers temporarily and leaves by itself when the original qi becomes a little fuller; phlegm-fluid retention stays long without leaving, and in severe cases overflows the stomach and obstructs the flesh. So phlegm-fluid retention that does not yet obstruct the flesh can all be called prolonged fluid retention. It is unlike pleural fluid retention, whose water counterflows above suddenly and irresistibly; unlike subcutaneous fluid retention, which overflows through the whole body, diffuse and impossible to purge; unlike thoracic fluid retention, which binds one-sidedly at the junction of the collaterals of lung and large intestine, obstructing qi so that Yangming counterflows and cannot follow its path and one cannot lie down. It and phlegm-fluid retention have the same cause and location, differing only in duration. Since phlegm-fluid retention is already listed as one of the four rheums, why list prolonged fluid retention separately and add to confusion in identifying patterns?

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on phlegm-fluid retention and cough. Retained fluid (water stagnating in the body) is divided into four kinds: phlegm-fluid retention, pleural fluid retention, subcutaneous fluid retention, and thoracic fluid retention.

- "Phlegm-fluid retention" is also the general name of this chapter; there is phlegm-fluid retention in the narrow sense (water in the stomach and intestines) and in the broad sense (all four rheums).
- Xu Bin criticizes the later theory of "five rheums" (adding prolonged fluid retention), holding that prolonged fluid retention is merely a temporary form of phlegm-fluid retention.

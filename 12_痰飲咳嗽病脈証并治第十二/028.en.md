# Clause 28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Those who retch frequently are originally thirsty; thirst means it is about to resolve. Now, on the contrary, there is no thirst; this is because there is propping rheum below the heart. Xiao Banxia tang (Minor Pinellia Decoction) governs it (the *Qianjin* says: Xiao Banxia tang plus fuling).

Xiao Banxia tang formula

Banxia 1 sheng; shengjiang half a jin.

Of the two ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil down to 1.5 sheng and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Retching is a disease of the stomach, not an essential sign of propping rheum; but it can verify that there is propping rheum below the heart. "Those who retch frequently are originally thirsty" means all retching belongs to fire, and much retching loses fluids, so thirst is the normal principle. In those who retch frequently, cold is always the root and fire the branch; when retching reaches thirst, the cold evil has gone; hence "thirst means it is about to resolve." No thirst, on the contrary, means that although the guest evil in the stomach can be exhausted, the water-rheum off to the side always remains, and the rheum qi can restrain dryness; hence "there must be water-rheum." But since the rheum is located to the side and not centered, it is called propping rheum; if it were in the center, it would come out with the retching. Banxia and shengjiang stop retching, remove counterflow, dry dampness, and bring down rheum, so they govern it. It is called Xiao Banxia tang because there is also Da Banxia tang of three ingredients — renshen, banxia, and honey — so the word "minor" distinguishes it.

## Notes

A clause attributing absence of thirst despite continued vomiting to propping rheum below the heart, and using Xiao Banxia tang.

- Xiao Banxia tang, of two ingredients, banxia and shengjiang, is regarded as the basic formula for stopping vomiting.

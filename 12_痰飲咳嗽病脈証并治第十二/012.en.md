# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a sick person drinks much water, there will surely be sudden panting and fullness. Whenever one eats little and drinks much, water lodges below the heart; in severe cases there are palpitations, in mild cases shortness of breath. A pulse wiry on both sides means cold; all are [prone to] vacuity after heavy purging. A pulse wiry on one side means rheum.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The two clauses on drinking much water are of the suspended-rheum type but do not become suspended rheum; unless the water lodges and accumulates in the ribsides with pulling pain, it cannot be called suspended. Yet when a sick person drinks much water, there will surely be panting and fullness: the water counterflows. "Sudden" means the momentum is abrupt, at the border of about to be suspended but not yet. When eating little and drinking much leads to palpitations and shortness of breath, it is the beginning of true phlegm-rheum. Saying "whenever" makes known that people whose central qi is not strong and whose qi is congested producing thirst must generally guard against this. It wishes people to know that rheum comes not only from fluids gathering into drool but from water actually entering from outside. When abundant, it encroaches on the heart, causing palpitations, because water is the enemy of fire. When mild, there is shortness of breath: heart qi is yang, water is yin, and yang is suppressed by yin. "Wiry on both sides" means both hands are wiry; with cold the defensive qi binds. But although the above is rheum and cold, it would not come to this without vacuity of the original qi; so it further notes the cause: "all after heavy purging, earth vacuous." If wiry on one side, it is undoubtedly rheum: the positions in front of the bar all govern the central qi, and if some are wiry and some not, clearly the rheum is one-sided and the pulse too is one-sided.

Discussion: There is also a pulse with two strands in one hand, also called double wiry. This pulse is often seen in people whose original qi is not robust, and it too belongs to the vacuous side. I generally warm and supplement the central qi while transforming phlegm, and recovery follows at once.

## Notes

A clause describing panting and fullness from drinking much water, and water lodging below the heart with palpitations or shortness of breath from eating little and drinking much, and distinguishing a pulse wiry on both sides (cold) from one wiry on one side (rheum).

- In the base there is a space between 皆大下後 and 虚. Most other sources read 善虚 ("prone to vacuity after heavy purging"); Xu Bin's edition notes the variant 喜虚. Xu Bin interprets it as "earth vacuous."
- The base writes 䨇 for 双; it is emended because all other sources read 双 (see the emendation note).

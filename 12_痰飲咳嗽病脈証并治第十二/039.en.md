# Clause 39 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If the cough and fullness stop at once but thirst comes again and the surging qi breaks out again, it is because xixin and ganjiang are hot medicinals. Taking them should cause continued thirst; if instead the thirst stops, it is thoracic fluid retention. With thoracic fluid retention there should by rule be veiling dizziness; with veiling dizziness there must be retching; for retching, add banxia again to remove the water.

Gui Ling Wei Gan minus Gui [plus] Jiang Xin Xia tang formula

Fuling 4 liang; gancao, xixin, and ganjiang, 2 liang each; wuwei[zi] and banxia, half a sheng each.

Of the six ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take half a sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Cold dissolves with heat, so the cough and fullness stop at once. But heat consumes the fluids, and when fluids are consumed there is thirst. Heat damages the original qi; when the original qi is damaged, yin insults yang, so the surging qi breaks out again. Hence "because xixin and ganjiang are hot medicinals." Thus the fluids are consumed and the stomach dried, and there should be continued thirst — "continued" meaning not stopping. Now it stops when it should not; hence "instead." Clearly there was thoracic fluid retention all along, so the fire does not overcome the water. But thoracic fluid retention must have definite evidence; hence "with thoracic fluid retention there should by rule be veiling dizziness; with veiling dizziness there must be retching." Retching is because there is water, so banxia is added again to remove it. It is also surging qi, yet gui is not used here because, with veiling dizziness and retching, driving out retained fluid is emphasized with banxia as the mainstay, and gui is not urgent.

Discussion: This too is surging qi; why was it treated so carefully and exclusively earlier? Earlier the guest cold of the lung had not gone, the medicine was harsh, the pathogenic cold took advantage of the kidney, pressed the true yang to float out, and it ran wild above and below unable to return; so the most yang gui had to enter the yin to subdue it. Here its recurrence is because the lung is damaged by heat and the original qi cannot resist the yin, and moreover thoracic fluid retention supports it, so they surge against each other. But without signs such as facial heat, it is not comparable to true yang floating up. So simply removing the water stops the surging by itself; that is, when the water is gone, lung and kidney regulate themselves.

## Notes

A clause stating that when Ling Gan Wuwei Jiang Xin tang has stopped the cough and fullness but thirst and surging qi return, this is due to the heat of ganjiang and xixin; but if the thirst instead stops, there is thoracic fluid retention, and with dizziness and vomiting banxia is added.

- 四 in the heading 去桂四薑辛夏湯 is read 加 ("plus") in most other sources and appears to be an error.
- The base reads 当遂満; it is emended to 当遂渇 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

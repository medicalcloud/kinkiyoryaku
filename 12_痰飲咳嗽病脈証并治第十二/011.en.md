# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Disease of phlegm above the diaphragm with fullness, dyspnea, coughing, and vomiting; when it breaks out there are cold and heat, back pain and lumbar ache, and spontaneous tearing, and the person shivers and the body twitches severely: there must be deep-lying fluid retention.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

When there is prolonged fluid retention in the diaphragm, gathered dampness produces phlegm and fullness, and shooting into the lung produces dyspnea and cough; this is its usual course. When, at irregular times, vomiting brings an outbreak with cold and heat, back pain, lumbar ache, spontaneous tearing, and severe shivering and twitching of the body, it means that through vomiting all the diseases break out together. Cold and heat, back pain, and lumbar ache are all exterior signs of Taiyang. Tearing: wind qi enters together with Yangming; in a thin person it drains outward, and with cold it becomes cold-strike and tears come out. Severe shivering and twitching mean the ying-nutrient qi has been made deficient by phlegm, both exterior and interior are insufficient, and the body cannot control itself; twitching is movement of the flesh; "severe" means the changed signs are mixed and various. But since it breaks out only after vomiting, we know that without vomiting it does not break out; there is something hidden that forms the root of the disease. Hence "there must be deep-lying fluid retention." That is, at first it too has phlegm fullness, dyspnea, and cough, no different from thoracic fluid retention; only because it does not break out at once do we know its location is somewhat secluded, hence "hidden."

Discussion: Among the four rheums, suspended and subcutaneous fluid retention are contracted suddenly and break out suddenly, not lingering patterns hard to distinguish. Only phlegm-fluid retention and thoracic fluid retention drag on without end. So is deep-lying fluid retention not the form of these two rheums that does not break out at once? But it is called hidden rather than lingering, and the meaning differs. Deep phlegm-fluid retention enters the stomach, shallow lingers in the chest; it always interferes with the central qi but does not reach the exterior qi. Thoracic fluid retention intrudes into a side place, neither interfering with the exterior qi nor obstructing the central qi. Only deep-lying fluid retention ordinarily produces phlegm fullness, dyspnea, and cough, and on vomiting the exterior signs all break out. We know that deep-lying fluid retention is an excess pathogen located high near the back, communicating inward with the central qi and connecting outward with the exterior qi, so that when the pathogen stirs, a great host rises together, like an ambush. This should be treated by treating exterior and interior together, as with Xiao Qinglong tang (Shoseiryuto) and Mufangji tang (Mokuboito) minus shigao (sekko) plus mangxiao (bosho) and fuling (bukuryo); it cannot be removed through urination.

## Notes

A clause calling "deep-lying fluid retention" the condition of phlegm above the diaphragm with chest fullness, dyspnea, cough, and vomiting, which in attacks produces cold and heat, back and lumbar pain, tearing, and shivering.

- Deep-lying fluid retention is retained fluid that is ordinarily latent and worsens in attacks when triggered. It is sometimes related to asthma attacks.

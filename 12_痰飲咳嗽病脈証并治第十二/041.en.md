# Clause 41 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If the face is hot as if drunk, this is stomach heat surging up and fuming the face; add dahuang (daio) to disinhibit it.

Ling [base: 茯] Gan [Wuwei jia] Jiang Xin [Ban] [Xing] [Da]huang tang

Fuling (bukuryo) 4 liang; gancao (kanzo) 3 liang; wuwei[zi] half a sheng; ganjiang (kankyo) 3 liang; xixin (saishin) 3 liang; banxia (hange) half a sheng; xingren (kyonin) half a sheng; dahuang 3 liang.

Of the eight ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take half a sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The face belongs to Yangming. When the stomach qi is exuberant, the face is hot as if drunk; this is the heat of the stomach qi fuming upward. Being as if drunk without wine, its heat is irresistible; so dahuang is added to disinhibit it. Though the heat of ganjiang and xixin is present, each performs its own function without interference.

Discussion: Since it was said earlier, "because xixin and ganjiang are hot medicinals," why does this formula only add banxia without removing ganjiang and xixin, and when the body swells still not remove them, and when the face is hot still not remove them? Those with thoracic fluid retention and long-standing thirst have had the ancestral qi in the chest eroded by water-cold for a long time, so cough and fullness arise very easily. When cough and fullness come and ganjiang and xixin are relied on to discharge the fullness and stop the cough, they naturally cannot be lacking; that is, as long as the retained fluid qi has not left, one must guard against the invasion of lung cold at every moment. The face hot as if drunk differs from the "flushed with heat as if drunk" of the first clause. There the disease broke out below due to surging qi; here it is merely the lung qi not flowing freely, stagnating outside as swelling and inside as stomach heat; so only xingren is used to disinhibit the qi in the chest, and dahuang to disinhibit the heat of stomach yin.

## Notes

A clause adding dahuang to the previous formula when the face is red and hot as if drunk, attributed to stomach heat surging upward. It is the last formula of the series of modifications beginning with Xiao Qinglong tang (Shoseiryuto).

- The base names the formula Fu Gan Jiang Wei Xin Xia Ren Huang tang; the other sources read Ling Gan Wuwei jia Jiang Xin Ban Xing Dahuang tang.
- The base reads 冒熱; it is emended to 胃熱 ("stomach heat") because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- Xu Bin distinguishes this facial heat from the facial heat due to true yang floating up in unit 37.

# Clause 38 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If the surging qi has come down at once, but on the contrary the cough increases with fullness of the chest, use Gui Ling Wuwei Gancao tang minus gui, plus ganjiang and xixin, to treat the cough and fullness.

Ling Gan Wuwei Jiang Xin tang (Poria, Licorice, Schisandra, Dried Ginger, and Asarum Decoction) formula

Fuling 4 liang; gancao, ganjiang, and xixin, 3 liang each; wuwei[zi] half a sheng.

Of the five ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take half a sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The surging qi coming down at once is because the power of gui and fuling went straight in like a single blade, and the kidney evil submitted, so it came down. The cough and fullness increasing on the contrary clearly means the cold hidden in the lung has not yet gone. But gui was already used in Qinglong tang and again in Gui Ling Wuwei Gancao tang; gui used twice without the evil submitting is because gui can remove congealed cold in the yang aspect but cannot drive out cold sunk and hidden within the viscera. So following the rule that gui cannot be used again, gui is removed, and only xixin, acrid and hot, entering the yin, and ganjiang, acrid and hot, pure yang, are taken to discharge the fullness, drive out the cold, and stop the cough.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] fuling 4 liang; wuweizi half a sheng; gancao 3 liang; ganjiang 3 liang.

## Notes

A clause using Ling Gan Wuwei Jiang Xin tang — removing guizhi and adding ganjiang and xixin — when Ling Gui Wei Gan tang has settled the surging qi but cough and chest fullness have worsened.

- Ganjiang and xixin warm and disperse the cold rheum remaining in the lung.
- The base reads 加生薑 ("add fresh ginger"); it is emended to 乾薑 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

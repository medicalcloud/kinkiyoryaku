# Clause 31 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Suppose a thin person has palpitations below the umbilicus, spits foamy drool, and [is dizzy]: this is water. Wuling san (Five Ingredients Powder with Poria) governs it.

Wuling san formula

Zexie 1 liang 1 *fen*; zhuling 3 *fen* (remove the skin); fuling 3 *fen*; baizhu 3 *fen*; gui 2 *fen* (remove the bark).

Powder the five ingredients above and take a square-inch spoonful with plain rice water, three times a day. Drink plenty of warm water; when sweat comes out, recovery follows.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In a thin person, the abdomen originally has little dampness. Yet there are palpitations below the umbilicus — palpitation being slight movement. Such palpitation occurs only in those about to develop running piglet after sweating in cold damage, or in those whose heart qi is damaged, who on fatigue have fever and throbbing at the umbilicus. Now there is no accumulated dampness inside, no sunken exterior outside, and no prior damage to the heart qi, yet suddenly there are palpitations below the umbilicus. In principle, water in the upper burner should not show signs at the umbilicus. Yet above there is still spitting of foamy drool, and even severe dizziness. Clearly there is water in the middle, so above it produces drool and dizziness, and below palpitations below the umbilicus. The heart is pressed by water, and the kidney takes advantage of the heart's vacuity to encroach on it; hence "this is water." So gui and fuling subdue the kidney evil, and zhuling, zexie, and baizhu drain water and fortify the stomach. Compared with Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang for phlegm-rheum, gancao is removed and zhuling and zexie added; that one emphasizes harmonizing the stomach with warm medicinals, this one is urgent to remove water. Moreover, "drink warm water; when sweat comes out, recovery follows" disperses the water both inside and out.

## Notes

A clause attributing palpitations below the umbilicus, spitting of foamy drool, and dizziness in a thin person to water, and using Wuling san.

- Wuling san, also found in the *Shanghan lun*, removes water through urination and sweating.
- In the base there is a blank after 吐涎沫而; all other sources have 癲眩 ("dizziness"; Xu Bin's edition 顚眩), which the translation supplies.
- For 桂 in the base formula, most other sources read 桂枝.

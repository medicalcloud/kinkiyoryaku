# Clause 37 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

After Qinglong tang has gone down [base: 巳], if there is much spitting, a dry mouth, a sunken pulse at the inch, a faint pulse at the cubit, reverse-flow of the hands and feet, qi surging from the lower abdomen up to the chest and throat, numbness of the hands and feet, the face flushed with heat as if drunk, which then flows back down to the inner thighs, difficult urination, and at times veiling dizziness again, give Fuling Guizhi Wuwei Gancao tang (Poria, Cinnamon Twig, Schisandra, and Licorice Decoction) to treat the surging qi.

Gui Ling Wuwei Gancao tang formula

Fuling 4 liang; guizhi 4 liang (remove the bark); gancao (mix-fried) 3 liang; wuweizi half a sheng.

Of the four ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In the earlier "cough with counterflow and leaning to breathe," it is clear that rheum evil invades the lung. If the person's lower part were replete and not vacuous, the rheum would go and the disease be removed. But if there is much vacuity and the right qi is not enough to resist the evil, then on taking the medicine, before the upper rheum can leave, the lower part cannot first bear the dispersal and its surging qi stirs; so the lung becomes dry as in atrophy and there is much spitting — spittle meaning the phlegm is thin like saliva. Also the mouth is dry: dryness means feeling the mouth parched, not thirst. A sunken inch pulse means the water has not yet gone. A faint cubit pulse means the lower origin has suddenly become vacuous; with vacuity, cold qi combines below, and the hands and feet have reverse-flow. Then the kidney evil takes advantage of the heart, and qi surges from the lower abdomen to the chest and throat — from abdomen to chest and from chest to throat, the height of rising. Numbness of the hands and feet means not merely reversal but outright loss of function. "The face flushed with heat as if drunk" is what is called "the face as if made up with cinnabar," true yang floating upward. But since it has not reached desertion, the yang flows back down to the inner thighs, meaning the yang that floated to the face turns back and makes heat qi in the inner sides of both thighs. The yang's returning downward seems somewhat better than when it floated out, but not returning to the kidney, it either fumes up to the face or shows down at the thighs — wild yang without a master. So the urine receives its dryness and is difficult, and again it follows the channel to offend above, producing veiling and dizziness. In all, the kidney evil stirs and the dragon-thunder fire has nowhere to return, like flickering lightning without a master. So gui and fuling subdue the kidney evil, and wuweizi is added to gather the lung qi, fearing that if the cough is severe and the fire cannot be gathered all the more, the surging qi will be all the less able to descend. Gancao regulates central earth to restrain water. When the kidney evil is gone, the qi naturally does not surge; hence "to treat the surging qi." This shows that at first removing rheum and stopping cough were central, but once surging qi breaks out, the disease is serious, and one can no longer pursue other aims and divide the power of the medicine.

## Notes

A clause using Ling Gui Wei Gan tang (Gui Ling Wuwei Gancao tang) to treat upward surging when, after taking Xiao Qinglong tang, a person with lower-origin vacuity develops surging qi, facial flushing, cold and numb limbs, difficult urination, and dizziness.

- It is understood that the dispersal by mahuang and the like in Xiao Qinglong tang stirred the surging qi (kidney qi) of a vacuous person.
- The clause names the formula Fuling Guizhi Wuwei Gancao tang; the heading reads Gui Ling Wuwei Gancao tang.
- 巳 in 下巳 is an error for 已.

# Clause 25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Propping rheum below the heart, with the person suffering from veiling dizziness: Zexie tang (Alisma Decoction) governs it.

Zexie tang formula

Zexie 5 liang; baizhu 2 liang.

Of the two ingredients above, with 2 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

When propping rheum is below the heart, though not centered, it is near the heart, so heart fire is eroded by water qi. The heart is the sovereign fire and the source of yang qi — what is called "fire is bright and looks outward" — and yang qi has authority. When rheum qi erodes it, yin qi is exuberant and clear yang is obstructed and suppressed, and it also interferes with the pathways of qi; so there is veiling dizziness. "Veiling" is as if something covered one; "dizziness" is seeing black. The kidney is the source of water; zexie, salty in flavor, enters the kidney, so it drains the root and the branch moves by itself. Baizhu strengthens the central qi so that water can no longer gather. But since draining water with zexie is central, it is called Zexie tang.

Discussion: Shizhen combined hidden rheum with the four rheums to make five rheums, saying, "When hidden rheum is below the heart, it becomes heart water, with signs such as veiling dizziness and cold and heat," apparently basing himself on this clause. [He] did not know that Zhongjing earlier said, "When there is lingering rheum below the heart, the person's back is cold in a patch the size of a palm," and "When there is phlegm-rheum below the heart, the person has propping fullness of the chest and ribsides and dizziness," and here again says, "propping rheum below the heart with veiling dizziness." Is it not that lingering rheum near the back shows the sign of a cold back and is centrally located, so it can only be called lingering rheum, not propping rheum? That phlegm-rheum is centrally located and large in momentum, so it causes propping fullness of the chest and ribsides with dizziness, and cannot be called propping rheum? That propping rheum below the heart, being near the heart, obstructs and suppresses the clear yang and shows dizziness and veiling, and being slightly to one side cannot be subsumed under lingering rheum, and not being severe cannot be named phlegm-rheum? If rheum below the heart were "hidden," lingering rheum is also below the heart, so why is it not called hidden? Moreover, below the heart is a passageway; how can it be called hidden? Zhongjing describes hidden rheum in only one clause, distinguishing it specially by "breaks out with vomiting"; we know it is lingering rheum somewhat secluded, like an ambush, unlike propping rheum, which leans toward the ribsides. As for the *Qianjin*'s Da Wuyin wan governing lingering, phlegm, *pi*, spillage, and flowing rheum, with notes saying spillage rheum "overflows above the diaphragm" and flowing rheum "flows in the large intestine," the names become ever more mixed and hard to verify. How can they compare with Zhongjing's precise, unalterable naming?

## Notes

A clause using Zexie tang for severe dizziness (veiling dizziness) due to propping rheum below the heart.

- A two-ingredient formula: zexie disinhibits water, and baizhu fortifies the spleen to prevent water from gathering. It is well known as a formula for dizziness.
- The base reads 其人若冒眩; it is emended to 苦 ("suffers") because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- Xu Bin's "Discussion" criticizes Li Shizhen's theory of "five rheums."

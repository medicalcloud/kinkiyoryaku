# Clause 23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Spillage rheum should be treated by inducing sweating; Da Qinglong tang (Major Green-Blue Dragon Decoction) governs it; Xiao Qinglong tang also governs it.

Da Qinglong tang formula

Mahuang 6 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); xingren 40 pieces (remove the skins and tips); shengjiang 3 liang; dazao 12 pieces; shigao a piece the size of a hen's egg (ground).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 9 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, to obtain what seems a slight sweat. If the sweating is profuse, dust with warm powder.

Xiao Qinglong tang formula

Mahuang (remove the nodes) 3 liang; shaoyao 3 liang; wuweizi half a jin; ganjiang 3 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); xixin 3 liang; guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); banxia half a sheng (washed in hot water).

Of the eight ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Spillage rheum is water that has already flowed and returned to the limbs, with aching heaviness of the body because there is no sweating. The exterior is invaded by cold qi and aches, and the flesh has taken on dampness and is heavy; it is entirely exterior. But water and cold are mixed, like wind and cold both damaging with water qi inside; so Da Qinglong tang and Xiao Qinglong tang govern it. Da Qinglong tang combines Guizhi tang and Mahuang tang, removes shaoyao, and adds shigao, so it suits cases where water qi is not severe and heat is present. If there is much cough with hidden cold, Xiao Qinglong tang is surely appropriate: it is Mahuang tang without xingren and Guizhi tang without shengjiang, plus wuweizi, ganjiang, banxia, and xixin; though it disperses the exterior, its real intent is to make the cold rheum come out below.

Discussion: Seeing that Zhongjing, discussing taiyang summerheat stroke, says, "A hot, aching, heavy body with a faint, weak pulse is caused by damage from cold water in summer, the water moving in the skin; Yiwu Guadi tang governs it," and also, "If sweating is induced, the aversion to cold becomes severe" — why does he here alone make it urgent to release the exterior with these two decoctions? Is it not that what spillage rheum affects does not originate in summerheat stroke, and the interstices are firmly closed, unlike summer when sweating is easy? There, in summer, the interstices are originally loose and summerheat stroke comes first, so vomiting is the mainstay. So we know that in summer, if the body is not hot and this pattern arises without summerheat stroke, these two decoctions are also appropriate.

[Part of Xu Bin's Xiao Qinglong tang formula:] mahuang 3 liang (remove the nodes); gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); shaoyao 3 liang.

## Notes

A clause stating that spillage rheum is treated by sweating, giving Da Qinglong tang and Xiao Qinglong tang. Both also appear in the *Shanghan lun*.

- Da Qinglong tang is Mahuang tang plus shigao, shengjiang, and dazao, used for exterior cold with internal heat. Xiao Qinglong tang is used for exterior cold with internal water-rheum (with cough).
- In the base, "Xiao Qinglong tang also governs it" follows a ○; it is treated here as one clause.
- For wuweizi in Xiao Qinglong tang, the base has half a jin, and all other sources half a sheng.
- "Warm powder" is a powder dusted on to stop sweating; its composition is not given.

# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When there is lingering rheum in the chest, the person is short of breath and thirsty, and the joints of the four limbs ache one after another. A sunken pulse means there is lingering rheum.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Lingering rheum is originally in the pathways of coming and going and can leave, but lingers temporarily; it is phlegm-rheum that is not severe, unlike propping rheum, which is one-sided and hard to remove. So lingering rheum is not listed among the four rheums but must be mentioned separately to show the distinction. Seeing that it says "below the heart" and "in the chest," we know it is of a kind with phlegm-rheum. The back being cold in a patch the size of a palm is rheum near the back obstructing the yang rising along the governing vessel, making the back cold; but the rheum qi is limited, so it is only the size of a palm.

Lingering rheum need not always hurt. But below the ribs is the residence of liver and gallbladder, and the shaoyang vessel passes from the supraclavicular fossa past the lower ribs. The rheum is near the ribsides, the evil attacks the liver and invades shaoyang, so pain below the ribs draws into the supraclavicular fossa. But the pain belongs to qi depression; coughing relieves it a little, so it stops temporarily.

When rheum lingers in the chest and obstructs the heart qi, the breath is shortened. When the lung does not move qi and the spleen does not convey essence, the evil gathers in the diaphragm and there is thirst. Aching joints of the limbs indicate cold evil entering from the exterior; but since the pulse is sunken, the rheum should be blamed.

Discussion: Zhongjing, describing joint-running, says, "The pulse is sunken and weak; from bathing in water while sweating, water qi invades the heart, so yellow sweat comes out and the joints ache." So we know that the clause on joint pain among lingering rheum is also said of evil entering from the exterior. If yellow sweat is further added, it should after all be treated as joint-running.

## Notes

A clause on lingering rheum in the chest with shortness of breath, thirst, and pain in the joints of the limbs, with a sunken pulse.

- Xu Bin's commentary explains the lingering-rheum clauses of units 8 through 10 together, taking lingering rheum to be phlegm-rheum that is not severe.

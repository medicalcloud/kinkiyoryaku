# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When there is prolonged fluid retention in the chest, the person is short of breath and thirsty, and the joints of the four limbs ache one after another. A deep pulse means there is prolonged fluid retention.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Prolonged fluid retention is originally in the pathways of coming and going and can leave, but lingers temporarily; it is phlegm-fluid retention that is not severe, unlike thoracic fluid retention, which is one-sided and hard to remove. So prolonged fluid retention is not listed among the four rheums but must be mentioned separately to show the distinction. Seeing that it says "below the heart" and "in the chest," we know it is of a kind with phlegm-fluid retention. The back being cold in a patch the size of a palm is retained fluid near the back obstructing the yang rising along the governing vessel, making the back cold; but the retained fluid qi is limited, so it is only the size of a palm.

Prolonged fluid retention need not always hurt. But below the ribs is the residence of liver and gallbladder, and the Shaoyang vessel passes from the supraclavicular fossa past the lower ribs. The retained fluid is near the ribsides, the pathogen attacks the liver and invades Shaoyang, so pain below the ribs draws into the supraclavicular fossa. But the pain belongs to qi depression; coughing relieves it a little, so it stops temporarily.

When retained fluid lingers in the chest and obstructs the heart qi, the breath is shortened. When the lung does not move qi and the spleen does not convey essence, the pathogen gathers in the diaphragm and there is thirst. Aching joints of the limbs indicate pathogenic cold entering from the exterior; but since the pulse is deep, the retained fluid should be blamed.

Discussion: Zhongjing, describing joint wind, says, "The pulse is deep and weak; from bathing in water while sweating, water qi invades the heart, so yellow sweating comes out and the joints ache." So we know that the clause on joint pain among prolonged fluid retention is also said of pathogen entering from the exterior. If yellow sweating is further added, it should after all be treated as joint wind.

## Notes

A clause on prolonged fluid retention in the chest with shortness of breath, thirst, and pain in the joints of the limbs, with a deep pulse.

- Xu Bin's commentary explains the prolonged-fluid-retention clauses of units 8 through 10 together, taking prolonged fluid retention to be phlegm-fluid retention that is not severe.

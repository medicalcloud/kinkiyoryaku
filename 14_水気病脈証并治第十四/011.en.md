# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

People with water disease have "sleeping silkworms" (swelling) under the eyes, a fresh, lustrous face and eyes, a hidden pulse, and wasting thirst. When suffering from water with an enlarged abdomen, inhibited urination, and a sunken, nearly vanishing pulse, there is water, and it may be purged.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is said of regular water. In all people with water disease, the spleen and stomach are invaded by water qi; the pouch below the eyes is called the socket, where the stomach vessel reaches, governed by spleen and stomach. With water disease, there is a shape like a sleeping silkworm. Water qi governs moistness, so the face and eyes are fresh, bright, and lustrous, unlike the dryness of wind. A hidden pulse is sunken. The person has wasting thirst because, with water in the skin, the true qi within is consumed, and consumption causes thirst; but it is not heat arriving suddenly, so it is simply wasting thirst, not occasional thirst. When water disease is below, the abdomen must be enlarged and urination inhibited, for without glomus and blockage water would not form. A sunken, nearly vanishing pulse is extreme sinking. Not all water disease may be purged, but when the sinking is extreme, the water is extreme, so it may be purged to remove the branch.

## Notes

A clause listing the features of a person with water disease (swelling under the eyes, lustrous face, hidden pulse, thirst) and stating that one with an enlarged abdomen, inhibited urination, and a sunken, nearly vanishing pulse may have the water purged.

- The base reads 腹水; it is emended to 腹大 ("enlarged abdomen") because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

# Clause 32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Appended formula

Fangji Huangqi tang from the *Waitai*: treats wind edema. A floating pulse means [the disease] is in the exterior. The person may sweat from the head; there is no other exterior disease; the patient has only heaviness below; above the lower back is in harmony, while below the lower back there should be swelling reaching the genitals, with difficulty bending and stretching (the formula appears under qi [read: wind]-damp).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

[Xu Bin's text:] ... no other exterior disease; the patient should have heaviness below; above the lower back is in harmony, while below there is swelling reaching the genitals, with difficulty bending and stretching (the formula appears earlier).

Earlier, in setting out formulas for wind edema, Zhongjing took a floating pulse, heavy body, sweating, and aversion to wind as the proper standard, governed by Fangji Huangqi tang, and acute wind with swelling of the whole body as a changed pattern, governed by Yuebi tang. But in the upper and lower body there are also cases in which wind-damp prevails on one side: the yang aspect resolves through sweating but the yin aspect has no sweat, or there is sweating from the head, harmony above and heaviness below, and the pulse ... yin swelling [sic]. This should still be treated gently as wind-damp, governed again by Fangji Huangqi tang, not with Yuebi tang as for acute wind. So he specially supplemented it with the *Waitai* formula and discussion to detail the variant forms of wind edema.

## Notes

An appended formula, Fangji Huangqi tang from the *Waitai*, for wind edema with the upper body in harmony and the lower body swollen and heavy. It is the last unit of the chapter on water qi.

- For 気 in the note 方見気湿中, most other sources read 風 ("wind-damp," referring to the wind-damp clause in the chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke), which the translation notes.
- The beginning of Xu Bin's commentary is part of the text of Xu Bin's edition, with slightly different wording. 脈陰腫 in the commentary is hard to interpret.

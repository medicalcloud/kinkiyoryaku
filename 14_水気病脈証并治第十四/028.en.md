# Clause 28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In yellow sweating disease, both shins are cold of themselves; if there is fever, it belongs to joint wind. Sweating after eating, and also frequent night sweats at dusk, is taxation qi. If after sweating there is, on the contrary, fever, over time the body will surely become encrusted; if the fever does not stop, malign sores will surely arise. If the body is heavy and becomes lighter each time after sweating, over time the body will surely twitch, there will be pain in the chest, and above the lower back there will surely be sweating while below there is none, with slack pain of the lower back and hips as if something were in the skin; in severe cases one cannot eat, the body aches and is heavy, there is restlessness, and urination is inhibited. This is yellow sweating. Guizhi jia Huangqi tang (Keishikaogito; Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Astragalus) governs it.

Guizhi jia Huangqi tang formula

Guizhi (keishi) and shaoyao (shakuyaku), 3 liang each; gancao (kanzo) 2 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) 3 liang; dazao (taiso) 12 pieces; huangqi (ogi) 2 liang.

Of the six ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng and take 1 sheng warm. After a short while drink a little over 1 sheng of hot thin gruel to aid the power of the medicine; keep warm to obtain a slight sweat. If there is no sweat, take more.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section discusses that although the changed signs within yellow sweating are mixed and various, all return to yellow sweating, and its treatment is largely the same with small differences. Yellow sweating arises from water qi damaging the heart, so heat gathers in the heart and chest, the sovereign fire cannot descend to interact with the kidney, and the shins are always cold of themselves. "Of themselves" means the genuine qi does not descend, not that the feet have separately received pathogen. If there is fever and the feet and shins are also hot, wind and cold are passing through the limb joints causing pain; hence "it belongs to joint wind." As for the times of sweating: heart fire damaged by water-damp cannot generate earth, the central qi is deficient and feeble, and since the heart governs blood, the ying-nutrient aspect has deficiency heat. So after eating, the stomach is taxed and fire stirs, causing sweat; at dusk, yin is agitated, causing sweat. Hence "this is ying-nutrient qi."

Then it supposes the conjunctions of sweating, fever, and heaviness to cover all the forms of the disease. After sweating the body should cool; if instead it is hot because the internal pathogen is exuberant, the yin qi of the skin is scorched by sweating, and over time it must become encrusted. If the fever further does not stop, the ying-nutrient qi becomes hot and putrid, producing malign sores. If the body is originally heavy, it is dampness. Becoming lighter each time after sweating means the exterior dampness is reduced by sweating, but only temporarily, unable finally to stop the heaviness; then the inner qi becomes ever more deficient, and with inner deficiency the flesh twitches. Pain in the chest is qi not moving. Or if the original qi cannot penetrate above and below, there is sweat above the lower back and none below. Then the original qi cannot reach below, and the lower back and hips are slack and painful — "slack" as if dislocated; "as if something were in the skin" means not agile. In severe, dangerous cases, the original qi in the chest is damaged and one cannot eat; the yin qi of the whole body is blocked and the body aches; the qi congests, causing restlessness; heart fire is depressed in the stomach and heat flows downward, making urine rough. But all these arise gradually, and their source is always water qi damaging the heart, the disease deepening daily. Hence "this is yellow sweating."

Guizhi jia Huangqi tang is used because regulating the ying-nutrient and wei-defense and freeing their qi means that supplementing the right is the way to drive out the pathogen. Compared with Fangji Huangqi tang (Boiogito), fangji (boi) is not used, meaning that in yellow sweating the dampness of the flesh and exterior is originally not much. Compared with Qi Shao Gui Jiu tang, the vinegar is removed and shengjiang, dazao, gancao, and gruel are added to harmonize and regulate the inner qi of the chest, attacking by supplementing, without taking the swiftness of vinegar. Compared with Huangqi Guizhi Wuwu tang (Ogikeishigomotsuto) for blood impediment, it has more raw gancao, taken to drain the pathogen entering the heart.

[Xu Bin's formula:] guizhi 3 liang; shaoyao 2 liang; gancao 2 liang; shengjiang 3 liang; huangqi 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces.

## Notes

A clause describing the various changed signs of yellow sweating and using Guizhi jia Huangqi tang.

- Cold shins, night sweats at dusk, encrusted skin or malign sores from fever after sweating, heaviness temporarily relieved by sweating, sweating only of the upper body, lumbar pain, loss of appetite, body aches, restlessness, and inhibited urination are all classed as yellow sweating.
- It is Guizhi tang (Keishito) plus huangqi, with the same administration of sipping gruel to induce sweating as Guizhi tang.
- For 労気 ("taxation qi"), Xu Bin's edition reads 栄気 ("ying-nutrient qi"), and his commentary explains accordingly.
- The base lacks 此 and reads 不無汗 and 煩燥; these are emended to 此為黄汗, 下無汗, and 煩躁 because all other sources so read (see the emendation notes). 巳 is an error for 已.

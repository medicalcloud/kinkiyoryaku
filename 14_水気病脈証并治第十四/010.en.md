# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Shaoyin pulse is tight and deep. Tight means pain; deep means water; urination is then difficult. When the pulse is any kind of deep, one should blame water, with swelling and heaviness of the body. In water disease, if the pulse comes out (floats and scatters), the patient dies.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that formed water qi may also be seen in the Shaoyin pulse. Shaoyin is the chi pulse. It is tight and deep: tight belongs to cold and governs pain; deep is yin binding and belongs to water. "Urination is then difficult" means that because the kidney suffers from water, urination becomes inhibited, not that difficult urination causes the water disease.

This is said excluding wind edema and skin edema. Water belongs to yin, and the deep pulse also belongs to yin; hence "when the pulse is any kind of deep, one should blame water." But one must still judge by combining swelling and heaviness of the body. "Any kind" means that when the pulse position differs, the origin of the disease differs, but in general one judges by sinking. Since the water disease pulse is deep, if it floats out, the yang qi is deserting upward; so it indicates death.

## Notes

A clause stating that a tight, deep Shaoyin (chi) pulse indicates water and pain, that a deep pulse should make one suspect water, and that in water disease a pulse that floats out is a sign of death.

- "The pulse comes out" means a pulse that should be deep floats and scatters, a sign of healthy qi deserting outward.

# Clause 21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wind water with a floating pulse, heavy body, sweating, and aversion to wind: Fangji Huangqi tang governs it. For abdominal pain, add shaoyao.

Fangji Huangqi tang formula

Fangji 1 liang; huangqi 1 liang 1 *fen*; baizhu 3 *fen*; gancao half a liang (mix-fried).

Cut the above; for each dose take 5 *qianbi* with 4 slices of shengjiang and 1 jujube, decoct in one and a half cups of water down to eight-tenths, remove the dregs, and take warm. After a good while take it again.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The first section discussing wind water mentioned aching joints; why, when the formula is given here, are there no aching joints but instead a heavy body and sweating? Earlier, aching joints were mentioned to distinguish, by the word "wind," from other waters, meaning that regular, skin, and stone water cannot have aching joints. But aching joints are not truly a sign of water. So earlier wind water was extended: "wind and qi strike each other, the body swells greatly"; "the face and eyes are greatly swollen with heat"; "slight swelling of the eye sockets, pulsating neck vessels, cough, pitting of the hands and feet that does not rise"; "the joints, on the contrary, do not ache, the body is instead heavy and aching, no thirst, sweating." None is as certain as heaviness of the body. Combined with a floating pulse, sweating, and aversion to wind, it is undoubtedly wind water. The extended signs may or may not accompany it, left to the practitioner's judgment. The medicine uses fangji to remove wind-damp, huangqi to reach the flesh directly, baizhu and gancao to regulate the inner qi and remove the root of dampness, and shengjiang and dazao to move the nutritive and defensive and diffuse the qi of the upper burner. Shaoyao is added for abdominal pain because the spleen is vacuous and this supplements it. Fangji Huangqi tang formula: fangji 1 liang; huangqi 1 liang 1 *fen*; baizhu 3 liang 3 *fen*; gancao 5 *qian* (mix-fried). Cut the above; for each dose take 5 *qian* with 4 slices of shengjiang and 3 jujubes, decoct in one and a half cups of water down to eight-tenths ...

## Notes

A clause using Fangji Huangqi tang for wind water with a floating pulse, heavy body, sweating, and aversion to wind. It is nearly the same as the wind-damp clause in the chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke, with the same formula.

- Fangji removes water-damp, huangqi secures the exterior, and baizhu and gancao supplement the spleen.
- Daizhige omits the formula, saying "the formula appears under dampness disease."

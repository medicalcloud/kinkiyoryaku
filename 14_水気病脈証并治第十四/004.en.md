# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Taiyang disease with a floating, tight pulse, by rule the joints should ache, but on the contrary they do not; instead the body is heavy and aching; the person is not thirsty; and when sweat comes out, there is immediate recovery: this is wind edema. If there is aversion to cold, this is extreme deficiency, contracted through sweating. If there is thirst without aversion to cold, this is skin edema. When the body is swollen and cold, like generalized impediment, with obstruction in the chest, inability to eat, gathering pain instead, and agitation at dusk with inability to sleep, this is yellow sweating. When the pain is in the joints, with cough and dyspnea but no thirst, this is spleen [lung] distension; it looks like swelling; inducing sweating cures it at once. But in all who suffer from these, if there is thirst with diarrhea and frequent urination, sweating must not be induced.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section says that among wind edema cases some resemble the Taiyang pulse but do not show Taiyang signs; some resemble it but are in fact skin edema; some resemble it but are in fact yellow sweating; and some resemble it but are neither skin edema nor yellow sweating but in fact lung distension. In Taiyang disease with a floating, tight pulse, by rule the joints should ache; so the earlier description of wind edema also says "the external signs are aching joints." Here, on the contrary, they do not ache. Also, Taiyang disease is not heavy, yet now with a Taiyang cold pulse the body is instead heavy and aching, there is no thirst, and sweating brings immediate recovery; clearly the wind has been softened by water, so it does not ache but is heavy. Wind originally has sweat, and it resolves through spontaneous sweating; so it is properly named "this is wind edema." But after sweating there should be no aversion to cold; if there is aversion to cold again, clearly the person has been made deficient by sweating; hence "this is extreme deficiency, contracted through sweating."

If to the previous signs is added thirst without aversion to cold, the thirst resembles wind edema, but without aversion to cold it is not wind edema; so it is again distinguished: "this is skin edema." But skin edema has no body heat, so its definite signs are noted: "the body is swollen and cold, like generalized impediment." Generalized impediment means the skin of the whole body receives pathogen and does not function; this is the earlier "external signs: puffy swelling that pits when pressed."

If to the previous signs are added obstruction in the chest, inability to eat, gathering pain, and agitation at dusk with inability to sleep, clearly entering water has damaged the heart, and the chest has received pathogen and is obstructed. The pathogen is high and hinders eating; the pathogen gathers and hurts; and the heart is vexed, with agitation at dusk and inability to sleep. These are signs only of yellow sweating, all in the chest; hence "this is yellow sweating."

If with the previous floating, tight pulse the joints still ache, with cough and dyspnea but no thirst, it resembles skin edema, but there is not much puffy swelling, and it is not skin edema either; hence "this is lung distension." The lung governs qi; receiving pathogen it coughs; it looks like swelling but is not really swelling. This too is wind soaking into the lung, so it is summed up: "inducing sweating cures it at once," showing that though the signs differ, the treatment may be the same.

The four phrases "in all who suffer from these" mean that although the patterns differ and all seem amenable to sweating, when there is thirst, diarrhea, or free urination, the pathogenic qi has entered inward and cannot be cured by a single sweat; hence "all [must not be made to sweat]."

## Notes

A clause distinguishing water diseases that resemble the Taiyang pulse (floating and tight) — wind edema, skin edema, yellow sweating, and lung distension — all curable by sweating, but prohibiting sweating in those with thirst, diarrhea, or frequent urination.

- For 脾 in 此為脾脹 ("spleen distension"), icntcm reads 肺 ("lung"), and Xu Bin's edition also suspects an error for 肺. Since the content matches the lung-distension clauses (cough, ascending qi, dyspnea) of the earlier chapter on atrophy of the lung lobes and lung abscess, the translation notes "lung."
- Interpretations differ on where "the body is swollen and cold, like generalized impediment" belongs; Xu Bin takes it as supplementing the signs of skin edema.
- The base reads 及不疼; it is emended to 反不疼 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

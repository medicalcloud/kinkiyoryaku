# Clause 31 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hardness below the heart as large as a plate, its rim like a circling plate [read: cup], produced by water-fluid retention: Zhi Zhu tang (Kijutsuto; Unripe Bitter Orange and White Atractylodes Decoction) governs it.

Zhi Zhu tang formula

Zhishi (kijitsu) 7 pieces; baizhu (byakujutsu) 2 liang.

Of the two ingredients above, with 5 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng and take warm in three doses. When the abdomen softens, then [it has dispersed] (the base breaks off at 即当).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The previous formula treated heart and kidney together. But this pattern also occurs where the central qi was originally deficient and phlegm-fluid retention suddenly binds; then this hardness below the heart is truly produced by water-fluid retention, and the retained fluid alone should be treated. So Zhi Zhu tang supplements with one and drains with the other. But since the condition is the same, how does one distinguish its water-fluid retention? Judging by duration, there must be differences in form and qi. The character 盤 ("plate") is an accidental error for 盃 ("cup"); do not cling to it. The meaning of the first part, "hard and as large as a plate," lies in size; that of the second, "its rim like a circling cup," lies in roundness, and should not again take the sense of large. Taken together, it is hard, large, and round.

## Notes

A clause using Zhi Zhu tang for the same hard, large mass below the heart as the previous clause, when due to water-fluid retention.

- Zhi Zhu tang, of two ingredients, zhishi and baizhu, dissolves stuffiness with zhishi and supplements the spleen and removes water with baizhu. It is the origin of the later Zhi Zhu wan.
- 盤 in 辺如旋盤 appears, from the previous clause and Xu Bin's commentary, to be an error for 杯 (盃, "cup").
- For the final 腹中耎即当, most other sources read 腹中軟即当散也 ("when the abdomen softens, it should have dispersed"); the base lacks 散也.

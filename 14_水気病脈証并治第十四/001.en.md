# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: Among diseases there are wind water, skin water, regular water, stone water, and yellow sweat.

Wind water: the pulse is floating of itself; the external signs are aching joints and aversion to wind.

Skin water: the pulse is also floating; the external signs are puffy swelling [base: 腑腫] that pits when pressed with a finger, no aversion to wind, an abdomen like a drum, and no thirst. Sweating should be induced.

Regular water: the pulse is sunken and slow; the external sign is spontaneous panting.

Stone water: the pulse is sunken of itself; the external signs are abdominal fullness without panting.

Yellow sweat: the pulse is sunken and slow, the body is feverish, the chest full, and the limbs, head, and face swollen; if it does not heal for a long time, it will surely produce abscess and pus.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The *Neijing* has only two items, water distension and stone water; Zhongjing specially established five to show that water disease varies in depth, wishing people to think of the meaning from the name and treat without error. So water arising from external evil with the evil in the channels is distinguished as "wind water," meaning it should be treated as wind. Water that arises from external evil but whose evil has already seeped into the skin, neither in the exterior nor the interior, is distinguished as "skin water," meaning it is in the skin and has not shed the wind. Water that arises not from wind but from binding of the three yin is distinguished as "regular water," meaning the water itself should be treated directly. When yin evil predominates and sinks below, it is distinguished as "stone water," meaning the disease is entirely below. When, also from wind evil or from water evil that, though external, damages the heart within, heat is depressed and produces yellow sweat, resembling wind water but without a floating pulse, it is distinguished as "yellow sweat," meaning the disease evil is the same as water but enters at the heart.

Water diseases are not far apart, so the *Neijing*'s chapter on water distension says generally, "Slight swelling above the eye sockets as if just risen from sleep, pulsation of the neck vessels, occasional cough, cold between the inner thighs, swollen shins, and an enlarged abdomen — the water has formed. Pressing the abdomen, it rises with the hand like a bag of water," without distinguishing. But when the causes differ, the treatments differ entirely. So Zhongjing first distinguished them by pulse. Floating is wind; when wind evil contends, the joints ache; when wind is still in the exterior, there is aversion to wind. The combination of these three cannot be shared by other patterns, so they are taken as the key to identifying wind water.

If the pulse is floating, which is wind, yet the body has puffy swelling — 胕 meaning puffy — and in severe cases pits under the finger, so puffy that the evil has left the channels and is in the skin: having left the channels, there is no aversion to wind; being in the skin, the abdominal skin is like a drum. The *Qianjin* has the further words "not full" here: though it looks distended outside, the disease is not inside, so it is not full. The wind is within the skin and not dry, so there is no thirst. Its treatment too should follow wind; hence "sweating should be induced." Thus skin water does not differ from wind water in pulse but differs in signs.

As for regular water, it is binding of the three yin, not of wind, so the pulse is sunken, and water belongs to yin, so it is slow. With the three yin bound, the yin qi of the lower burner no longer harmonizes with the yang in the chest, so water qi blocks the yang above, causing panting — the *Neijing*'s "pulsation of the neck vessels, panting and cough, is called water." That the eye sockets are like silkworms, both shins swollen, and the abdomen enlarged goes without asking. But since its distinction from stone water lies not here, only panting is mentioned.

As for stone water, the pulse is also sunken but not slow. The *Neijing* says, "Yin and yang bound, much yin and little yang, is called stone water; the lesser abdomen is swollen." So we know that the abdominal fullness here is swelling of the lesser abdomen. The disease is solely in the lower burner, not of the whole body, so there is no panting. That the neck vessels pulsate, there is cough, and the eye sockets are like silkworms may be the same as regular water, differing in degree.

Yellow sweat arises from entering water while sweating, the water evil damaging the heart, or from exposure to wind while sweating. Sweat and water both belong to water qi. Because it enters inward and binds, and binding makes the heat depressed and yellow, the pulse too is sunken and slow. The heart receives the evil and is depressed, so the body is feverish. The damage is above, so the chest is full. Evil of the yang region follows yang, running to the limbs, and the head and face swell. If it does not heal for a long time, the evil qi invades the yin, the nutritive qi becomes hot, congeals and stagnates, and produces abscess.

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on water qi. It divides water disease into five kinds — wind water, skin water, regular water, stone water, and yellow sweat — and gives the pulse and external signs of each.

- Wind water: floating pulse, joint pain, aversion to wind (acute edema due to wind evil).
- Skin water: floating pulse, pitting edema of the skin, no aversion to wind, drum-like abdomen, no thirst; treated by sweating.
- Regular water: sunken, slow pulse, panting.
- Stone water: sunken pulse, abdominal (lower abdominal) fullness without panting.
- Yellow sweat: sunken, slow pulse, fever, chest fullness, swelling of the limbs, head, and face, yellow sweat; if prolonged, suppuration.
- For 腑腫, the other sources read 胕腫 ("puffy swelling").
- Xu Bin's commentary is in two parts: the first explains the meanings of the five names, the second the signs of each.

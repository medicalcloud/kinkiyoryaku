# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: Among diseases there are wind edema, skin edema, generalized edema, stony edema, and yellow sweating.

Wind edema: the pulse is floating of itself; the external signs are aching joints and aversion to wind.

Skin edema: the pulse is also floating; the external signs are puffy swelling [base: 腑腫] that pits when pressed with a finger, no aversion to wind, an abdomen like a drum, and no thirst. Sweating should be induced.

Generalized edema: the pulse is deep and slow; the external sign is spontaneous dyspnea.

Stony edema: the pulse is deep of itself; the external signs are abdominal fullness without dyspnea.

Yellow sweating: the pulse is deep and slow, the body is feverish, the chest full, and the limbs, head, and face swollen; if it does not heal for a long time, it will surely produce abscess and pus.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The *Neijing* has only two items, water distension and stony edema; Zhongjing specially established five to show that water disease varies in depth, wishing people to think of the meaning from the name and treat without error. So water arising from external pathogen with the pathogen in the meridians is distinguished as "wind edema," meaning it should be treated as wind. Water that arises from external pathogen but whose pathogen has already seeped into the skin, neither in the exterior nor the interior, is distinguished as "skin edema," meaning it is in the skin and has not shed the wind. Water that arises not from wind but from binding of the three yin is distinguished as "generalized edema," meaning the water itself should be treated directly. When yin pathogen predominates and sinks below, it is distinguished as "stony edema," meaning the disease is entirely below. When, also from pathogenic wind or from pathogenic water that, though external, damages the heart within, heat is depressed and produces yellow sweating, resembling wind edema but without a floating pulse, it is distinguished as "yellow sweating," meaning the disease pathogen is the same as water but enters at the heart.

Water diseases are not far apart, so the *Neijing*'s chapter on water distension says generally, "Slight swelling above the eye sockets as if just risen from sleep, pulsation of the neck vessels, occasional cough, cold between the inner thighs, swollen shins, and an enlarged abdomen — the water has formed. Pressing the abdomen, it rises with the hand like a bag of water," without distinguishing. But when the causes differ, the treatments differ entirely. So Zhongjing first distinguished them by pulse. Floating is wind; when pathogenic wind contends, the joints ache; when wind is still in the exterior, there is aversion to wind. The combination of these three cannot be shared by other patterns, so they are taken as the key to identifying wind edema.

If the pulse is floating, which is wind, yet the body has puffy swelling — 胕 meaning puffy — and in severe cases pits under the finger, so puffy that the pathogen has left the meridians and is in the skin: having left the meridians, there is no aversion to wind; being in the skin, the abdominal skin is like a drum. The *Qianjin* has the further words "not full" here: though it looks distended outside, the disease is not inside, so it is not full. The wind is within the skin and not dry, so there is no thirst. Its treatment too should follow wind; hence "sweating should be induced." Thus skin edema does not differ from wind edema in pulse but differs in signs.

As for generalized edema, it is binding of the three yin, not of wind, so the pulse is deep, and water belongs to yin, so it is slow. With the three yin bound, the yin qi of the lower jiao no longer harmonizes with the yang in the chest, so water qi blocks the yang above, causing dyspnea — the *Neijing*'s "pulsation of the neck vessels, dyspnea and cough, is called water." That the eye sockets are like silkworms, both shins swollen, and the abdomen enlarged goes without asking. But since its distinction from stony edema lies not here, only dyspnea is mentioned.

As for stony edema, the pulse is also deep but not slow. The *Neijing* says, "Yin and yang bound, much yin and little yang, is called stony edema; the lesser abdomen is swollen." So we know that the abdominal fullness here is swelling of the lesser abdomen. The disease is solely in the lower jiao, not of the whole body, so there is no dyspnea. That the neck vessels pulsate, there is cough, and the eye sockets are like silkworms may be the same as generalized edema, differing in degree.

Yellow sweating arises from entering water while sweating, the pathogenic water damaging the heart, or from exposure to wind while sweating. Sweat and water both belong to water qi. Because it enters inward and binds, and binding makes the heat depressed and yellow, the pulse too is deep and slow. The heart receives the pathogen and is depressed, so the body is feverish. The damage is above, so the chest is full. Pathogen of the yang region follows yang, running to the limbs, and the head and face swell. If it does not heal for a long time, the pathogenic qi invades the yin, the ying-nutrient qi becomes hot, congeals and stagnates, and produces abscess.

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on water qi. It divides water disease into five kinds — wind edema, skin edema, generalized edema, stony edema, and yellow sweating — and gives the pulse and external signs of each.

- Wind edema: floating pulse, joint pain, aversion to wind (acute edema due to pathogenic wind).
- Skin edema: floating pulse, pitting edema of the skin, no aversion to wind, drum-like abdomen, no thirst; treated by sweating.
- Generalized edema: deep, slow pulse, dyspnea.
- Stony edema: deep pulse, abdominal (lower abdominal) fullness without dyspnea.
- Yellow sweating: deep, slow pulse, fever, chest fullness, swelling of the limbs, head, and face, yellow sweating; if prolonged, suppuration.
- For 腑腫, the other sources read 胕腫 ("puffy swelling").
- Xu Bin's commentary is in two parts: the first explains the meanings of the five names, the second the signs of each.

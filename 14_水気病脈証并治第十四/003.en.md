# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse at the inch opening is deep and slippery, there is water qi within; when the face and eyes are greatly swollen with heat, it is called wind edema. If one sees that above the eye sockets [base: 目裹] there is slight swelling, as of a silkworm just risen from sleep, the neck vessels pulsate, there is frequent cough, and pressing on the hands and feet leaves a pit that does not rise, it is wind edema.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

These two sections take variant forms of wind edema and still give them the proper name to show the distinction. The wind edema pulse is originally floating; now it is deep and slippery, meaning there is water qi bound within, which seems to belong to generalized edema. But the face and eyes are greatly swollen with heat. Above the high vertex only wind can reach, and wind is a yang pathogen, hence heat. Though the pulse is deep, one cannot speak of it apart from external wind, so it is still properly named "wind edema." Slight silkworm-like swelling of the eye sockets with pulsating neck vessels and cough are signs of generalized edema. Yet pressing the hands and feet leaves a pit that does not rise. What rises with the hand is water; since it does not rise, we know it is not generalized edema but qi water. Wind and qi must bind each other, so this too is [properly named wind edema].

## Notes

A clause stating that even with a deep, slippery pulse, facial swelling with heat is wind edema, and that swollen eyelids, pulsating neck vessels, cough, and pitting of the hands and feet that does not rebound are also wind edema.

- For 目裹, most other sources read 目窠 ("eye socket, eyelid").
- Xu Bin's commentary is cut off at the end.

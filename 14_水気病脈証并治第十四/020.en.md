# Clause 20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: A patient suffers from water: the face, eyes, body, and limbs are all swollen, and urination is inhibited. Taking the pulse, [the master] did not speak of water, but said instead, "There should be pain in the chest, qi surging up into the throat as if a piece of roasted meat [were stuck], and slight cough and panting." On examination it was as the master said. What kind of pulse was it?

The master said: The pulse at the inch opening is sunken and tight. Sunken means water; tight means cold. Sunken and tight contend, binding at Origin Pass. At first it is slight, and while young and strong one does not notice it. After the yang declines, the nutritive and defensive interfere with each other, yang is damaged and yin exuberant, the bound cold stirs slightly, the kidney qi surges up, the throat is blocked and choked, and below the ribs there is tense pain. The physician, thinking it lingering rheum, purged heavily; the qi struck back and did not leave, and the disease was not removed. Afterward he induced vomiting repeatedly; the stomach became vacuous and vexed, the throat dry with desire to drink water, urination inhibited, water and grain not transformed, and the face, eyes, hands, and feet puffy and swollen. Then he gave Tingli wan to purge water, and at the time there seemed to be slight improvement; but with excessive eating and drinking, the swelling returned as before, the chest and ribsides suffered pain like running piglet, and the water rose and overflowed, causing puffiness, cough, and panting counterflow. One should first attack the surging qi [base: 衛 "defensive"] to stop it, then treat the cough; when the cough stops, the panting will recover by itself. Treat the new disease first; the [original] disease should come after.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that regular water may form where the true origin is greatly vacuous; it became water through mistreatment, and further mistreatment changed it into new diseases; but the new diseases should be treated first. When water disease reaches swelling of the face, eyes, body, and limbs with inhibited urination, its momentum is severe. Yet the patient seemed not to suffer from water but from chest pain and surging qi; so [the questioner] wondered whether this was a changed sign possible within water disease and asked what the pulse was like, not knowing that such signs cannot originally belong to water qi. Before this, the inch pulse must have been sunken and tight: sunken indicates slight water, tight indicates accumulated cold. Tight and sunken means accumulated cold carrying slight water, bound at Origin Pass. At first water and cold were both slight, and in vigorous youth the evil could not overcome the right, so it went unnoticed. When yang declined, the hidden evil gradually interfered with the nutritive and defensive; yang was daily damaged and yin daily more exuberant, and the bound cold stirred slightly, able to carry the kidney qi surging upward, no longer merely interfering. Because it carried the kidney [qi] — the straight branch of the kidney vessel rises through the diaphragm into the lung, follows the throat, and flanks the root of the tongue, and its branch leaves the lung, connects with the heart, and pours into the chest — the throat was blocked and choked and below the ribs there was tense pain. Had one then warmed the kidney and drained the cold, the disease would surely have gone. Instead, taking it for lingering rheum, one purged heavily without treating the root, so the disease qi did not submit and remained bound. Repeated vomiting then attacked what was blameless and damaged the central qi. The stomach became vacuous and vexed; vomiting damaged the yang of the upper burner and yin fire took advantage, so the throat was dry and one wanted water. Then the qi of spleen and stomach declined, the evil lodged in the blood aspect, urination was inhibited, and water and grain were not transformed. The stomach qi was not strong and water qi overwhelmed the lung, so the face, eyes, hands, and feet were puffy. Purging water with Tingli wan, though not a root-treating formula, treated the branch first since the branch disease was severe, so there was slight improvement — that is, the swelling receded. With immoderate eating it swelled again, and with chest and rib pain like running piglet added, the kidney evil ran rampant, and water qi rose and overflowed, causing cough and panting counterflow. But cough is not the root of the disease; the root is in the kidney. Hence "one should first attack the surging qi to stop it," like Ling Gui Wei Gan tang in the phlegm-rheum section. When the cough stops, the panting recovers by itself without treatment. Because the root of the disease is very deep and cannot be removed quickly, one must first remove the acute disease; then the original disease can be treated. Hence "treat the new disease first; the disease should come after." One should know that surging qi, cough, and panting are all new diseases; "the disease" in "the disease should come after" refers to water qi. But the cold bound at Origin Pass is again the root of the water disease.

## Notes

A question-and-answer clause describing the course in which cold and water bound at Origin Pass (*guanyuan*) stirred with declining yang, causing kidney qi to surge upward, and mistaken purging and emesis worsened the edema and other symptoms. It shows the principle of treating the new disease (surging qi, cough) first and the original water disease afterward.

- For 衛 in 当先攻撃衛気, most other sources read 衝 ("surging qi"), which also fits the context; the translation follows it.
- The base reads 象苦奔豚; it is emended to 若 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- Xu Bin cites Ling Gui Wei Gan tang from the chapter on phlegm-rheum and cough as a formula to stop the surging qi.

# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is floating and slow. A floating pulse means heat; a slow pulse means submersion. When heat and submersion contend, it is called sinking. The instep yang pulse is floating and rapid. A floating pulse means heat; a rapid pulse means stopping. When heat and stopping contend, it is called hiding. When sinking and hiding contend, it is called water. Sinking means the network vessels are vacuous; hiding means urination is difficult. When vacuity and difficulty contend, water runs into the skin, and there is water [disease].

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section discusses how regular water forms. The human body moves vigorously without cease, accomplishing the work of clouds moving and rain falling. So human sweat is named after the rain of heaven and earth, and human qi after its swift wind. The inch opening governs the upper part, like the way of heaven that must descend to aid and be bright; hence "yin is born of yang." The instep yang pulse governs the lower part, like the axis of the earth that must rise and revolve; hence "defensive qi arises from the lower burner."

Now the inch pulse is floating and slow. Floating governs heat, yet slowness also appears; slow means the original qi is submerged below. Both a heat pulse and a submersion pulse appear, so the heat is vacuity heat and the submersion is true submersion; hence "when heat and submersion contend, it is called sinking," meaning the original qi that should descend to aid sinks and does not rise again. Now the instep yang pulse is floating and rapid. Floating governs heat, yet rapidity also appears; rapid means the defensive qi stops below. Both a heat pulse and a stopping pulse appear: as for guest qi it is heat, as for true qi it is stopping; hence "when heat and stopping contend, it is called hiding," meaning the defensive qi that should rise is hidden and cannot ascend. What descends from above does not return and finally sinks; what rises from below stops and remains hidden long; then the revolving qi is nearly extinguished. When extinguished, yin water takes advantage; hence "when sinking and hiding contend, it is called water," showing it is not merely guest water.

Fearing that people would not understand sinking and hiding, it continues: the network vessels are where yang essence and yin qi come and go; when the yang qi of the inch sinks below, the network vessels are vacuous. Urination is where the waterway exits; when the true qi of the instep yang stops below, surplus qi is fire, and when fire-heat is severe, urination is difficult. Then above it cannot move its water and below it cannot expel its water; how can water be prevented from running wild and producing disordered evil? Hence "when vacuity and difficulty contend, water runs into the skin, and there is water." Water is the body's yin qi combining with water-rheum and overflowing sideways. The two meanings of sinking and hiding are both seen in a floating pulse. Who but one truly enlightened about the ascent and descent of heaven and earth and the way of yin and yang could say a single word of this? This is why Zhongjing is the teacher of ten thousand generations.

## Notes

A clause explaining the formation of regular water by combining the inch and instep yang pulses.

- The floating, slow inch pulse (heat and submersion) is named "sinking," the floating, rapid instep yang pulse (heat and stopping) "hiding," and when sinking and hiding contend, water results; with vacuity of the network vessels and difficult urination, water overflows into the skin.
- Xu Bin explains with the analogy of clouds and rain in heaven and earth and human qi and sweat.

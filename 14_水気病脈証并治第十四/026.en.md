# Clause 26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold limbs with skin edema: Puhui san governs it (the formula appears above).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Note: for skin edema there was earlier the main text "its pulse is also floating," the extended statement "no aversion to cold, like generalized impediment," and the text "twitching of the limbs," all leading to the Fangji Fuling tang (Boibukuryoto) formula. Here it again says "cold limbs with skin edema." This section follows the meaning of "a deep pulse belongs to Shaoyin": skin edema originally belongs to the skin, but with cold limbs the disease seems rooted in the kidney; so Puhui san is given separately to govern it. Puhui san is the ash of cattail mats with huashi (kasseki), taking its resolving, disinhibiting, cool, and slippery qualities to discharge the kidney pathogen; it is said specifically of Shaoyin water combined with skin edema that cannot bear excessive warming.

Discussion: Skin edema is originally wind entering the skin, but this comes after the discussion of Shaoyin water because of the coldness of the limbs. Interior water, not being wind edema, is generalized edema; because wind has entered the interior it is not comparable to stony edema nor to wind edema, so its name was specially changed to "interior." How clear, like a hanging mirror, is the subtlety of pattern identification in the wording and naming! As for the use of medicines, for pregnancy with water qi, heavy body, difficult urination, shivering aversion to cold, and dizziness on rising, Kuizi Fuling tang is used; it seems usable for regular and stony edema too, but it is not made the mainstay, meaning that when the flesh swells and the momentum is like a prairie fire, petty percolating and lubricating cannot accomplish anything. Later worthies' moxibustion at Water Divide, Yuyuliang wan (Uyoryogan), and Cheniu Bawei wan as aftercare all hit the mark every time and may be said to supplement what earlier people did not reach. But when the water bursts its banks sideways, it is like heaven and earth sinking, and one cannot cling to ordinary principles; so Zihe had Shenyou wan and Daoshui wan, used in the hope of a lucky chance. Always, black lips mean damage to the liver, a flattened supraclavicular fossa damage to the heart, a protruding umbilicus damage to the spleen, a flat back damage to the lung, and flat, full soles damage to the kidney; the five damages are incurable, though occasionally some recover. But how can one rely on these as the mainstay? So although Zhongjing, at critical moments at the bedside, did not necessarily refrain from drastic formulas, in writing his book and giving formulas he generally did not include them. His establishment of methods is rigorous.

## Notes

A clause using Puhui san for skin edema accompanied by cold limbs. The formula is in the chapter on wasting-thirst, inhibited urination, and strangury.

- For the note "appears above," most other sources read "appears under wasting-thirst."
- Xu Bin's "Discussion" addresses the treatment of water disease in general, mentioning later moxibustion, Yuyuliang wan, and the drastic purgatives of Zhang Congzheng (Zihe).

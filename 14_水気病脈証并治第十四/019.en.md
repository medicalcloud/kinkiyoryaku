# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: The pulse at the inch opening is sunken and slow. Sunken means water; slow means cold. When cold and water contend, the instep yang pulse is hidden, and water and grain are not transformed. When the spleen qi declines, there is duck-stool diarrhea; when the stomach qi declines, the body swells. When the shaoyang pulse is low and the shaoyin pulse fine, in men urination is inhibited, in women the menses do not flow. The menses are blood; when blood is not free, it becomes water. This is called the blood aspect.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of regular water leaning toward the lower burner. The earlier "inch pulse floating and slow" was heat and submersion contending, named sinking. Here it is sunken and slow; sunken is water, slow is cold. When water and cold contend, the instep yang pulse is naturally depressed and hidden, and yin cold acts so that grain cannot be transformed. But there is a slight distinction: the spleen qi governs the interior, so when it declines there is duck-stool diarrhea; the stomach qi governs the exterior, so when it declines the body swells. In addition, the shaoyang pulse is low — shaoyang being the gallbladder pulse at the left bar — and the shaoyin pulse fine — shaoyin being the kidney pulse at the left cubit. Low means low and weak; fine means faint and depleted. Liver and kidney govern the lower burner, so in men urination is inhibited and in women the menses do not flow. The menses are blood. Men too belong to blood, but only women have menses as evidence. So we know that through the blood aspect not being free, obstruction gradually builds and water disease forms. Both signs and pulse are in the lower burner, which governs yin and blood; hence "blood aspect." It is the same for men and women.

## Notes

A clause using the inch, instep yang, shaoyang, and shaoyin pulses to describe regular water leaning toward the lower burner and "blood aspect" water arising from stagnant blood.

- For 䳱溏, most other sources read 鶩溏 ("duck-stool diarrhea").
- After this clause, Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*) and Daizhige have an additional clause ("Question: There are blood-aspect and water-aspect [diseases]; why? ..." — menses stopping first and water disease afterward is the blood aspect, hard to treat; water disease first and menses stopping afterward is the water aspect, easy to treat), which the base lacks.

# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: The pulse at the inch opening is deep and slow. Deep means water; slow means cold. When cold and water contend, the anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse is hidden, and water and grain are not transformed. When the spleen qi declines, there is duck-stool diarrhea; when the stomach qi declines, the body swells. When the Shaoyang pulse is low and the Shaoyin pulse thready, in men urination is inhibited, in women the menses do not flow. The menses are blood; when blood is not free, it becomes water. This is called the blood aspect.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of generalized edema leaning toward the lower jiao. The earlier "cun pulse floating and slow" was heat and submersion contending, named sinking. Here it is deep and slow; deep is water, slow is cold. When water and cold contend, the anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse is naturally depressed and hidden, and yin cold acts so that grain cannot be transformed. But there is a slight distinction: the spleen qi governs the interior, so when it declines there is duck-stool diarrhea; the stomach qi governs the exterior, so when it declines the body swells. In addition, the Shaoyang pulse is low — Shaoyang being the gallbladder pulse at the left guan — and the Shaoyin pulse thready — Shaoyin being the kidney pulse at the left chi. Low means low and weak; thready means feeble and depleted. Liver and kidney govern the lower jiao, so in men urination is inhibited and in women the menses do not flow. The menses are blood. Men too belong to blood, but only women have menses as evidence. So we know that through the blood aspect not being free, obstruction gradually builds and water disease forms. Both signs and pulse are in the lower jiao, which governs yin and blood; hence "blood aspect." It is the same for men and women.

## Notes

A clause using the cun, Fuyang, Shaoyang, and Shaoyin pulses to describe generalized edema leaning toward the lower jiao and "blood aspect" water arising from stagnant blood.

- For 䳱溏, most other sources read 鶩溏 ("duck-stool diarrhea").
- After this clause, Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*) and Daizhige have an additional clause ("Question: There are blood-aspect and water-aspect [diseases]; why? ..." — menses stopping first and water disease afterward is the blood aspect, hard to treat; water disease first and menses stopping afterward is the water aspect, easy to treat), which the base lacks.

# Clause 25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In water disease, if the pulse is sunken and small, it belongs to shaoyin; if floating, it is wind. Vacuous distension without water is qi water; inducing sweating cures it at once. For a sunken pulse, Mahuang Fuzi tang (Ephedra and Aconite Decoction) is appropriate; for a floating one, Xingzi tang (Apricot Kernel Decoction) is appropriate.

Mahuang Fuzi tang formula

Mahuang 3 liang; gancao 2 liang; fuzi 1 piece (blast-fried).

Of the three ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, remove the froth on top, add the other medicinals, boil down to 2.5 sheng, and take eight-tenths [other sources: 8 ge] warm, three times a day.

Xingzi tang (not seen; probably Mahuang Xingren Gancao Shigao tang)

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Note: Zhongjing gave formulas earlier for wind water, skin water, and interior water, but none for so-called stone water. Looking at the formulas given, they seem to address the hand and foot taiyang and taiyin, while interior water and acute wind also address yangming with shigao. Here he specially sets out separately, "in water disease, if the pulse is sunken and small, it belongs to shaoyin," and immediately follows with "for a sunken pulse, Mahuang Fuzi tang is appropriate." Then might this formula be the main formula for so-called stone water? Then, right after the Mahuang Fuzi Gancao formula, it says "for a floating pulse, Xingzi tang is appropriate." Since a floating pulse is not treated with the earlier wind-water or skin-water formulas, does it not mean that the Xingzi formula is for regular water that occasionally has a floating pulse, becoming wind water, for which this formula should be used? The Mahuang Fuzi Gancao formula is essentially mahuang and gancao; because it is shaoyin, fuzi is added to release the true yang of the dragon fire and cooperate with mahuang and gancao to open the long-eroded yin. The Xingzi formula: since metal is imprisoned and cannot move water, the floating pulse blames the heat of lung metal, and it drains qi to discharge the repletion of water. Vacuous swelling without water is of the wind kind, what is called "wind and water contend; when qi is strong it becomes water"; so it too is treated mainly by sweating.

[Xu Bin's text:] ... 2.5 sheng, take 8 ge warm, three times a day. Xingzi tang (Lin Yi says: not seen; probably Mahuang Xingren Gancao Shi[gao tang]).

## Notes

A clause distinguishing a sunken, small pulse (shaoyin) from a floating one (wind) in water disease, using Mahuang Fuzi tang for the sunken and Xingzi tang for the floating, and stating that vacuous distension without water (qi water) is cured by sweating.

- Mahuang Fuzi tang (mahuang, gancao, fuzi) has the same composition as Mahuang Fuzi Gancao tang in the shaoyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (with different amounts).
- The formula of Xingzi tang has not survived; the interlinear note (by the Song editors) suggests it may be Mahuang Xingren Gancao Shigao tang.
- Xu Bin considers Mahuang Fuzi tang the main formula for stone water, for which the text gives no formula.
- The base reads 小陰 and lacks 発; these are emended to 少陰 and 発其汗 because all other sources so read (see the emendation notes).

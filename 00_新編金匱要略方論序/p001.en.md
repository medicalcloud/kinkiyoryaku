# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhang Zhongjing wrote the *Shanghan zabing lun* (Treatise on Cold Damage and Miscellaneous Diseases) in sixteen fascicles altogether. In the present age only the *Shanghan lun* in ten fascicles is transmitted; the book on miscellaneous diseases has not been seen, though one or two of its items are recorded in the formularies of various authors. When the Hanlin academician Wang Zhu was in the imperial libraries, he found among worm-eaten bamboo slips Zhongjing's *Jingui yuhan yaolüe fang* in three fascicles: the first discussed cold damage, the second discussed miscellaneous diseases, and the third recorded the formulas, together with treatments for women. He copied it and transmitted it among the literati, but only a few families had it. Formulas and patterns that matched each other were tried on people, and their effects were like those of a god. Yet sometimes there were patterns without formulas, or formulas without patterns, so that for rescuing illness and treating disease it was incomplete. The state commanded scholar-officials to collate and correct medical books. Your servant [Sun] Qi first collated the *Shanghan lun*, next collated the *Jingui yuhan jing*, and now has also finished collating this book. Accordingly, each formula has been placed under its pattern so that it is easy to look up and use in an emergency. Further, formulas scattered among various authors have been collected and appended to the end of each chapter to broaden its methods. Because the cold-damage part is lengthy [base: 支多] and abridged, we took from miscellaneous diseases onward, ending with dietary prohibitions: twenty-five chapters in all, with 262 formulas after removing duplicates, arranged into three fascicles, upper, middle, and lower, and named according to the old title *Jingui fanglun*.

Your servant Qi once read the biography of Hua Tuo in the *Records of Wei*, which says: "He brought out a book in one fascicle and said, 'This book can save lives.'" Yet whenever I look at the diseases Hua Tuo treated, most favored the strange and marvelous and did not accord with the classics of the sages. Your servant Qi holds that what saves lives must be the book of Zhongjing. Great indeed is the sage method of the Fiery Emperor Shennong, which has come down to our flourishing age! Respectfully, His Majesty has grandly inherited the great succession, caring for and nurturing [base: 肓] the common people, promulgating formularies to relieve suffering, so that harmonious qi overflows and all things are brought into harmony.

## Notes

The preface written when the Northern Song Bureau for Revising Medical Texts collated and presented the *Jingui yaolüe* (the Song officials' preface). There is no commentary by Xu Bin.

- It describes how the book came about: Wang Zhu found the *Jingui yuhan yaolüe fang* in three fascicles in the imperial libraries, and from its second and third fascicles (miscellaneous diseases and formulas) the editors placed the formulas under their patterns, added formulas from other authors as appended formulas, and compiled three fascicles of twenty-five chapters and 262 formulas.
- For *Shanghan zabing lun*, the other sources read *Shanghan zubing lun*.
- For the base's 支多 the other sources read 文多 ("lengthy"), and for 撫肓 they read 撫育 ("care for and nurture"); the translation follows them.

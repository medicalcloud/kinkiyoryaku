# §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhongjing's *Jingui* records formulas from Qi Bo and the Yellow Emperor's *Suwen* and *Nanjing*, nearly a thousand fascicles. Troubled that they were jumbled and burdensome, and hard to obtain even when sought, he traveled throughout the nine regions of China and the borderlands, gathering the rare and unusual, collecting what had been lost, and selecting the sinew and marrow of the various classics to make a single volume of formulas and discussions. For rescuing and treating violent diseases, it lets one know their proper sequence. These medicines and minerals were all made by immortals; taking them, one will surely be free from early or untimely death in the future. Even for cases treated too late, or harmed by a physician's mistakes, they are fortunately set out in detail. Academician Wang Zhu of the Song found this book among worm-eaten bamboo slips, and Lin Yi and others collated and reprinted it, but since the Yuan and Jin dynasties few in the world have seen it; where various authors record the *Jingui*'s formulas and treatments, they mostly obtained them from other books. Otherwise, why is there not a single person who can tell its whole story? Alas, I, a humble person, have fortunately been able to read it reverently; how could I not treasure it? Recorded by Yu Qiao, the True Recluse of Shannan.

## Notes

Yu Qiao's colophon. Placed after the Song officials' preface, it tells the origin of the *Jingui*, notes that the book had rarely been seen since the Yuan and Jin, and records the writer's joy at being able to read it.

- Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*) and the other sources do not have this colophon.
- 㒹 in 㒹末 is read as 顛 ("whole story").

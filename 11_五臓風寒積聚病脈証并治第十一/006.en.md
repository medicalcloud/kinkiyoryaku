# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The dead zang organ [pulse] of the liver: superficially it is weak; on pressure it is like a rope and does not come, or it is crooked like a crawling snake; the patient dies.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Above the high vertex, only wind can reach. Wind by nature shakes upward, so the head and eyes twitch. The liver vessel rises through the diaphragm; now there is pathogen in the ribsides, so they hurt. The liver governs the sinews; when dried by wind the sinews tense, so one stoops, like a tree bent by wind: the root is weak and the pathogen strong, so it cannot resist. The postnatal basis is the spleen and stomach. When wood pathogen is exuberant, earth loses; sweet benefits the spleen, so craving sweet is self-rescue. The *Neijing* says, "The liver suffers from tension; quickly eat sweet to relax it," which relaxes wood to help earth.

The four limbs belong to the spleen and are the root of all yang, but the two arms are like the body of a branch. When struck by cold, the wood qi is afflicted, so they cannot be raised. Cold is a yin pathogen, so the yin receives it; when the yin receives pathogen and becomes hot, the liver qi pours upward along the meridian, going behind the throat and entering the nasopharynx. The root of the tongue is where the qi vessels pass, so the tongue root is dry. Also, the spleen vessel connects with the tongue root, and when liver qi is exuberant, the spleen vessel also becomes hot. The gallbladder governs sighing, and liver and gallbladder are exterior and interior; when the liver is ill, the gallbladder is depressed, and when depressed one sighs. Hence the heart and ribsides hurt and one cannot turn over, because a divergent vessel of the gallbladder passes through the heart and along the ribsides. The ascending branch of the liver vessel flanks the stomach and passes through the diaphragm; when ill there is retching counterflow, so on eating one vomits. When there is vomiting counterflow, heat lodges there, the Shaoyang qi is depressed, and sweat comes out.

The liver lies below; weak when taken superficially is the picture of floating wood. Like a rope on pressure and not coming means it has the picture but cannot complete its arrival. Crooked like a crawling snake is what the *Neijing* calls "the liver not wiry has no stomach qi"; the organ's own pulse appears, so the patient dies.

Discussion: The above says that when wind and cold are contracted and the yin of the liver is damaged, the wood qi cannot spread its luxuriance, and everything in the body that relies on yin for nourishment produces various changed signs like these. This details the internal picture of the liver struck by wind and cold. What the *Neijing* says — "When the liver is struck by wind, there is much sweating, aversion to wind, frequent sorrow, a bluish color, dry throat, frequent anger, and at times hatred of women; the diagnosis is below the eyes, the color green" — speaks of the external picture of the liver receiving exterior pathogen.

## Notes

A clause on the dead zang organ pulse of the liver. A pulse weak superficially, hard like a rope on pressure without arriving, or crooked like a crawling snake indicates exhaustion of the genuine qi of the liver and death.

- Xu Bin's commentary explains the liver clauses of units 4 through 6 together.

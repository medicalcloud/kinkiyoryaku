# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: Among the sections on exhaustion of the triple burner, exhaustion of the upper burner causes frequent belching; what does this mean? The master said: The upper burner receives [the qi of] the middle burner; when [its] qi is not yet harmonious and cannot digest grain, there is belching. When the lower burner is exhausted, there is enuresis and fecal incontinence; its qi is not harmonious and cannot restrain itself. It need not be treated; in time it will recover.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The triple burner is the pathway of water and grain, where qi begins and ends. The upper burner is at the upper opening of the stomach, governed at Chest Center; the middle burner is at the middle stomach duct, governed beside the umbilicus; the lower burner is at the upper opening of the bladder, governed one inch below the umbilicus. Internal diseases must be treated by distinguishing the three burners, hence the section names; a section name denotes what it manages. Exhaustion is exhaustion of qi. Belching (噫) is like eructation without a sour odor, a faint sound like the character 意. But belching is a sign of the spleen, yet here it is put in the section on upper-burner exhaustion; so [the questioner] doubted and asked, not knowing that the central qi actually commands the triple burner. Hence "the upper burner receives qi from the middle burner; when [its] qi is not yet harmonious and cannot digest grain." Then the stomach is ill; when ill, the spleen cannot disperse essence upward to the lung; the qi received by the upper burner is exhausted, and the qi of the disease comes out above as belching. This is why belching is placed in the section on upper-burner exhaustion.

From this it discusses how disharmony of the middle burner can also affect the lower burner. Urine and stool are governed by the lower burner, but if the central qi is not harmonious and cannot restrain itself, it can also make them lose their regularity, with enuresis and fecal incontinence. But the lower burner in fact obeys the middle burner; when the middle burner qi is harmonized, the original qi gradually recovers and the two excretions are regulated. Hence "it need not be treated; in time it will recover," meaning the lower burner need not be treated. If enuresis and fecal incontinence truly belonged to kidney vacuity of the lower burner, one should promptly augment the source of fire to dispel the shadow of yin; how could one say it need not be treated?

Discussion: Zhongjing, discussing lung atrophy, says, "Spitting foamy drool without cough, the person is not thirsty and will surely have enuresis and frequent urination; the reason is that the upper part is vacuous and cannot restrain the lower; this is cold in the lung." So we know that the inability to restrain stool discussed here also means the upper part being vacuous and unable to restrain the lower. But since the middle burner can cause disease in both upper and lower burners, why do the upper and lower diseases not arise together, but either belching or enuresis and incontinence? Is it not that if the ancestral qi of the upper burner is truly strong, the disharmonious qi of the middle burner cannot invade above and affects only below, and if the lower burner is replete, it cannot invade below and affects only above? So "the upper burner is exhausted" means the upper part too was vacuous first, and "the lower burner is exhausted" means the lower part too was vacuous first. But since these are not original diseases of the upper and lower burners, both are explained by disharmony of the central qi, to distinguish them from diseases arising in the upper and lower burners themselves.

## Notes

A question-and-answer clause on the signs when the qi of the triple burner is exhausted (belching in the upper burner, enuresis and fecal incontinence in the lower).

- Both arise from disharmony of the middle-burner qi and recover naturally when the middle burner recovers, so no treatment is needed.
- The base writes 噍 for 焦; it is emended because all other sources read 焦 (see the emendation notes).

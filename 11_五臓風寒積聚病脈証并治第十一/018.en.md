# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: Among the sections on exhaustion of the sanjiao, exhaustion of the upper jiao causes frequent belching; what does this mean? The master said: The upper jiao receives [the qi of] the middle jiao; when [its] qi is not yet harmonious and cannot digest grain, there is belching. When the lower jiao is exhausted, there is enuresis and fecal incontinence; its qi is not harmonious and cannot restrain itself. It need not be treated; in time it will recover.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The sanjiao is the pathway of water and grain, where qi begins and ends. The upper jiao is at the upper opening of the stomach, governed at Chest Center; the middle jiao is at the middle stomach duct, governed beside the umbilicus; the lower jiao is at the upper opening of the bladder, governed one cun below the umbilicus. Internal diseases must be treated by distinguishing the three jiao, hence the section names; a section name denotes what it manages. Exhaustion is exhaustion of qi. Belching (噫) is like eructation without a sour odor, a faint sound like the character 意. But belching is a sign of the spleen, yet here it is put in the section on upper-jiao exhaustion; so [the questioner] doubted and asked, not knowing that the central qi actually commands the sanjiao. Hence "the upper jiao receives qi from the middle jiao; when [its] qi is not yet harmonious and cannot digest grain." Then the stomach is ill; when ill, the spleen cannot disperse essence upward to the lung; the qi received by the upper jiao is exhausted, and the qi of the disease comes out above as belching. This is why belching is placed in the section on upper-jiao exhaustion.

From this it discusses how disharmony of the middle jiao can also affect the lower jiao. Urine and stool are governed by the lower jiao, but if the central qi is not harmonious and cannot restrain itself, it can also make them lose their regularity, with enuresis and fecal incontinence. But the lower jiao in fact obeys the middle jiao; when the middle jiao qi is harmonized, the original qi gradually recovers and the two excretions are regulated. Hence "it need not be treated; in time it will recover," meaning the lower jiao need not be treated. If enuresis and fecal incontinence truly belonged to kidney deficiency of the lower jiao, one should promptly augment the source of fire to dispel the shadow of yin; how could one say it need not be treated?

Discussion: Zhongjing, discussing atrophy of the lung lobes, says, "Spitting foamy drool without cough, the person is not thirsty and will surely have enuresis and frequent urination; the reason is that the upper part is deficient and cannot restrain the lower; this is cold in the lung." So we know that the inability to restrain stool discussed here also means the upper part being deficient and unable to restrain the lower. But since the middle jiao can cause disease in both upper and lower jiao, why do the upper and lower diseases not arise together, but either belching or enuresis and incontinence? Is it not that if the ancestral qi of the upper jiao is truly strong, the disharmonious qi of the middle jiao cannot invade above and affects only below, and if the lower jiao is in excess, it cannot invade below and affects only above? So "the upper jiao is exhausted" means the upper part too was deficient first, and "the lower jiao is exhausted" means the lower part too was deficient first. But since these are not original diseases of the upper and lower jiao, both are explained by disharmony of the central qi, to distinguish them from diseases arising in the upper and lower jiao themselves.

## Notes

A question-and-answer clause on the signs when the qi of the sanjiao is exhausted (belching in the upper jiao, enuresis and fecal incontinence in the lower).

- Both arise from disharmony of the middle-jiao qi and recover naturally when the middle jiao recovers, so no treatment is needed.
- The base writes 噍 for 焦; it is emended because all other sources read 焦 (see the emendation notes).

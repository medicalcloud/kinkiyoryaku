# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When weeping caused by evil (邪哭) makes the ethereal and corporeal souls restless, the blood and qi are scant. Scant blood and qi belongs to the heart. When the heart qi is vacuous, the person is fearful; when he closes his eyes and tries to sleep, he dreams of traveling far, the essence-spirit separates and scatters, and the souls wander at random. Decline of yin qi produces withdrawal (*dian*); decline of yang qi produces mania (*kuang*).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The earlier section on heart damage spoke of the heart damaged by a guest evil, and once damaged the signs and pulse differ from those of the initial strike. This speaks separately of those whose blood and qi are vacuous and who contract evil because the heart qi is insufficient. When evil enters the body, the physical body should fall ill; why then do the souls become restless at once? It is that there are cases where, as soon as the evil enters, the souls become restless; this is due to scant blood and qi, and the reason for the scantiness again belongs to vacuity of the heart. He wishes people who meet this pattern to make calming the spirit and supplementing the heart the mainstay. Closing the eyes and dreaming of traveling far, with the souls wandering at random, describes the picture of restlessness; the separation and scattering of essence-spirit annotates the root of the wandering. The heart is the office of the sovereign; once it loses its command, in those with yin vacuity the evil first takes advantage of the yin, producing withdrawal, and in those with yang vacuity it first takes advantage of the yang, producing mania. Withdrawal and mania differ, but they are the same in the heart losing its mastery. But all this speaks of people whose other viscera have no disease, showing that among those who contract evil there are such differences; it is not the proper disease of those struck by wind or cold. So it is spoken of separately.

## Notes

A clause describing mental instability caused by evil when the heart's blood and qi are scant: anxiety, fear, dreams of traveling far, restless souls; decline of yin qi produces withdrawal (a depressive mental illness), decline of yang qi produces mania (an agitated mental illness).

- The meaning of the opening 邪哭 is unclear; Xu Bin's edition suggests 哭 may be an error for 入 ("when evil enters and makes the souls restless").
- "Decline of yin produces withdrawal, decline of yang produces mania" is the reverse of the *Nanjing*'s difficulty 20 ("double yang is mania, double yin is withdrawal"), and some regard it as an error.

# Clause 17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The dead-zang organ [pulse] of the kidney: superficially it is hard; on pressure it is chaotic like a rolling pellet, and extends further down into the chi; the patient dies.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The kidney pulse governs stone. Hard superficially means it does not sink but beats outward; the yang has left the yin position. Chaotic like a rolling pellet on pressure means the body of stone has changed into agitated movement, the true yang bursting out. As for extending further down into the chi, on pressure a pulse form can still be seen about an cun behind the chi. A long pulse seems to be surplus, but [such a view] does not know that the kidney pulse is originally deep, and a healthy person has no pulse form below the chi; because earth can restrain water, *kan* (water) flows within the earth. Now what should move hidden comes out above instead; the original qi is not secure and deserts outward; the kidney is about to be cut off. Hence death.

Discussion: The distinctions of wind and cold of the five zang organs aim to make people examine carefully when treating wind attack and cold-strike, seemingly supplementing what the discussions of wind attack and cold-strike lack; so no formulas are given. Only liver distension, lower back pain due to cold dampness, and spleen constraint have formulas, because they are diseases that linger and are particularly distinctive.

## Notes

A clause on the dead-zang organ pulse of the kidney. A pulse hard superficially, chaotic like a rolling pellet on pressure, and extending below the chi indicates the genuine qi of the kidney deserting outward, and death.

- Xu Bin's "Discussion" explains why the five-zang organs wind and cold clauses have no formulas.

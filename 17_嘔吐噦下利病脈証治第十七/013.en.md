# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When there is retching and vomiting with the disease above the diaphragm, and afterward one longs for water, it has resolved; give it [water] quickly. For one who longs for water, use the following formula.

Zhuling san (Choreisan; Polyporus Powder)

Zhuling (chorei), fuling (bukuryo), and baizhu (byakujutsu), equal amounts.

Pound the three ingredients above into a powder and take a square-inch spoonful with a drink, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Retching and vomiting with signs of the heart and abdomen is not originally the essential pattern of vomiting. Speaking of the usual, the disease is above the diaphragm, generally pathogenic heat binding with retained fluid. When one comes to long for water, the retained fluid pathogen has gone; hence "resolved." "Give it quickly" is for fear that one cannot bear the dry pathogen. But the original yang has not yet recovered, and one must guard against lodged fluid arising again. So zhuling, removing water, is the sovereign, and fuling and baizhu cultivate the healthy qi. Shengjiang (shokyo) and banxia (hange) are not used because the retching has stopped, and diffusing might instead stir deficient qi; bringing down counterflow and dissolving phlegm is also not urgent. Zhuling san formula.

Pound the three ingredients above into a powder and take a square-inch spoonful with a drink, three times a day.

## Notes

A clause stating that longing for water after vomiting due to retained fluid above the diaphragm is a sign of impending recovery; water is given, and Zhuling san is used to prevent retained fluid from collecting again.

- For "use the following formula," the other sources read "Zhuling san governs it."

# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dry retching, vomiting counterflow, and spitting of foamy drool: use the following formula.

Banxia Ganjiang san (Hangekankyosan; Pinellia and Dried Ginger Powder)

Banxia (hange) and ganjiang (kankyo), equal amounts.

Pound the two ingredients above into a powder; take a square-inch spoonful, decoct with 1.5 sheng of fermented water (jiangshui) down to 7 ge, and take it in a single dose.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Compared with the earlier clause on dry retching, spitting of foamy drool, and headache, this merely lacks headache and adds the two words "vomiting counterflow." There Zhuyu tang (Shuyuto) is used, here Banxia Ganjiang tang; why? Cold in the upper jiao with much drool in the mouth is the same. But earlier there was headache: turbid yin counterflowing upward blocks the pathogen in the head, hence pain, just as turbid yin counterflowing upward blocks the pathogen in the chest, hence fullness. So both used renshen (ninjin), shengjiang (shokyo), and dazao (taiso) to assist yang, and the bitter warmth of wuzhuyu (goshuyu) to bring down turbid yin. Here there is vomiting counterflow, clearly heavy cold in the stomach causing unceasing vomiting counterflow. So renshen is not used; ganjiang alone regulates the center and banxia brings down counterflow. That is, compared with the earlier upward counterflow of turbid yin, the pathogenic cold is the same but differs in height; it has not reached the point of blocking the pathogen in the head or chest, so the deficiency is also not yet severe.

## Notes

A clause using Banxia Ganjiang san for dry retching, vomiting counterflow, and spitting of foamy drool.

- Xu Bin compares it with unit 9 (Zhuyu tang), which also has spitting of foamy drool, and explains that here cold in the stomach predominates and deficiency is not severe, so renshen is not used.
- For "use the following formula," the other sources read "Banxia Ganjiang san governs it."

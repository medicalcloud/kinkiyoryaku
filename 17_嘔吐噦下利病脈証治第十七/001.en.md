# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When one who retches frequently has abscess pus, the retching must not be treated; when the pus is exhausted, recovery follows by itself.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The causes of frequent retching differ: guest cold damages the stomach, or phlegm congests and qi counterflows; surplus qi is fire, so the *Neijing* says, "All vomiting and sourness belong to heat." So moving phlegm, bringing down counterflow, clearing fire, and warming the center are all acceptable. But if there is abscess pus, the heat is in the ying-nutrient aspect, not the qi aspect; retching because of it means the toxin is exuberant. Treating it with the methods for retching — moving phlegm and bringing down counterflow — is certainly useless, and when accumulated heat has become toxin, could it bear warm, hot medicinals? Hence "the retching must not be treated." But even without treating the retching, since it is not due to qi but to heat toxin of the ying-nutrient aspect, when the pus is exhausted the pathogen declines, and when the pathogen declines the retching stops. Hence "when the pus is exhausted, recovery follows by itself."

## Notes

The opening clause of the chapter on retching, vomiting, hiccup, and diarrhea. One who retches because of an internal suppurating abscess must not be given treatment to stop the retching; when the pus has been vomited out, recovery follows by itself.

- The idea is that the retching is a reaction expelling pus and should not be suppressed.

# Clause 25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diarrhea with reverse-cold of the hands and feet and no pulse: apply moxibustion [base: 炙 "roast"]. If they do not warm, if the pulse does not return, and instead there is slight dyspnea: death. When the Shaoyin [pulse] yields to the fuyang [pulse], it is favorable.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that death from diarrhea must begin with expiry of the kidney; if it has not expired but is only weak, that again is the ordinary course. Diarrhea reaching reverse-cold of the hands and feet means the yang qi in the spleen has long been depleted and the true yang in the kidney has deserted downward; so, like one struck by cold, the hands and feet are reverse-cold with no pulse, and the qi of life is nearly extinguished. Yet at this time, as the healthy qi is about to expire, the pathogenic qi is also about to expire, so moxibustion is applied to restore the yang in the kidney. If the hands and feet still do not warm and the pulse does not return, the healthy qi has completely deserted. And slight dyspnea is the already-lost true yang coming out above: Shaoyin has already expired but instead displays an image of surplus — clearly a lamp about to go out flaring up again — so there is death. Now the diarrhea pattern is pathogenic water overwhelming earth; the Shaoyin pulse governs water and the fuyang pulse governs earth. So Shaoyin yielding to fuyang means water losing and earth winning, which is instead favorable. 負 means to lose; when they restrain and harm each other, it is called 負.

## Notes

A clause stating that diarrhea with cold limbs and no palpable pulse is treated with moxibustion, and death follows if the limbs do not warm, the pulse does not return, and slight dyspnea appears instead. The same clause appears in the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 362).

- The base's 炙 ("roast") is 灸 ("moxibustion") in most other sources and is read accordingly.
- The Shaoyin pulse (Taixi at the inner ankle) being weaker than the fuyang pulse (Chongyang on the instep) is understood as water yielding to earth, which is favorable.

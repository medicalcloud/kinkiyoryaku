# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse at the inch opening is feeble and rapid. Feeble means there is no qi; with no qi the ying-nutrient is deficient; with the ying-nutrient deficient the blood is insufficient; with the blood insufficient the chest is cold.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This section traces the cause of deficiency cold in the stomach and verifies it by the cun pulse. The inch opening governs the upper jiao; feeble means little original yang qi in the chest. The ying-nutrient qi follows the wei-defensive qi, and blood is the ying-nutrient formed into a stream. With no qi to draw and fill the ying-nutrient qi, the ying-nutrient is deficient; deficient, the blood is scant and insufficient, unable to be full as in a healthy person. Though yin fire flames and shows a rapid picture, the ying-nutrient and wei-defense in the chest are actually deficient and the original yang greatly deficient; how could it not be cold?

## Notes

A clause stating that a feeble, rapid cun pulse shows insufficient qi leading to insufficient ying-nutrient blood and cold in the chest, tracing the cause of the previous clause (stomach deficiency cold).

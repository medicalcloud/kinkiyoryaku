# Clause 33 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diarrhea with a deep and slow pulse, the person's face slightly red, slight heat in the body, and diarrhea with clear grain: there will surely be depression and veiling, and it resolves with sweating; the patient will surely have slight heat [other sources: slight reverse-cold]. The reason is that the face bears yang, because there is deficiency below.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of diarrhea with much interior and little exterior; but the pathogen can never ultimately overcome the healthy qi, so though the transmuted signs are many, the disease can resolve. All of it stems from deficiency, and it is not an untreatable pattern. In diarrhea with a deep and slow pulse, it goes without saying that deep is cold and slow is deficiency. But the slightly red face is a trace of yang, and slight heat in the body is the pathogen running to the exterior. Since the exterior is slight and there is diarrhea with clear grain, why will there surely later be depression and veiling, resolution with sweat, and slight reverse-cold? Depression and veiling belong to deficiency cold, and slight reverse-cold is also deficiency cold. Because there is slight heat in the body, the healthy qi is somewhat winning, so resolution with sweat can be predicted, but one cannot guarantee there will be no depression and veiling or no reverse-cold. Therefore it further traces the cause of the slightly red face seen earlier: the so-called "bearing yang" is due to deficiency below.

## Notes

A clause stating that diarrhea with clear grain, a deep slow pulse, a slightly red face, and slight fever resolves through depression-veiling and sweating, the redness of the face being "bearing yang" due to deficiency below. The same clause appears in the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 366).

- For 熱 in "the patient will surely have slight heat," Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*), Daizhige, and Xu Bin's text read 厥 ("reverse-cold"), and Xu Bin's commentary also reads "slight reverse-cold." The *Shanghan lun* also reads "slight reverse-cold."
- For 鬱冒 ("depression and veiling") and 微熱 in "slight heat in the body," the base reads 鬱胃 and 微執, but all other sources read 冒 and 熱, so they have been emended (see the emendations).

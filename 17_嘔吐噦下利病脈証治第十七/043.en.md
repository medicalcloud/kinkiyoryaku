# Clause 43 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

After diarrhea there is further vexation, and on pressure the area below the heart is soft: this is deficiency vexation. Zhizi Chi tang governs it.

Zhizi Chi tang (Gardenia and Fermented Soybean Decoction)

Zhizi (gardenia) 14 pieces; xiangchi (fermented soybean) 4 ge.

Of the two ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water first boil the zhizi down to 2.5 sheng, add the chi, boil down to 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, and divide into two doses. Take one dose warm; if there is vomiting, stop.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Both deficiency and excess can have vexation. In diarrhea it already belongs to the deficiency side, and further, softness below the heart on pressure is not comparable to stuffiness, binding, pain, or fullness. So zhizi and chi are used to provoke light ejection and clear out the heat. Xiangchi governs vexation and oppression and can also regulate the center and bring qi down, and zhizi can further clear depressed fire of the heart, lung, stomach, and large and small intestines.

Discussion: Zhongjing also said, "If there has been slightly sloppy stool from before, taking this decoction cannot cause ejection upward but instead causes discharge downward." Here, for vexation after diarrhea this decoction is specifically used, because slightly sloppy stool from before means habitually weak spleen qi, whereas the diarrhea spoken of here is visiting pathogen taking advantage of the interior, not habitually weak spleen qi, and the area is soft on pressure. So one knows the vexation is diaphragm deficiency: residual pathogen in Taiyang that the body lacks the strength to drive out. Hence light ejection to diffuse and lift it is the subtle point. Zhizi Chi tang formula: zhizi 14 pieces (broken); xiangchi 4 ge (wrapped in silk floss). ## Notes

A clause stating that persistent vexation after diarrhea, with softness below the heart on pressure, is deficiency vexation, treated with Zhizi Chi tang. Nearly the same clause appears in the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 375).

- "Deficiency vexation" refers to vexation from heat remaining in the chest and diaphragm without bound excess pathogen.
- The end of Xu Bin's commentary breaks off.

# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When retching comes first and thirst afterward, it is about to resolve. When thirst comes first and retching afterward, water has lodged below the heart; this belongs to those with rheum. Those who retch are originally thirsty; if now, on the contrary, they are not thirsty, it is because there is propping rheum below the heart; this belongs to propping rheum.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

These two clauses say that retching and thirst always depend on each other, so water can be distinguished by their order, and rheum known by the absence of thirst, showing a method of distinguishing rheum within the treatment of retching. Retching first means there is foul drool within; when the drool is exhausted and thirst comes, the disease qi has resolved. If thirst comes first, one must drink much, and much drink is the same as foul drool, so there is retching; we know water is lodged below the heart, but it is a suddenly arising disease, not necessarily long-standing propping rheum; so it says only "this belongs to those with rheum." But much retching must damage the fluids, so thirst is the inevitable principle for those who retch. If now, on the contrary, there is no thirst, unless there were rheum qi lodged to one side below the heart moistening the dry fire, how could thirst be avoided? But if the rheum were in the central passage, how could it not come out with the retching? So we know this rheum is not in the passage. Hence "this is propping rheum" — propping meaning off to the side, not centered.

## Notes

A clause distinguishing, by the order of vomiting and thirst, cases about to resolve, cases of those with rheum (water lodged below the heart), and cases of propping rheum. The content overlaps with clauses in the chapter on phlegm-rheum and cough (units 28 and 42).

# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When retching comes first and thirst afterward, it is about to resolve. When thirst comes first and retching afterward, water has lodged below the heart; this belongs to those with retained fluid. Those who retch are originally thirsty; if now, on the contrary, they are not thirsty, it is because there is thoracic fluid retention below the heart; this belongs to thoracic fluid retention.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

These two clauses say that retching and thirst always depend on each other, so water can be distinguished by their order, and retained fluid known by the absence of thirst, showing a method of distinguishing retained fluid within the treatment of retching. Retching first means there is foul drool within; when the drool is exhausted and thirst comes, the disease qi has resolved. If thirst comes first, one must drink much, and much drink is the same as foul drool, so there is retching; we know water is lodged below the heart, but it is a suddenly arising disease, not necessarily long-standing thoracic fluid retention; so it says only "this belongs to those with retained fluid." But much retching must damage the fluids, so thirst is the inevitable principle for those who retch. If now, on the contrary, there is no thirst, unless there were retained fluid qi lodged to one side below the heart moistening the dry fire, how could thirst be avoided? But if the retained fluid were in the central passage, how could it not come out with the retching? So we know this retained fluid is not in the passage. Hence "this is thoracic fluid retention" — propping meaning off to the side, not centered.

## Notes

A clause distinguishing, by the order of vomiting and thirst, cases about to resolve, cases of those with retained fluid (water lodged below the heart), and cases of thoracic fluid retention. The content overlaps with clauses in the chapter on phlegm-fluid retention and cough (units 28 and 42).

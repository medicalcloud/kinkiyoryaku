# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse is floating and hesitant. Floating means deficiency; deficiency [other sources: hesitant] damages the spleen; when the spleen is damaged it does not grind. What is eaten in the morning is vomited in the evening, what is eaten in the evening is vomited in the morning, and the lodged grain is not transformed. This is called stomach reflux. If the pulse is tight and hesitant, the disease is difficult to treat.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Vomiting is a disease of the stomach, and the spleen qi communicates with the stomach; the Fuyang is the pulse of spleen and stomach, so it is examined again. The anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse is floating and also hesitant. Earth governs the central region and is neither deep nor floating; now it is very floating, so we know it is deficient. When deficient, the qi of spleen and stomach does not interact, and the spleen yin is damaged and cannot hold its qi firmly; so the pulse is floating and hesitant. When the right is deficient, the basis of fermentation is lost, so it does not grind. Therefore between morning and evening it cannot hold the grain, which lodges untransformed. This is the origin of stomach reflux. But if the pulse is not tight, the stomach qi can still overcome the pathogen. If it is further tight and hesitant — tight being pathogenic cold and hesitant exhaustion of fluids — the right does not overcome the pathogen; hence "difficult to treat."

## Notes

A clause describing stomach reflux by a floating, hesitant anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse, and stating that a tight, hesitant pulse means it is hard to cure.

- Stomach reflux is vomiting in the evening what was eaten in the morning and in the morning what was eaten in the evening, with undigested food, attributed to a damaged spleen unable to grind grain.
- For 虚 in 虚則傷脾, most other sources read 濇 ("hesitant").

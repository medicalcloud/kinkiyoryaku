# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The instep yang pulse is floating and rough. Floating means vacuity; vacuity [other sources: rough] damages the spleen; when the spleen is damaged it does not grind. What is eaten in the morning is vomited in the evening, what is eaten in the evening is vomited in the morning, and the lodged grain is not transformed. This is called stomach reflux. If the pulse is tight and rough, the disease is difficult to treat.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Vomiting is a disease of the stomach, and the spleen qi communicates with the stomach; the instep yang is the pulse of spleen and stomach, so it is examined again. The instep yang pulse is floating and also rough. Earth governs the central region and is neither sunken nor floating; now it is very floating, so we know it is vacuous. When vacuous, the qi of spleen and stomach does not interact, and the spleen yin is damaged and cannot hold its qi firmly; so the pulse is floating and rough. When the right is vacuous, the basis of fermentation is lost, so it does not grind. Therefore between morning and evening it cannot hold the grain, which lodges untransformed. This is the origin of stomach reflux. But if the pulse is not tight, the stomach qi can still overcome the evil. If it is further tight and rough — tight being cold evil and rough exhaustion of fluids — the right does not overcome the evil; hence "difficult to treat."

## Notes

A clause describing stomach reflux by a floating, rough instep yang pulse, and stating that a tight, rough pulse means it is hard to cure.

- Stomach reflux is vomiting in the evening what was eaten in the morning and in the morning what was eaten in the evening, with undigested food, attributed to a damaged spleen unable to grind grain.
- For 虚 in 虚則傷脾, most other sources read 濇 ("rough").

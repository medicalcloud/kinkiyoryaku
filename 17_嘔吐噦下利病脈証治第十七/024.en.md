# Clause 24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diarrhea with a deep and wiry pulse: there is heaviness below. With a large pulse: it has not yet stopped. With a feeble, weak, and rapid pulse: it is about to stop by itself, and even with fever there is no death.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Diarrhea means there is pathogen in the interior, but upper and lower, light and heavy differ, and all are distinguished by the pulse. Suppose the pulse is deep: that is cold; wiry is binding of qi. Deep and wiry means the disease pathogen binds in the lower jiao, so the yang pathway of the lower body does not move and there is heaviness. A large pulse governs deficiency and also governs exuberance of pathogen, so when it is large the diarrhea has not yet stopped. Feeble and weak means the pathogen has declined and the healthy qi has also declined. Rapid is a yang pulse; seen within feebleness and weakness, it means the yang qi is about to recover, so one knows it is about to stop by itself. "Diarrhea with unceasing heat means death" refers to yang perishing outside and yin perishing inside. If the pulse is already feeble, weak, and rapid, the pathogen has gone; when the pathogen has gone and there is fever, even if residual pathogen remains, the healthy qi is about to overcome it; hence "no death."

## Notes

A clause distinguishing by the pulse whether diarrhea has tenesmus, has not yet stopped, or is about to stop naturally. The same clause appears in the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 365).

- From here to unit 35, many clauses overlap with the diarrhea clauses of the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun*.

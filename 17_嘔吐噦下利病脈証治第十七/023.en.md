# Clause 23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the qi of the six fu has expired on the outside, the hands and feet are cold, there is qi ascent, and the legs contract. When the qi of the five zang has expired on the inside, diarrhea cannot be restrained; when the downward discharge is severe, the hands and feet are insensitive.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that whenever a disease becomes critical, the qi of the zang-fu must expire first, and the zang especially govern diarrhea. That a person may have diarrhea for a long time yet live like a normal person is because the qi of the fu and zang has not yet expired. If the qi of the six fu first expires on the outside — the six fu being yang, and yang being what warms the hands and feet and controls the sanjiao — once that qi has expired outside, the hands and feet have no yang to move them and are cold; the chest has no yang to control the yin of the lower jiao, so there is qi ascent; the yang pathway below the legs does not move, so there is yin without yang and the legs contract and cannot stretch. If the qi of the five zang first expires on the inside, the kidney cannot act as the gate of the stomach, and diarrhea cannot be restrained; the extreme of not being restrained is "severe downward discharge," and then the hands and feet have no yin to hold the yang, and when the zang qi do not govern and gather one another, there is insensitivity. Insensitivity means being able neither to stretch nor to contract.

## Notes

A general clause opening the section on diarrhea, describing the signs when the qi of the six fu and of the five zang has expired.

- When the qi of the six fu (yang) expires, there are cold limbs, qi ascent, and contracted legs; when the qi of the five zang (yin) expires, there is unrestrainable diarrhea and numbness of the hands and feet.

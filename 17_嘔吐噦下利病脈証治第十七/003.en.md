# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: A sick person's pulse is rapid; rapid means heat; he should disperse grain and crave food, yet instead he vomits. Why? The master said: Because sweating was induced, the yang became faint and the diaphragm qi vacuous, so the pulse became rapid. This rapidity is guest heat, which cannot disperse grain, because the stomach is vacuous and cold. A wiry pulse means vacuity: the stomach qi has no surplus; what is eaten in the morning is vomited in the evening, and it changes into stomach reflux. The cold is above, yet the physician purged instead, making the pulse wiry; hence it is called vacuity.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This discusses that the pulse of vomiting, arising from mistaken sweating, may at first be rapid, and after mistaken purging becomes wiry. A rapid pulse does not go beyond the two fires, sovereign and ministerial: if the inch is rapid, there are sores of throat, mouth, and tongue, or vomiting of blood, cough and spitting, and lung abscess; if both bars are rapid, stomach fire or liver fire; if the cubit is rapid, yin vacuity or ministerial fire. So [the questioner] was puzzled that "rapid means heat; he should disperse grain and crave drink, yet instead he vomits," believing a rapid pulse must indicate heat, not knowing that vacuity can also make the pulse rapid. Moreover, vomiting is seen; vomiting is a temporary disease of the diaphragm, and with a rapid pulse it is very clear that it is not the two fires. So the cause of the disease is traced: "because sweating was induced." Sweating damages yang and makes yang qi faint. Only when the true yang qi of the body is sufficient does it, like the sun in midheaven, make a person warm and harmonious; when yang is vacuous, dry fire takes advantage. Hence "the diaphragm qi vacuous, so the pulse became rapid." The rapidity is not the original gentle yang, so it is guest heat; guest heat sickens the stomach, so how could it help the stomach disperse grain? It is called heat, but in fact there is no yang to move [the food] downward. Hence "because the stomach is vacuous and cold." If the pulse is further wiry, the yang qi in the stomach is insufficient and bound; hence "the stomach qi has no surplus" — no surplus strength to overcome the grain qi. So eaten in the morning and vomited in the evening shows that the stomach does receive grain but cannot disperse it; so it changes into stomach reflux. Its origin is cold in the upper burner, which originally called for warming the stomach to aid dispersal and conveyance; purging mistakenly again makes the faint yang show a wiry form — what is called "wiry means the defensive qi is bound." Hence "vacuity."

## Notes

A question-and-answer clause describing how mistaken sweating made yang qi vacuous and the pulse rapid, and mistaken purging made it wiry, resulting in stomach reflux (vomiting in the evening what was eaten in the morning). The first half is the same as a clause in the middle taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 122).

- The rapid pulse is not heat but "guest heat" from vacuity cold of the stomach.
- The base reads 消数; it is emended to 消穀 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

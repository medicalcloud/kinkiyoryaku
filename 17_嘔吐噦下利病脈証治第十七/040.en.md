# Clause 40 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diarrhea with delirium: there is dry stool. Xiao Chengqi tang (Shojokito) governs it.

Xiao Chengqi tang (Minor Order-Restoring Decoction)

Dahuang (daio) 4 liang; houpo (koboku; magnolia bark) 2 liang (mix-fried); zhishi (kijitsu; unripe bitter orange), large ones, 3 pieces (mix-fried).

Of the three ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses (stop once there is a bowel movement).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This clause, like the earlier "hardness below the heart," is a case of something in the stomach. But here there is only delirium, so the stool is already dry; dry-heat qi steams, and the genuine qi of the zang has been damaged, so the violent urgency of mangxiao (bosho) would instead be unable to wash away the pathogen. So only zhishi, houpo, and dahuang are used, meaning that when the stomach is already dry and hot, it should be attacked gradually. Compared with the delirium of chest bind, the addition of diarrhea means less heat and more dryness.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] dahuang 4 liang; zhishi 3 pieces (mix-fried); houpo 3 liang (mix-fried).

## Notes

A clause stating that diarrhea with delirium is due to dry stool and is purged with Xiao Chengqi tang. Nearly the same clause appears in the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 374).

- It is often understood as hard stool retained in the bowel with fluid flowing out around it.
- Daizhige and the formula appended to Xu Bin's commentary give houpo as 3 liang.

# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

After vomiting, thirst with wanting water and drinking greedily: Wenge tang governs it. It also governs slight wind with a tight [base: 者] pulse and headache.

Wenge tang (Clam Shell Decoction)

Wenge (clam shell) 5 liang; mahuang, gancao, and shengjiang, 3 liang each; shigao (gypsum) 5 liang; xingren 50 pieces; dazao 12 pieces.

Of the seven ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng and take 1 sheng warm. When sweat comes out, there is immediate recovery.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is the earlier "thirst wishing to drink water." Drinking greedily means water is not enough to stop the dryness. Moreover, after vomiting and not necessarily stomach reflux, there is little deficiency and much heat. So the salty cold of wenge, clearing heat and dispersing binding, is the mainstay; mahuang, xingren, gancao, and shigao course the heat in the qi aspect; and shengjiang and dazao diffuse depression in the upper jiao. But since mahuang effuses yang, it also governs slight wind. Still, the formula seems mainly to clear heat. If the pulse is tight — tightness being cold blocking fire above — there is headache. This sentence is added to show that a person with congested heat who drinks greedily may also have a tight pulse and headache, just as headache was mentioned in the commentary on the earlier clause on dry retching with spitting of foamy drool. But since there is no spitting of foamy drool, there is little cold in the chest, so mahuang and xingren can cure it.

## Notes

A clause using Wenge tang for thirst after vomiting with greedy drinking of water.

- Wenge tang is Ma Xing Gan Shi tang (mahuang, xingren, gancao, shigao) plus wenge, shengjiang, and dazao.
- The base's "pulse 者 [particle] headache" is "tight pulse and headache" in all other sources (Xu Bin's text has the variant form 𦂳), and Xu Bin's commentary also reads "tight pulse." The base appears to be in error.
- The lower Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* has a "Wenge san," but it is a different, single-ingredient formula.

# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Retching with a weak pulse, urination again free, slight heat in the body, and reverse-flow: difficult to treat. Use the following formula.

Sini tang (Counterflow Cold Decoction)

Fuzi 7 pieces [other sources: 1 piece] (used raw); ganjiang 1.5 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried).

Of the three ingredients above, with 3 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses. A strong person may use one large fuzi and 3 liang of ganjiang.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is the method of abandoning the branch to treat the root. Retching with slight heat is a sign that exterior pathogen wants to come out. But a weak pulse means the inside is deficient. Free urination shows there is no heat in the lower jiao. And reverse-flow means the pathogenic cold of Shaoyin is again heavy. Then the earlier retching and heat are exterior but very slight. If one also treated the fire and the retained fluid, the lower part would become colder; hence "difficult to treat," and Sini tang governs it, following outright the method for Shaoyin disease to pare away the root cold, so that the true yang is aided and the slight exterior resolves by itself. Using fuzi raw carries the sense of dispersal.

## Notes

A clause stating that retching with a weak pulse, free urination, slight fever, and cold limbs (reverse-flow) is hard to cure, using Sini tang. The same clause appears in the Jueyin chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 377).

- The base's "7 pieces" of fuzi is "1 piece" in all other sources and appears to be an error.

# Clause 80 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chinaberry without fruit kills people.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

[Xu Bin's text:] Most poisons work by borrowing poison to damage the origin. At the right time, one should drink a decoction of gancao (kanzo) and jinian; it treats all poisons generally.

Commentary: This sums up how the poisons above harm people. How could a mere thread of poison harm a person? It borrows a little toxic qi that seeps into the original qi, and the original qi is instead made to serve the toxic qi, until it becomes untreatable — what is called "a single spark, at its height, sets the prairie ablaze." So one should mainly bank up the root qi with sweet and cold things such as gancao and jinian, and also dissolve and resolve the toxic qi; then there will naturally be no failure to recover. Hence they are medicines treating all poisons generally. One sees that the various antidotes are not as precise and apt as these two, and one can also understand that antidotes are generally taken from sweet and cool things.

## Notes

A clause stating that chinaberry without fruit is poisonous.

- The base ends the chapter here, but the other sources continue: "All poisons are mostly borrowed poisons given to the unknowing; at the right time, one should drink a decoction of gancao and jinian; it removes all poisons generally." Xu Bin's text has the same clause with some differences in wording, and Xu Bin's commentary concerns it.

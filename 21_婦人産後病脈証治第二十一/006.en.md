# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Seven or eight days after childbirth, without a Taiyang pattern, the lesser abdomen is hard and painful: this is lochia not exhausted. No bowel movement, restlessness with fever, the pulse on palpation slightly excess, fever redoubled, restlessness at late afternoon, not eating, and delirium upon eating, which subsides once night comes: Da Chengqi tang (Daijokito) governs it suitably. The heat is in the interior and binds in the bladder (the formula appears under tetany).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This clause says that after childbirth, when the lochia is not exhausted, though there is blood stasis, the excess of the disease is not in the blood: heat in the abdomen causes the blood to bind in the bladder. The key to distinguishing it lies especially in the words "subsides once night comes." Seven or eight days after childbirth, the original deficiency has somewhat recovered. Without a Taiyang pattern, it is not the exterior pattern of headache, fever, and aversion to cold. Hardness and pain of the lesser abdomen — what is it if not unexhausted lochia? But no bowel movement means dry heat in the intestines and stomach; vexation, agitation, and fever mean excess heat attacking upward; and a slightly excess pulse is not comparable to deficiency. Further, redoubled fever with restlessness at late afternoon is a pattern of depressed heat in the spleen and stomach, and delirium upon eating makes stomach heat all the more certain. All these are signs of heat binding in the intestines and stomach, not the essential pattern of unexhausted lochia. Moreover, it subsides once night comes; if the disease were truly in yin, it should worsen at night, and that it instead subsides at night — is that not excess heat bound inside? So Da Chengqi tang governs it, intending to free the heat binding and carry on the original qi; then the lochia flows by itself. There is no need, as above, simply to purge static blood; for if one only removed blood without eliminating the heat, even the blood might not be removed. So it sums up: "the heat is in the interior" — the interior of exterior and interior in the *Shanghan lun*, meaning the interior should be attacked; and "binds in the bladder" means the blood happened to bind because of heat, not a disease of the blood binding by itself, so the blood should not be attacked.

## Notes

A clause stating that seven or eight days after childbirth, unexhausted lochia with a hard, painful lower abdomen, together with constipation, fever, late-afternoon agitation, and delirium, is due to heat binding in the interior and is treated with Da Chengqi tang.

- The base lacks 不 in 不大便 ("no bowel movement"), but all other sources have it, so it has been supplied (see the emendations).

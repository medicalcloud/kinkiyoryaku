# Clause 5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: For abdominal pain in a parturient woman, by rule one should use Zhishi Shaoyao san. If it does not heal, there is dry blood in the abdomen attached below the navel; Xia Yuxue tang governs it suitably. It also governs inhibited menses.

Xia Yuxue tang (Precipitating Static Blood Decoction) formula

Dahuang 2 liang; taoren 20 pieces; zhechong (ground beetle) 20 pieces (dry-fried, legs removed).

Powder the three ingredients above and mix with refined honey into four pills. Decoct one pill in 1 sheng of wine down to 8 ge and take it in a single dose. New blood comes down like pig's liver.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that if a parturient woman's abdominal pain is truly spleen deficiency with qi obstruction, Zhishi Shaoyao san, dispelling malign qi and contracting healthy qi, will certainly heal it; if it does not heal, one must not blame deficiency: there must be static blood. But postpartum blood cannot stagnate above; hence "below the navel." Once there is static blood, one should attack the blood exclusively and not, by habit, let the two words "deficiency cold" hamper the medicine's power. So dahuang, taoren, and zhechong attack it drastically and directly, meaning that removing the disease is itself supplementation. Since it exclusively removes static blood, it also governs inhibited menses. It says "new blood" and also "like pig's liver": blood that has bound suddenly.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] dahuang 3 liang; taoren 20 pieces; zhechong 20 pieces (legs removed).

## Notes

A clause stating that postpartum abdominal pain unrelieved by Zhishi Shaoyao san is due to dry (static) blood below the navel, purged with Xia Yuxue tang.

- A formula that attacks and purges static blood with dahuang, taoren, and zhechong.
- For "dry blood," Xu Bin's text reads "residual blood" and Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*) "static blood."

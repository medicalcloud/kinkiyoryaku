# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Postpartum wind attack with fever, a bright red face, dyspnea, and headache: Zhuye tang governs it.

Zhuye tang (Bamboo Leaf Decoction) formula

Zhuye (bamboo leaf) 1 handful; gegen 3 liang; fangfeng (saposhnikovia) 1 liang; jiegeng, guizhi, renshen, and gancao, 1 liang each; fuzi 1 piece (blast-fried); dazao 15 pieces; shengjiang 5 liang.

Of the ten ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil down to 2.5 sheng and take warm in three doses; cover warmly to induce sweating. For stiffness of the neck, use one large fuzi broken into bean-sized pieces, and decoct [base: 前 "the preceding"] the medicine, skimming off the foam. For retching, add half a sheng of banxia, washed.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Fever and headache in wind attack are exterior pathogen. But a bright red face is not a trifling pale red; it is what is called "a face as if made up with vermilion," the true yang floating upward. Added to this, dyspnea means qi is high and does not descend. Clearly, after childbirth there is great deficiency, the original yang cannot secure itself, and exterior pathogen is mixed in; naturally attack and supplementation should be applied together. So guizhi, gancao, fangfeng, gegen, jiegeng, shengjiang, and dazao clear the pathogen above, zhuye clears the heat of the gallbladder fu, and renshen and fuzi bank up the original qi and bring back the yang about to desert. But naming the decoction after zhuye, one should know that the root is cold and the branch hot, and the gallbladder dwells in the middle [lacuna] then both root and branch are calm. Zhuye truly has merit [lacuna] especially severe, so a large fuzi is added. With retching there is counterflow and water, so [banxia] is added [lacuna].

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] guizhi 1 liang; renshen 1 liang; gancao 1 liang; fuzi 1 piece (blast-fried).

... out. If the neck is stiff, use one large fuzi broken into bean-sized pieces (should be the character 入) [put into] the preceding medicine.

## Notes

A clause using Zhuye tang for postpartum wind attack with fever, a bright red face, dyspnea, and headache.

- A formula combining attack and supplementation: medicinals dispersing exterior pathogen (zhuye, gegen, fangfeng, guizhi, jiegeng) with renshen and fuzi supplementing deficient yang.
- For 煎薬 ("decoct the medicine"), the base reads 前薬; most other sources read 煎薬, so it has been emended (see the emendation notes). Xu Bin's text has the note 該是入字 ("should be the character 入") before 前薬, reading "put into the preceding medicine."

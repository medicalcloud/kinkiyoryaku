# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: Women who have just given birth have three diseases: first, convulsions; second, depression and veiling; third, difficult defecation. What does this mean? The master said: Just after childbirth the blood is deficient and there is much sweating, so wind is easily contracted; hence convulsions. With blood loss and sweating again, there is much cold; hence depression and veiling. With loss of fluids, the stomach is dry; hence difficult defecation.

When a parturient woman has depression and veiling, her pulse is feeble and weak, she cannot eat [most other sources: retches and cannot eat], her stool is instead hard, and there is only sweating of the head. The reason is that with blood deficiency there is reverse-flow, and with reverse-flow there must be veiling. When one with veiling is about to resolve, there must be great sweating. Because blood deficiency causes reverse-flow below and solitary yang comes out above, there is sweating of the head. A parturient woman sweats readily because with lost yin and deficient blood, yang qi alone is exuberant; so there should be sweating, and then yin and yang are restored. Hard stool with retching and inability to eat: Xiao Chaihu tang (Shosaikoto) governs it (the formula appears under retching and vomiting).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Parturient women [lacuna] miscellaneous diseases [lacuna] are not fixed, but those obtained from childbirth are counted as three diseases [lacuna] speaking of the disease [lacuna] the three causes are one. First, convulsions: convulsions means the body is hot [lacuna] cold, the face red, sudden clenched jaw, and arched-back rigidity. The *Maijing* says, "In one with convulsions, the [lacuna] is firm and straight up and down." Second, depression and veiling: depression and veiling means oppression and clouding [lacuna]. Third, difficult defecation: difficult means the stool comes out hard, not that it is blocked. Different people have the same disease, so the questioner was puzzled, not knowing that just after childbirth the blood is deficient, blood deficiency brings much sweating, and pathogen enters by taking advantage of deficiency, so wind is easily contracted. 喜 means "easily." When wind enters a body of deficient blood, there is no genuine qi to resist it, so the wind takes charge and causes convulsions, as a dead tree warps when it meets wind and dryness. With blood loss and sweating again, the genuine qi is consumed and internal cold arises by itself; hence "much cold." When cold lodges in a body deficient in both yin and yang, yin fire is depressed and veils the top, as if something covers it. Blood and sweat both derive from fluids. When the blood is deficient and sweat comes out, the fluids are already lost and dry pathogen soon arises; dryness brings heat, heat brings parching, and parching makes the stool hard to pass.

Commentary: What follows says that although there are three diseases after childbirth, convulsions are still rare, and only depression-veiling and hard stool are always found together; so it details their causes and treatment. Depression and veiling in a parturient woman is much deficiency and little pathogen, so the pulse is feeble and weak: the center qi is deficient. With deficiency of the center, yin fire counterflows causing retching and inability to eat. Inability to eat suggests a weak stomach prone to diarrhea, but people do not know that lost fluids dry the stomach, so the stool is instead hard. Internal deficiency and dryness leave the body's yin and yang disharmonious, so there is no sweat on the body but only on the head. These several signs accompany depression and veiling. Then it further details the cause. Why veiling? With blood deficiency, yin cannot hold yang, and there is reverse-flow below; 厥 means exhaustion, and cold. When it is cold below, there is depression above like veiling. "When one with veiling is about to resolve, there must be great sweating" shows that one should let the person sweat spontaneously, not apply sweating or purging. Why sweating of the head? With blood deficiency and reverse-flow below, the yin qi below is exhausted and yang becomes solitary yang; solitary yang goes out above, and there is sweating of the head. But why, when there is already sweating of the head, is sweating still welcomed for resolution? If yin were not lost, the blood would not yet be greatly deficient; but a parturient woman's blood loss is excessive and yin is lost, so yang becomes solitary yang and, compared with yin, yang alone is exuberant. So sweating is welcomed to diminish yang and bring it to yin, and then yin and yang are balanced; hence "then they are restored." Hard stool is not much heat but deficiency-dryness; retching is not cold but counterflow of gallbladder qi; inability to eat is not excess pathogen but deficiency heat in the stomach. So chaihu (saiko), renshen (ninjin), gancao (kanzo), huangqin (ogon), banxia (hange), shengjiang (shokyo), and dazao (taiso) harmonize it.

## Notes

A clause explaining the causes of the three postpartum diseases (convulsions, depression-veiling, difficult defecation) and using Xiao Chaihu tang for depression-veiling with hard stool, retching, and inability to eat.

- All three are attributed to deficiency of blood and fluids from blood loss and sweating at childbirth.
- Between "her pulse is feeble and weak" and "cannot eat," Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*), Daizhige, and Xu Bin's text have 嘔 ("retches").
- There are two commentaries by Xu Bin; the first has many lacunae (marked 欠).

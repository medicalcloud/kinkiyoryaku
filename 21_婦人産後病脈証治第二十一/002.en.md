# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the disease has resolved and one can eat, and after seven or eight days fever arises again, this is stomach excess. Da Chengqi tang (Daijokito) governs it (the formula appears under [tetany]).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This passage says that when an excess pattern arises after great deficiency, it should be treated as excess. When the disease has resolved and one can eat, the qi of the meridians and zang-fu is all balanced, and there is no original postpartum disease to suspect. If after seven or eight days there is again fever without aversion to cold, and no exterior pattern to suspect, it is clearly an image of food relapse; hence "stomach excess." Da Chengqi tang drives it out drastically, for fear that delay would lead to deficiency. Comparing the wording and the facts: even after the great deficiency of postpartum depression and veiling, the drastic medicine is not shunned like this; how much more so in other diseases!

## Notes

A clause stating that renewed fever after postpartum depression-veiling has healed and eating has resumed is stomach excess, purged with Da Chengqi tang.

- For the formula's location, "産中" is "痙中," "痙病中," or "痓病" in the other sources. Da Chengqi tang is in the chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke.

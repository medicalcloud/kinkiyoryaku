# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Postpartum [wind attack] continuing for several tens of days without resolving, with slight headache, aversion to cold, heat from time to time, oppression below the heart, dry retching, and sweating: although it is long, the yangdan pattern still continues. Yangdan tang may be given (that is, Guizhi tang; the formula appears under diarrhea).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

[... Gui]zhi plus huangqin. Commentary: this passage says that when postpartum wind attack drags on without healing and exterior and interior signs appear mixed, one should still remove the wind. For a mild wind attack unresolved for several tens of days, it seems the exterior cannot be blamed. But headache, aversion to cold, sweating, and intermittent heat are all exterior signs. Oppression below the heart and dry retching mean the Taiyang pathogen wants to enter inward but the interior does not accept it. The *Shanghan lun* has Yangdan tang, which is Guizhi tang plus huangqin, treating Taiyang wind attack with heat. Now, with long-standing wind and unceasing heat, the yangdan pattern is still present; why not give Yangdan tang? One must not delay and so make a mistake.

## Notes

A clause stating that postpartum wind attack unhealed after several tens of days, with continuing headache, aversion to cold, fever, and sweating, is a yangdan pattern, for which Yangdan tang is given.

- The note in the main text identifies Yangdan tang as "Guizhi tang," but Xu Bin takes it as Guizhi tang plus huangqin.
- For 久 ("long") in "although it is long," the base reads 又, but all other sources read 久, so it has been emended (see the emendations). Xu Bin's text has, after 産後, the note 該有中風 ("中風 should be here").

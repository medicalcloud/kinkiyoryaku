# Clause 7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a sick person has a hot body and cold feet, a stiff and tense neck and nape, aversion to cold, at times heat in the head, a red face, red eyes, the head alone shaking, sudden clenched jaw, and an arched back, it is tetany. If sweating is induced, cold and dampness combine, the exterior becomes all the more vacuous, and the aversion to cold becomes severe. After sweating has been induced, the pulse is like a snake (one version says: the pulse is chilly).

## Notes

A clause listing the typical signs of tetany and describing the changes when sweating is mistakenly induced. Almost the same text appears in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke.

- Hot body, cold feet, stiff neck and nape, aversion to cold, red face and eyes, shaking head, clenched jaw, and an arched back (opisthotonos) are the signs of tetany.
- If sweating is induced, the exterior becomes vacuous and cold-damp binds, so the aversion to cold worsens.
- "The pulse is like a snake" is unclear; it is variously interpreted as a pulse that wriggles like a crawling snake or one that is hard and does not extend. The interlinear 滄滄 reads 浛 or 浛浛 in Wikisource and other sources.
- 巳 is taken as a graphic error for 已 ("finished").
- Xu Bin's commentary is attached after unit 9 (the clause on the tetany pulse).

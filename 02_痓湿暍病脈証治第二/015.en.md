# Clause 15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with aching pain and vexation in the joints and a deep, thready (one version reads moderate) pulse is called damp impediment (the *Yuhan* says "dampness-strike"). The signs of damp impediment are inhibited urination and, on the contrary, easy defecation. One should only disinhibit the urine.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This discusses dampness carrying wind, with dampness predominating and therefore causing impediment and fixation. Fever and aversion to wind are Taiyang disease. Then dampness predominates, producing aching pain. In Taiyang disease the pathogen enters from the exterior; when dampness carries wind, the wind runs to the empty spaces and so flows into the joints. The joints are where the body's mechanisms gather and meet. The wind qi stagnates within and presses on the heart, so there is vexation. But the wind is bound by dampness and loses its nature as wind, so the pulse is deep and thready, and we know dampness predominates. Hence it is called dampness-strike, also damp impediment. "Impediment" means fixed and not departing. When the qi is impeded by dampness, qi transformation is sluggish, and urination may be inhibited. The large intestine governs the fluids; with dampness it is instead easy and not rough. Those with wind disease are mostly dry and blocked, so ease of defecation due to predominant dampness is called "on the contrary." "One should only disinhibit the urine": when urination is free, the qi transforms; when the qi transforms, the dampness moves. We see that one need not cling to Taiyang and treat the wind, nor is it comparable to pain in the bones and joints that should be warmed and dispersed.

## Notes

The start of the clauses on dampness disease. It describes "damp impediment," centered on joint pain, and gives disinhibiting urination as the treatment. The same clause appears in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke.

- Dampness is a heavy, turbid pathogen that lodges in the joints, causing pain and vexation. The deep, thready pulse is attributed to predominant dampness.
- With internal dampness, urination is scanty and stools are, on the contrary, soft and easy. The treatment is to disinhibit urination and remove dampness (the later saying "treating dampness without disinhibiting urination is not the proper treatment" is often quoted).
- *Yuhan* in the interlinear note refers to the *Jingui yuhan jing*.

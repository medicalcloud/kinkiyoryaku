# Clause 24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage for eight or nine days, with wind and dampness contending, the body aching and vexed, inability to turn over by oneself, no retching, no thirst, and a floating, deficient, hesitant pulse: Guizhi Fuzi tang (Cinnamon Twig and Aconite Decoction) governs it. If the stool is hard and urination is spontaneously free, Qu Gui jia Baizhu tang (the same minus Cinnamon Twig plus White Atractylodes) governs it.

Guizhi Fuzi tang formula

Guizhi 4 liang (remove the bark); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); fuzi (aconite) 3 pieces (blast-fried, remove the skin, broken into 8 pieces); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the five ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses.

Baizhu Fuzi tang (White Atractylodes and Aconite Decoction) formula

Baizhu 2 liang; fuzi 1.5 pieces (blast-fried, remove the skin); gancao 1 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 1.5 liang (sliced); dazao 6 pieces.

Of the five ingredients above, with 3 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses. If after one dose the body feels numb, take another dose after about half a day, and finish all three doses. The person may seem as if veiled (dizzy and muddled); do not be alarmed. This is because the zhu and fu[zi] together run into the skin, driving out the water qi, which has not yet been removed.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that when wind-damp comes after cold damage together with deficiency cold in the yin aspect, one should attend to the root and distinguish the methods of moving yang and drying dampness. Cold damage for eight or nine days is the time when right and pathogen resolve. But because wind and dampness contend, the body aches and is vexed, and one cannot turn over by oneself. Since neither heat nor sweating is mentioned, the exterior pathogen is about to resolve and the heat is slight. If there were retching and thirst, there would be heat in the interior; since there is neither, [there is no interior heat]. The floating pulse is wind; floating with deficiency and roughness means cold-damp is inside and the outer yang does not move. So shaoyao is removed from Guizhi tang and fuzi added, to open the cold impediment and move the wind-damp throughout the body. But guizhi is what moves the ying-nutrient and wei-defense and runs to the exterior. If the stool is hard and urination spontaneously free, there is no disease of exterior or interior; the disease is in the body's frame, and there is no reason to treat the exterior. So guizhi is removed and zhu added to strengthen the qi of the intestines and stomach, so that the power of drying dampness comes out from within; then the wind carrying dampness in the body's frame is resolved not through the exterior but by transformation through heat. Hence it says ... [the rest is missing].

[Xu Bin's formula:] baizhu 1 liang; fuzi 1 piece (skin, blast-fried, removed [sic]); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 1.5 liang (sliced); dazao 6 pieces (split).

## Notes

A clause using Guizhi Fuzi tang and Baizhu Fuzi tang (Qu Gui jia Baizhu tang) for body pain from wind and dampness contending during the course of cold damage. The same clause appears in the lower Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun* (corresponding to clause 174).

- Guizhi Fuzi tang is like Guizhi tang without shaoyao and with an increased amount of fuzi; it is said to warm yang and remove wind-damp.
- When the stool is hard and urination free, guizhi is removed and baizhu added. The clause calls it Qu Gui jia Baizhu tang, while the formula heading calls it Baizhu Fuzi tang. Its amounts are about half those of Guizhi Fuzi tang.
- The muddled, "veiled" feeling after taking it is a reaction of the water qi moving as the medicine takes effect, and is no cause for alarm.
- The base's 孹 appears to be an error for 擘 ("split"), and 一放半 for 一枚半 ("1.5 pieces") (other sources read 擘 or 劈, and 一枚半). The translation follows these.
- Xu Bin's commentary breaks off (ending with 故曰其), followed by part of the Baizhu Fuzi tang formula from Xu Bin's edition, whose amounts differ from the base.

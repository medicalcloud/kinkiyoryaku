# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When tetany becomes illness (one version has the character 剛 "hard" above 痓), with fullness in the chest, clenched jaw, [the back arched so that] lying down one does not touch the mat, hypertonicity of the legs, and inevitably grinding of the teeth, Da Chengqi tang (Major Qi-Infusing Decoction) may be given.

Da Chengqi tang formula

Dahuang (rhubarb) 4 liang (washed in wine); houpo (magnolia bark) half a jin (mix-fried, remove the bark); zhishi (unripe bitter orange) 5 pieces (mix-fried); mangxiao (mirabilite) 3 ge.

Of the four ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the two items [houpo and zhishi] down to 5 sheng, remove the dregs, add the dahuang, boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, put it on the fire again and bring it to a slight boil once or twice, and take warm in two doses. When purging is obtained, stop taking it.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Gegen tang was used earlier precisely to prevent the pathogenic cold from entering inward and turning into Yangming. If one does not plan early, the rigidity of the back and nape attacks from outside without stopping and enters inward, causing fullness in the chest. The Taiyang pathogen is still unresolved; the qi is closed and the jaw clenches; opisthotonos keeps one from touching the mat when lying down. When the pathogen enters inward it necessarily becomes heat; yang heat attacks inward, so the legs become hypertonic and the teeth grind. This is because the Taiyang pathogen has combined with Yangming, and the Yangming vessel starts at the leg and connects with the teeth. So one attacks the stomach directly, using mangxiao, dahuang, zhishi, and houpo to clear the heat and bring down the qi, so that the pathogen of Taiyang and Yangming disperses together through central earth. This purges the heat, not the food.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] ... [add the] dahuang, boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, put it on a low fire again and bring it to a boil once or twice, and take warm (溢, read as 温) in two doses. When purging is obtained, do not take the rest.

## Notes

A clause using Da Chengqi tang when tetany has progressed and heat has become in excess in the interior.

- Fullness in the chest, clenched jaw, arched back not touching the bed (opisthotonos), hypertonic legs, and teeth grinding are severe signs of tetany. Taking this as pathogen entering Yangming with excess heat, one purges with Da Chengqi tang.
- The phrase "may be given" (可与) is often understood to imply cautious or unavoidable use.
- The base's 弗 appears to be an error for 沸 ("boil"), as the other sources read; it is translated as 沸.
- The end of Xu Bin's commentary is the latter half of the formula in Xu Bin's edition, which has entered the commentary; its beginning is missing. 溢 in 分溢再服 appears to be an error for 温 ("warm").

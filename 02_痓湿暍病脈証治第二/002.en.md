# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In taiyang disease, with fever, sweating, and no aversion to cold, it is called soft tetany.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

These two clauses are the *Shanghan lun*'s method of distinguishing "cold damages the nutritive, wind damages the defensive," taken to distinguish hard and soft tetany; the wording is abbreviated. Tetany is 痙, meaning rigidity. Tetany always has definite signs such as stiffness of the back and nape, so once "tetany" is said, the wording is abbreviated and [rigidity] is not mentioned. But in treating tetany, distinguishing hard from soft is most important, so at the very beginning it singles out no sweating with aversion to cold as hard, and sweating without aversion to cold as soft, to show the key to pattern identification.

Fever, no sweating, and aversion to cold are originally signs of cold damage. If they are seen in tetany with a stiff nape and straight back, [it is because] defensive yang and the true yang in the kidney are originally connected; now cold and dampness contend in the taiyang channel, the qi invades the shaoyin, and the true yang does not reach out, so there is aversion to cold instead. Cold is forceful and severe by nature, hence "hard." Fever, sweating, and no aversion to cold are originally signs of wind damage combined with yangming. If they are seen in tetany with a stiff nape and straight back, it is taiyang and yangming damaged by dampness together with wind, not comparable to cold evil invading inward. Wind is warm and gentle by nature, hence "soft." It is not that soft tetany is a stiff nape with a soft body. The later Gualou Guizhi tang (Trichosanthes and Cinnamon Twig Decoction) is the main formula for soft tetany, and [its clause] says "the body is stiff and rigid," which makes this clear.

## Notes

Continuing from the previous unit, this clause calls tetany with sweating and no aversion to cold "soft tetany."

- The distinction of hard and soft tetany follows the *Shanghan lun*'s distinction between cold damage (no sweating) and wind-strike (sweating), and leads to the later clauses on Gegen tang (Pueraria Decoction; hard tetany) and Gualou Guizhi tang (soft tetany).
- Xu Bin explains hard and soft by the natures of cold and wind, and notes that in soft tetany, too, the body is stiff rather than soft.

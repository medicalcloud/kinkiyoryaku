# Clause 26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang summerheat stroke, with fever and aversion to cold, a heavy and aching body, and a wiry, fine, scallion-stalk, slow pulse. After urinating, he shivers with goose flesh and the hands and feet are counterflow cold. With slight exertion the body immediately becomes hot; the mouth opens and the front teeth are dry. If sweating is induced, the aversion to cold becomes severe; if warm needling is added, the fever becomes severe; if purged repeatedly, the strangury becomes severe.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is what Jiegu called "contracted while at rest, it is summerheat stroke, a yin pattern." *Ye* is summerheat. "Taiyang summerheat stroke": the taiyang vessel is the outer guard of the whole body, and all contraction of the six qi passes through it; so summerheat too must enter through taiyang. Being taiyang, there is fever and aversion to cold. In summer the qi overflows into the grandchild network vessels, and at this time damp earth governs the season; one is damaged by summerheat, perhaps together with water, so the body is heavy and aches. Summerheat always damages the qi, so the pulse is wiry, fine, scallion-stalk, and slow — vacuous pulses.

But summerheat [stroke] does not mean being struck by heat. Summerheat is received within while yin cold binds the outside; this is the kind Dongyuan described as "seeking coolness in a spacious hall." So there is no sweating and no thirst, and with dampness there is heaviness and pain. Now the bladder governs the outside of the whole body; great heat damages the network vessels; the network vessels are outside and correspond to the bladder; so after urination there may be shivering with goose flesh. There is evil in the network vessels; when urination is finished and the qi draws in, the hair seems to stand up; this is the sign of the correspondence between the bladder and the network vessels. When the bladder channel has received summerheat evil and become overly strong, the qi of the kidney organ weakens and the yang qi cannot connect smoothly, so the hands and feet may be counterflow cold. This is the qi obstructed by imbalance of vacuity and repletion between organ and bowel. When summerheat is shut in by coolness, the heat gathers inside at the heart, and with exertion fire stirs and joins it, so with slight exertion the body becomes hot at once. Although the kidney has not received the evil, when the bladder bowel is ill, kidney yin is scorched. The teeth are the surplus of the bones, and the front teeth especially are where the governing vessel pours, so the mouth opens and the front teeth are dry.

In such cases summerheat has damaged the qi but not the body, and the evil is not deep. Harmonizing the center and diffusing it out depends on the practitioner's judgment at the bedside, so Zhongjing gives no formula but says only, "If sweating is induced, the aversion to cold becomes severe," just as when those with dampness are made to sweat, the exterior becomes all the more vacuous and the aversion to cold becomes severe. Again, "if warm needling is added, the fever becomes severe": fire-heat damages the nutritive qi. Again, "if purged repeatedly, the strangury becomes severe": summerheat at first has not entered the abdomen; purging it lets the bladder receive the summerheat, which scorches the yin and causes strangury. Since fire, sweating, and purging are all prohibited, the method of treatment can be inferred from this. Dongyuan's main use of Dashun san (Great Rectifying Powder) comes close to it, but the severity differs, so one should not cling to it. Or, where vomiting is possible, induce vomiting.

## Notes

A clause on the signs of summerheat stroke and the prohibitions of sweating, warm needling, and purging. The same clause appears in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke.

- It lists fever, aversion to cold, heaviness and pain of the body, a wiry, fine, scallion-stalk, slow pulse (vacuous pulses), chills and cold extremities after urination, heat with slight exertion, and breathing with an open mouth with dry front teeth. This is taken as summerheat having damaged qi and fluids.
- Sweating further depletes yang and increases the aversion to cold; warm needling increases the heat; purging damages yin and causes strangury.
- For 口前開板歯燥, all other sources read 口開前板歯燥 ("the mouth opens and the front teeth are dry"); in the base 開 is misplaced, and the translation follows the other sources.
- 巳 is an error for 已.
- "Jiegu" in Xu Bin's commentary is Zhang Yuansu (founder of the Yishui school); "Dongyuan" is Li Gao. The distinction between summerheat stroke (yin pattern) and heat stroke (yang pattern) is known as theirs.

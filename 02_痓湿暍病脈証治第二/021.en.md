# Clause 21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In those with dampness whose body aches with vexation, Mahuang jia Zhu tang (Maokajutsuto; Ephedra Decoction plus Atractylodes) may be given; inducing sweating is appropriate. Take care not to attack it with fire.

Mahuang jia Zhu tang formula

Mahuang (mao) 3 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi (keishi) 2 liang (remove the bark); gancao (kanzo) 2 liang (mix-fried); xingren (kyonin; apricot kernel) 70 pieces (remove the skins and tips); baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes) 4 liang.

Of the five ingredients above, with 9 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 2.5 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 8 ge warm. Cover to obtain what seems a slight sweat.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Dampness should be treated by sweating, but since it was said earlier that "if [the sweat] pours out greatly, the dampness instead does not leave," we know that sweating has its own method. So Mahuang tang (Maoto) is the main sweating formula, and the single ingredient zhu is added as the ground for securing the root and clearing dampness; then both inside and outside are served. Sweating can also be induced by attacking with fire, but that is not for those with dampness; so it also warns against it.

[Xu Bin's formula:] mahuang 2 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); gancao 1 liang (mix-fried); baizhu 4 liang.

## Notes

A clause inducing sweating with Mahuang jia Zhu tang in dampness disease with body aches.

- Mahuang jia Zhu tang is Mahuang tang (mahuang, guizhi, gancao, xingren) with baizhu added. It is understood that Mahuang tang induces sweating while baizhu removes dampness and prevents excessive sweating.
- "Attacking with fire" refers to forcing sweat with heated needles, fumigation, hot compresses, and the like, which is prohibited for those with dampness.
- The base writes the formula name 麻黄加木湯; it is emended to 朮 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).
- The formula at the end of Xu Bin's commentary is part of the formula in Xu Bin's edition; it lacks xingren and the amounts differ from the base.

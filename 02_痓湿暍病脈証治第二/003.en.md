# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Taiyang disease, with fever and a deep, thready pulse, it is called tetany; it is difficult to cure.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The ancients regarded rigidity as tetany. Its external signs resemble cold damage, but it differs in that the pulse is deep, slow, wiry, and thready, and the nape and back arch back, stiff and hard, as in an epileptic seizure. The previous two clauses distinguished hard and soft by the absence or presence of sweating; here it is distinguished again by a deep, thready pulse. Greater yang disease with fever is wind attack in the exterior. Dampness further entangles it, carrying cold qi inward within the meridians, causing the sinews and vessels to contract, the back and nape to stiffen, and the pulse to be deep and thready instead. Deep and thready is the sign that cold-damp is at work and the pathogen is about to invade the yin. Then the nape and back are rigid, hence the name tetany. The tetany pulse is originally hidden, wiry, and thready; [when it is so,] the original qi is exhausted, so it is difficult to cure. It is not that there is another kind of tetany with a floating, large pulse that is easy to cure while this deep, thready one is difficult. This is clear from the fact that not a single "floating" or "large" appears in Zhongjing's clauses before or after.

## Notes

A clause calling Taiyang fever with a deep, thready pulse "tetany" and giving it a poor prognosis.

- A deep, thready pulse despite exterior signs (fever) is considered a sign that healthy qi has weakened and the pathogen is about to go deep.
- The corresponding clause in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke lacks the words "it is difficult to cure."

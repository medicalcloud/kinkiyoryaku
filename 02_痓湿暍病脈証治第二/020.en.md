# Clause 20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the disease of those with dampness, the body aches, there is fever, the face is yellow with panting, the head aches, the nose is blocked with vexation, the pulse is large, he can eat and drink by himself, and within the abdomen all is harmonious without disease. The disease is cold-damp in the head, and so the nose is blocked [the base reads 寒 "cold"]. Putting medicine into the nose will cure it (the *Maijing* reads "the sick person pants" and lacks the thirteen characters from "in the disease of those with dampness" to "with panting").

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that when dampness contends with cold and is concentrated in the head, decoctions should not be taken. That those with dampness have body aches and fever is usual. The face is yellow because of depressed dampness, and the evil qi invades inward causing panting and vexation, so it seems there is evil both within and without. But headache and nasal blockage show that it is most severe in the head. Moreover, the pulse is large, which means the interior is not weak. He can eat and drink, and the abdomen is harmonious. Though there is vexation and panting, it is the evil in the channels invading inward; the interior is replete and has no disease, and the evil is only in the head. Hence "the disease is cold-damp in the head, and so the nose is blocked." Disease that is above should be made to go out from above. Hence "putting medicine into the nose will cure it." It does not place the blame on the lung.

## Notes

A clause stating that when dampness disease consists mainly of symptoms from cold-damp in the head (headache, nasal blockage) and there is no disease in the abdomen, it is treated by putting medicine into the nose rather than by internal medicine. The same clause appears in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke.

- The medicine to be put into the nose is not specified. Later writers suggest, for example, blowing Guadi san (Melon Stalk Powder) into the nose.
- The base reads 故鼻寒 ("so the nose is cold"), but it should refer to the same symptom as the earlier "nasal blockage" (鼻塞), and all other sources read 鼻塞; 寒 is taken as an error for 塞 in the translation (the base is not emended because not all five sources agree).
- The note on the *Maijing* is by the Song editors. The base says "thirteen characters," while Wikisource reads "eleven." In the base text, the span from 湿家病 to 而喘 is eleven characters.

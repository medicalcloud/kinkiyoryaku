# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Taiyang disease, with fever, no sweating, and aversion to cold instead, it is called hard tetany (one version reads 痓; the same below).

## Notes

The first half of a pair of clauses dividing tetany (*chi*/*jing* disease, in which the nape and back stiffen and arch) into hard and soft tetany according to the presence of sweating. Almost the same clause appears in the chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke in the *Shanghan lun*.

- Tetany without sweating and with aversion to cold (resembling cold damage) is hard tetany. Xu Bin's commentary is attached to the next unit.
- The base's interlinear note "one version reads 痓; the same below" makes no sense, since the main text also reads 痓. The Song edition of the *Shanghan lun* has "one version reads 痙," so the original 痙 was probably miswritten as the similar-looking 痓. Wikisource and others read 痙 in the text and lack this note.
- 痓 and 痙 are often confused; the view that 痙 ("stiffness, convulsion") is correct is widely held. The translation follows the base's 痓.

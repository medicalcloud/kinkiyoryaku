# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease without sweating, with scanty urination instead, qi surging up into the chest, clenched jaw and inability to speak: it is about to become hard tetany, and Gegen tang (Pueraria Decoction) governs it.

Gegen tang formula

Gegen (pueraria) 4 liang; mahuang (ephedra) 3 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); shaoyao 2 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 3 liang; dazao 12 pieces.

Of the seven ingredients above, pound them, and with 1 sheng [sic] of water first boil the mahuang and gegen, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth, add the other ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Cover to obtain what seems a slight sweat; there is no need to sip porridge. Otherwise follow the method of Guizhi tang for care and prohibitions.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

In hard tetany, the back and nape are rigid, there is no sweating with fever, and also aversion to cold instead; this originally belongs to the picture of cold-damp lodged within, with both yin and yang damaged. Fever is Taiyang disease; no sweating is the essential sign of cold damaging the ying-nutrient. At this time the pathogen is still in the exterior, not the interior, yet urination is scanty instead and qi surges up into the chest; clearly the pathogen following the Taiyang meridian is invading the zang organs from the bowel and stirring the surging qi. Moreover, the clenched jaw and inability to speak mean that Taiyang, which governs opening, has instead closed, and the voice cannot be produced; both yin and yang are damaged. That rigidity and aversion to cold will inevitably follow goes without saying. Hence "it is about to become hard tetany."

The medicine is the whole of Guizhi tang with gegen and mahuang added, treating wind and cold together. Now, the foot Yangming vessel starts at the nose, crosses at the root of the nose, and takes in the Taiyang vessel at its side; so when [the pathogen] invades from Taiyang to Yangming, the stiffness of the neck and nape tends to continue and gradually the chest becomes full. Therefore gegen in particular governs it, to prevent the tendency to combine [Taiyang and Yangming], making it the main formula for hard tetany without sweating. Moreover, guizhi can originally treat surging qi.

## Notes

A clause using Gegen tang for a state about to become hard tetany.

- No sweating (the mark of hard tetany), scanty urination, qi surging into the chest, and a clenched jaw with inability to speak are taken as a stage before hard tetany, before opisthotonos appears.
- Gegen tang is the same formula as Gegen tang in the middle Taiyang chapter of the *Shanghan lun*.
- For "1 sheng of water," Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe*), Daizhige, and Xu Bin's edition read 1 dou, and Wikisource (*Jingui yaolüe fanglun*) and icntcm read 7 sheng. Since 2 sheng cannot be boiled off from 1 sheng, the base appears to be in error. The translation follows the base with [sic].

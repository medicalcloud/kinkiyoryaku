# Clause 9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The tetany pulse, when pressed, is tight like a bowstring, moving straight up and down (one version reads: pounding and wiry. The *Maijing* says: In those with tetany, the pulse is hidden and hard, straight up and down).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The earlier statement that no sweating with aversion to cold is hard tetany and sweating without aversion to cold is soft tetany is a method of distinguishing tetany, not the essential pattern of tetany itself. So it again sets out the most complete tetany pattern, detailing the appearance during illness, and further says that in treating it one must not over-sweat, and that the pulse has a regular form.

The sick person's body is hot because the Taiyang exterior pathogen is originally exuberant. In a person whose blood and fluids are depleted, when pathogenic cold further carries dampness and binds in the wei-defense aspect, yang qi does not descend and the feet are cold. Dampness follows the Taiyang meridian down to the nape and slightly invades Yangming, so the neck and nape are stiff and tense. The true yang does not reach the exterior, so there is aversion to cold. Thus the Taiyang meridian is entirely cold-damp, and heat is blocked above, producing heat in the head, red face, red eyes, and shaking of the head alone. Taiyang governs opening; when cold-damp contends with it, opening and closing are impaired, the voice cannot be produced, and the jaw suddenly clenches. The fluids are depleted and the pathogen exuberant, the sinews lose their nourishment, and the back arches. These are the essential signs of tetany.

If sweating is then induced, the cold may be entangled by dampness and not leave, and the sweating merely makes the exterior deficient. Therefore it says, "cold and dampness combine, the exterior becomes all the more deficient, and the aversion to cold becomes severe." (Taiyang.) After sweating, the pulse does not move up and down but moves in the middle like a snake: the healthy qi is deficient and the pathogen has also weakened. Then sudden distension and enlargement of the abdomen means the pathogen in the meridians is about to come out from within; hence "it is about to resolve." If the pulse is even and as before, and instead hidden and wiry, pathogenic cold lingers in the meridians and the tetany is still present. Further, the tetany pulse, when pressed, is tight like a bowstring, moving straight up and down; the *Maijing* also says, "In those with tetany, the pulse is hidden and hard, straight up and down." In general it does not depart from deep and tight. The hidden and wiry [pulse] here is also of the deep and tight kind.

## Notes

A clause on the characteristic pulse of tetany.

- The pulse is tight and taut like a bowstring, extending straight from the cun to the chi; it is understood as corresponding to stiff, contracted sinews.
- The *Maijing* quotation in the interlinear note appears again as main text two units later (unit 11). Xu Bin's edition lacks the interlinear note.
- Xu Bin's commentary also explains the preceding units 7 and 8. The "(Taiyang)" in the commentary is a remnant of an interlinear note in Xu Bin's text; its meaning is unclear.

# Clause 27 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang heat stroke is summer heat stroke (*ye*). Sweating with aversion to cold, and a hot body with thirst: Baihu jia Renshen tang (White Tiger Decoction plus Ginseng) governs it.

Baihu Renshen tang formula

Zhimu (anemarrhena) 6 liang; shigao (gypsum) 1 jin (crushed); gancao 2 liang; jingmi (non-glutinous rice) 6 ge; renshen (ginseng) 3 liang.

Of the five ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil until the rice is cooked and the decoction is ready, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is what Jiegu called "contracted while active, it is heat stroke, a yang pattern." Taiyang is struck directly by summer heat; this is true summer heat. With summer heat, the heat is exuberant and sweat comes out; with summer heat carrying pathogen, there is aversion to cold. But although there is aversion to cold, when summer heat damages a person the heart receives it first, so the body is hot and there is thirst. Heat inevitably damages the qi, so it is treated with Baihu jia Renshen tang. Or Cangzhu Baihu tang (White Tiger Decoction with Atractylodes) is used, because in late summer damp earth is at work, so there is also yin dampness.

## Notes

A clause using Baihu jia Renshen tang for heat stroke (being struck directly by summer heat).

- There is sweating and aversion to cold, but also body heat and thirst; it is a state of exuberant heat damaging qi and fluids. Baihu tang clears the heat, and renshen supplements qi and fluids.
- The formula heading reads Baihu Renshen tang; most other sources read Baihu jia Renshen tang.
- The base reads 熱 ("hot") for 熟 ("cooked"); it is emended because all other sources read 熟 (see the emendation note).

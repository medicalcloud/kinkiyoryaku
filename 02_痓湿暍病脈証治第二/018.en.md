# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When those with dampness are purged, if sweat comes out on the forehead, there is slight panting, and urination is free (one version says inhibited), the patient dies. If diarrhea does not stop, the patient also dies.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Dampness in the human body is a disease between the channels and the flesh interstices. The large bowel is the gate of the body's original qi. If the large bowel is stirred, the evil in the channels does not leave and the original qi is suddenly pared away. Therefore in treating dampness one must never purge from start to finish. This is clear from the first section's "one should only disinhibit the urine" and the later section's "it should by rule be resolved by sweating." In Zhongjing's later formulas for treating dampness, there are only the method of warming to dry it and the method of drying it with wind [medicinals]. Dongyuan followed this idea and devised Shengyang Chushi tang (Yang-Raising Dampness-Eliminating Decoction) and Qianghuo Shengshi tang (Notopterygium Dampness-Overcoming Decoction). This is clear proof that one must never purge.

Zhongjing has the phrase "if purging is done too early, there will be hiccup," which seems to imply that too early is wrong but later purging is permissible. [Such a reading] does not know that this concerns someone sweating from the head whose exterior is not yet resolved, out of concern that [the evil] might enter inward; when exterior evil has entered inward, one may purge; it does not say that in treating dampness one may purge.

So "when those with dampness are purged," the yang becomes vacuous because cold purgative medicinals suddenly attack, and the kidney yang deserts first. When the kidney falls ill first, the heart responds. The forehead is the region of the heart; when kidney water overwhelms it, sweat comes out on the forehead. Slight panting is the solitary yang deserting upward. If, further, urination is free, both upper and lower desert together, so the patient dies. Even if the yang of the upper burner has not reached desertion, if diarrhea does not stop — the kidney is the master of yin and governs the two excretions, and their not stopping is yin desertion — so the patient also dies.

## Notes

A clause describing fatal patterns after dampness disease has been mistakenly purged. The same clause appears in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke.

- Sweating on the forehead, slight panting, and spontaneous urination are signs of yang qi deserting from above and below. Unstoppable diarrhea is a sign of yin desertion.
- Xu Bin stresses that dampness disease must never be purged, citing Li Dongyuan's (Li Gao's) Shengyang Chushi tang and Qianghuo Shengshi tang as examples.

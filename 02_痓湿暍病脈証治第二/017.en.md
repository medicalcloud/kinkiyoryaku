# Clause 17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In those with dampness, if the person sweats only from the head, the back is stiff, and he wants to be covered with bedding and face the fire, and if purging is done too early, there will be hiccup, or fullness in the chest, inhibited urination (one version says free urination), and something like fur on the tongue; this is because there is heat in the cinnabar field and cold above the chest. He is thirsty and wants to drink but cannot, so the mouth is dry and vexed.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This details and distinguishes the signs of those with dampness in whom the ying-nutrient is hot and the qi cold, with upper and lower, inside and outside, obstructing each other. In those with dampness, sweating only from the head means cold-damp is blocking the yang in the head. Moreover, cold-damp contends within the meridians, so the back is stiff; and he cannot bear the cold and wants to be covered with bedding and face the fire; clearly the exterior pathogen is one-sidedly obstructing the outside, with heat outside and cold inside. If one purges early without waiting for it to turn to heat, this is the so-called "attacking the heat will surely cause hiccup." Or the yang of the upper jiao is insufficient and the chest is full; the bladder is hot and urination is inhibited; and the tongue has what looks like fur but is not fur; clearly there is heat in the cinnabar field, so urination is inhibited, and cold above the chest, so the chest is full and the tongue furred. Even though he is thirsty and wants to drink, he cannot drink; this is not thirst from heat above, but the ying-nutrient aspect of the lower jiao is hot and wants water, while the qi aspect of the upper jiao is cold and cannot drink, so the mouth is merely dry and vexed. Then, in order to regulate the cold and heat and harmonize upper and lower, should not one who treats dampness have a separate, flexible method?

## Notes

A clause describing the changes caused by mistakenly purging too early a person with dampness who has cold-damp in the exterior. The same clause appears in the *Shanghan lun*'s chapter on tetany, dampness, and summer heat stroke.

- Sweating from the head, stiffness of the back, and aversion to cold (wanting bedding and fire) are signs of cold-damp in the exterior. Purging this early causes hiccup, chest fullness, inhibited urination, etc.
- "Heat in the cinnabar field and cold above the chest" expresses a discrepancy of heat below and cold above, which is said to be why he is thirsty but cannot drink.
- 胎 in "something like fur on the tongue" is the same as 苔 ("fur").
- The base reads 丹曰; it is emended to 丹田 because all other sources so read (see the emendation note).

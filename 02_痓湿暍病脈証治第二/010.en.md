# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Tetany with moxibustion sores is difficult to cure.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Treating tetany ultimately centers on clearing the exterior. In those with moxibustion sores, the acupoints are penetrated, fire-heat is exuberant within, and yin qi is already deficient. With the later Gualou Guizhi tang (Karokeishito; Trichosanthes and Cinnamon Twig Decoction) and Gegen tang (Kakkonto; Pueraria Decoction), one is wary that they do not keep away from heat; with Da Chengqi tang (Daijokito; Major Qi-Coordinating Decoction), one further worries about damaging the yin. Hence "difficult to cure."

## Notes

A clause stating that tetany with sores from moxibustion is difficult to cure.

- The fire-heat of moxibustion has already damaged the fluids and yin-blood, so neither sweating nor purging can easily be used.
- The base reads 炙 ("roast") for 灸 ("moxibustion"); it is emended because all other sources read 灸 (see the emendation note).

# Clause 19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When wind and dampness contend, and the whole body aches and hurts, it should by rule be resolved by sweating. At a time when the weather is overcast and the rain does not stop, the physician says sweating may be induced; yet after sweating the disease is not cured. Why is this? When sweating is induced and the sweat pours out greatly, only the wind qi leaves and the damp qi remains; therefore it is not cured. In treating wind-damp, when inducing sweating, one should only make it seem as though sweat were faintly about to come out; then wind and dampness both leave.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that when wind and dampness are equal, it should be resolved by sweating, but not excessively. When wind and dampness contend and there is aching pain, by rule it should be resolved by sweating. When the weather is overcast and rainy, the dampness is even greater, and there is no doubt that sweating may be induced; why then is it not cured? Wind is urgent by nature and can be driven out suddenly; dampness is stagnant by nature and should be resolved gradually. When sweat pours out greatly, the wind leaves suddenly but the dampness does not, so it is not cured. If it is induced slightly, [the sweat] comes out slowly; and when slow, wind and dampness both leave. That being so, dampness in the human body is sticky and hard to remove; if even sudden sweating is not allowed, how much less sudden purging! So the earlier section says, "if purged, the patient dies," while here it says only "not cured," showing that this is an improper use of the method, not comparable to mistaken purging.

## Notes

A clause stating that when treating wind-damp disease by sweating, the sweat should be slight rather than profuse.

- If sweating is induced for dampness disease during a rainy spell and the sweat is profuse, the wind leaves but the dampness remains and it is not cured; a gentle, slight sweat removes both wind and dampness.
- This principle connects with the administration instructions of later formulas such as Mahuang jia Zhu tang (Maokajutsuto): "cover to obtain what seems a slight sweat."

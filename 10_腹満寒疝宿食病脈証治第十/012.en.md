# Clause 12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When pressing below the heart reveals fullness and pain, this is excess; it should be purged. Da Chaihu tang (Daisaikoto; Major Bupleurum Decoction) is appropriate and governs it.

Da Chaihu tang formula

Chaihu (saiko) half a jin; huangqin (ogon) 3 liang; shaoyao (shakuyaku) 2 liang; banxia (hange) half a sheng (washed); zhishi (kijitsu) 4 pieces; dahuang (daio) [base: 大棗 "dazao (taiso)"] 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces; shengjiang (shokyo) 5 liang.

Of the eight ingredients above, with 1 dou 2 sheng of water boil down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This too is a formula of double resolution, but it is designed for cases in which Taiyang has already passed to Shaoyang. Pain below the heart on pressure is disease of something tangible; hence "this is excess; it should be purged." Da Chaihu tang is used because, without departing from the harmonization of Xiao Chaihu tang (Shosaikoto), it slightly pares away the tangible pathogen.

[Part of Xu Bin's formula:] zhishi 4 pieces (mix-fried); dahuang 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces; shengjiang 5 liang.

## Notes

A clause using Da Chaihu tang for an excess pattern with fullness and pain below the heart on pressure.

- Da Chaihu tang is Xiao Chaihu tang without renshen (ninjin) and gancao (kanzo), plus dahuang, zhishi, and shaoyao, treating excess of Shaoyang and Yangming together. It also appears in the *Shanghan lun*.
- In the base formula "dazao 2 liang" and "dazao 12 pieces" both appear; the former is an error for "dahuang 2 liang" (the other sources read 大黄), which the translation follows. For shaoyao, all other sources give 3 liang.

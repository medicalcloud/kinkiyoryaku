# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Great cold and pain within the heart and chest, retching with inability to eat or drink, cold in the abdomen surging up so that the skin rises, showing what looks like heads and feet, pain above and below that cannot bear touch or approach: the following decoction is appropriate.

Da Jianzhong tang (Major Center-Fortifying Decoction) formula

Shujiao 2 ge (with the "sweat" [base: 汁 "juice"] removed); ganjiang 4 liang; renshen 2 liang.

Of the three ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add 1 sheng of malt sugar, decoct over a low fire down to 1.5 sheng, and take warm in two doses. After about the time it takes to cook rice, one may drink 2 sheng of gruel, then take the next dose. For one day one should eat gruel and keep warmly covered.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

From here on are all methods for treating cold pain. Within the heart and chest is originally where yang qi works; now there is great cold obstructing the healthy qi, so there is pain. The healthy qi wants to descend while yin cold counterflows upward, so there is retching. The stomach yang is impeded by cold, so one cannot eat or drink. Further, the abdomen too is cold, the cold qi floats to the skin showing the color of false heat, and above and below both hurt so that one cannot bring a hand near. This is cold qi, carrying deficiency, filling above and below, inside and out. But the fault is not in the kidney, so ganjiang and renshen are combined with malt sugar to establish the central qi, and shujiao, which reaches downward by nature, warms and rouses the yang of the lower jiao as well; it is the main formula for warming the center.

[Xu Bin's formula:] shujiao 2 ge (stir-fried to remove the sweat); ganjiang 4 liang; renshen 1 liang.

... take it; for one day one should eat gruel and keep warmly covered.

## Notes

A clause using Da Jianzhong tang for great cold in the chest and abdomen with severe pain, with intestinal movement visible through the skin (bulges like things with heads and feet), so painful it cannot be touched.

- Da Jianzhong tang strongly warms the middle jiao with shujiao and ganjiang and supplements with renshen and malt sugar. It is sometimes related to disorders of intestinal motility (such as ileus).
- For "the following decoction" the other sources read "Da Jianzhong tang governs it." For 汁 in the shujiao entry, most other sources read 去汗 ("stir-fry to remove the oil").

# Clause 18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold *shan* with pain in the abdomen, and pain of the ribsides with abdominal tension: the following formula is appropriate.

Danggui Shengjiang Yangrou tang (Angelica, Fresh Ginger, and Mutton Decoction) formula

Danggui 3 liang; shengjiang 5 liang; yangrou (mutton) 1 jin.

Of the three ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil down to 3 sheng and take 7 ge warm, three times a day. If cold predominates, increase the shengjiang to 1 jin; if pain predominates with retching, add 2 liang of jupi and 1 liang of baizhu. When shengjiang is increased, also add 5 sheng of water, boil down to 3 sheng 2 ge, and take it.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

When cold *shan* reaches abdominal pain and the ribsides also hurt, both abdomen and ribsides are governed by cold qi and there is no longer any boundary. With added abdominal tension, the ying-nutrient blood within is insufficient, and the yin qi cannot nourish, so there is contraction and tension without relaxation. So danggui and mutton both supplement and warm, and shengjiang diffuses and disperses the cold. Mutton is used rather than renshen, according to "when the body is insufficient, supplement it with flavor." Adding jupi and baizhu for much pain with retching is because stomach deficiency predominates.

## Notes

A clause using Danggui Shengjiang Yangrou tang for cold *shan* due to blood deficiency and cold, with abdominal pain, rib-side pain, and abdominal tension. The same formula appears for postpartum abdominal pain in the later chapter on postpartum diseases.

- Danggui supplements blood, shengjiang disperses cold, and mutton warms and supplements; it doubles as a food remedy.
- For "the following formula," the other sources read "Danggui Shengjiang Yangrou tang governs it."

# Clause 22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: How does one tell that a person's illness involves abiding food? The master said: The pulse at the inch opening is floating and large, but on pressure, on the contrary [base: 及], hesitant; at the chi it is also feeble and hesitant; so we know there is abiding food. Da Chengqi tang (Daijokito) governs it.

## Notes

A clause on the pulse and treatment of abiding food (food that remains undigested and stagnant in the stomach and intestines). Xu Bin's commentary is attached after unit 24.

- The floating, large cun pulse that is hesitant on pressure, and the feeble, hesitant chi pulse, are attributed to food blocking the middle jiao so that qi and blood do not move.
- For 及 in 按之及濇, all other sources read 反 ("on the contrary"), which the translation follows.

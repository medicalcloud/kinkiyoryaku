# Clause 22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: How does one tell that a person's illness involves abiding food? The master said: The pulse at the inch opening is floating and large, but on pressure, on the contrary [base: 及], rough; at the cubit it is also faint and rough; so we know there is abiding food. Da Chengqi tang governs it.

## Notes

A clause on the pulse and treatment of abiding food (food that remains undigested and stagnant in the stomach and intestines). Xu Bin's commentary is attached after unit 24.

- The floating, large inch pulse that is rough on pressure, and the faint, rough cubit pulse, are attributed to food blocking the middle burner so that qi and blood do not move.
- For 及 in 按之及濇, all other sources read 反 ("on the contrary"), which the translation follows.

# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a thin person has pain around the umbilicus, there must be wind-cold, and the grain qi does not move. If he is purged instead, the qi will surely surge upward; if it does not surge, there will be stuffiness below the heart.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that abdominal fullness and cold *shan* both arise from cold striking within, but abdominal fullness is sometimes excess, while cold *shan* generally belongs to cold, and they differ in how they break out.

Abdominal fullness is originally a disease of the spleen and stomach, and no pulse is more pertinent than the Fuyang. When the anterior tibial (Fuyang) pulse is feeble and wiry, feeble means yang deficiency and wiry means guest cold. Being deficient and receiving cold, and the abdomen being governed by the spleen, how could it not be full? The *Neijing* says, "When the zang organs are cold, disease of fullness arises." If it is not full, the spleen and stomach originally have heat, and the pathogen avoids excess and attacks deficiency; so the cold binds the heat and defecation is, on the contrary, difficult. When the pathogen attacks both ribsides and binds below them, both flanks ache. That the feeble, wiry pulse appears at the Fuyang below and the pain arises in the flanks naturally differs from wind received from above. Hence "this is deficiency cold rising from below." Internal cold must not be dispersed through the exterior; with warmth it leaves at once. Hence "one should [give] warming medicinals."

○ If the abdomen is entirely full: if deficient, it is formless cold and does not hurt; if excess, it is tangible pathogen and hurts, so it may be purged. When the tongue is yellow because of stomach heat, purging the heat makes the yellow leave with the heat; this shows purging is necessary.

○ When abdominal fullness waxes and wanes, it is not a disease clinging to the true [qi] of the zang organs. So with yang it lessens and with yin the fullness increases. Hence "this is cold; warming medicinals should be given."

○ If below is deficient and cold, there should be abdominal pain; but if the kidney is further extremely deficient and cannot secure itself, cold congests the spleen qi, producing a withered-yellow complexion — "withered" being a dark, pale yellow. When kidney cold enters upward, there is agitation without thirst (lacuna) there is actually pathogenic cold in the stomach, and the lower jiao has unceasing spontaneous diarrhea. This (lacuna) adding strength without fullness means the original qi has drained too much and cannot become full even if it would. Hence "with unceasing diarrhea, he dies."

○ As for cold *shan*, the pathogen arises not only in the spleen and stomach, so the pulse is examined solely at the inch opening. When the pulse is wiry, the wei-defensive qi is bound by pathogenic cold and does not move. Wind (lacuna) combines with the liver; the ribsides are the residence of the liver; so there is tension and pain below the ribs. The pathogen [enters] from (lacuna), hence shivering with aversion to cold.

○ But in those prone to cold-strike, [cold] always first enters through the skin and hair together with Yangming. So the counterpart of the lung (skin and hair) receives the pathogen, and clear snivel comes out with fever. The pathogen invades the stomach, causing yawning. When the pathogen does not move to the exterior, the complexion is harmonious. But when it does not move to the meridians of the exterior, it runs to the orifices of the exterior; so there is frequent sneezing.

○ If the cold that has struck does not move to the exterior but invades the interior, there is diarrhea. This is pathogen entering by taking advantage of deficiency; so we know the interior is deficient. But when the external pathogen is held back by the internal cold, the great qi cannot run entirely to the orifices; so he wants to sneeze but cannot, and we know there is cold in the belly.

○ Pain around the umbilicus is wind-cold lodged there. Being thin and weak, there is even less reason to suspect phlegm. If defecation is difficult, it is because the stomach is cold and the grain qi does not move. Purging him instead, the lower jiao, already deficient, is attacked by pathogen, and the mistaken purging stirs the kidney qi; so the qi will surely surge. If it does not surge, the yang in the kidney is still sufficient to resist; so the wind-cold at the umbilicus combines and stagnates below the heart, forming stuffiness.

## Notes

A clause stating that pain around the umbilicus in a thin person is due to wind-cold, and that mistaken purging causes the qi to surge upward, or if it does not surge, stuffiness below the heart.

- Xu Bin's commentary explains the clauses of units 1 through 8 together, separated by ○. The lacunae (闕) are in the Siku quanshu edition.

# Clause 16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold qi with reverse-flow: Chi wan (Red Pill) governs it.

Chi wan formula

Fuling 4 liang; banxia 4 liang (washed; [one] formula uses gui); wutou 2 liang (blast-fried); xixin 1 liang (the *Qianjin* reads renshen).

Powder the six [other sources: four] ingredients above, add zhenzhu (cinnabar) for color, make refined-honey pills the size of hemp seeds, and take three pills down with wine before meals, twice in the day and once at night. If there is no effect, increase slightly until there is an effect.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This is of the kind of direct strike in the *Shanghan lun*. There is no distress in the chest or abdomen, only reverse-flow. The four limbs are where yang qi arises; cold qi blocks it, so the yang qi does not connect smoothly and there are cold limbs, and the yin qi surges and fills, producing counterflow. So wutou and xixin subdue the internal cold, and fuling and banxia bring down the counterflowing phlegm qi. Coloring with zhenzhu: when cold, the qi floats, so something heavy settles it and also protects the heart. Zhenzhu is cinnabar.

[Xu Bin's text:] Powder the four ingredients above, add zhenzhu for color, make refined-honey pills the size of hemp seeds, take before meals with wine ...

## Notes

A clause using Chi wan for cold qi causing cold, counterflowing limbs (reverse-flow).

- Chi wan disperses cold with wutou and xixin and removes water-fluid retention with fuling and banxia; it is named for the red color given by cinnabar. The combination of wutou and banxia was later listed among the "eighteen incompatibilities," but here they are in the same formula.
- The formula has four ingredients, but the base says "six"; most other sources read "four."
- For 真珠 ("pearl"), most other sources read 真朱 (cinnabar); Xu Bin also reads 真朱 and identifies it as cinnabar.

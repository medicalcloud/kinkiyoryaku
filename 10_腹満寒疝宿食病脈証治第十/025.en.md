# Clause 25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When abiding food is in the upper stomach duct, one should induce vomiting; Guadi san (Melon Stalk Powder) is appropriate.

Guadi san formula

Guadi 1 *fen* (dry-fried until yellow); chixiaodou 1 *fen* (boiled).

Pound the two ingredients above into a powder; boil 7 ge of fermented soybeans to obtain the juice, mix in one *qian*-spoon of the powder, and take warm. If there is no vomiting, add a little more; stop when there is a satisfying vomit (it must not be given to those who have lost blood or are vacuous).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Abiding food is mostly in the stomach. But when one has suddenly eaten too much and it cannot go down, or the qi is congested above, the food has not yet descended into the stomach. What is above is made to go out over the top. So a small amount of guadi makes it well up, and chixiaodou is added to remove the counterflow of yin.

## Notes

A clause stating that abiding food in the upper stomach should be expelled by vomiting with Guadi san. Guadi san also appears in the *Shanghan lun*.

- It is not used for those who have lost blood or are weak.
- For 一銭匙, most other sources read 一銭匕.

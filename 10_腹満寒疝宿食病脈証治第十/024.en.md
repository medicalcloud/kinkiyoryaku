# Clause 24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diarrhea with not eating or drinking: there is abiding food; it should be purged. Da Chengqi tang is appropriate.

Da Chengqi tang formula (see under tetany above)

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

[On unit 13:] Earlier there is a clause "abdominal fullness that at times lessens [is cold and] should be warmed"; here it is distinguished by "lessening so little as to be not worth mentioning." It shows that what lessens slightly but is in fact not lessened should be treated as repletion with Da Chengqi tang. Compared with Sanwu tang this adds mangxiao, because there is much heat.

In general, whether a person's disease is exterior or interior, whether abiding food is transformed depends on that person's stomach qi. Not all diseases necessarily involve abiding food, but where it is present it must be distinguished and treated; that is, if the tangible evil is not removed, the intangible evil cannot be transformed. For example, the inch opening governs yang, and floating and large is a yang pulse, which does not necessarily indicate abiding food; but when grain qi is congested and exuberant, it too can be floating and large. However, when eating and drinking are immoderate, the yin receives it, and when the yin receives it, the blood is damaged first; so on pressure it is, on the contrary, rough. But a rough pulse does not specifically indicate abiding food; we know it is abiding food because the roughness is within the floating and large. The cubit is especially governed by yin. Yin arises from yang; the yin in the blood has been damaged by food, and further the middle burner is obstructed by food so that the qi is not diffused, and below the source of transformation loses its generation; so it too is faint and rough. The evil belongs to the tangible, so it is fitting to expel it drastically with Da Chengqi tang.

If rapid and slippery: these are yang pulses, and slippery especially indicates internal repletion. What is this if not surplus of grain qi?

If there is diarrhea, the stomach is not in harmony. If, further, there is no desire to eat, what is this if not damage from food with aversion to food? So one knows abiding food without examining the pulse. In all these Da Chengqi tang is appropriate; since they all belong to the tangible, gentle treatment is not permissible.

## Notes

A clause stating that a person with diarrhea who does not eat should also be purged with Da Chengqi tang if there is abiding food. It is regarded as an example of "treating flow by promoting flow" (curing diarrhea by purging).

- For 不飲食 ("not eating or drinking"), most other sources read 不欲食 ("no desire to eat").
- The base lacks 当; it is supplied because all other sources have it (see the emendation note).
- The first paragraph of Xu Bin's commentary concerns unit 13 (fullness that does not lessen), and the rest explains the abiding-food clauses of units 22 to 24 together.

# Clause 20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is rapid and tight, it is wiry, like a bowstring, not moving under pressure. A rapid, wiry pulse means one should purge the cold. A tight, large, slow pulse means there must be hardness below the heart. A large, tight pulse means there is yin within yang, and it may be purged.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This says that wiry and tight is the main pulse of cold *shan*, but there are rapid-and-tight and large-and-tight variants, all with yin within [yang], and all should have the cold purged; so this sums up the variations of the cold *shan* pulse. Tight is originally a cold pulse. Even if rapid and tight, tight does not depart from wiry. Being like a bowstring and not moving under pressure means that because of the tightness there is a taut, strained quality. The seven characters "like a bowstring, not moving under pressure" annotate the tight pulse most aptly. So just below it says "rapid and wiry" without mentioning tight again, meaning wiry is tight. But though rapid, yin is within yang; hence "one should purge the cold." If tight, large, and slow — large is a yang pulse, but carrying tightness and also slow, the internal cold is severe and binds as stuffiness; hence "there must be hardness below the heart," the kind called "hard below the heart, large as a plate." If only large and tight, it is clearly yang wrapping yin; hence "there is yin within yang, and it may be purged," i.e., purging with the earlier Dahuang Fuzi Xixin tang.

## Notes

A clause summarizing the variations of the cold *shan* pulse and stating that the cold should be purged while warming (warm purging).

- A rapid, tight pulse is a wiry pulse taut as a bowstring, with excess cold, so purge. Tight, large, and slow: hardness below the heart. Large and tight: yin (cold) within yang, so purging is permissible.
- Xu Bin's "Dahuang Fuzi Xixin tang" refers to the earlier Dahuang Fuzi tang.

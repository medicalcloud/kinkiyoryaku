# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold qi in the abdomen, with thunderous rumbling and cutting pain, counterflow fullness of the chest and ribsides, and vomiting: Fuzi Jingmi tang (Bushikobeito; Aconite and Rice Decoction) governs it.

Fuzi Jingmi tang formula

Fuzi (bushi) 1 piece (blast-fried); banxia (hange) half a sheng; gancao (kanzo) 1 liang; dazao (taiso) 10 pieces; jingmi (kobei) half a sheng.

Of the five ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water boil until the rice is cooked and the decoction is ready, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The excellence of this formula lies in the rice. Rumbling with pain means there is cold qi in the abdomen. That the fullness is not in the abdomen but in the chest and ribsides means the pathogen is high while the pain is low; the cold is in excess and rises from below — what is called "when the kidney is deficient, cold stirs within." So there is also retching counterflow without fever. Fuzi warms the kidney and disperses cold, banxia removes retching counterflow, and rice alone, with gancao and dazao, regulates the stomach and establishes the central qi. Zhu is not used for fear of congesting the qi.

## Notes

A clause using Fuzi Jingmi tang for cold in the abdomen with rumbling and severe pain, distension of the chest and ribsides, and vomiting.

- Fuzi warms interior cold, banxia stops vomiting, and rice, gancao, and dazao nourish the stomach.

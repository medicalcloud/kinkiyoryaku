# Clause 10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hu Huo disease resembles cold damage: the patient is silent and wants to sleep but cannot close the eyes, and is restless whether lying down or getting up. Erosion of the throat is "confusion" (*huo*); erosion of the genitals is "fox" (*hu*). He does not want to eat or drink and dislikes the smell of food; his face and eyes are now red, now black, now white. When the upper part is eroded, the voice is hoarse (one version reads 嗄). Gancao Xiexin tang (Licorice Heart-Draining Decoction) governs it.

Gancao Xiexin tang formula

Gancao 4 liang; huangqin (scutellaria), renshen, ganjiang (dried ginger), 3 liang each; huanglian (coptis) 9 liang; dazao 12 pieces; banxia (pinellia) half a jin.

Of the seven ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

When the lower part is eroded, the throat is dry; wash it with Kushen tang (Flavescent Sophora Decoction).

For erosion of the anus, fumigate it with xionghuang (realgar).

Xionghuang fumigation formula

Powder the single ingredient above, burn it inside two tubular tiles put together, and fumigate toward the anus (the *Maijing* says: In the sick person [the disease] either rises with the breath to erode the throat, or from the lower jiao erodes [base: 飲 "drinks"] the anus and genitals; erosion above is confusion, erosion below is fox. For Hu Huo disease, Zhuling san (Polyporus Powder) governs it).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

[Xu Bin's formula:] gancao 4 liang (mix-fried); huangqin 3 liang; ganjiang 3 liang; banxia half a sheng; huanglian 1 liang; dazao 12 pieces (split); renshen 3 liang. Of the seven ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take warm.

[Ku]shen and xiong[huang] fumigation formula: decoct 1 sheng of kushen with 1 dou of water down to 7 sheng, remove the dregs, and fumigate and wash, three times a day. Xiong... [the rest is missing].

(Below there is the character 外.)

Hu Huo is worms. The worms are not foxes or confusion, but they are named after the disease, wishing people to grasp the meaning through the name. In general these are all diseases caused by damp-heat toxin. Hence "resembles cold damage": warm heat cannot help somewhat resembling cold damage, but the disease is not in the exterior. The yin aspect has received heat, so he is silent and wants to sleep. Yet he cannot close his eyes because yin fire is blocking the yang at the eyes. He is restless lying down or getting up because the disease is inside and outside and he cannot be at ease. Then, when the toxin is exuberant above and invades and erodes the throat, it is "confusion": heat spreads as if a confusing, disordering qi had been felt and fleas (worms) were generated. When the toxin is concentrated below and invades and erodes the genitals, it is "fox": it harms softly and hides darkly, like the secretive nature of a fox. "Erosion" means something seems to be eating it, but its form is not seen, like an eclipse of the sun or moon. Damp-heat is already exuberant, and yin fire damages the stomach, so he has no thought of food and dislikes its smell. The face is the outward manifestation of Yangming, and the eyes that of Jueyin; within, the toxic qi comes and goes, so they are now red, now black, now white, changing without constancy.

When the toxin is exuberant in the upper part, the damage is to the qi, and the voice is hoarse. The medicine used is Banxia Xiexin tang (Pinellia Heart-Draining Decoction). That is, although the disease is due to damp-heat toxin, if the central qi is made to move vigorously, the qi cannot counterflow, and how could heat gather above in the throat? So renshen, gancao, ganjiang, and dazao, which strengthen the central qi, are the sovereign; huangqin and huanglian, which clear heat, are the minister; and banxia, which brings down counterflow, is the assistant. When the toxin is exuberant in the lower part, the damage is to the blood, and the throat is dry; the larynx belongs to yang and the pharynx to yin. The medicine is fumigation and washing with kushen, to remove wind, clear heat, and kill worms. When it erodes the anus, it not only follows the meridian up to invade the throat, but the damp-heat is severe and causes ulceration below. So it is fumigated with xionghuang, whose power to kill worms, remove wind, and resolve toxin is even greater.

## Notes

A clause on the signs of Hu Huo disease (ulcers of the throat, genitals, and anus) and its treatment with Gancao Xiexin tang, Kushen tang, and xionghuang fumigation. Because of the oral and genital ulcers with eye symptoms, it is often compared to Behçet's disease today.

- Erosion of the throat is "confusion," erosion of the genitals "fox"; internally Gancao Xiexin tang is given, the genitals are washed with Kushen tang, and the anus is fumigated with xionghuang.
- The base lacks the formula of Kushen tang. Most other sources give "Kushen tang formula: kushen 1 sheng; decoct with 1 dou of water down to 7 sheng, remove the dregs, and fumigate and wash, three times a day."
- The amount of huanglian is 9 liang in the base and 1 liang in most other sources. For banxia, some sources read half a sheng.
- For 熏方 at the end of the xionghuang fumigation formula, all other sources read 熏之 ("fumigate it"). 飲 in the *Maijing* quotation of the interlinear note reads 蝕 ("erode") in all other sources and appears to be an error in the base; it is translated as 蝕.
- Xu Bin's commentary names the medicine "Banxia Xiexin tang," though both the text and Xu Bin's own formula have Gancao Xiexin tang. The character in Xu Bin's 生蚤 (gaiji KR0558) is rendered 蚤 ("flea") by its glyph, but the sense is probably "generating worms." The note "(below there is the character 外)" at the start of the commentary is unclear.

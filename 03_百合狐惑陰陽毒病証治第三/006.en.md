# Clause 6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Lily disease unresolved after a month that has turned into thirst: Baihe Xi fang (Lily Bulb Washing Formula) governs it.

Baihe Xi fang

Of the above, soak 1 sheng of baihe in 1 dou of water overnight and wash the body [with it]. After washing, eat boiled noodles (*zhubing*); do not [add] salt or fermented soybeans.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

There is yang thirst and yin thirst. If lily disease is unresolved after a month and turns into thirst, there is no doubt that it is yin vacuity with blazing fire. When yin is vacuous and the evil qi spreads, how could the yang not fall ill along with it? So one washes the skin and hair with baihe, so that the pores of the skin and hair become balanced and pass qi to the yin. This is the principle that "the lung receives the court of the hundred vessels and conveys the essence and fluids to the skin and hair; the vessels combine the essence and move qi to the bowels." Eating boiled noodles borrows the qi of wheat to aid the essence and fluids. Not eating salt or fermented soybeans is out of fear of damaging the yin-blood.

[Xu Bin's formula:] Soak 1 sheng of baihe in 1 dou of water overnight and wash the body. After washing, eat boiled noodles (do not eat salt or fermented soybeans).

## Notes

A clause using an external method — washing the body with water in which baihe has soaked — for lily disease that has dragged on and developed thirst.

- Since the lung corresponds to the skin and hair, the qi of baihe is taken in through the skin to moisten the lung and stop the thirst.
- *Zhubing* is boiled food made from wheat flour (noodles). Salt and fermented soybeans are avoided because salty flavor increases thirst.
- The base reads 煮 for 者; it is emended because all other sources read 者 (see the emendation note). 巳 in 洗巳 is an error for 已.

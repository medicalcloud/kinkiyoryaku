# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A sick person has a rapid pulse but no heat, slight vexation, is silent and wants only to lie down, and sweats. In the first three or four days after contracting it, the eyes are red like a turtledove's eyes; after seven or eight days, the four canthi of the eyes turn black (one version has the character 黄 "yellow" here). If he can eat, pus has already formed. Chidou Danggui san (Sekishozutokisan; Aduki Bean and Angelica Powder) governs it.

Chixiaodou Danggui san (Sekishozutokisan) formula

Chixiaodou (sekishozu; aduki bean) 3 sheng (soak until sprouts come out, then dry in the sun); danggui (Chinese angelica).

Pound the two ingredients above into a powder and take a square-inch spoonful with fermented rice water (*jiangshui*), three times a day.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of a person ill with damp-heat invading the yin, similar to Hu Huo but more severe. So "a sick person," following the Hu Huo pattern, is a general term, like "a sick person" raised at the start of the discussion of static blood in the chapter on fright palpitations; it does not refer to Hu Huo disease. This is clear because the medicine used afterward is entirely different from that for Hu Huo.

A rapid pulse means the yin aspect is hot. No heat means it is not in the exterior. Further, slight vexation, silence with only wanting to lie down, and sweating show that the yin aspect is hot. But in the beginning it is limited to slight heat, so for two or three days the eyes are red like a turtledove's eyes. The eyes connect with Jueyin, and heat qi takes advantage of them, so they are red. *Jiu* is the turtledove. After seven or eight days the heat reaches its extreme and the flesh is damaged, so the four canthi turn black. When fire overwhelms the stomach, one can eat instead. When the flesh is damaged there is pus. Hence "pus has already formed." But in Hu Huo one only wants to sleep; here wanting to lie down means being drowsy and wanting to sleep, the pathogen overwhelming only the yin, and more severe. Chidou and danggui are used because chixiaodou is good at removing dampness, resolving toxin, and clearing heat, and danggui is acrid and dispersing and governs disease of the yin aspect of the lower jiao. So it leads the beans into the blood aspect to remove the damp-heat toxin; it is not supplementing.

[Xu Bin's formula:] chixiaodou 3 sheng (soak until hairs [sprouts] come out, then dry in the sun); danggui 10 liang.

## Notes

A clause using Chixiaodou Danggui san when red eyes progress to blackening of the canthi and pus forms (a type of Hu Huo disease, or a separate disease).

- Chixiaodou disinhibits damp-heat and resolves toxin, and danggui moves the blood and expels pus. The same formula appears in the later clause on bleeding (blood before stool).
- The base gives no amount for danggui. Wikisource and others have 3 liang; Xu Bin's edition has 10 liang.
- 牙 in 浸令牙出 is the same as 芽 ("sprout"). 巳 in 膿巳成 is an error for 已.
- The clause's "Chidou Danggui san" is "Chixiaodou Danggui san" in the formula heading.
- Xu Bin's "two or three days" differs from the text's "three or four days" (Xu Bin's text reads 三両日).

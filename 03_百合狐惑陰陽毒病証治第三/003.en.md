# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Lily bulb disease after purging: Huashi Daizhe tang (Talc and Hematite Decoction) governs it.

Huashi Daizhe tang formula

Baihe 7 pieces (split); huashi (talc) 3 liang (crushed, in a cotton bag); daizheshi (hematite) one piece the size of a pellet (wrapped in cotton).

Of the above, first wash the baihe with water and soak it overnight; white froth should come out. Discard that water, then decoct with 2 sheng of spring water down to 1 sheng and remove the dregs. Separately, decoct the huashi and daizhe with 2 sheng of spring water down to 1 sheng and remove the dregs. Afterward combine them and decoct again down to 1.5 sheng, and take warm in divided doses.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The twelve meridians all pay court to the lung, and the qi opening forms the cun. So Zhongjing, explaining lily bulb disease, says, "the hundred vessels have one source, and all give rise to the disease." Does this not mean that the indescribable diseases of the hundred vessels take the lung as their single source in becoming ill? Baihe is sweet, neutral, and slightly bitter in flavor, white in color, yin within yang, a medicinal that supplements the lung. Seeing that it is used as the chief ingredient and the disease is named after baihe, is not Zhongjing's intent to treat through the lung all the more evident?

Why then does he not mention the lung explicitly? Lily bulb disease is residual pathogen of cold damage lingering in the yang meridians and soaking into the yin of the various bowels, with no healthy qi to command it; each falls ill on its own, pulling on one another as if issuing from one source, yet not one of the signs is a lung sign. Thus we know that although the disease is not in the lung, the lung's function of regulation is in fact not operating. Therefore, because baihe closes at night and belongs to yin, is white and returns to the lung, and its scales cling together, closing everywhere, it is used chiefly to supplement the healthy qi of the lung so that it joins with the other organs, and to put their stagnation in order.

When [lily bulb disease] comes after sweating, excessive sweating has damaged yang; yang is deficient and heat depressed, and one can neither attack nor supplement. So baihe, together with zhimu, which protects the lung, clears the stomach, and enriches the kidney, nourishes the yin, and spring water is added to clear the heat; then the yang pathogen transforms by itself. When it comes after purging, excessive purging has damaged yin; the deficient pathogen is in the yin, the yin is deficient and fire counterflows, and neither attacking nor supplementing helps. So baihe, together with huashi, which frees the orifices, and daizhe, which settles counterflow, frees the yang qi, and spring water is added to drain the heat; then the yin qi regulates itself.

[Xu Bin's formula:] baihe 7 pieces; huashi 3 liang (crushed, wrapped in cotton); daizheshi (like a pellet, crushed, wrapped in cotton).

## Notes

A clause using Huashi Daizhe tang after lily bulb disease has been mistakenly purged.

- After purging, the yin is damaged, qi counterflows upward, and urination becomes inhibited; huashi disinhibits heat and daizheshi settles counterflow.
- For the base's 弾大一枚, most other sources read 如弾丸大一枚 ("one piece the size of a pellet"). For 綿嚢 ("cotton bag"), all other sources read 綿裹 ("wrapped in cotton").
- Xu Bin explains lily bulb disease as a lung disease, and also discusses the preceding Baihe Zhimu tang.

# Clause 1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Discussion: In lily bulb disease, the hundred vessels have one source, and all give rise to the disease. The patient wants to eat but cannot eat, is always silent, wants to lie down but cannot, wants to walk but cannot. Food and drink are sometimes delicious, and at other times he cannot bear the smell of food. He seems cold but is not cold, seems hot but is not hot. The mouth is bitter and the urine red. No medicine can cure it; on taking medicine he vomits and has diarrhea severely, as if possessed by spirits. The body seems in harmony, and the pulse is feeble and rapid.

If he has a headache every time he urinates, he recovers only after sixty days. If when urinating the head does not ache but he shivers, he recovers in forty days. If urination is comfortable and there is only dizziness, he recovers in twenty days. The signs may appear in advance before [another] illness, or appear four or five days into an illness, or appear slightly after twenty days or a month of illness. In each case treat according to the signs.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

This speaks of people with cold damage or deficiency/consumption in whom, in either case, healthy qi cannot fend off the pathogen, so that it soaks through; the pulse and signs become confused, and one can no longer distinguish the meridians. Lily bulb disease means that all the hundred vessels of the body are ill, yet as if there were a source governing them, so that each falls ill and none can hold its disease by itself. One perceives only that walking, standing, sitting, lying, eating, and drinking are all hindered, with signs such as cold and heat, bitter mouth, red urine, and vomiting and diarrhea; moreover, on taking medicine it gets worse, while the body seems in harmony instead, with nothing at all to grasp except a feeble, rapid pulse. [This] seems to belong to (lacuna), turning into affliction. Among the signs, inability to eat and silence with inability to lie down seem to belong to (lacuna); bitter mouth seems to belong to Shaoyang; red urine seems to belong to Taiyang; vomiting and diarrhea seem to belong to the sanjiao. It is a disease of the bowels that has not yet entered deeply into the zang organs; so there is fear that the pathogen lingers long in the yang meridians and binds in the brain, making it hard to shake off quickly; but it is not an incurable disease.

Only when there is headache on urination do we know it is deep; hence "recovers after sixty days," meaning that after the moon has circled twice and yin prevails, the damaging pathogen subsides by itself. No headache but shivering means the disease is somewhat shallower. Comfortable urination with dizziness means the pathogen is even shallower. So the days to recovery become progressively shorter. As for the order and timing of the onset, all depends on the depth of the internal qi and of the accumulated pathogen. Hence "in each case treat according to the signs."

The *Qianjin* says, "When the condition is aversion to cold with retching, the disease is in the upper jiao; it should recover in twenty-three days. When the condition is abdominal fullness, slight dyspnea, and hard stool, with one bowel movement every three or four days that is at times somewhat loose, the disease is in the middle jiao; it should recover in sixty-three days. When the condition is dribbling, difficult urination, the disease is in the lower jiao; it should recover in thirty-three days. In each case treat according to the signs." Then we know that in this disease the bound pathogen is within, with slight distinctions among the three jiao, and that its treatment too should distinguish the three jiao and harmonize them.

## Notes

The general clause on lily bulb disease (*baihe bing*). Lily bulb disease shows elusive symptoms: the patient's mind is unsettled, appetite, sleep, and walking do not go as wished, there seems to be cold and heat but is not, the mouth is bitter and the urine red. It is named after lily bulb (*baihe*), the chief medicinal of the formulas used to treat it.

- "The hundred vessels have one source" is understood to mean that all the vessels of the body return to one source (often said to be the heart or lung), so that when it is ill, symptoms extend throughout the body.
- The days until recovery (sixty, forty, or twenty) are judged by the presence of headache on urination, etc.
- Many interpretations relate it to residual heat after febrile disease or to emotional disorders.
- For 浙然, all other sources read 淅然 ("shivering"), and 浙 appears to be a graphic error; it is translated as 淅然. For 黙然, most other sources read 黙黙.
- The lacunae (闕) in Xu Bin's commentary are in the Siku quanshu edition.

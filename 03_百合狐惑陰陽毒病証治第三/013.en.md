# Clause 13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yin toxin disease: the face and eyes are green-blue, the body aches as if beaten with a stick, and the throat is sore. At five days it can be treated; at seven days it cannot. Shengma Biejia tang without xionghuang and shujiao governs it.

Shengma Biejia tang formula

Shengma 2 liang; danggui 1 liang; shujiao (stir-fried to remove the "sweat" [base: 汁 "juice"]) [1] liang; gancao 2 liang; biejia one piece the size of a finger (mix-fried); xionghuang half a liang (ground).

Of the six ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water boil down to 1 sheng and take it in a single dose; the elderly and children take it in two doses. Obtain sweating (in the *Zhouhou* and *Qianjin* formularies, Shengma tang is used for yang toxin, without biejia and with gui; Gancao tang is used for yin toxin, without xionghuang).

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

When pathogenic cold strikes the yin meridians directly and does not resolve for a long time, it becomes toxin. Hence there is yin toxin disease. In this disease, [the cold] strikes the kidney directly and soaks into the liver and spleen; the cold qi is piercing, and wherever it reaches there is pain. The face and eyes are where the essence of the liver and spleen reaches. Earth receives the invasion of cold, and wood takes advantage of it, so the color is green-blue. Cold invades the flesh and contends with the wei-defensive qi, so there is pain as if beaten with a stick. The throat also hurts because the Shaoyin vessel rises to the pharynx, so anyone with hidden cold must have a sore throat. The larynx belongs to yang, but when the pain is severe the qi responds. But pathogen always passes on and goes deeper, and when deep it is hard to treat; so it also says, "at five days it can be treated; at seven days it cannot."

Shengma Biejia tang is used with only shujiao and xionghuang removed because when yin pathogen becomes toxin, even though it is yin, it has the qi of yin dryness; warming it does not help, and attacking it is also one-sided and of little help. So the warmth of shujiao and the violence of xionghuang are removed, and only biejia and danggui are used to go to the liver and harmonize the yin to stop pain, and shengma and gancao to raise and disperse from the spleen and transform the cold. That is, breaking it directly carries the danger of harsh dryness, which is not as good as obtaining the effect of dispersal and resolution through acrid and neutral [medicinals].

[Xu Bin's formula:] shengma 2 liang; shujiao 1 liang (stir-fried to remove the sweat); xionghuang 5 *qian* (ground); gancao ... [the rest is missing].

For yin toxin, remove shujiao and xionghuang.

## Notes

A clause using Shengma Biejia tang without xionghuang and shujiao for yin toxin (green-blue face and eyes, body aching as if beaten, sore throat), followed by the formula of Shengma Biejia tang.

- For yang toxin the formula is used as is; for yin toxin, xionghuang and shujiao are removed. Since removing the warm shujiao for yin toxin seems unnatural, this has long been debated.
- In the base the amount of shujiao reads 炒去汁両 with the number missing. All other sources read 炒去汗一両, and the translation supplies it accordingly. "Sweat" (汗) refers to stir-frying shujiao to bring out its oil.
- The interlinear note, by the Song editors, records the differences in the yang toxin and yin toxin formulas in the *Zhouhou* and *Qianjin*.

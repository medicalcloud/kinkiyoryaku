# Clause 8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Lily bulb disease that has turned into fever (another [read: one] version reads "into cold and heat"): Baihe Huashi san (Lily Bulb and Talc Powder) governs it.

Baihe Huashi san formula

Baihe 1 liang (mix-fried); huashi 3 liang.

Make the above into a powder and take a square-inch spoonful with a drink, three times a day. There should be slight diarrhea; [then] stop taking it, and the heat will be removed.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

Zhongjing once said, "When it arises in the yang region, the person shivers with cold and has fever." Then we know that turning into fever means the internal heat does not stop, soaks into the flesh and skin, and the yang aspect also becomes hot. So huashi clears the heat in the abdomen, harmonizing the inside and calming the outside, together with baihe to strengthen and regulate the lung qi. Spring water is not used because the heat is already outside, and one does not want excessive cold to damage the yin. Hence "there should be slight diarrhea," meaning that when the qi is somewhat dredged and the yin calmed, [the heat] is removed.

## Notes

A clause using Baihe Huashi san when lily bulb disease has turned into fever.

- Huashi disinhibits urination to remove heat, and baihe moistens the lung. One stops taking it when urination flows and the stool becomes slightly loose.
- For 二作 in the interlinear note, all other sources read 一作 ("one version reads"), and 二 appears to be an error for 一.

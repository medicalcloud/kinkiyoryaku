# Clause 2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In liver disease, forbid acrid; in heart disease, forbid salty; in spleen disease, forbid sour; in lung disease, forbid bitter; in kidney disease, forbid sweet. In spring do not eat liver; in summer do not eat heart; in autumn do not eat lung; in winter do not eat kidney; in the four seasonal junctures do not eat spleen. Explanation: one does not eat liver in spring because liver qi is in its prime and spleen qi is defeated; if one eats liver, it further supplements the liver, and the defeat of spleen qi becomes even worse and cannot be rescued. Moreover, while the liver is in its prime, one must not let dead qi enter the liver, for fear of harming the ethereal soul. If it is not its prime, [the liver] is vacuous, and supplementing it with liver is good. The other viscera follow the same rule.

## Translation of Xu Bin's commentary

The five phrases beginning "in liver disease, forbid acrid" are for fear of aiding the enemy. The five phrases beginning "in spring do not eat liver" are for fear that a declining viscus will be cut off one-sidedly. The theory of dead qi entering the liver has a very subtle rationale. When a viscus is at its time of prime, its vital qi arises; if dead liver is joined to it, the dead qi borrows the prime and revives, so the dead qi overrides the liver and attacks the life-giving qi. If it is not the time of prime, then even with dead qi, it cannot ride on the prime, and there is no vital qi to draw it, so the dead qi does not revive; it merely serves to supplement. Hence "supplementing it with liver is good."

## Notes

A clause stating the flavors forbidden in diseases of the five viscera and the animal organs not to be eaten in each season.

- The forbidden flavors follow the restraining cycle of the five phases (e.g., acrid = metal restrains liver = wood).
- For 敗 in 敗尤甚, the base reads 反, but all other sources read 敗, so it has been emended (see the emendations).
